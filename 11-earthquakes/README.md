Earthquakes is the unit where Waves (Unit 8) and Plate Tectonics (Unit 10)
meet: earthquakes happen at plate boundaries, and everything we know about
them — location, depth, size — is measured through wave behavior.

## Key concepts to master

- **Cause** — sudden release of stress built up at plate boundaries
  (especially at faults), radiating energy outward as seismic waves.
- **Seismic wave types** — P-waves (primary, longitudinal, fastest, travel
  through solids and liquids), S-waves (secondary, transverse, slower,
  travel only through solids), and surface waves (slowest, most
  destructive, travel along Earth's surface).
- **Focus vs. epicenter** — the focus (hypocenter) is where the rupture
  starts underground; the epicenter is the point on the surface directly
  above it.
- **Locating an epicenter** — using the difference in P-wave and S-wave
  arrival times at three or more seismic stations (triangulation), a
  direct application of the `v = fλ` / wave-speed relationships from Unit 8.
- **Measuring earthquakes** — magnitude (energy released, e.g., moment
  magnitude scale) vs. intensity (observed damage/shaking, e.g., Modified
  Mercalli scale) — two different things often confused.
- **Earthquake engineering and safety** — how building design (base
  isolation, flexible framing) reduces damage, directly connecting back to
  the collision-safety design thinking from the Momentum unit.

## Vocabulary checklist

fault · focus (hypocenter) · epicenter · P-wave · S-wave · surface wave ·
seismograph · magnitude · intensity · triangulation

## Learning objectives

By the end of this unit, students will be able to:

1. Explain why earthquakes occur at plate boundaries, connecting stress buildup and release to the plate tectonics model from Unit 10.
2. Distinguish P-waves, S-waves, and surface waves by speed, motion type, and the materials they can travel through.
3. Use P-wave/S-wave arrival-time differences at multiple stations to locate an earthquake's epicenter.
4. Distinguish magnitude from intensity and explain why the same earthquake can have different reported intensities in different locations.
5. Explain at least one engineering strategy that reduces earthquake damage.
