# NGSS Standards Alignment — California "Physics of the Universe" Course Model

This project follows the California Next Generation Science Standards
(CA NGSS) **Physics and Earth Science** integrated course model, known in
the CA Science Framework as **"Physics of the Universe."** Per course
guidance, the standards below are the actual **learning goals** for this
project — every unit is built to prepare students to demonstrate these
performance expectations (PEs), not just to "cover a topic."

**Sources:**
- CDE — [NGSS for California Public Schools](https://www.cde.ca.gov/pd/ca/sc/ngssstandards.asp)
- CA Science Framework, [Chapter 7: High School Three-Course Model](https://www.cde.ca.gov/ci/sc/cf/documents/scifwchapter7.pdf) — "Physics of the Universe: Integrating Physics and Earth and Space Sciences"
- [nextgenscience.org](https://www.nextgenscience.org/) — full PE text, clarification statements, and assessment boundaries

The official course groups its performance expectations into six
**Instructional Segments (IS1–IS6)**. This project's unit folders map onto
those segments as shown below; a few PEs are cross-referenced in more than
one unit because the official framework itself bundles them that way
(e.g., seismic waves appear in both the Waves unit and the Earthquakes unit).

## Full performance-expectation text (for instructor reference)

<details>
<summary><strong>IS1 — Forces and Motion</strong> (Newton's Laws, Momentum units)</summary>

- **HS-PS2-1.** Analyze data to support the claim that Newton's second law of motion describes the mathematical relationship among the net force on a macroscopic object, its mass, and its acceleration. *(Assessment limited to 1D motion, non-relativistic speeds.)*
- **HS-PS2-2.** Use mathematical representations to support the claim that the total momentum of a system of objects is conserved when there is no net force on the system. *(Limited to two macroscopic bodies in one dimension.)*
- **HS-PS2-3\*.** Apply scientific and engineering ideas to design, evaluate, and refine a device that minimizes the force on a macroscopic object during a collision. *(E.g., a football helmet or a parachute.)*
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>IS2 — Forces at a Distance</strong> (Electricity unit)</summary>

- **HS-PS2-4.** Use mathematical representations of Newton's Law of Gravitation and Coulomb's Law to describe and predict the gravitational and electrostatic forces between objects. *(Limited to systems with two objects.)*
- **HS-PS2-6\*.** Communicate scientific and technical information about why molecular-level structure is important in the functioning of designed materials. *(E.g., conductors, flexible durable materials, pharmaceuticals.)*
- **HS-ESS1-4.** Use mathematical or computational representations to predict the motion of orbiting objects in the solar system. *(Covered conceptually alongside gravitation.)*
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>IS3 — Energy Conversion and Renewable Energy</strong> (Work & Energy, Magnetism units)</summary>

- **HS-PS3-1.** Create a computational model to calculate the change in energy of one component in a system when changes in other components and energy flows are known.
- **HS-PS3-2.** Develop and use models to illustrate that energy at the macroscopic scale can be accounted for as either motions of particles or energy stored in fields.
- **HS-PS3-3\*.** Design, build, and refine a device that works within given constraints to convert one form of energy into another. *(E.g., wind turbines, solar cells, generators.)*
- **HS-PS2-5.** Plan and conduct an investigation to provide evidence that an electric current can produce a magnetic field and that a changing magnetic field can produce an electric current.
- **HS-PS3-5.** Develop and use a model of two objects interacting through electric or magnetic fields to illustrate the forces between objects and the changes in energy of the objects due to the interaction.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>IS4 — Nuclear Processes and Earth History</strong> (Plate Tectonics, Earthquakes, Nuclear Physics units)</summary>

- **HS-PS1-8.** Develop models to illustrate the changes in the composition of the nucleus of the atom and the energy released during the processes of fission, fusion, and radioactive decay. *(Qualitative models only; limited to alpha, beta, gamma decay.)*
- **HS-ESS1-5.** Evaluate evidence of the past and current movements of continental and oceanic crust and the theory of plate tectonics to explain the ages of crustal rocks.
- **HS-ESS1-6.** Apply scientific reasoning and evidence from ancient Earth materials, meteorites, and other planetary surfaces to construct an account of Earth's formation and early history.
- **HS-ESS2-1.** Develop a model to illustrate how Earth's internal and surface processes operate at different spatial and temporal scales to form continental and ocean-floor features. *(Mountains, valleys, trenches, ridges — constructive vs. destructive processes.)*
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>IS5 — Waves and Electromagnetic Radiation</strong> (Waves, Light, Earthquakes units)</summary>

- **HS-PS4-1.** Use mathematical representations to support a claim regarding relationships among the frequency, wavelength, and speed of waves traveling in various media. *(Explicitly includes seismic waves traveling through Earth — the direct link to the Earthquakes unit.)*
- **HS-PS4-2.** Evaluate questions about the advantages of using digital transmission and storage of information.
- **HS-PS4-3.** Evaluate the claims, evidence, and reasoning behind the idea that electromagnetic radiation can be described either by a wave model or a particle model.
- **HS-PS4-4.** Evaluate the validity and reliability of claims in published materials about the effects of different frequencies of electromagnetic radiation absorbed by matter.
- **HS-PS4-5\*.** Communicate technical information about how technological devices use wave behavior/interactions to transmit and capture information and energy.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>IS6 — Stars and the Origins of the Universe</strong> (extension of Nuclear Physics unit)</summary>

- **HS-ESS1-1.** Develop a model based on evidence to illustrate the life span of the sun and the role of nuclear fusion in the sun's core to release energy that reaches Earth as radiation.
- **HS-ESS1-2.** Construct an explanation of the Big Bang theory based on astronomical evidence of light spectra, motion of distant galaxies, and composition of matter in the universe.
- **HS-ESS1-3.** Communicate scientific ideas about the way stars, over their life cycle, produce elements.
</details>

\* Asterisked PEs integrate science with engineering (HS-ETS1 practices).

## Using this document

- **Instructors:** cite the relevant PE code(s) directly on assessments and report-outs — administrators and NGSS-aligned gradebooks generally expect the exact `HS-PSx-x` / `HS-ESSx-x` code.
- **Students:** you don't need to memorize the codes, but the "What's assessed" column tells you the *kind* of task (math modeling vs. evidence evaluation vs. design) you should expect on that unit's assessment — study accordingly.
- This document will be updated as semester 2 units move from **outline** to **full content** (Cornell notes, quiz, resources) — see each unit's `README.md` for its current status.
