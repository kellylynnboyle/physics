# Unit 3: Newton's Laws of Motion

**NGSS alignment:** HS-PS2-1 (use Newton's second law quantitatively to design/evaluate a solution for motion under force). Also supports the engineering-design tie-ins (HS-ETS1) many CA course models pair with this unit (e.g., egg-drop, crumple-zone labs).

**Suggested pacing:** 2.5 weeks (12–13 class periods), including 2 lab days (friction lab + Newton's 2nd Law/Atwood-style cart lab) and 1 review/assessment day.

## 1. Key Concepts Students Must Master

| # | Concept | Why it matters |
|---|---------|-----------------|
| 1 | Newton's 1st Law (Inertia) | An object at rest stays at rest, and an object in motion stays in motion at constant velocity, unless acted on by a net (unbalanced) force. |
| 2 | Mass vs. weight | Mass (kg) is the amount of matter, constant everywhere. Weight (N) = mg, a force that changes with local gravity. |
| 3 | Newton's 2nd Law | `F_net = ma`. Acceleration is directly proportional to net force and inversely proportional to mass. |
| 4 | Free body diagrams (FBDs) | A diagram showing all forces acting ON one object, each as an arrow with direction and relative size — the essential problem-solving tool for this unit. |
| 5 | Normal force | The support force perpendicular to a surface; NOT always equal to weight (changes on inclines, in elevators, when other forces have vertical components). |
| 6 | Friction | Static friction (prevents motion, up to a max value) vs. kinetic friction (opposes motion once sliding); `f = μN`. |
| 7 | Newton's 3rd Law | For every action force, there is an equal and opposite reaction force — acting on the OTHER object, not the same object. |
| 8 | Equilibrium | Net force = 0 does not mean no forces are acting — it means all forces balance (this includes objects at rest AND objects moving at constant velocity). |

## 5. Practice Problems (progression: easy → hard)

1. A 5 kg box sits at rest on a table. Draw the free body diagram and find the normal force.
2. A 1000 kg car accelerates at 2 m/s². What net force is required?
3. A 10 kg sled is pulled across level ice (frictionless) by a 20 N horizontal force. Find its acceleration.
4. A 10 kg box is pulled across a floor (μ_k = 0.3) by a 40 N horizontal force. Find the net force and acceleration, accounting for friction.
5. **Incline problem:** A 4 kg block sits on a frictionless 30° incline. Find the normal force and the acceleration of the block down the incline.
6. **Multi-step:** Two blocks (3 kg and 5 kg) are connected by a string over a frictionless pulley (Atwood machine). Find the acceleration of the system and the tension in the string.

## 6. Real-World Applications

- **Car safety:** seatbelts and airbags apply Newton's 1st Law (inertia) — your body keeps moving forward in a crash unless a force stops it.
- **Rocket propulsion:** Newton's 3rd Law — expelling exhaust gases downward pushes the rocket upward.
- **Vehicle design:** friction coefficients determine tire grip and stopping distance; engineers balance friction against fuel efficiency.
- **Elevators:** apparent weight changes (felt through the normal force) as the elevator accelerates up or down — a direct application of F_net = ma.
- **Sports:** a football lineman's mass and the force they can generate determine how much they can accelerate an opponent (F = ma in action).

## 7. Learning Objectives

By the end of this unit, students will be able to:

- [ ] State and apply Newton's three laws to real-world scenarios.
- [ ] Draw accurate free body diagrams for objects on flat surfaces, inclines, and in systems of connected objects.
- [ ] Distinguish between mass and weight, and correctly calculate weight from mass.
- [ ] Calculate normal force and friction force in level and inclined scenarios.
- [ ] Correctly identify Newton's 3rd Law action-reaction pairs (same magnitude, opposite direction, acting on two different objects).
- [ ] Apply F_net = ma to solve for unknown force, mass, or acceleration, including multi-force and multi-object systems.

## 8. Assessment Criteria

| Level | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| **Exceeds (4)** | Solves multi-body systems (pulleys, inclines with friction) using free body diagrams; correctly explains why action-reaction pairs never cancel on the same object. |
| **Meets (3)** | Draws correct FBDs for single objects on flat or inclined surfaces; applies F_net = ma accurately including friction. |
| **Approaching (2)** | Draws mostly correct FBDs but omits or mislabels a force; confuses mass and weight occasionally. |
| **Beginning (1)** | Cannot construct a free body diagram; treats mass and weight as interchangeable; cannot identify action-reaction pairs. |

**Suggested evidence:** unit test, friction lab report (measuring μ_s and μ_k experimentally), FBD practice set graded for completeness/accuracy, quiz retake after reviewing `quiz/index.html` missed-concept report.
