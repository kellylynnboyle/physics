## Topic: Forces and Motion

| Cues / Questions | Notes |
|---|---|
| Newton's 1st Law — what does it say? | An object stays at rest or at constant velocity unless acted on by a net (unbalanced) force. Also called the Law of Inertia. |
| What is inertia? | The tendency of an object to resist changes in its motion; directly related to mass — more mass = more inertia. |
| Newton's 2nd Law — the equation? | `F_net = m a`, or `a = F_net / m`. Net force causes acceleration in the same direction as the net force. |
| Mass vs. weight — what's the difference? | Mass (kg): amount of matter, same everywhere. Weight (N): force of gravity on an object, `W = mg`, changes with location (e.g., less on the Moon). |
| What is a free body diagram (FBD)? | A diagram of ONE object showing every force acting ON it as an arrow (direction + relative size). Forces the object exerts on OTHER things are never included. |
| What is normal force? | The perpendicular support force a surface exerts on an object. On a flat surface with no other vertical forces, N = mg. On an incline, N = mg cos θ. |
| Static vs. kinetic friction? | Static friction prevents an object from starting to move (up to a maximum value `f_s,max = μ_s N`). Kinetic friction acts once the object is already sliding (`f_k = μ_k N`), and is usually smaller than max static friction. |
| Newton's 3rd Law — what does it say? | For every action force, there's an equal and opposite reaction force. Key: the two forces act on TWO DIFFERENT objects, so they never cancel each other out. |
| What does "equilibrium" mean? | Net force = 0. This includes objects at rest AND objects moving at constant velocity — equilibrium does NOT mean zero forces, it means balanced forces. |
| Common mistake with inclines? | Forgetting that normal force is reduced on an incline: N = mg cos θ, not mg. Gravity must be split into components parallel (mg sin θ) and perpendicular (mg cos θ) to the incline surface. |
