## Five checks before you write the summary

1. What does Model 1 explain that Model 2 cannot? *(Think scale.)*
2. What does Model 2 explain that Model 1 cannot? *(Think mechanism — why matter holds together at all.)*
3. Where does the energy come from, where is it stored, and where does it go?
4. Your question says "where **AND** when." Where is the easy half. What makes the timing sudden after a long quiet period?
5. Apply the same three questions to Krakatoa: what was loading, what was holding it back, and what finally gave way?

## Sentence starters

- Model 1 shows ______, but it cannot show ______, because ______.
- Model 2 explains ______ by showing that the net force between particles depends on ______.
- Energy is stored in ______ while ______, and is released as ______ when ______.
- The long quiet period happens because ______, even though ______.

## Self-Check

*Check it only if you can say it out loud without looking.*

- [ ] I can name the three frames the class uses to evaluate a model, and use them to state one advantage and one limitation of Model 1 and of Model 2.
- [ ] I can explain what Model 2 (inverter magnets) shows about how net force changes with the distance between particles, including where the net force is zero.
- [ ] I can state, in order, whether forces are balanced or unbalanced through the whole bending-to-breaking sequence, and explain what happens at the instant of breaking.
- [ ] I can explain why a solid breaks at its thinnest cross-section rather than somewhere else, in terms of force per unit area.
- [ ] I can explain where energy is stored while matter is deformed, and what forms it takes when bonds break.
- [ ] I can explain why springs are a better bond analogy than strings or rigid sticks.
- [ ] I can explain why earthquakes have long quiet periods between them, using friction and accumulated elastic energy rather than just "plates move slowly."
- [ ] I can connect this same particle-level model to why Krakatoa built pressure for months before erupting.
