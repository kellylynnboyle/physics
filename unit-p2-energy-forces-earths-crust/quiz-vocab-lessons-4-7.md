**The chain that connects all of this:** Strong force loses to electric force in an unstable nucleus → radioactive decay releases energy (E=mc²) → that energy becomes kinetic energy in mantle particles → uneven heating drives convection → convection pushes on plates → stress builds until forces go unbalanced → earthquake or eruption.

---

## Forces & Deformation — Lesson 4

| Term | What it means |
|---|---|
| Net force | The single overall force on an object after adding up every force acting on it (a vector sum). Net force decides whether motion changes. |
| Balanced forces | Net force = 0. Nothing changes its motion — but that does not mean nothing is happening. Energy can still be loading the whole time (e.g., bonds stretching). |
| Unbalanced forces | Net force ≠ 0. Something changes — speeds up, slows down, or changes direction. A stable structure fails once the forces on it stop canceling. |
| Elastic deformation | A temporary change in shape (stretch, compress, bend) from an applied force. The object springs back once the force is removed — as long as it stays under its elastic limit. |
| Elastic limit | The most a material can deform and still spring back. Past it, the deformation becomes permanent, or the material breaks. |
| Elastic energy (in a compressed material) | Energy stored in the electric bonds between particles while a material is deformed — potential energy waiting to release when it springs back or snaps. |
| Electric force | The attraction/repulsion between charged particles. This **is** the bond holding atoms and materials together — gets weaker as particles move farther apart. |

## Seismic Waves & Earth's Layers — Lesson 5

| Term | What it means |
|---|---|
| P wave (primary) | Seismic wave that compresses and expands matter in the direction it travels. Fastest wave (~11 km/s through rock). Travels through solids, liquids, and gases. |
| S wave (secondary) | Seismic wave that shakes matter side to side, perpendicular to travel direction. Slower (~3 km/s through rock). Travels **only** through solids — the key clue for the liquid outer core. |
| Seismic anomaly | A wave reading (speed, arrival time, or whether it shows up at all) that does not match what a uniform (homogeneous) Earth would predict — evidence the material is different than expected. |
| Crust | The thin, solid, rocky outermost layer. Combined with the rigid top of the mantle, it forms the lithosphere — the tectonic plates. |
| Mantle | The thick middle layer, mostly solid magnesium-iron silicate rock. Not uniform: rigid at the top (part of the lithosphere), softer below that (asthenosphere, where plates float), solid lower mantle beneath. |
| Outer core vs. inner core | Outer core: liquid iron — slows P-waves and blocks S-waves entirely. Inner core: solid iron — even hotter, but the pressure at Earth's center is so extreme it stays solid. |

## Convection — Lesson 6

| Term | What it means |
|---|---|
| Convection (Matter–Energy–Force) | Circulation caused by uneven heating. **Force:** hot material expands, gets less dense, and rises (buoyancy beats gravity); cool material sinks. **Matter:** this physically moves rock. **Energy:** the moving rock carries heat from hot regions to cool ones. In the mantle this takes millions of years because the rock is so viscous. |

## Radioactivity & Energy — Lesson 7

| Term | What it means |
|---|---|
| Kinetic energy | Energy of motion. Radioactive decay converts stored nuclear energy into kinetic energy, making particles in the mantle move and vibrate faster. |
| Radioactive decay | An unstable nucleus breaks apart and releases energy. It happens because the electric force pushing protons apart and the strong force holding the nucleus together are no longer balanced. This is the main heat source driving mantle convection. |
| Strong (nuclear) force | The force holding protons and neutrons together in the nucleus — strong enough at short range to beat the electric repulsion between protons. In unstable atoms it eventually loses that battle, which is why decay happens. |
| E = mc² (mass & energy connection) | Mass and energy are the same thing (Einstein). A tiny bit of mass (m) converted, multiplied by a huge number (c², speed of light squared), equals an enormous amount of energy. That is why decaying just a little mass releases enough energy to heat the whole mantle for millions of years. |
