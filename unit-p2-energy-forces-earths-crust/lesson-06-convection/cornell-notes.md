## The Matter–Energy–Force (M-E-F) box for convection

| Lens | Notes |
|---|---|
| **MATTER** — particle level | Warmer regions have faster-moving particles and are **LESS dense**. |
| **ENERGY** | Warmer regions have more energy, as kinetic energy (motion of particles). We see it as temperature. |
| **FORCES** | Warmer regions feel a net force **UPWARD** (buoyancy). Cold regions feel a net force **DOWNWARD**. |

**Why buoyancy wins for warm material:** particles moving faster push each other slightly farther apart on average, so the same mass of material takes up more volume — lower density. Density = mass ÷ volume, so lower density means the buoyant force (upward, from the denser material around/below it) now exceeds gravity's downward pull on that parcel, and it rises. The reverse happens as it cools: particles slow down, pack closer, density rises, and gravity wins — it sinks.

## Cue / Question | Notes

| Cue / Question | Notes |
|---|---|
| The mantle tank / the Gizmo | Heat the bottom: hot liquid rises, cools at the top, sinks at the sides, returns — a **convection cell**. Density = mass ÷ volume; higher temperature → lower density. Higher viscosity → slower convection. Turn the burner off and there is no temperature difference, so no density difference, so no cell. |
| Where convection shows up on Earth | **Mantle:** rising rock at mid-ocean ridges (plates diverge), sinking at subduction zones (plates converge — Japan gets earthquakes, volcanoes, tsunamis). **Air at a coast:** onshore breeze by day, offshore at night, because land heats and cools faster than water. |
| Mantle vs. tank | The tomography data (Lesson 5) does not show a tidy cycle — hottest spots can be near the top and convection in the mantle is non-uniform, more like a lava lamp than a textbook loop. |
