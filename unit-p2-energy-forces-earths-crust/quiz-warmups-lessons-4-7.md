# Lessons 4–7 Quiz — Warm-Up Prompts

**OpenSciEd High School Physics · Unit P.2: Energy, Forces, & Earth's Crust**

The class deck includes the quiz-day warm-up slides. They are the closest thing to a blueprint you'll get for the actual quiz: **three prompts, one per lesson pair.** Practice answering each one in full sentences with a diagram, timed.

**For the short answer:** whatever it asks, structure it as **Matter / Energy / Forces** — three labelled sentences. That's the M-E-F triangle the class has used all unit, and it's how the teacher's own notes are organized.

---

## Lessons 3–4 warm-up

**Prompt:** What happens to matter when more force is applied? What happens to energy when force is applied, and when it is removed? Two pictures are both stable — how would the force diagrams be different, and which picture has more energy in the system?

**A full answer needs:**
- Balanced-vs-unbalanced language for each picture.
- Energy stored in stretched bonds — where it's coming from and where it's going.
- The key point: "stable" can still mean lots of stored energy. The more-deformed picture has more energy even though both are balanced.

## Lessons 5–6 warm-up

**Prompt:** Discuss the particle view at each SubMachine tomography location. How would the particle density and motion be different?

**A full answer needs:**
- **Red/negative anomaly = warmer = faster particles = less dense** (Hawaii, Afar, Costa Rica, our location).
- **Blue/positive = cooler = slower = denser** (Himalayas, Central US).
- Connect the temperature/density reading to what's happening at the surface above it (volcano vs. stable plain).

## Lesson 6 warm-up

**Prompt:** The burner is placed under the right side of the tank. What would you expect to see in terms of motion (net force) and temperature/energy in different places?

**A full answer needs:**
- Liquid above the burner heats, gets less dense, feels a net upward force and rises.
- It cools at the surface, becomes denser, and sinks on the left.
- A single cell circulates right-up, left-down.
- Warmest at bottom right, coolest at top left — label a sketch with this.

## Rehearsal drill

Pick any one of the three prompts above and write the short answer in **eight minutes, timed**, structured as Matter / Energy / Forces. Then check it against the fuller answer in that lesson's `cornell-notes.md` in this unit.
