# Physics 11 — Semester 1 Mechanics: Project Outline & Checklist

**Course:** Physics & Earth Science course model — 11th grade
**Semester 1 focus:** Kinematics, Projectile Motion, Newton's Laws, Momentum, Work & Energy
**Standards:** [NGSS for California](https://www.cde.ca.gov/pd/ca/sc/ngssstandards.asp) — HS-PS2 (Motion & Stability: Forces and Interactions), HS-PS3 (Energy)

This is the shareable syllabus/checklist for the unit. Instructors can use
it to track pacing and assessment; students can use it to self-check
progress before each unit test. Detailed notes, quizzes, and resources for
each row live in `units/<unit>.md` and the interactive app at
`app/index.html`.

---

## How to use this project

1. **Read the unit page** (`units/0X-topic.md`) for key concepts and Cornell notes.
2. **Take the interactive quiz** in `app/index.html` for that unit — it targets the questions students miss most often, with instant explanations.
3. **Work the practice problems** listed on the unit page.
4. **Self-check against the objectives checklist** below before the unit assessment.

---

## Unit 1 — Kinematics

- [ ] I can distinguish distance/displacement and speed/velocity, and explain why one pair is scalar and the other is vector.
- [ ] I can calculate acceleration from a change in velocity and correctly reason about its direction relative to motion.
- [ ] I can solve for an unknown (position, velocity, acceleration, or time) using the four constant-acceleration kinematics equations.
- [ ] I can read position-time and velocity-time graphs: extract velocity from slope, acceleration from slope, and displacement from area.
- [ ] I can apply free-fall motion (g = 9.8 m/s²) to vertical-motion problems, including at the peak of a toss.

**Assessment criteria:** Unit test includes graph interpretation (2–3 items), at least one multi-step kinematics-equation problem, and one conceptual question on vectors vs. scalars. Full credit requires labeled units and correct sign conventions throughout.

---

## Unit 2 — Projectile Motion

- [ ] I can explain why horizontal and vertical motion are independent during projectile flight.
- [ ] I can break an initial velocity into horizontal (vₓ) and vertical (v_y) components using sine and cosine.
- [ ] I can calculate time of flight, maximum height, and range for a projectile launched and landing at the same height.
- [ ] I can predict and explain the range-angle relationship, including why complementary angles (e.g., 30°/60°) give equal range.
- [ ] I can solve "horizontal launch off a height" problems (e.g., off a cliff or table).

**Assessment criteria:** Unit test includes one angled-launch problem, one horizontal-launch-from-height problem, and one conceptual question on the independence of horizontal/vertical motion (e.g., the "dropped vs. thrown ball" scenario).

---

## Unit 3 — Newton's Laws

- [ ] I can state and apply Newton's First Law (inertia) to explain constant-velocity or at-rest motion.
- [ ] I can apply Newton's Second Law (ΣF = ma) to solve for net force, mass, or acceleration.
- [ ] I can correctly identify Newton's Third Law action-reaction pairs, including that they act on *different* objects.
- [ ] I can draw a complete free body diagram for an object, including gravity, normal force, applied force(s), tension, and friction.
- [ ] I can distinguish static and kinetic friction, and explain why static friction is a variable (not fixed) force up to its maximum.
- [ ] I can distinguish mass (kg, invariant) from weight (N, location-dependent, = mg).

**Assessment criteria:** Unit test requires at least one fully-labeled free body diagram, one multi-force ΣF=ma problem, and one conceptual item testing 3rd-law pairs or the "balanced forces ≠ no forces" misconception.

---

## Unit 4 — Momentum

- [ ] I can calculate momentum (p = mv) and impulse (J = FΔt), and relate them through the impulse-momentum theorem.
- [ ] I can apply conservation of momentum to solve for an unknown velocity or mass in a two-object collision or explosion.
- [ ] I can distinguish elastic, inelastic, and perfectly inelastic collisions, and state which quantities are conserved in each.
- [ ] I can explain real-world impulse applications (airbags, crumple zones, padding) in terms of extending Δt to reduce force.
- [ ] I can apply conservation of momentum separately along the x- and y-axes for a 2D collision.

**Assessment criteria:** Unit test includes one 1D conservation-of-momentum problem, one conceptual item on elastic vs. inelastic collisions, and one short-answer item connecting impulse to a real safety design (airbag, helmet, bumper).

---

## Unit 5 — Work & Energy

- [ ] I can calculate work (W = Fd·cosθ) and identify when a force does zero work (perpendicular to displacement).
- [ ] I can apply the work-energy theorem (W_net = ΔKE) to relate net work to a change in speed.
- [ ] I can calculate kinetic energy (KE = ½mv²) and gravitational potential energy (PEg = mgh).
- [ ] I can apply conservation of mechanical energy to frictionless systems (e.g., ramps, pendulums, roller coasters).
- [ ] I can calculate power (P = W/t = Fv) and distinguish it from work/energy.
- [ ] I can explain where "lost" mechanical energy goes in a system with friction (heat, sound) without violating conservation of energy overall.

**Assessment criteria:** Unit test includes one energy-conservation problem (height ↔ speed), one work-energy-theorem problem, one conceptual item on KE's v² dependence, and one power calculation.

---

## Semester 1 capstone checklist

- [ ] Completed all five unit quizzes in `app/index.html` with a review of every missed question's explanation.
- [ ] Completed the practice problem sets for all five units.
- [ ] Can identify, for any of the five topics, one real-world application and explain the physics behind it.
- [ ] Reviewed free body diagrams, kinematics graphs, and conservation-law problems as a combined review set before the semester exam.

## Instructor pacing note

Each unit is designed for roughly 1.5–2 weeks including labs, formative
quizzing (via the app), and a unit assessment — a 5-unit semester fits a
standard ~18-week term with room for labs and review weeks. Adjust to your
school's bell schedule and lab access.
