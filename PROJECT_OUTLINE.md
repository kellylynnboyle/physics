# 11th Grade Physics — Mechanics Learning Project

**Outline & Checklist** for students and instructors. Covers five core mechanics units: Kinematics, Projectile Motion, Newton's Laws, Momentum, and Work & Energy. Designed for an algebra-based introductory physics course (Physics 1 level — no calculus required).

Each unit folder in [`topics/`](./topics) contains:
- **`notes.md`** — Cornell-style notes (cue questions + notes + summary) covering every key concept
- **`quiz.html`** — an interactive, self-grading quiz built from the questions students most often miss on this topic
- **`resources.md`** — curated learning resources, practice problems, real-world applications, and a skills checklist

---

## How to Use This Project

**Students:** Work through each unit in order — read the Cornell notes, work the practice problems, then take the interactive quiz. Retake the quiz until you consistently score 80%+. Check off the skills checklist in each `resources.md` as you master them.

**Instructors:** Use the learning objectives and assessment criteria below to track class progress, assign problems, or adapt the quiz questions for formal assessments. Each topic's "most-missed questions" quiz is built around well-documented student misconceptions and can double as a warm-up, exit ticket, or review-day diagnostic.

To open a quiz, open its `quiz.html` file directly in any web browser — no installation or server required.

## Unit 1 — Kinematics

📁 [`topics/01-kinematics/`](./topics/01-kinematics/)

**Learning Objectives** — students will be able to:
- Distinguish distance from displacement, and speed from velocity
- Interpret and construct position-time, velocity-time, and acceleration-time graphs
- Apply the four constant-acceleration kinematic equations to solve for unknowns
- Solve free-fall problems, including motion at the peak of a vertical throw
- Correctly apply sign conventions to determine speeding up vs. slowing down

**Assessment Criteria:**
- [ ] Scores 80%+ on the Kinematics interactive quiz
- [ ] Correctly solves at least 5 of 6 practice problems in `resources.md`
- [ ] Can explain, in writing or verbally, why velocity and acceleration are independent quantities

## Unit 2 — Projectile Motion

📁 [`topics/02-projectile-motion/`](./topics/02-projectile-motion/)

**Learning Objectives** — students will be able to:
- Decompose an initial velocity into horizontal and vertical components
- Explain why horizontal and vertical motion are independent of each other
- Calculate range, maximum height, and time of flight for level-ground launches
- Solve horizontally-launched (off a height) projectile problems
- Explain why launch mass does not affect trajectory shape (no air resistance)

**Assessment Criteria:**
- [ ] Scores 80%+ on the Projectile Motion interactive quiz
- [ ] Correctly solves at least 5 of 6 practice problems in `resources.md`
- [ ] Can justify, using equations, why complementary launch angles produce equal range

## Unit 3 — Newton's Laws

📁 [`topics/03-newtons-laws/`](./topics/03-newtons-laws/)

**Learning Objectives** — students will be able to:
- State and apply Newton's 1st, 2nd, and 3rd Laws to physical scenarios
- Draw accurate free body diagrams for objects in a variety of situations
- Distinguish mass from weight and apply W = mg correctly
- Solve for normal force, tension, and friction in equilibrium and accelerating systems
- Solve inclined-plane problems using force components

**Assessment Criteria:**
- [ ] Scores 80%+ on the Newton's Laws interactive quiz
- [ ] Correctly solves at least 5 of 6 practice problems in `resources.md`
- [ ] Draws a correct, fully-labeled free body diagram for a novel scenario without prompting

---

## Unit 4 — Momentum

📁 [`topics/04-momentum/`](./topics/04-momentum/)

**Learning Objectives** — students will be able to:
- Calculate momentum (p = mv) and impulse (J = FΔt) for moving objects
- Apply the impulse-momentum theorem to real-world safety scenarios
- Distinguish elastic, inelastic, and perfectly inelastic collisions
- Apply conservation of momentum to collisions and explosions using correct vector signs
- Explain why momentum is always conserved in an isolated system, while kinetic energy is not

**Assessment Criteria:**
- [ ] Scores 80%+ on the Momentum interactive quiz
- [ ] Correctly solves at least 5 of 6 practice problems in `resources.md`
- [ ] Can correctly set up a conservation-of-momentum equation for a two-object collision, including signs

---

## Unit 5 — Work & Energy

📁 [`topics/05-work-energy/`](./topics/05-work-energy/)

**Learning Objectives** — students will be able to:
- Calculate work using W = Fd cosθ and identify when a force does zero work
- Apply the work-energy theorem to relate net work and change in kinetic energy
- Apply conservation of mechanical energy in frictionless scenarios
- Explain "energy loss" to friction as a conversion to thermal energy, not destruction
- Calculate and compare power output between different processes

**Assessment Criteria:**
- [ ] Scores 80%+ on the Work & Energy interactive quiz
- [ ] Correctly solves at least 5 of 6 practice problems in `resources.md`
- [ ] Can correctly identify, for a given force/displacement pair, whether work is positive, negative, or zero

## Suggested Sequencing & Pacing

This project follows the standard mechanics sequence, where each unit builds on the last:

1. **Kinematics** — the vocabulary and math tools (vectors, graphs, equations) used in every later unit
2. **Projectile Motion** — applies kinematics in two dimensions simultaneously
3. **Newton's Laws** — introduces *why* motion changes (force), building on kinematics' *how*
4. **Momentum** — a second, often more powerful, lens on force interactions (impulse) and collisions
5. **Work & Energy** — a third lens (energy) that solves many problems force methods alone can't handle efficiently

A typical high school pacing is roughly 1.5–2 weeks per unit, including one review/quiz day.

## Suggested Cumulative Assessment

After completing all five units, a comprehensive test should draw at least 3–4 questions from each unit's quiz bank, plus one multi-concept problem that spans at least two units (e.g., a projectile that also requires momentum conservation on landing, or a Newton's-Laws-plus-energy incline problem).
