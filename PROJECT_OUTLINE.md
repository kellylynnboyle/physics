# Physics 11 Mechanics Unit — Project Outline & Checklist

**Audience:** 11th grade physics (first-year mechanics)
**Prerequisites:** Algebra I/II, basic trigonometry (SOH-CAH-TOA), graphing linear/quadratic functions
**Suggested duration:** 5–7 weeks (see pacing guide below)

This document is the master checklist for the unit. Each topic below links to its own folder with Cornell notes, a self-grading quiz of commonly-missed questions, and a resource list.

---

## Unit-Level Learning Objectives

By the end of this project, students should be able to:

- [ ] Distinguish scalar vs. vector quantities and apply that distinction consistently across all five topics
- [ ] Select the correct kinematic, dynamic, or conservation-law model for a given physical situation
- [ ] Move fluently between verbal descriptions, diagrams (motion maps, free-body diagrams), graphs, and equations for the same physical event
- [ ] Solve multi-step problems that combine two or more topics (e.g., a projectile-momentum collision, or Newton's laws feeding into a work-energy calculation)
- [ ] Check answers for reasonableness using units, order-of-magnitude estimation, and limiting cases

## Unit-Level Assessment Criteria

| Level | Description |
|---|---|
| **Beginning** | Recognizes vocabulary and formulas but cannot select the right one without prompting; struggles to extract given values from word problems |
| **Developing** | Solves single-step, single-concept problems correctly; needs support on multi-step or multi-representation problems |
| **Proficient** | Solves standard multi-step problems independently; correctly identifies which conservation law or kinematic equation applies; shows units and reasonable sig figs |
| **Advanced** | Solves novel/combined-topic problems; can justify *why* a method applies (not just *that* it works); catches and corrects sign/direction errors without prompting |

Suggested grading split per topic: **40% Cornell notes completion & quality, 30% practice problem set, 30% quiz score** — adjust to your grading policy.

## Topic 1 — Kinematics
📁 [`01-kinematics/`](./01-kinematics/)

**Key concepts:** position, displacement vs. distance, velocity vs. speed, acceleration, the four kinematic equations, position/velocity/acceleration graphs, free fall

**Learning objectives:**
- [ ] Differentiate displacement/distance and velocity/speed using signed (vector) values
- [ ] Read and sketch position-time, velocity-time, and acceleration-time graphs, including converting between them (slope and area-under-curve)
- [ ] Select and apply the correct kinematic equation from the "big four" based on which variable is missing
- [ ] Solve free-fall problems using g = 9.8 m/s²

**Assessment criteria:** Proficient = correctly sets up a sign convention *before* substituting numbers, and gets free-fall problems right without being told "this is a free-fall problem."

**Files:** [Cornell notes](./01-kinematics/cornell-notes.md) · [Quiz](./01-kinematics/quiz.html) · [Resources](./01-kinematics/resources.md)

## Topic 2 — Projectile Motion
📁 [`02-projectile-motion/`](./02-projectile-motion/)

**Key concepts:** independence of horizontal/vertical motion, trajectory as parabola, time of flight, range, maximum height, launch angle, initial velocity components

**Learning objectives:**
- [ ] Decompose an initial velocity into horizontal and vertical components using trig
- [ ] Apply constant-velocity equations horizontally and constant-acceleration (free fall) equations vertically to the *same* time variable
- [ ] Solve for time of flight, range, and maximum height for both level-ground and elevated launches
- [ ] Explain why horizontal and vertical motion are independent (no horizontal acceleration if air resistance is ignored)

**Assessment criteria:** Proficient = never applies g to the horizontal component, and correctly uses the same t for both axes.

**Files:** [Cornell notes](./02-projectile-motion/cornell-notes.md) · [Quiz](./02-projectile-motion/quiz.html) · [Resources](./02-projectile-motion/resources.md)

## Topic 3 — Newton's Laws
📁 [`03-newtons-laws/`](./03-newtons-laws/)

**Key concepts:** inertia (1st law), F = ma (2nd law), action-reaction pairs (3rd law), free-body diagrams, net force, friction (static/kinetic), normal force, weight vs. mass, tension

**Learning objectives:**
- [ ] Draw a correct free-body diagram for an object in a given scenario (incline, pulley, multiple contacts)
- [ ] Compute net force by vector addition and apply F_net = ma to find acceleration
- [ ] Distinguish mass (invariant, kg) from weight (force, N, location-dependent)
- [ ] Identify genuine action-reaction pairs (same type of force, opposite objects) vs. two forces that merely balance
- [ ] Incorporate friction (f = μN) into net force calculations

**Assessment criteria:** Proficient = free-body diagram includes *only* forces acting ON the object (not forces it exerts on other things), with correct directions and relative magnitudes.

**Files:** [Cornell notes](./03-newtons-laws/cornell-notes.md) · [Quiz](./03-newtons-laws/quiz.html) · [Resources](./03-newtons-laws/resources.md)

## Topic 4 — Momentum
📁 [`04-momentum/`](./04-momentum/)

**Key concepts:** momentum p = mv, impulse J = FΔt = Δp, conservation of momentum, elastic vs. inelastic collisions, perfectly inelastic collisions, momentum as a vector

**Learning objectives:**
- [ ] Calculate momentum and impulse, including sign/direction
- [ ] Apply conservation of momentum to solve for unknown velocities before/after a collision or explosion
- [ ] Classify a collision as elastic, inelastic, or perfectly inelastic and know what is/isn't conserved in each
- [ ] Connect impulse-momentum theorem to real-world safety design (airbags, crumple zones, padding)

**Assessment criteria:** Proficient = keeps momentum's vector (signed) nature intact through the whole problem, and correctly identifies total system momentum as conserved even when kinetic energy is not.

**Files:** [Cornell notes](./04-momentum/cornell-notes.md) · [Quiz](./04-momentum/quiz.html) · [Resources](./04-momentum/resources.md)

## Topic 5 — Work & Energy
📁 [`05-work-energy/`](./05-work-energy/)

**Key concepts:** work W = Fd cos θ, kinetic energy, gravitational and elastic potential energy, work-energy theorem, conservation of mechanical energy, power, energy lost to friction/heat

**Learning objectives:**
- [ ] Calculate work done by a force, including cases where force and displacement are not parallel
- [ ] Apply the work-energy theorem to relate net work to change in kinetic energy
- [ ] Apply conservation of mechanical energy to frictionless systems, and extend it to systems with friction by tracking energy "lost" to heat
- [ ] Distinguish work, energy, and power, and calculate power as the rate of energy transfer

**Assessment criteria:** Proficient = recognizes when a normal or tension force does zero work (perpendicular to motion) and correctly sets up energy bar charts / conservation equations for multi-stage problems (e.g., a roller coaster with friction on part of the track).

**Files:** [Cornell notes](./05-work-energy/cornell-notes.md) · [Quiz](./05-work-energy/quiz.html) · [Resources](./05-work-energy/resources.md)
