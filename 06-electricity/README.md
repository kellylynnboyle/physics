Electricity extends the force framework from Newton's Laws (Unit 3) to a
new kind of force that acts at a distance without contact: the
electrostatic force between charges.

## Key concepts to master

- **Electric charge** — a fundamental property of matter; comes in two
  types (positive/negative); like charges repel, opposite charges attract;
  charge is conserved and quantized (multiples of the elementary charge).
- **Charging methods** — friction, conduction (direct contact), and
  induction (redistribution without contact).
- **Coulomb's Law** — `F = kq₁q₂/r²`, describing the force between two
  point charges; mathematically parallel to Newton's Law of Gravitation
  (`F = Gm₁m₂/r²`) but for charge instead of mass, and can be attractive
  *or* repulsive.
- **Electric field** — `E = F/q`, the force per unit charge at a point in
  space; a way of describing how a charge affects the space around it,
  independent of whether a second charge is actually present.
- **Conductors vs. insulators** — how easily charge moves through a
  material, explained at the molecular/electron-structure level.
- **Basic circuits (supporting content)** — voltage, current, resistance,
  and Ohm's Law (`V = IR`) as the practical, lab-facing application of
  charge and field concepts, even though circuits aren't a standalone NGSS
  performance expectation in this course.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this unit, students will be able to:

1. Explain charging by friction, conduction, and induction using the electron-transfer model.
2. Apply Coulomb's Law to calculate the force between two point charges, including how force changes with distance (inverse-square relationship).
3. Compare and contrast Coulomb's Law and Newton's Law of Gravitation.
4. Explain, at the molecular/structural level, why some materials conduct electricity well and others don't.
5. Build and analyze a simple series circuit using Ohm's Law.

## NGSS alignment

**HS-PS2-4.** Use mathematical representations of Newton's Law of
Gravitation and Coulomb's Law to describe and predict the gravitational
and electrostatic forces between objects. *(Limited to systems with two
objects.)*

**HS-PS2-6\*.** Communicate scientific and technical information about why
molecular-level structure is important in the functioning of designed
materials — e.g., why electrically conductive materials are often made of
metal. Full text and instructional-segment context:
[`../NGSS-ALIGNMENT.md`](../NGSS-ALIGNMENT.md).

**Suggested lab:** measure the force between charged objects (e.g.,
charged pith balls or a Van de Graaff setup) at varying distances and
compare the data to Coulomb's Law's inverse-square prediction.
