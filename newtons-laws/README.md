# Unit 3: Newton's Laws

The study of *why* things move the way they do — connecting forces to
motion (kinematics, Unit 1, described *how*; this unit explains the
cause).

## Key concepts students need to master

- **Newton's First Law (inertia)** — an object at rest stays at rest, and
  an object in motion stays in motion at constant velocity, unless acted
  on by a net (unbalanced) force. Objects in equilibrium have **zero net
  force**, which does *not* mean zero velocity — a car cruising at
  constant speed in a straight line is in equilibrium too.
- **Newton's Second Law (ΣF = ma)** — a *vector* equation. Forces must be
  resolved into x- and y-components before summing; acceleration points
  in the same direction as the net force, not necessarily the same
  direction as velocity.
- **Newton's Third Law** — for every action force there is a reaction
  force, equal in magnitude and opposite in direction, acting on a
  **different** object. Because they act on different objects, action-
  reaction pairs never cancel each other out.
- **Mass vs. weight** — mass (kg) is a scalar measure of inertia and does
  not change with location. Weight (N) is a force, weight = mg, and
  changes with the local gravitational field.
- **Normal force** — the perpendicular contact force a surface exerts;
  it is **not always equal to mg**. It adjusts based on other forces
  present: it equals only mg cosθ on a frictionless incline, it's
  greater than mg in an upward-accelerating elevator, less than mg in a
  downward-accelerating elevator, and altered further by any additional
  applied force with a vertical component.
- **Friction** — static friction (before motion starts) can range from 0
  up to a maximum of μₛN, adjusting to exactly prevent motion; kinetic
  friction (during sliding) is fixed at f = μₖN. Static friction is only
  *at* μₛN at the verge of slipping.
- **Free-body diagrams** — isolating one object and drawing every force
  acting *on* it (not forces it exerts on other things) as vectors from
  its center.
- **Tension** — the pulling force transmitted through a rope or string.
  At this level (massless string, massless/frictionless pulley), tension
  is the same throughout the string, including on both sides of a pulley.
- **Forces on an incline** — decomposing gravity into a component
  parallel to the surface (mg sinθ, drives acceleration down the slope)
  and a component perpendicular to the surface (mg cosθ, balanced by the
  normal force).
- **Systems of connected objects** — blocks connected by a string, or in
  contact and pushed together, share the same magnitude of acceleration
  (a constraint), but each object still needs its own free-body diagram
  and its own ΣF = ma analysis.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this unit, students will be able to:

1. State each of Newton's three laws in words and apply each to a
   real-world scenario.
2. Draw a correct, complete free-body diagram for an object under
   multiple forces (gravity, normal, applied, tension, friction).
3. Distinguish mass from weight and calculate an object's weight given
   its mass (and vice versa).
4. Calculate the normal force and friction force in level, inclined, and
   vertically-accelerating (elevator) scenarios.
5. Correctly identify Newton's Third Law force pairs — same type of
   force, same magnitude, opposite direction, acting on two different
   objects — and distinguish them from balanced-force pairs acting on one
   object.
6. Solve multi-force problems (incline + friction + tension, connected
   blocks) by choosing convenient axes and applying ΣF = ma along each.

## Assessment criteria

- **Quiz target:** ≥ 80% on `quiz.html`, with correct reasoning on the
  Third Law force-pairing and normal-force questions specifically (these
  are the most commonly missed).
- **Problem sets:** Full free-body diagram, axis choice, ΣF = ma
  equation(s), and solve-with-units shown, not just a final number.
- **Mastery check:** Given a word problem with no diagram, the student
  draws their own free-body diagram, correctly labels every force
  (including any Third Law counterpart acting on a different object),
  and sets up ΣF = ma along chosen axes without prompting.
