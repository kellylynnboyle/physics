## Topic: Work, Kinetic/Potential Energy, and Conservation

| Cues / Questions | Notes |
|---|---|
| What is the definition of work? | `W = F d cos θ`, where θ is the angle between the applied force and the displacement direction. Work is a scalar, measured in Joules (J). |
| When is work equal to zero? | When force is perpendicular to displacement (θ = 90°, cos 90° = 0), OR when there is no displacement at all — even if a large force is applied (e.g., pushing on a wall that doesn't move). |
| What is kinetic energy? | `KE = ½ m v²`. Energy of motion. Always ≥ 0. |
| Work-Energy Theorem? | `W_net = ΔKE = KE_f − KE_i`. The total (net) work done on an object changes its kinetic energy. |
| What is gravitational PE? | `PE_grav = m g h`, where h is height above a CHOSEN reference point. The reference point is arbitrary — only the CHANGE in PE (ΔPE) has physical meaning. |
| What is elastic (spring) PE? | `PE_spring = ½ k x²`, where k = spring constant, x = displacement from the spring's natural (equilibrium) length. |
| Conservation of Mechanical Energy — when does it apply? | Only when NO non-conservative forces (friction, air resistance) act: `KE_i + PE_i = KE_f + PE_f`. |
| What happens to mechanical energy when friction acts? | Mechanical energy is NOT conserved — some energy converts to heat/sound. Total energy (including thermal) is still conserved, but KE + PE decreases. |
| How do I include friction in an energy equation? | `KE_i + PE_i = KE_f + PE_f + E_lost_to_friction`, where `E_lost_to_friction = f × d` (friction force times distance slid). |
| What is power? | `P = W / t` — the rate at which work is done or energy is transferred. Units: Watts (W) = J/s. |
| Biggest mistake to avoid? | Assuming work is simply "force times distance" without checking the angle between force and displacement — and forgetting to subtract energy lost to friction in real (non-ideal) systems. |

## Worked Example (fill in blanks during class)

A 3 kg block starts at rest at the top of a frictionless 4 m tall ramp, then slides onto a rough horizontal surface (μ_k = 0.2). (g = 9.8 m/s²)

- PE at top: PE = mgh = ______ J
- KE at bottom of ramp (energy conserved, frictionless): KE = ______ J
- Speed at bottom of ramp: v = √(2·KE/m) = ______ m/s
- Friction force on rough surface: f = μ_k mg = ______ N
- Distance to stop (all KE converted to friction work): d = KE / f = ______ m
