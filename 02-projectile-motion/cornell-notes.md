## Topic: Independence of Motion

| Cues / Questions | Notes |
|---|---|
| Core idea of projectile motion | Horizontal and vertical motions happen at the same time but do **not** affect each other. |
| What causes vertical motion? | Gravity — constant downward acceleration, g. |
| What causes horizontal motion? | Nothing, once launched (ignoring air resistance) — horizontal velocity stays constant the whole flight. |
| Classic demo | A ball dropped straight down and a ball launched horizontally from the same height hit the ground at the **same time** — their vertical motions are identical even though their horizontal motions differ completely. |

| Cues / Questions | Notes |

| Vertical component | v_y = v sin θ |
| Why do this at all? | Because the horizontal and vertical directions obey different rules (constant velocity vs. constant acceleration), so they must be solved as two separate 1-D kinematics problems. |

## Topic: The Trajectory

| Cues / Questions | Notes |
|---|---|
| Shape of the path | Parabola. |
| What happens to vₓ throughout flight? | Nothing — it never changes. |
| What happens to v_y throughout flight? | Decreases (going up), reaches 0 at the peak, then increases in magnitude (going down) — changes at a constant rate of −g. |
| Velocity at the peak | vₓ is unchanged and nonzero; v_y = 0. Total velocity is at its **minimum**, not zero (unless launched straight up). |
| Symmetry | If launch height = landing height, time up = time down, and the launch/landing speeds (magnitude) are equal. |

## Topic: Range and Launch Angle

| Cues / Questions | Notes |
|---|---|
| Which angle maximizes range (level ground)? | 45° |
| What about 30° vs. 60°? | Complementary angles (sum to 90°) give the **same range**, but different heights and flight times — the steeper angle goes higher and hangs in the air longer. |
| Range equation | R = v² sin(2θ) / g — only valid when launch height = landing height. |
