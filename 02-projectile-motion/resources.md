# Projectile Motion — Resources, Practice, and Applications

## Learning Resources

- **Simulation:** [PhET — Projectile Motion](https://phet.colorado.edu/en/simulations/projectile-motion) — fire projectiles at different angles/speeds and see the trajectory, range, and time of flight update live; use "air resistance off" for classroom problems
- **Video:** [Khan Academy — Two-dimensional motion](https://www.khanacademy.org/science/physics/two-dimensional-motion) — full derivation of the component method
- **Video:** [The Organic Chemistry Tutor — Projectile Motion Physics Problems](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=organic+chemistry+tutor+projectile+motion) — worked problems including launches from a height
- **Reference:** [The Physics Classroom — Projectile Motion](https://www.physicsclassroom.com/class/vectors/Lesson-2/What-is-a-Projectile) — conceptual walkthrough of horizontal/vertical independence
- **Interactive:** [Desmos — Projectile Motion Grapher](https://www.desmos.com/calculator) — plug in v₀ and θ as sliders to visualize how range and height change with angle

## Practice Problems

1. A ball is launched at 25 m/s at 30° above horizontal from level ground. Find the time of flight, maximum height, and range.
2. A stone is thrown horizontally at 15 m/s from the top of a 20 m cliff. Find the time to hit the ground and the horizontal distance traveled.
3. Compare the range of a projectile launched at 40° vs. one launched at 50°, both at 20 m/s. What do you notice, and why?
4. A soccer ball is kicked at 18 m/s at 40°. Find its velocity (magnitude and direction) at the moment it hits the ground (level ground).
5. A dart is thrown horizontally at a target 3 m away. If the dart leaves the hand at the same height as the bullseye, explain why it will land below the bullseye — then calculate how far below if the dart travels at 8 m/s.
6. An arrow is shot at 35° with initial speed 40 m/s from a height of 1.5 m (the archer's hand). Find total time of flight (this one needs the quadratic formula on the vertical equation).
7. At what two angles will a projectile launched at 20 m/s achieve a range of 30 m (assume level ground, use the range formula and solve for θ)?

*(Have students verify their own answers using the PhET simulation above by matching v₀ and θ and comparing measured range/height/time to their calculated values.)*

## Real-World Applications

- **Sports:** basketball free throws, golf drives, and long jumps are all optimized using projectile motion — coaches analyze launch angle and speed to maximize distance or accuracy
- **Military/ballistics history:** early artillery tables were built from exactly these equations (this is historically where much of kinematics was first formalized)
- **Water fountains and irrigation:** nozzle angle and pressure (launch speed) are engineered using range/height formulas to cover a target area
- **Space launches:** while orbital mechanics is more complex, the "independent horizontal/vertical motion" idea is the conceptual seed for understanding orbital trajectories
