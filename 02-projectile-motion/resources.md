# Projectile Motion — Resources, Practice, and Applications

## Learning resources

- **OpenStax College Physics** — Chapter 3, "Two-Dimensional Kinematics," section on projectile motion. openstax.org/details/books/college-physics-2e
- **Khan Academy** — "Two-dimensional motion" unit, especially the "Projectile motion" video series. khanacademy.org
- **PhET Interactive Simulations** — "Projectile Motion" simulation: adjust launch angle, speed, and air resistance and watch the trajectory update live; great for testing the "independence of components" idea. phet.colorado.edu, search "Projectile Motion"
- **The Physics Classroom** — "Projectiles" tutorial, includes practice with "angled" and "horizontal" launch categories separately. physicsclassroom.com/class/vectors
- **CrashCourse Physics, Episode 5** — "Projectile Motion" conceptual overview.

## Practice problems

1. A soccer ball is kicked at 18 m/s at 40° above the horizontal. Find the time of flight, max height, and range.
2. A stone is thrown horizontally at 12 m/s from the top of a 20 m tower. Find the time to hit the ground and the horizontal distance traveled.
3. A basketball is launched at 8 m/s at 55° and lands in the hoop at the same height it was launched from. Find its range.
4. An arrow is shot at 45 m/s at 20° above horizontal from a platform 5 m above the ground (target is at ground level). Find the total time of flight. *(Hint: the vertical displacement is −5 m at landing, not 0.)*
5. Two projectiles are launched at the same speed, one at 30° and one at 60°. Without calculating, predict which has the greater range and which reaches greater height, then verify using `Range = v₀²sin(2θ)/g`.
6. **Conceptual:** A rescue plane flying horizontally drops a supply package. Compared to a package simply dropped straight down from the same height, does the plane's package take more, less, or the same time to hit the ground? Explain using independence of components.
7. **Conceptual:** At the exact top of its arc, is a projectile's speed zero? Explain what is and isn't zero at that point.

## Real-world applications

- **Sports** — basketball free throws, football punts, and golf drives are all modeled (to a first approximation) as projectile motion; coaches and broadcasters use launch angle and speed data to analyze performance.
- **Firefighting** — aerial firefighting planes calculate horizontal lead distance to drop retardant on a target, using the same independence-of-components logic as problem 6 above.
- **Space and ballistics history** — early ballistics tables (artillery range charts) were essentially projectile-motion lookup tables before calculators existed.
- **Water fountains and irrigation systems** — nozzle angle and pressure (launch speed) are tuned using projectile-motion range equations to cover a target area evenly.
- **Search and rescue air drops** — supply packages are released before a plane is directly over the target, using time-of-flight and constant horizontal velocity to predict landing point.
