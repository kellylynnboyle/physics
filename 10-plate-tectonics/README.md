Plate Tectonics is this course's first Earth & Space Science unit (Earth
and Space Sciences, ESS strand). It reuses the "system of forces" thinking
from Newton's Laws and Momentum, applied at a planetary scale: Earth's
crust is broken into plates that interact at their boundaries the same way
colliding objects do.

## Key concepts to master

- **Earth's internal structure** — crust, mantle, outer core, inner core,
  and how density/temperature differences drive mantle convection.
- **Plate boundaries** — divergent (spreading, e.g., mid-ocean ridges),
  convergent (colliding, e.g., subduction zones and mountain building),
  and transform (sliding past, e.g., strike-slip faults).
- **Evidence for plate tectonics** — matching coastlines, fossil
  distribution across continents, seafloor spreading patterns, and
  paleomagnetic striping.
- **Seafloor age patterns** — oceanic crust gets older with distance from
  mid-ocean ridges; continental crust gets older with distance from
  ancient cores — used as evidence to date crustal rocks.
- **Surface features as constructive/destructive process evidence** —
  mountains, valleys, trenches, and ridges result from a combination of
  constructive forces (volcanism, tectonic uplift) and destructive
  processes (weathering, erosion).

## Vocabulary checklist

lithosphere · asthenosphere · mantle convection · divergent boundary ·
convergent boundary · transform boundary · subduction · seafloor spreading ·
paleomagnetism · crustal age

## Learning objectives

By the end of this unit, students will be able to:

1. Describe Earth's layered internal structure and explain how mantle convection drives plate motion.
2. Classify a plate boundary as divergent, convergent, or transform from a diagram or description, and predict the resulting geologic features.
3. Evaluate evidence (seafloor age, paleomagnetism, fossil distribution) supporting the theory of plate tectonics and use it to explain the relative ages of crustal rocks.
4. Develop a model illustrating how Earth's internal and surface processes form specific continental and ocean-floor features.

## NGSS alignment

**HS-ESS2-1.** Develop a model to illustrate how Earth's internal and
surface processes operate at different spatial and temporal scales to
form continental and ocean-floor features. *(Mountains, valleys, plateaus,
trenches, ridges, seamounts — constructive vs. destructive mechanisms.)*

**HS-ESS1-5.** Evaluate evidence of the past and current movements of
continental and oceanic crust and the theory of plate tectonics to explain
the ages of crustal rocks. Full text:
[`../NGSS-ALIGNMENT.md`](../NGSS-ALIGNMENT.md).

**Suggested lab:** map real seafloor age data (or a provided dataset) and
have students identify spreading centers and subduction zones from the
age-vs-distance pattern alone — direct evidence-evaluation practice for
HS-ESS1-5.
