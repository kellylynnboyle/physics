# Learning Objectives, Assessment Criteria, and Rubrics

## 1. Objective → evidence → standard map

| Unit | Core objective | Evidence of learning | NGSS PE |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Kinematics | Describe motion with graphs and equations | Graph sketches, constant-a problem set, motion-graph lab | HS-PS2-1 (foundation) |
| 2 Projectile motion | Predict trajectory by treating x and y independently | Predict-and-test lab, component problem set | HS-PS2-1 |
| 3 Newton's laws | Use ΣF = ma with free-body diagrams | FBD portfolio, a–F lab CER, problem set | HS-PS2-1, HS-PS2-4 |
| 4 Momentum | Use conservation of momentum and impulse | Collision lab, problem set, egg-drop design | HS-PS2-2, HS-PS2-3 |
| 5 Work & energy | Account for energy transfers and conversions | Energy bar charts, ramp lab, computational model, device design | HS-PS3-1, HS-PS3-2, HS-PS3-3 |

## 2. Mastery levels (apply to each unit)

| Level | Label | Typical evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | Extending | ≥ 90% on quiz; solves multi-step or novel problems; explains the *why* with a model; catches errors in sample work |
| 3 | Proficient (mastery) | ≥ 80% on quiz; correct set-up, units, and signs on standard problems; lab claims supported by data |
| 2 | Developing | 60–79%; correct ideas but frequent set-up, sign, or unit errors; needs hints |
| 1 | Beginning | < 60%; relies on misconceptions; needs reteaching |

Reassessment: students at Level 1–2 correct their quiz (explain each miss using the "common trap") and retake a mixed set within one week.

## 3. Problem-solving rubric (each worked problem, 4 points)

| Criterion | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Representation | None | Partial diagram/graph | Correct diagram/graph | Diagram + labeled knowns/unknowns + coordinate choice |
| Physics principle | Missing/wrong | Named but misapplied | Correct law or equation | Law justified from the situation |
| Math and units | Many errors | Some errors | Correct with units | Correct, units checked, sig figs |
| Reasonableness | None | Mentioned | Checked | Checked and interpreted |

## 4. Lab report rubric (CER format, 20 points)

| Criterion | Points | Proficient looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Question and prediction | 3 | Testable question; prediction with reasoning |
| Procedure and data | 5 | Clear variables; repeated trials; tables with units |
| Analysis | 5 | Correct graphs, slope/area interpreted, uncertainty estimated |
| Claim–evidence–reasoning | 5 | Claim answers the question; data cited; physics law links them |
| Error and improvement | 2 | Realistic error sources and one specific improvement |

## 5. Engineering design rubric (momentum and energy tasks, 16 points)

| Criterion | Points | Proficient looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Criteria and constraints | 3 | Success metric, materials, and limits stated |
| Physics rationale | 4 | Uses Δp = FΔt or energy conversion to justify design |
| Testing and data | 4 | At least 2 trials per design; measured results |
| Revision | 3 | Specific change based on data; retest |
| Communication | 2 | Clear diagram and summary |

## 6. Unit grading weights (suggested)

| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Unit quiz and test | 40% |
| Labs and design tasks | 25% |
| Practice sets / Cornell notes | 15% |
| FBD / bar-chart / graph portfolios | 10% |
| Reflection and corrections | 10% |

## 7. Quiz bank coverage (40 items)

| Topic | Items | Misconceptions targeted |
|---|---|---|
| Kinematics | 8 | constant v vs. acceleration; v = 0 vs. a = 0; speed vs. velocity; graph slope/area; sign of acceleration |
| Projectile motion | 8 | independence of x and y; peak of path; constant horizontal velocity; range vs. angle; components |
| Newton's laws | 8 | motion needs force; third-law pairs; mass vs. weight; apparent weight; friction at constant v |
| Momentum | 8 | p vs. KE; impulse and Δt; conservation in sticking collisions; recoil; vector sign |
| Work & energy | 8 | work needs displacement; KE ∝ v²; energy conversions; power; path independence |

Instructors can add items in `quizzes/questions.js` and use class results to reteach the most-missed items.
