# NGSS Alignment (Grade 11 Physics & Earth Science, Semester 1)

Source: California NGSS, High School Physical Science and Earth/Space Science. <https://www.cde.ca.gov/pd/ca/sc/ngssstandards.asp>
**Verify the exact PE wording and codes against the CDE page before distributing.** The wording below is paraphrased.

## Important note on kinematics and projectiles

NGSS has no stand-alone performance expectation for kinematics or projectile motion. They are the mathematical and data-analysis foundation for HS-PS2-1 (Newton's second law) and for energy models. Units 1 and 2 therefore map to HS-PS2-1 plus the Science and Engineering Practices (SEPs) for analyzing data and using mathematics. The course model expects the same.

## Performance expectations used this semester

| PE | Paraphrased learning goal | Units |
|---|---|---|
| HS-PS2-1 | Analyze data to support the claim that Newton's second law describes the mathematical relationship among net force, mass, and acceleration. | 1, 2, 3 |
| HS-PS2-2 | Use mathematical representations to support the claim that total momentum of a system of objects is conserved when there is no net outside force. | 4 |
| HS-PS2-3 | Design, evaluate, and refine a device that minimizes the force on a macroscopic object during a collision. | 4 |
| HS-PS2-4 | Use mathematical representations of Newton's law of gravitation and Coulomb's law to describe and predict forces. (Gravity portion this semester; Coulomb in Semester 2.) | 3 |
| HS-PS3-1 | Create a computational model to calculate the change in energy of one component of a system when the other components and energy flows are known. | 5 |
| HS-PS3-2 | Develop and use models to illustrate that energy at the macroscopic scale is the combination of energy associated with motion and energy associated with position (fields). | 5 |
| HS-PS3-3 | Design, build, and refine a device that converts one form of energy into another, within given constraints. | 5 |

HS-PS3-4 (thermal energy, second law) is touched briefly in Unit 5 when friction converts mechanical energy to thermal energy, and is developed fully later.
