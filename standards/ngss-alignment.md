# NGSS Alignment: First Semester Physics (California)

California adopted the NGSS in 2013. The standards below are the performance expectations (PEs) used as learning goals for Semester 1. They follow the **Physics & Earth Science** course model for high school. Official source: <https://www.cde.ca.gov/pd/ca/sc/ngssstandards.asp>

> Wording is paraphrased from memory of the NGSS HS-PS PEs. Before distributing to families or admin, verify each code and clarification statement against the CDE page above.

| PE | Paraphrased performance expectation | Unit(s) | Evidence in this project |
|---|---|---|---|
| **HS-PS2-1** | Analyze data to support the claim that Newton's second law describes the relationship among net force, mass and acceleration. | 1, 2, 3 | Kinematics graphs and data skills (U1), gravity-only motion (U2), ΣF = ma lab CER (U3) |
| **HS-PS2-2** | Use mathematical representations to support the claim that total momentum is conserved when there is no net force on a system. | 4 | Collision cart lab, conservation problems |
| **HS-PS2-3** | Apply science and engineering ideas to design, evaluate and refine a device that minimizes force on a macroscopic object during a collision. | 3, 4 | Egg-drop / helmet design with impulse math |
| **HS-PS2-4** | Use mathematical representations of Newton's Law of Gravitation and Coulomb's Law to describe and predict forces. | 3 (gravity), Sem. 2 (electric) | Weight on other planets, F = mg as a local case |
| **HS-PS3-1** | Create a computational model to calculate the change in energy of one component of a system when the change in energy of the other components and energy flows are known. | 5 | Energy bar charts, spreadsheet model |
| **HS-PS3-2** | Develop and use models to illustrate that energy can be accounted for as a combination of energy associated with motion and energy associated with relative position of objects. | 2, 5 | KE/PE models, projectile energy |
| **HS-PS3-3** | Design, build and refine a device that works within given constraints to convert one form of energy into another. | 5 | Energy conversion device project |

**Semester 2 (preview, not covered here):** HS-PS2-5 (electric current and magnetic fields), HS-PS3-5 (field interactions and energy), HS-PS4-1 to PS4-5 (waves, light, communication), HS-PS1-8 (nuclear), and Earth science PEs for plate tectonics and earthquakes (e.g., HS-ESS2-1, HS-ESS2-3).

## Three-dimensional learning map

| Dimension | Where students practice it |
|---|---|
| **Science & Engineering Practices** | SEP 2 Models (energy bar charts, FBDs) · SEP 3 Investigations (labs) · SEP 4 Analyze data (graphs) · SEP 5 Math/computation (equations, spreadsheet) · SEP 6 Explanations & design (egg drop, energy device) · SEP 7 Argument (CER) |
| **Crosscutting Concepts** | Cause & Effect (forces → motion) · Systems & System Models (isolated systems in collisions) · Energy & Matter (conservation/transfer) · Patterns (parabolas, linear and quadratic relationships) |
| **Disciplinary Core Ideas** | PS2.A Forces and Motion · PS2.B Types of Interactions · PS3.A Definitions of Energy · PS3.B Conservation of Energy and Energy Transfer · PS3.C Relationship Between Energy and Forces · PS3.D Energy in Chemical Processes (in design project) |

## Note on kinematics and projectile motion
Units 1 and 2 are not standalone NGSS PEs. They provide the quantitative tools (vectors, graphs, constant acceleration, components) that students need to meet HS-PS2-1 and HS-PS3-2. They are assessed as **supporting skills** and reported separately from the standards-based grade.
