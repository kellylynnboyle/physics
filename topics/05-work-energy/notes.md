# Cornell Notes — Work & Energy

**Topic:** Work, Energy, & Power | **Unit:** 5 | **Course:** 11th Grade Physics

---

## Notes

| Cue / Question | Notes |
|---|---|
| Definition of work | `W = Fd cosθ`, where θ is the angle between the force and the displacement direction. Work is a scalar, measured in joules (J). |
| When is work zero? | When force is perpendicular to displacement (θ = 90°, cosθ = 0) — e.g., carrying a bag horizontally while gravity pulls straight down, or any centripetal force in uniform circular motion. Also zero if there's no displacement at all. |
| Positive vs. negative work | Positive work (0° ≤ θ < 90°) adds energy to an object. Negative work (90° < θ ≤ 180°) removes energy — e.g., friction opposing motion, or a decelerating force. |
| Kinetic energy | `KE = ½mv²`. Energy of motion. Since it depends on v², doubling speed quadruples KE — it does NOT scale linearly with velocity. |
| Gravitational potential energy | `PE_g = mgh`, measured relative to a chosen reference height (h = 0 point is your choice, but must stay consistent within a problem). |
| Elastic potential energy | `PE_s = ½kx²`, where k is the spring constant and x is displacement from the spring's natural (unstretched/uncompressed) length. |
| Work-Energy Theorem | `W_net = ΔKE = KE_f − KE_i`. The net work done on an object equals its change in kinetic energy — this connects forces (dynamics) to energy directly. |
| Conservation of mechanical energy | `KE_i + PE_i = KE_f + PE_f`, valid ONLY when no non-conservative forces (friction, air resistance, applied pushes) do work on the system. |
| Non-conservative forces & energy "loss" | Energy is never actually destroyed — friction converts mechanical energy into heat and sound (thermal/internal energy). Total energy of the universe is conserved; only *mechanical* energy decreases when non-conservative forces act. |
| Power | `P = W/t = ΔE/t`. The rate at which work is done or energy is transferred. Units: watts (W) = J/s. Two machines can do the same total work but have very different power if their times differ. |
| Power (alternate form) | `P = Fv` (force times velocity), useful for constant-force, constant-velocity situations like a car engine at cruising speed. |
| Efficiency | `Efficiency = (useful energy output / total energy input) × 100%`. Always less than 100% for real machines because some energy always converts to heat/friction. |
| KE vs. momentum — common confusion | KE depends on v² (mass matters less proportionally); momentum depends on v¹ (linear). Two objects with equal momentum do NOT necessarily have equal KE, and vice versa. |

---

## Summary

Work transfers energy: W = Fd cosθ, and only the force component along the direction of motion counts — a force perpendicular to displacement does zero work. The work-energy theorem (W_net = ΔKE) links forces directly to changes in motion energy, while conservation of mechanical energy (KE + PE = constant) applies only when friction and other non-conservative forces are absent or ignored. Power measures the *rate* of energy transfer, not the amount, so don't confuse a "powerful" process with an "energetic" one. Energy is never destroyed by friction — it converts to heat, which is why "energy loss" in these problems really means mechanical energy converting to thermal energy.
