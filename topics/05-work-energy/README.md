# Topic 5: Work, Energy & Power

Energy methods let you solve many motion problems *without* ever using kinematics or forces directly — often much faster, especially when forces change during the motion (like a spring or a curved track).

## Key concepts to master

- **Work** — W = F·d·cosθ, where θ is the angle between the force and the displacement; measured in joules (J)
- **Zero work** — a force does zero work if it's perpendicular to displacement (e.g., normal force on level ground) or if there's no displacement at all
- **Kinetic energy** — KE = ½mv²
- **Work-energy theorem** — W_net = ΔKE (the net work done on an object equals its change in kinetic energy)
- **Gravitational potential energy** — PE_grav = mgh (relative to a chosen reference height)
- **Elastic potential energy** — PE_spring = ½kx² (for an ideal spring, displacement x from equilibrium)
- **Conservation of mechanical energy** — KE + PE = constant, *only* when non-conservative forces (friction, air resistance, applied forces) do zero net work
- **Non-conservative forces and energy "loss"** — friction converts mechanical energy to heat; it isn't destroyed, just no longer mechanical
- **Power** — P = W/t = Fv (rate of doing work or transferring energy), measured in watts (W)

## Learning objectives

By the end of this topic, students can:
1. Calculate work done by a constant force, including when force and displacement are not parallel
2. Apply the work-energy theorem to find a final speed, force, or distance
3. Apply conservation of mechanical energy to solve motion problems, correctly identifying when it does and doesn't apply
4. Calculate power in various contexts (motors, human exertion, energy transfer rate)
5. Explain, using energy, why a frictionless roller coaster's speed at the bottom of a hill doesn't depend on the shape of the hill — only its height

## Practice problems

1. A 60 kg box is pushed 4 m across a floor by a 100 N horizontal force. How much work is done?
2. A person carries a 10 kg bag horizontally across a room, walking 5 m. How much work does gravity do on the bag during this motion? Explain why.
3. A 1000 kg roller coaster car starts at rest at the top of a 30 m hill (frictionless track). Find its speed at the bottom using energy conservation.
4. A 2 kg block sliding at 5 m/s on a rough floor (μ_k = 0.2) comes to rest. Use the work-energy theorem (with friction as the net force) to find how far it slides.
5. A spring with k = 200 N/m is compressed 0.15 m. How much elastic potential energy is stored? If this energy is fully converted to kinetic energy in a 0.5 kg ball, what is the ball's launch speed?
6. A motor lifts a 50 kg load 10 m straight up in 8 seconds at constant velocity. Calculate the work done and the average power output of the motor.

## Real-world applications

- **Roller coasters** — nearly frictionless energy conversion between PE and KE is the entire design principle
- **Hybrid/electric vehicle regenerative braking** — converting kinetic energy back into stored (electrical) energy instead of losing it all to heat
- **Human metabolism and exercise** — power output (watts) as a measure of exertion, used in cycling and fitness tracking
- **Hydroelectric dams** — gravitational PE of stored water converted to KE, then electrical energy
- **Pole vaulting/archery** — elastic PE (bent pole, drawn bowstring) converted to KE
