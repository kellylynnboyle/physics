# Projectile Motion: Most-Missed Questions
Take the [interactive version](../../site/index.html#projectile) for instant feedback. The "Misconception" line names the trap each question targets.

### 1. A ball is rolled horizontally off a table at the same instant a second ball is dropped from the same height. Which hits the floor first?
- A. The dropped ball
- B. The rolling ball
- C. They land at the same time
- D. Cannot be determined

### 2. At the highest point of a projectile's path (launched at an angle), which statement is true?
- A. Velocity is zero
- B. Acceleration is zero
- C. Vertical velocity is zero, horizontal velocity is not
- D. Horizontal velocity is zero

### 3. Ignoring air resistance, what horizontal force acts on a ball once it leaves the bat?
- A. A forward force from the bat that fades
- B. A force equal to its weight
- C. No horizontal force
- D. A force proportional to its speed

### 4. On level ground, which launch angle gives the greatest range for a given speed (no air resistance)?
- A. 30°
- B. 45°
- C. 60°
- D. 90°

### 5. Two balls launched at the same speed, one at 30° and one at 60°. Compare their ranges and flight times.
- A. Same range; the 60° ball stays in the air longer
- B. Same range; same time
- C. The 60° ball goes farther and stays longer
- D. The 30° ball goes farther

### 6. A projectile is launched at 20 m/s at 30° above the horizontal. What is its initial vertical velocity?
- A. 17.3 m/s
- B. 10 m/s
- C. 20 m/s
- D. 34.6 m/s

### 7. A ball is kicked horizontally off a cliff at 10 m/s and lands 20 m from the base. If it is kicked at 20 m/s from the same cliff, where does it land?
- A. 20 m
- B. 40 m
- C. 80 m
- D. 10 m

### 8. Which quantity is constant throughout the flight of a projectile (no air resistance)?
- A. Speed
- B. Vertical velocity
- C. Horizontal velocity
- D. Velocity

---

## Answer key
1. **C** — *Misconception: Horizontal speed affects fall time.* Both start with zero vertical velocity and share the same vertical acceleration and height, so they take the same time to fall. Horizontal speed has no effect on that.
2. **C** — *Misconception: Everything stops at the top.* Only the vertical component is zero at the peak. The horizontal velocity stays constant, and gravity still accelerates the object downward.
3. **C** — *Misconception: Motion requires a continuing force.* After the ball leaves the bat the only force is gravity (vertical). The ball keeps its horizontal velocity by inertia.
4. **B** — *Misconception: Higher launch = farther.* Range ∝ sin 2θ, which is maximized at θ = 45°. A 90° launch goes straight up with zero range.
5. **A** — *Misconception: Equal range implies equal time.* Complementary angles give equal range, but the 60° launch has a larger vertical component, so it spends more time in the air (with a smaller horizontal speed).
6. **B** — *Misconception: Mixing up sin and cos.* v_y = v sin θ = 20 sin 30° = 10 m/s. The 17.3 m/s value is the horizontal component (20 cos 30°).
7. **B** — *Misconception: Speed changes the fall time.* Fall time depends only on cliff height, so it is unchanged. Range = vₓ·t doubles when vₓ doubles: 40 m.
8. **C** — *Misconception: Confusing velocity components with total velocity.* Horizontal velocity is constant because aₓ = 0. Speed and vertical velocity change continuously, so the velocity vector changes too.
