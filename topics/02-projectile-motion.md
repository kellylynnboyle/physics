# Unit 2 – Projectile Motion

NGSS: HS-PS2-1 · Objectives PM1–PM4 (see [standards-and-assessment.md](../standards-and-assessment.md))

## Key concepts
- Independence of horizontal and vertical motion
- Vector components: v_x = v cosθ, v_y = v sinθ
- Horizontal launch; angled launch
- Time of flight, range, maximum height
- Neglect air resistance

## Cornell notes

| Cue / Question | Notes |
|---|---|
| What is a projectile, and what is its only acceleration? | An object moving under gravity alone. a_y = 9.8 m/s² down; **a_x = 0**. Path is a parabola. |
| Why can I treat x and y separately? | The two motions are independent. Horizontal: constant velocity, x = v_x t. Vertical: free fall. Time connects them. |
| If one ball is dropped and another fired horizontally from the same height, which lands first? | **Same time.** Fall time depends only on height: t = √(2h/g). Ex: h = 45 m → t = √(90/9.8) = 3.03 s; at 10 m/s the range is 30.3 m. |
| How do I split an angled launch into components? | v_x = v cosθ, v_y = v sinθ. Ex: 20 m/s at 30°: v_x = 17.3 m/s, v_y = 10 m/s. |
| What is the velocity and acceleration at the top of the path? | v_y = 0 but **v_x ≠ 0**, so the ball is still moving (speed = v_x). Acceleration is still 9.8 m/s² down. |
| How do I find time of flight, height, and range for an angled launch (level ground)? | t = 2v_y/g · H = v_y²/2g · R = v_x t. Ex: 20 m/s at 30°: t = 2.04 s, H = 5.10 m, R = 35.3 m. |
| Which launch angle gives maximum range, and which angles give equal range? | 45° (level ground, no air). Complementary angles (e.g. 30° and 60°) give the same range. |

**Memory tricks:** "Horizontal: constant speed, vertical: constant acceleration" · "Time is the bridge between x and y" · "Never mix x and y numbers in one equation."

**Summary:** A projectile is two simple motions at once. Solve vertical motion for time, then use that time in the horizontal motion.

## Practice problems
1. A ball leaves a 45 m cliff horizontally at 10 m/s. Find the fall time and horizontal range. **3.03 s; 30.3 m**
2. A ball is launched at 20 m/s, 30° above horizontal. Find time of flight, max height and range. **2.04 s; 5.10 m; 35.3 m**
3. Explain with components why a 30° and a 60° launch at the same speed have the same range.
4. A pilot drops a package while flying level at 80 m/s. Where is the plane relative to the package when it lands? (**Directly above**, since both keep v_x.)
