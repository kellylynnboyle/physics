# Unit 2: Projectile Motion

**Suggested pacing:** about 2 weeks · **Standards link:** extends Unit 1 toward HS-PS2-1 (see [docs/standards-alignment.md](../docs/standards-alignment.md))

## Learning objectives

Students will be able to:

- [ ] **P1.** Explain that horizontal and vertical motion are independent and are linked only by time.
- [ ] **P2.** Resolve an initial velocity into horizontal and vertical components using trigonometry.
- [ ] **P3.** Solve horizontally launched projectile problems (time, range, impact velocity).
- [ ] **P4.** Solve angled-launch problems (time of flight, maximum height, range).
- [ ] **P5.** Predict how changing launch speed or angle changes range and height, and justify the prediction.
- [ ] **P6.** Design and carry out an experiment to test a prediction of where a projectile will land.

## Key concepts

| Concept | What students must be able to say or do |
|---|---|
| Independence of motion | Vertical: constant acceleration g. Horizontal: constant velocity (no horizontal force). |
| Components | vₓ = v cosθ, v_y = v sinθ (θ measured from the horizontal). |
| Time links the axes | Find t from the vertical motion, then use it in x = vₓt. |
| Trajectory | Parabolic path; symmetric if the launch and landing heights are equal. |
| Peak of the path | v_y = 0, vₓ unchanged, a = g downward. |
| Range | R = v₀² sin(2θ) / g (same launch and landing height). Max at 45°; complementary angles give equal ranges. |
| Air resistance | Ignored in class models; real trajectories are shorter and not symmetric. |

## Cornell notes

| Cue / Questions | Notes |
|---|---|
| What is a projectile? | Object moving under gravity alone after launch. |
| How do horizontal and vertical motion relate? | Independent. Horizontal: constant v. Vertical: free fall. Same time t for both. |
| How do I split a launch velocity? | vₓ = v cosθ, v_y = v sinθ. Draw the right triangle to check. |
| What is true at the top? | v_y = 0, vₓ ≠ 0, a = 9.8 m/s² down. |
| How do I find time of flight (same height)? | t = 2v_y/g. For a horizontal launch from height h: t = √(2h/g). |
| How do I find range? | R = vₓ · t. |
| Which angle gives max range? | 45° (no air resistance, same height). 30° and 60° (or any angles adding to 90°) match. |
| How does doubling v₀ change range? | Range × 4 (R ∝ v₀²). |
| Why do a dropped ball and a horizontally thrown ball land together? | Same vertical motion; horizontal speed doesn't affect fall time. |
| Sketch: trajectory with vₓ and v_y arrows at 3 points | vₓ arrow same length all along; v_y shrinks, zero at top, grows again downward. |

**Summary (student writes 3–4 sentences):** Explain how splitting a projectile's motion into two independent parts lets us predict where it lands.

## Most-missed questions

The [interactive quiz](../quiz/index.html) covers:

1. Horizontal velocity stays constant.
2. A dropped ball and a horizontally rolled ball land together.
3. At the top, velocity is not zero (horizontal part remains).
4. 45° gives maximum range.
5. Complementary angles give the same range.
6. Fall time from a cliff depends only on height.
7. Sine vs. cosine when resolving components.
8. Acceleration at the top is still g.
9. Total time = 2 × time to the peak.
10. Doubling speed quadruples range.

## Practice problems

<details>
<summary>Problems (answers below)</summary>

1. A ball is thrown horizontally at 15 m/s from a 19.6 m cliff. Find the time in the air, the horizontal distance and the impact speed.
2. A ball is launched at 20 m/s at 30° above the horizontal from level ground. Find the time of flight, maximum height and range.
3. A football is kicked at 25 m/s at 45°. What is its range?
4. A projectile launched at 20° has the same range as one launched at what other angle (same speed)?
5. A plane flying horizontally at 100 m/s at an altitude of 500 m releases a package. How far ahead of the release point (horizontally) does it land? Ignore air resistance.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. t = √(2·19.6/9.8) = **2.0 s**; x = 15 × 2.0 = **30 m**; v_y = 9.8 × 2.0 = 19.6 m/s, so speed = √(15² + 19.6²) ≈ **24.7 m/s**.
2. vₓ = 17.3 m/s, v_y = 10 m/s. t = 2(10)/9.8 ≈ **2.04 s**; H = 10²/(2·9.8) ≈ **5.1 m**; R = 17.3 × 2.04 ≈ **35.3 m**.
3. R = 25²/9.8 ≈ **63.8 m**.
4. **70°** (20° + 70° = 90°).
5. t = √(2·500/9.8) ≈ 10.1 s; x = 100 × 10.1 ≈ **1,010 m**.

</details>

## Lab and hands-on ideas

- **Predict the landing spot:** roll a ball down a ramp off a table, measure speed with photogates (or timing), calculate where it lands, then place a cup there. Class "accuracy challenge."
- **Angle vs. range:** launch a spring-loaded launcher or water-bottle rocket at 15°, 30°, 45°, 60°, 75°, and graph the results.
- **Two-ball drop:** simultaneous drop and horizontal launch, recorded with video or listened to for a single "thud."
- **PhET *Projectile Motion* simulation:** turn air resistance on and off to compare to the model.

## Real-world applications

- **Sports:** basketball free-throw arc, long jump takeoff angle, soccer free kicks, javelin and discus.
- **Emergency response and firefighting:** aiming a hose stream to hit a target at a given distance and height.
- **Engineering:** water fountains, irrigation sprinklers and conveyor belt launch design.
- **Safety and design:** predicting where debris or cargo will land.
- **Why 45° isn't perfect in real life:** air resistance and different launch/landing heights shift the best angle (a shot-putter's best angle is lower, about 35–42°).

## Assessment criteria

| Level | Evidence for objectives P1–P6 |
|---|---|
| **4 Advanced** | Solves angled launches to different heights, justifies parameter changes with equations, and explains how air resistance modifies the model. |
| **3 Proficient** | Resolves components correctly, links time between axes, and solves horizontal and level-ground launches. Quiz ≥ 75%. |
| **2 Developing** | Solves horizontal launches with help; mixes up components or applies g to the horizontal direction. Quiz 60–74%. |
| **1 Beginning** | Treats projectile as a single straight-line motion; cannot resolve components. Quiz < 60%. |
