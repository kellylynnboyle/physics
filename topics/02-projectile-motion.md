# Unit 2: Projectile Motion

**Pacing:** ~2 weeks · **Quiz:** [Projectile Motion](../quiz/index.html) · **Standards support:** HS-PS2-1 (using mathematical representations to predict motion)

> A projectile moves under gravity alone. Treat horizontal and vertical motion as two independent 1-D problems linked by time.

## Learning objectives

- [ ] **P1.** Resolve a launch velocity into horizontal and vertical components.
- [ ] **P2.** Explain why horizontal velocity is constant and vertical acceleration is g for an ideal projectile.
- [ ] **P3.** Solve horizontally launched problems (cliff, table, plane drop).
- [ ] **P4.** Solve angled-launch problems for time of flight, maximum height and range.
- [ ] **P5.** Predict how range changes with angle and speed, including the complementary-angle result.
- [ ] **P6.** Evaluate the assumption of "no air resistance" and describe how drag changes real trajectories.

## Key concepts and equations

| Concept | Equation |
|---|---|
| Components | vₓ = v cosθ, v_y = v sinθ |
| Horizontal motion (a = 0) | x = vₓt |
| Vertical motion (a = −g) | y = y₀ + v_y t − ½gt², v_y(t) = v_y0 − gt |
| Time of flight (level ground) | T = 2v sinθ / g |
| Maximum height | H = (v sinθ)² / 2g |
| Range (level ground) | R = v² sin2θ / g |
| Speed at any moment | v = √(vₓ² + v_y²) |
| Launch angle at any moment | θ = tan⁻¹(v_y / vₓ) |

| | Horizontal (x) | Vertical (y) |
|---|---|---|
| Initial velocity | | |
| Acceleration | 0 | −g |
| Displacement | | |
| Time | **shared** | **shared** |

## Cornell notes

| Cue / Questions | Notes |
|---|---|
| What is a projectile? | An object moving only under gravity (air resistance neglected). Its path is a parabola. |
| Why are x and y independent? | Gravity is vertical, so it only changes v_y. The x-motion has no force and keeps a constant vₓ. |
| What links x and y? | **Time.** Find t from the vertical motion, then use it for x (or vice versa). |
| What happens at the top of the path? | v_y = 0, but vₓ is unchanged and a = g down. The speed is not zero. |
| Horizontal launch: what is v_y0? | Zero. Fall time t = √(2h/g). Range = vₓt. |
| What angle gives max range? | 45° on level ground. Angles that add to 90° give equal range. |
| How does speed affect range? | R ∝ v². Doubling speed gives 4× range. |
| What changes with air resistance? | Both vₓ and v_y decrease, range is shorter, the path is asymmetric and the best angle is below 45°. |

**Summary (write your own):** _A projectile is two independent motions at once: constant velocity sideways and free fall vertically. Time is the shared variable. Use components and the problem-solving table._

## Common misconceptions

1. A heavier or faster-moving ball "falls slower" than a dropped one. *(Same fall time.)*
2. A force keeps the projectile moving forward after launch.
3. At the highest point, the projectile stops or has zero acceleration.
4. A 60° shot goes farther than a 45° shot. *(Only 45° is farthest.)*
5. A package dropped from a plane falls straight down relative to the plane's path. *(It stays below the plane.)*
6. Using the total speed in the range or time formula instead of components.

## Practice problems (g = 10 m/s²)

| # | Problem | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A ball rolls off a 20 m cliff at 12 m/s. Find time in the air, horizontal range, and impact speed. | 2 s; 24 m; ≈ 23.3 m/s |
| 2 | A ball is launched at 30 m/s at 30°. Find vₓ and v_y, time of flight, max height and range. | 26 m/s, 15 m/s; 3 s; 11.25 m; ≈ 78 m |
| 3 | A soccer ball kicked at 20 m/s at 45° lands on level ground. How far does it go? | 40 m |
| 4 | Two shots, same speed, angles 30° and 60°. Compare ranges and flight times. | Equal range; 60° has the longer time (√3 times) |
| 5 | A plane at 80 m/s drops supplies from 500 m. How far ahead of the drop point do they land? | 800 m (t = 10 s) |
| 6 | A ball is thrown horizontally at 15 m/s from a 45 m tower. What angle below the horizontal does the velocity make at impact? | t = 3 s, v_y = 30 m/s, θ = tan⁻¹(2) ≈ 63° |
| 7 | *Challenge:* A stunt car must clear a 10 m gap at the same height. Minimum launch speed at 45°? | 10 m/s (R = v²/g) |
| 8 | *Challenge:* A basketball leaves the hand at 7 m/s at 50°. How long until it reaches its peak? | v_y = 5.36 m/s, so 0.54 s |

## Lab and activity ideas

- **Projectile Motion (PhET):** vary angle, speed and mass; turn air resistance on and off.
- **Ball-launcher range lab:** measure range at 15°, 30°, 45°, 60°, 75°, graph R vs. angle.
- **Horizontal launch prediction:** predict where a marble lands off a table, then place a cup to test it.
- **Video analysis:** film a basketball free throw and plot x-t and y-t using Tracker.

- **Gaming and animation:** physics engines use the same equations.
