## Key concepts

- **1st law:** an object keeps its velocity (including v = 0) unless a net force acts — no force is needed to *keep* moving
- **2nd law:** ΣF = ma (vector; same direction as a)
- **3rd law:** forces come in equal-magnitude, opposite-direction pairs acting on **different** objects
- **Weight** W = mg (force, N) vs **mass** (kg, amount of matter)
- **Normal force** N is perpendicular to the surface; it is *not* always equal to mg
- **Friction:** f_k = μ_k N (sliding); static friction adjusts up to f_s,max = μ_s N
- **Free-body diagram:** one object, only forces on it, drawn as arrows from its center
- **Equilibrium:** ΣF = 0 → constant velocity (may be zero)

## Cornell notes

| Cue | Notes |
|-----|-------|
| Inertia | Resistance to changes in motion; more mass = more inertia. |
| Net force zero means… | a = 0: at rest **or** constant velocity. |
| ΣF = ma | Add forces as vectors, then divide by m. a ∥ ΣF. |
| Units | 1 N = 1 kg·m/s². |
| Mass vs weight | Mass doesn't change with location; weight = mg does (Moon g ≈ 1.6 m/s²). |
| Normal force | Pushes perpendicular from the surface. Elevator accelerating up → N > mg. |
| Friction | Opposes sliding. Constant-velocity push = friction exactly. |
| 3rd-law pairs | "A on B" and "B on A", same type of force, equal and opposite, different objects. Weight and normal force are **not** a pair. |
| FBD steps | 1) Pick object 2) Draw only forces on it 3) Choose axes 4) Resolve components 5) ΣF = ma each axis. |
| Incline | Along slope: mg sinθ; perpendicular: N = mg cosθ. |

## Most-missed questions

1. "Constant velocity needs a constant force" (false — net force is zero)
2. Truck hits car: which force is bigger? (equal — 3rd law)
3. Weight vs normal force as a 3rd-law pair (they are not)
4. Mass vs weight on the Moon
5. Apparent weight in an accelerating elevator
6. ΣF = ma with friction included (net, not applied, force)

## Practice problems

1. A 4 kg cart is pulled by a 20 N force with 6 N friction. Find a. *(3.5 m/s²)*
2. Find weight on Earth and the Moon (g = 1.6) of a 70 kg student. *(686 N; 112 N)*
3. A 1200 kg car accelerates at 2.5 m/s². Net force? *(3000 N)*
4. A 60 kg person in an elevator accelerates upward at 1.5 m/s². Scale reading? *(678 N)*
5. A 10 kg box slides at constant speed when pushed with 25 N. Find μ_k. *(25/98 ≈ 0.26)*
6. Challenge: A 5 kg block on a frictionless 30° incline. Find a and N. *(4.9 m/s²; 42.4 N)*

## Real-world applications

- Seatbelts and headrests: inertia in crashes
- Rockets: action–reaction with exhaust gas
- Elevators and amusement rides: apparent weight
- Tires, brakes, shoes: designing for friction; ABS brakes keep static friction
