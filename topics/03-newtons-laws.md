## Key concepts
| Concept | Meaning | Formula |
|---|---|---|
| 1st law (inertia) | No net force → constant velocity (including rest) | ΣF = 0 ⇒ a = 0 |
| 2nd law | Net force gives acceleration | ΣF = ma (N = kg·m/s²) |
| 3rd law | Forces come in equal, opposite pairs on *different* objects | F_AB = −F_BA |
| Weight | Gravitational force | W = mg |
| Normal force | Surface push, perpendicular to surface | Found from ΣF = 0 perpendicular to surface |
| Friction | Opposes sliding (or tendency to slide) | f_s ≤ μ_sN, f_k = μ_kN |
| Incline | Components of weight | along: mg sinθ · perpendicular: mg cosθ |
| Universal gravitation | Mutual attraction | F = Gm₁m₂/r² |

## Cornell notes
| Cues / questions | Notes |
|---|---|
| What does inertia mean? | The tendency to keep the current motion; measured by mass. |
| How do I draw an FBD? | Dot for the object; arrows for forces *on* it only (weight, normal, friction, tension, applied, drag). No "force of motion." |
| How do I solve a 2nd-law problem? | FBD → choose axes → write ΣF = ma for each axis → solve. |
| Mass vs. weight? | Mass (kg) is constant everywhere. Weight (N) = mg changes with g. |
| What is a 3rd-law pair? | Same type, equal size, opposite direction, on **different** objects, e.g. Earth pulls on you / you pull on Earth. |
| Does the normal force always equal mg? | No. On an incline N = mg cosθ; in an accelerating elevator N = m(g + a). |
| Static vs. kinetic friction? | Static adjusts up to a maximum; kinetic is constant and a bit smaller. |
| Terminal velocity? | Drag grows with speed until it equals weight; then net force = 0. |

**Summary:** _Forces cause changes in velocity. The net force and the mass tell me the acceleration. Every force has a partner on the other object._

## Practice problems
1. A 1200 kg car accelerates at 2.5 m/s². What net force acts on it? *(3000 N)*
2. A 60 kg person rides an elevator accelerating up at 1.5 m/s². What does the scale read? *(≈ 678 N)*
3. A 20 kg crate is pushed with 80 N; μ_k = 0.25. Find the acceleration. *(1.55 m/s²)*
4. A 10 kg box sits on a 25° incline with no slipping. Find the friction force and the minimum μ_s. *(41.4 N; 0.47)*
5. Draw FBDs for (a) a book on a table, (b) a car braking, (c) a skydiver at terminal velocity.
6. Weight of a 70 kg astronaut on the Moon (1.62 m/s²) and on Mars (3.71 m/s²)? *(113 N; 260 N)*
7. **Design (HS-PS2-3 preview):** Build a bumper from craft materials that keeps an egg intact when a cart hits a wall at 1 m/s.
8. **Challenge:** Two blocks (3 kg and 5 kg) are connected by a string over a pulley (Atwood). Find a and T. *(2.45 m/s²; 36.8 N)*
