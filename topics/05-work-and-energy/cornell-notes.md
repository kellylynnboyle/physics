# Cornell Notes: Work & Energy

**Name:** ____________  **Date:** ________  **Period:** ____
**Essential question:** How can tracking energy help us predict motion without tracking every force?

| Cue / Question (write after class) | Notes (write during class) |
|---|---|
| What is work? | Energy transferred by a force acting through a displacement: W = Fd cosθ (J). θ = 0° → max positive; 90° → zero; 180° → negative (slows object). |
| When is work zero? | No displacement, or force ⟂ displacement (e.g., carrying a box horizontally, centripetal force). |
| Kinetic energy? | KE = ½mv². Depends on v², so doubling speed → 4× KE. |
| Gravitational PE? | PE = mgh relative to a chosen zero level. Only height **changes** matter. |
| Elastic PE? | PE = ½kx² for a spring (k in N/m, x = stretch/compression). |
| Work-energy theorem? | W_net = ΔKE = ½mv_f² − ½mv_i². |
| Conservation of energy? | Energy changes form and moves between objects but is never created or destroyed. Without friction: KE + PE = constant. With friction: KE₁ + PE₁ = KE₂ + PE₂ + thermal. |
| Power? | P = W/t = Fv, in watts (1 W = 1 J/s). Rate of energy transfer. |
| Efficiency? | η = useful out / total in (always < 100%). Wasted energy mostly becomes thermal. |
| Energy bar charts? | Draw bars for KE, PE, thermal at the start and end. Total bar height stays equal. |
| Problem-solving steps? | 1) Define system and states 1 and 2. 2) Pick zero height. 3) Write energies at each state. 4) Include friction work if present. 5) Solve, check units. |

**Worked example.** Roller coaster starts at rest at 30 m. At 10 m: mg(30) = mg(10) + ½mv² → v = √(2·9.8·20) ≈ 19.8 m/s. Mass cancels.

**Sketch area:** Draw energy bar charts for a ball (a) at the top of a throw, (b) halfway down, (c) just before landing, (d) after it stops in the ground.
