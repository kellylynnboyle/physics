# Unit 5 — Work & Energy

**Standards:** HS-PS3-1, HS-PS3-2, HS-PS3-3 · **Time:** ~3 weeks · **Notes:** [cornell-notes.md](cornell-notes.md) · **Quiz:** `../../quizzes/index.html` (Work & Energy)

## Learning objectives

| Code | Objective | Level-3 evidence |
|---|---|---|
| E1 | Calculate work, including angle | W = Fd cosθ; recognizes when W = 0 |
| E2 | Calculate KE, gravitational PE, elastic PE | Uses ½mv², mgh, ½kx² with units (J) |
| E3 | Apply conservation of energy and the work–energy theorem | Solves speed-at-a-point problems for ramps, pendulums, coasters |
| E4 | Calculate power and efficiency | P = W/t; efficiency = useful out / total in |
| E5 | Model energy transfers (HS-PS3-1, 3-2) | Builds an energy bar chart or spreadsheet model for a system |

## Key concepts checklist
- [ ] Work W = F d cosθ (J); only the force component along displacement does work
- [ ] Work done by friction is negative and transfers energy to thermal energy
- [ ] Kinetic energy KE = ½mv² (scales with v²)
- [ ] Gravitational PE = mgh (relative to a chosen zero level)
- [ ] Elastic PE = ½kx²
- [ ] Work–energy theorem: W_net = ΔKE
- [ ] Conservation of mechanical energy (no non-conservative forces); with friction, E_total conserved but mechanical E is not
- [ ] Energy transfer vs transformation; systems and boundaries (HS-PS3-2)
- [ ] Power P = W/t = Fv (W); 1 hp ≈ 746 W
- [ ] Efficiency = useful output ÷ total input; energy "lost" is dispersed as thermal energy, not destroyed
- [ ] Energy at the macroscopic scale: motion + position (fields)

## Most-missed ideas
1. Carrying a heavy box horizontally at constant velocity does **no** work on the box (force ⟂ displacement).
2. KE depends on v²: double the speed → four times the KE and four times the braking distance.
3. Energy is not "used up"; it is transferred/dispersed. Total energy is conserved.
4. Choosing the zero level for PE inconsistently; only changes in PE matter.
5. Speed from energy doesn't depend on mass or path shape (frictionless).
6. Power vs energy (kWh is energy, kW is power).

## Practice problems
1. Pull a sled 10 m with 50 N at 60° above horizontal. Work? [250 J]
2. A 2 kg book is lifted 3 m. Increase in PE? [58.8 J]
3. A ball is dropped from 5 m. Speed just before impact? [9.9 m/s]
4. A coaster starts at rest 20 m up. Frictionless speed at 5 m height? [17.1 m/s]
5. A 1000 kg car at 20 m/s brakes to a stop with constant friction force 8000 N. Stopping distance? [25 m]
6. A spring (k = 200 N/m) is compressed 0.10 m. Stored energy? If it launches a 0.05 kg ball, speed? [1.0 J; 6.3 m/s]
7. A motor does 1500 J of work in 5 s. Power? [300 W]
8. A machine takes in 400 J and delivers 100 J of useful work. Efficiency? Where does the rest go? [25%; thermal]
9. *Challenge (HS-PS3-1):* Build a spreadsheet model of a ball on a ramp with friction: columns for height, KE, PE, thermal energy; verify total energy constant.

## Lab ideas
- **Energy skate park/PhET → real ramp:** compare predicted vs measured speed at the bottom of a ramp; compute energy lost to friction.
- **Pendulum energy:** photogate/video at the bottom of the swing; check mgh = ½mv².
- **Spring-launch:** compress by different x; show launch KE ∝ x².
- **Efficiency of a device:** hand-crank generator or hot-plate kettle; measure input vs useful output.
- **Capstone design (HS-PS3-3):** build a device that converts one energy form to another under constraints (rubber-band car, solar/wind-powered lifter, Rube-Goldberg stage).

## Real-world applications
- Roller coasters and ski jumps (PE ↔ KE).
- Hybrid and electric cars: regenerative braking recovers KE.
- Hydroelectric dams, wind turbines, solar panels (energy conversion chains — bridge to Semester 2).
- Fitness: power output in cycling/stairs; food Calories as energy.
- Appliance labels: kWh, efficiency ratings (LED vs incandescent).
