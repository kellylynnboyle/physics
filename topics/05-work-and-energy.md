# Unit 5 – Work & Energy

NGSS: HS-PS3-1, HS-PS3-2, HS-PS3-3 · Objectives E1–E5 (see [standards-and-assessment.md](../standards-and-assessment.md))

## Key concepts
- Work W = Fd cosθ
- Kinetic energy, gravitational and elastic potential energy
- Work–energy theorem; conservation of mechanical energy
- Power and efficiency; energy transformations and dissipation

## Cornell notes

| Cue / Question | Notes |
|---|---|
| What is work, and when is it zero? | W = Fd cosθ, in joules (J = N·m). Only the force component **along** the displacement does work. Zero if d = 0 or force ⟂ motion (carrying a box horizontally at constant velocity). Ex: 10 N at 60° over 5 m: 10(5)(0.5) = 25 J. |
| What is kinetic energy, and why does speed matter so much? | KE = ½mv². Doubling v quadruples KE. Ex: 1000 kg at 20 m/s: 200,000 J. |
| What is gravitational potential energy? | PE = mgh (relative to a chosen zero level). Ex: 2 kg raised 3 m: 2(9.8)(3) = 58.8 J. |
| What is elastic potential energy? | PE_spring = ½kx². Ex: k = 200 N/m, x = 0.1 m: 1.0 J. |
| What is the work–energy theorem? | W_net = ΔKE. Ex: car 1000 kg at 20 m/s stopped by 8000 N friction: 200,000 = 8000d → d = 25 m. |
| When can I use conservation of mechanical energy? | When only gravity/springs do work (no friction): KE + PE constant. Ex: coaster from rest at 20 m: ½v² = gh → v = √(2·9.8·20) = 19.8 m/s at bottom. With friction, some energy becomes thermal, but **total energy** is still conserved. |
| What are power and efficiency? | P = W/t (watts, W = J/s). Efficiency = useful output/input × 100%. Ex: lift 50 kg by 2 m in 4 s: 980 J/4 s = 245 W. 350 J out of 500 J in: 70%. |
| How do I track energy in a system? | Draw an energy bar chart (before/after): list KE, PE_g, PE_s, thermal. Energy changes form but total is conserved (HS-PS3-1, 3-2). |

**Memory tricks:** "W = Fd cosθ" · "Speed squared in KE" · "Initial energy = final energy + losses" · "Power = work per time."

**Summary:** Work transfers energy. Energy is stored as motion or position and changes form without being created or destroyed. Conservation of energy often solves problems faster than forces.

## Practice problems (g = 9.8 m/s²)
1. A 1000 kg car going 20 m/s brakes with 8000 N friction. Stopping distance? **25 m**
2. Spring k = 200 N/m compressed 0.1 m. Stored energy? **1.0 J**
3. A device takes in 500 J and delivers 350 J useful. Efficiency? **70%**
4. A 50 kg student climbs 2 m in 4 s. Average power? **245 W**
5. A 0.2 kg ball falls from 5 m. Speed just before landing? **9.9 m/s** (√(2·9.8·5) = √98)

## Real-world applications
Roller coasters · hydroelectric and wind power · hybrid-car regenerative braking · food Calories vs usable energy · light-bulb efficiency (LED vs incandescent) · stopping distances (KE grows with v²).
