## Key concepts
| Concept | Statement / Equation | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| Work | W = F d cos θ (only the force component along displacement does work) | J |
| Kinetic energy | KE = ½mv² | J |
| Gravitational PE | PE_g = mgh | J |
| Elastic PE | PE_s = ½kx² | J |
| Work–energy theorem | W_net = ΔKE = ½mv_f² − ½mv_i² | J |
| Conservation of energy | KE_i + PE_i + W_other = KE_f + PE_f (+ thermal) | J |
| Power | P = W/t = Fv | W |
| Efficiency | useful output ÷ total input × 100% | % |

## Cornell notes

**Essential question:** *How is energy transferred and transformed, and why is the total always accounted for?*

| Cue | Notes |
|---|---|
| What is work? | Energy transferred by a force over a distance. W = Fd cos θ. Scalar, can be + (force along motion), − (against motion), or 0 (⊥ or no displacement). |
| When is work zero? | Holding a box still; carrying it horizontally at constant velocity (force ⊥ displacement); circular motion (centripetal force ⊥ v). |
| Kinetic energy | Energy of motion. KE = ½mv². Depends on v², so doubling speed quadruples KE. |
| Potential energy | Stored energy due to position/configuration. PE_g = mgh (choose a zero height). PE_s = ½kx². |
| Work–energy theorem | The net work done on an object equals its change in KE. Positive work → speeds up; negative → slows down. |
| Conservation of energy | Energy is never created or destroyed: it transfers between objects and changes form. In an isolated system, total energy is constant. |
| Mechanical energy | KE + PE. Conserved only if no friction/drag/other non-conservative work. |
| With friction | Friction converts mechanical energy into thermal energy: KE_i + PE_i − f·d = KE_f + PE_f. Total energy is still conserved. |
| Energy bar charts | Draw bars for KE, PE_g, PE_s, thermal at the start and end; the total height must stay equal (HS-PS3-1 modeling). |
| Path independence | Speed at the bottom depends on height only (no friction), not on the path's shape. |
| Power | Rate of energy transfer. 1 W = 1 J/s. 1 horsepower ≈ 746 W. kWh is a unit of energy (3.6 MJ). |
| Efficiency | Real machines are < 100% efficient: some energy always becomes thermal energy. |
| Problem strategy | 1) Choose start/end states. 2) List energies at each. 3) Include work by non-conservative forces. 4) Solve. 5) Check units, sign and magnitude. |

## Most-missed ideas (these appear in the quiz)
1. Effort ≠ work (force ⊥ displacement).
2. KE depends on v², PE_s on x².
3. Energy method: v = √(2gh), mass cancels.
4. Using the component of force along the displacement.
5. Work vs. power (rate).
6. Path independence on frictionless ramps.
7. ΔKE uses v_f² − v_i², not (Δv)².

## Practice problems (g = 10 m/s²)
1. A 20 N force pushes a crate 5 m in the direction of the force. Work? *[100 J]*
2. A 2 kg ball is lifted 3 m. Gain in PE? Speed just before it hits the ground when dropped from there? *[60 J; √60 ≈ 7.7 m/s]*
3. A 1500 kg car goes from 0 to 25 m/s. Work done by the net force? *[468,750 J ≈ 4.7 × 10⁵ J]*
4. A 0.5 kg block is pushed against a spring (k = 200 N/m), compressing it 0.1 m, then released on a frictionless surface. Maximum speed? *[PE = 1 J → v = 2 m/s]*
5. A 50 kg skateboarder starts at rest at the top of a 4 m ramp, with friction removing 300 J. Speed at the bottom? *[PE = 2000 J; KE = 1700 J; v = √68 ≈ 8.3 m/s]*
6. A 70 kg person climbs 3 m stairs in 5 s. Power output? *[420 W]*
7. A motor uses 1000 J of electrical energy and lifts a load gaining 650 J of PE. Efficiency? *[65%]*
8. **Challenge (HS-PS3-1 spreadsheet model):** Model a ball dropped and bouncing back to 80% of its height each time. Build a spreadsheet of height, PE, KE at impact and energy lost per bounce. *[Check: each bounce keeps 80% of PE; 20% becomes thermal and sound.]*

## Lab / hands-on
- **Ramp-and-cart energy lab:** measure speed at the bottom from several heights; graph KE vs. PE lost; calculate % of energy lost to friction.
- **Spring lab:** graph force vs. stretch (k = slope) and PE vs. x².
- **Pendulum lab:** compare predicted and measured speeds at the bottom; vary mass and length.
- **HS-PS3-3 Design Project:** build a device that converts energy (e.g., a rubber-band car, solar-powered motor, hand-crank generator lighting an LED). Report the efficiency and the energy flow diagram.

## Real-world connections
- Roller coasters: energy transformations and the height of the first hill.
- Transportation: regenerative braking in hybrids/EVs; energy used by cars and bikes.
- Renewable energy: California hydroelectric dams, wind and solar and battery storage; pumped storage.
- Human body: calories as energy, power output when cycling or climbing.
- Safety: the stopping distance quadruples when speed doubles (work–energy).
