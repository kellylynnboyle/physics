# Unit 5: Work & Energy

**Pacing:** ~4 weeks · **Quiz:** [Work & Energy](../quiz/index.html) · **Standards:** HS-PS3-1 (computational model of energy), HS-PS3-2 (energy at macroscopic scale), HS-PS3-3 (design an energy-conversion device), HS-PS3-4 (thermal energy transfer)

> Energy is never created or destroyed; it changes form and moves between objects. Work is one way energy is transferred.

## Learning objectives

- [ ] **E1.** Calculate work done by a constant force at any angle, including zero and negative work.
- [ ] **E2.** Calculate kinetic, gravitational potential and elastic potential energy.
- [ ] **E3.** Apply the work-energy theorem, W_net = ΔKE.
- [ ] **E4.** Use conservation of mechanical energy for tracks, pendulums, springs and falling objects (HS-PS3-1).
- [ ] **E5.** Account for non-conservative forces by tracking thermal energy (HS-PS3-2).
- [ ] **E6.** Calculate power and efficiency; convert between W, kWh and J.
- [ ] **E7.** Build and evaluate an energy-conversion device and report its efficiency (HS-PS3-3).

## Key concepts and equations

| Concept | Equation / unit |
|---|---|
| Work | W = Fd cosθ (joule, J = N·m) |
| Kinetic energy | KE = ½mv² |
| Gravitational PE | PE_g = mgh |
| Elastic PE | PE_s = ½kx² (Hooke's law F = kx) |
| Work-energy theorem | W_net = ΔKE |
| Conservation of energy | E_initial + W_external = E_final; with friction: KE_i + PE_i = KE_f + PE_f + E_thermal |
| Power | P = W/t = Fv (watt, W = J/s) |
| Efficiency | η = useful output / total input |
| Energy bill unit | 1 kWh = 3.6 × 10⁶ J |
| Thermal energy from friction | E_th = f_k · d |

**Work sign guide**

| Angle between F and displacement | Work |
|---|---|
| 0° | positive (adds energy) |
| 90° | zero |
| 180° | negative (removes energy) |

## Cornell notes

| Cue / Questions | Notes |
|---|---|
| What is work? | Energy transferred by a force acting through a displacement: W = Fd cosθ. No displacement or perpendicular force means no work. |
| What is kinetic energy? | Energy of motion, ½mv². Depends on v², so doubling speed quadruples KE. |
| What is gravitational PE? | Stored energy due to height: mgh. Depends only on the change in height, not on the path. |
| What is elastic PE? | Energy in a compressed or stretched spring: ½kx². |
| What does the work-energy theorem say? | Net work on an object equals its change in KE. Positive net work speeds it up; negative net work slows it. |
| What is conserved? | Total energy in a closed system. Mechanical energy (KE + PE) is conserved only if there is no friction or drag. |
| Where does the "lost" energy go? | Into thermal energy (and sound). Total energy is still conserved. |
| Power vs. energy? | Power is the rate of energy transfer. A kWh is energy; a kW is power. |
| How do I set up an energy problem? | Choose two states (initial and final), write E_i + W_other = E_f, and solve. No need for the details of the path. |
| Efficiency? | η = E_useful/E_input. It is always below 100% because of heat losses. |

**Summary (write your own):** _Work transfers energy. Energy is conserved and changes form among kinetic, potential and thermal. Energy methods skip the path and often beat Newton's-law methods._

## Common misconceptions

1. Holding or carrying something does physics "work" (it is effort, not work).
2. KE is proportional to v (it is v²).
3. Friction "uses up" energy (it converts it to thermal energy).
4. Steeper ramps give more speed at the bottom (only the height matters, without friction).
5. Work is always positive.
6. Power and energy are the same (kW vs. kWh).
7. Doubling the spring compression doubles the stored energy (it quadruples it).

## Practice problems (g = 10 m/s²)

| # | Problem | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A 50 N force at 60° above the horizontal pulls a crate 10 m. Find the work done. | 250 J |
| 2 | Find the KE of a 1000 kg car at 20 m/s; then at 40 m/s. | 200 kJ; 800 kJ |
| 3 | A frictionless roller coaster starts from rest at 30 m. Find its speed at ground level. | √600 ≈ 24.5 m/s |
| 4 | A 20 kg load is lifted 3 m in 4 s. Find work and power. | 600 J; 150 W |
| 5 | A spring (k = 200 N/m) is compressed 0.10 m. Find the stored energy and the launch speed of a 0.02 kg ball. | 1 J; 10 m/s |
| 6 | A 2 kg block moving at 5 m/s slides to a stop on a surface with μ_k = 0.25. Find the stopping distance. | 5 m |
| 7 | A 0.5 kg pendulum bob is released at a height of 0.2 m above the lowest point. Find the speed at the bottom. | 2 m/s |
| 8 | A 1500 W heater runs for 3 hours. Find the energy in kWh and J, and the cost at $0.20 per kWh. | 4.5 kWh; 1.62 × 10⁷ J; $0.90 |
| 9 | A 60 W bulb converts 6 W into light. Find its efficiency. | 10% |
| 10 | *Challenge:* A 1 kg block is released from rest at height 1.0 m on a 30° incline with μ_k = 0.2. Find its speed at the bottom of the ramp. (cos30° ≈ 0.87) | Friction work = 0.2·10·0.87·2 = 3.5 J; v = √(2(10 − 3.5)) ≈ 3.6 m/s |

## Lab and activity ideas

- **Energy Skate Park (PhET):** build tracks, add friction, watch the energy bar charts.
- **Ramp and cart:** measure speed at the bottom from different heights, graph v² vs. h, and find the slope (2g).
- **Spring lab (Masses & Springs):** measure k, then verify ½kx² = ½mv² with a launched cart.
- **Staircase power lab:** time yourself climbing the stairs and compute your power output.
- **Energy-conversion device (HS-PS3-3):** build a hand-crank generator, solar oven or solar cell charger, measure efficiency, and propose an improvement.

## Real-world connections

- **Renewable energy:** hydroelectric dams convert gravitational PE to electricity.
- **Transportation:** regenerative braking in hybrid and electric vehicles recovers kinetic energy.
- **Amusement parks:** roller coaster design relies entirely on conservation of energy.
- **Home energy bills:** kWh, appliance power and efficiency ratings.
- **Sports:** pole vault (KE → elastic PE → gravitational PE), trampolines, and archery.
