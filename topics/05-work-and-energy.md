## Key concepts
| Concept | Meaning | Formula |
|---|---|---|
| Work | Force along displacement | W = Fd cosθ (J = N·m) |
| Kinetic energy | Energy of motion | KE = ½mv² |
| Gravitational PE | Position in a gravity field | PE = mgh |
| Elastic PE | Stretched/compressed spring | PE = ½kx² |
| Work–energy theorem | Net work changes KE | W_net = ΔKE |
| Conservation | No friction: total mechanical energy constant | KE₁ + PE₁ = KE₂ + PE₂ |
| With friction | Energy becomes thermal | KE₁ + PE₁ = KE₂ + PE₂ + F_f d |
| Power | Rate of energy transfer | P = W/t = Fv (W) |
| Efficiency | Useful output / input | η = E_out / E_in |

## Cornell notes
| Cues / questions | Notes |
|---|---|
| What counts as work? | A force that causes displacement along its direction. Perpendicular force: W = 0. |
| Can work be negative? | Yes: when force opposes motion (friction, braking). |
| Why is KE ∝ v²? | From the work–energy theorem; doubling speed needs 4× the work. |
| What is PE? | Energy stored by position (height) or deformation (spring). Choose the zero level to suit you. |
| Energy bar charts | Show KE, PE, thermal before/after. Total height stays equal. |
| Where does energy "go"? | Conservation means it transforms: mechanical → thermal, sound, light, etc. It is never used up. |
| How to choose a method? | Force/time/acceleration known → Newton. Heights and speeds only (no time) → energy. |
| Power vs. energy | Power is a *rate*. A 100 W bulb for 10 s uses 1000 J. |

**Summary:** _Energy changes form but the total in a closed system is constant. Work is the energy transferred by a force. Energy methods skip the details of the path._

## Practice problems
1. A 2 kg book is lifted 1.5 m. Work done and ΔPE? *(29.4 J each)*
2. A 70 kg cyclist coasts down a 12 m hill from rest with no friction. Speed at the bottom? *(15.3 m/s)*
3. A 0.5 kg ball is thrown up at 12 m/s. Max height using energy? *(7.35 m)*
4. A 1200 kg car brakes from 25 m/s to rest; friction force is 6000 N. Stopping distance? *(62.5 m)*
5. A 60 kg student climbs 4 m of stairs in 5 s. Power? *(≈ 470 W)*
6. A spring (k = 400 N/m) launches a 0.2 kg cart from 0.15 m compression on a frictionless track. Speed? *(6.7 m/s)*
7. A 500 kg coaster car starts from rest at 30 m. By the time it reaches 5 m, friction has taken 20% of the energy it released in dropping. Speed at 5 m? *(≈ 19.8 m/s)*
8. **Design (HS-PS3-3):** Build a device that converts gravitational PE into the motion of a load (e.g., a falling mass lifting another). Calculate efficiency.
9. **Computational model (HS-PS3-1):** In a spreadsheet, model a pendulum's KE and PE vs. time; verify the total stays constant.
