# Unit 5: Work & Energy

**Suggested pacing:** about 3 weeks · **Standards:** HS-PS3-1 (computational model of energy changes), HS-PS3-2 (energy as motion or stored in fields), HS-PS3-3 (design a device converting energy). HS-PS3-4 (thermal energy) is a natural extension via friction. See [docs/standards-alignment.md](../docs/standards-alignment.md).

## Learning objectives

Students will be able to:

- [ ] **E1.** Calculate work done by a constant force, including at an angle (W = Fd cosθ), and identify when no work is done.
- [ ] **E2.** Calculate kinetic energy, gravitational potential energy and elastic potential energy.
- [ ] **E3.** Apply the work–energy theorem (W_net = ΔKE).
- [ ] **E4.** Apply conservation of mechanical energy, and account for energy transferred to thermal energy by friction (HS-PS3-1).
- [ ] **E5.** Calculate power and relate it to work and time.
- [ ] **E6.** Use energy bar charts or a spreadsheet model to track energy changes in a system (HS-PS3-1, HS-PS3-2).
- [ ] **E7.** Design, build and refine a device that converts energy from one form to another within constraints (HS-PS3-3).

## Key concepts

| Concept | What students must be able to say or do |
|---|---|
| Work | W = Fd cosθ (J). Needs a force and a displacement along the force. Perpendicular force does no work. |
| Kinetic energy | KE = ½mv². Scales with v². |
| Gravitational PE | PE = mgh. Depends on height change, not path. |
| Elastic PE | PE = ½kx² for a spring. |
| Work–energy theorem | W_net = ΔKE = ½mv_f² − ½mv_i². |
| Conservation of energy | Energy is transferred or transformed, not created or destroyed. Friction converts mechanical energy to thermal. |
| Energy as motion or fields | Energy stored in gravitational, elastic, electric or magnetic fields vs. energy of motion (HS-PS3-2). |
| Power | P = W/t = Fv (W). 1 hp ≈ 746 W. |
| Efficiency | useful output / total input; always less than 100% for real devices. |

## Cornell notes

| Cue / Questions | Notes |
|---|---|
| What is work in physics? | W = Fd cosθ. Force acting through a displacement. Units: joules (J = N·m). |
| When is work zero? | No displacement, or force ⟂ displacement (carrying a box level). |
| Can work be negative? | Yes: force opposes motion (friction), θ > 90°. |
| What is kinetic energy? | Energy of motion. KE = ½mv². Doubling v ⇒ 4× KE. |
| What is gravitational potential energy? | Stored energy by position in a gravitational field. PE = mgh. |
| What is elastic potential energy? | Energy stored in a stretched or compressed spring: ½kx². |
| What is the work–energy theorem? | Net work on an object = change in its KE. |
| What does conservation of energy say? | E_total constant for the whole system. Without friction: KE + PE constant. With friction: lost mechanical energy becomes thermal. |
| What is power? | Rate of doing work, P = W/t. Units: watts (1 W = 1 J/s). |
| Why does a roller coaster's speed at the bottom not depend on the track's shape? | Energy conservation: only the drop in height matters (no friction). |

**Summary (student writes 3–4 sentences):** Describe how energy changes form as a ball is thrown up and returns, and explain what changes if air resistance is included.

## Most-missed questions

The [interactive quiz](../quiz/index.html) covers:

1. No work when the force is perpendicular to the motion.
2. Work is in joules, power is in watts.
3. KE = ½mv² (square the speed).
4. PE = mgh (include g).
5. v = √(2gh) from energy conservation.
6. Power is work divided by time.
7. Work–energy: use the difference of the squares.
8. Path shape doesn't matter without friction.
9. Friction converts KE to thermal energy (not destroyed).
10. Spring energy ½kx² (square x).

## Practice problems

<details>
<summary>Problems (answers below)</summary>

1. A 40 N force pulls a sled 10 m along the ground at 60° above horizontal. How much work does the force do?
2. A 0.5 kg ball is dropped from 10 m. How fast is it moving when it is 4 m above the ground? Ignore air resistance.
3. A 1200 kg car moving at 25 m/s brakes to a stop with a constant friction force of 6000 N. How far does it travel?
4. A spring (k = 400 N/m) is compressed 0.15 m and launches a 0.10 kg block from a frictionless surface. Find the stored energy and the launch speed.
5. A 70 kg student climbs a 3 m flight of stairs in 4.0 s. What is the power output?

</details>

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. W = 40 × 10 × cos60° = **200 J**.
2. Drop of 6 m: v = √(2 × 9.8 × 6) ≈ **10.8 m/s**.
3. KE = ½(1200)(25²) = 375,000 J; d = 375,000/6000 = **62.5 m**.
4. PE = ½(400)(0.15²) = **4.5 J**; v = √(2 × 4.5 / 0.10) ≈ **9.5 m/s**.
5. P = (70)(9.8)(3)/4.0 ≈ **515 W**.

</details>

## Lab and hands-on ideas

- **Energy skate park (PhET *Energy Skate Park*):** build tracks, toggle friction, use bar charts.
- **Ball-drop energy audit (HS-PS3-1):** drop a ball, measure rebound height, compute PE lost and the fraction of mechanical energy converted to other forms.
- **Spring launcher:** compare ½kx² to measured KE (via photogate speed) and discuss losses.
- **Stair-run power lab:** compute personal power output and compare with household devices.
- **Energy-conversion design challenge (HS-PS3-3):** build a device (e.g., rubber-band car, hand-crank generator lighting an LED, solar-powered cart) with constraints on cost and materials; measure efficiency and iterate.
- **Spreadsheet model:** compute KE, PE and total energy at each time step from motion-sensor data.

## Real-world applications

- **Roller coasters:** the first hill is the tallest because it stores all the energy the ride will use.
- **Hybrid and electric vehicles:** regenerative braking converts KE back into stored electrical energy.
- **Hydroelectric dams and pumped storage:** converting gravitational PE to electricity and back.
- **Appliance ratings:** watts vs. kilowatt-hours on utility bills; efficiency labels.
- **Bows, trampolines and pole vaulting:** elastic PE converted to KE and gravitational PE.
- **Stopping distance:** braking work equals the car's KE, which is why speed matters so much.

## Assessment criteria

| Level | Evidence for objectives E1–E7 |
|---|---|
| **4 Advanced** | Applies work–energy and conservation to multi-stage problems with friction, builds a computational energy model, and evaluates a device's efficiency with data. |
| **3 Proficient** | Calculates W, KE, PE and P correctly with units; applies conservation of energy to frictionless problems and accounts for thermal energy qualitatively. Quiz ≥ 75%. |
| **2 Developing** | Uses single formulas correctly; struggles with angle in work, squared terms, or choosing between energy and force methods. Quiz 60–74%. |
| **1 Beginning** | Confuses work, energy and power; believes friction destroys energy. Quiz < 60%. |
