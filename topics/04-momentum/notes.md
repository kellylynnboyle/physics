# Cornell Notes — Momentum

**Topic:** Momentum & Impulse | **Unit:** 4 | **Course:** 11th Grade Physics

---

## Notes

| Cue / Question | Notes |
|---|---|
| What is momentum? | `p = mv`. A vector quantity describing "quantity of motion" — depends on both mass and velocity. Units: kg·m/s. Direction matters: momentum in opposite directions has opposite signs. |
| What is impulse? | `J = FΔt` — the product of force and the time interval it acts over. Impulse is also a vector, and it equals the **change** in momentum. |
| Impulse-Momentum Theorem | `J = Δp = mΔv = m(v_f − v_i)`. A given change in momentum can result from a large force over a short time, OR a small force over a long time — same impulse, same Δp. |
| Why airbags/padding work | They increase the time (Δt) over which a collision force acts, which decreases the peak force needed to produce the same Δp (impulse-momentum theorem). |
| Conservation of momentum | In an **isolated system** (no net external force), total momentum before an interaction equals total momentum after: `p_before = p_after` (system total, not per-object). |
| Applies to a system, not one object | An individual object's momentum can change (it experiences internal forces from other objects in the collision) — it's the SYSTEM total that stays constant, as long as no external force acts on the system. |
| Elastic collisions | Both momentum AND kinetic energy are conserved. Objects bounce off each other without permanent deformation or heat loss (e.g., idealized billiard balls). |
| Inelastic collisions | Momentum is conserved, but kinetic energy is **not** — some KE converts to heat, sound, or deformation. Most real-world collisions are inelastic. |
| Perfectly inelastic collisions | The objects stick together after colliding and move with a common final velocity: `m₁v₁ + m₂v₂ = (m₁+m₂)v_f`. This type loses the maximum possible kinetic energy while still conserving momentum. |
| Explosions ("negative collisions") | A single object splits into pieces that fly apart. Total momentum before (often zero, if starting at rest) equals the vector sum of all pieces' momenta after. |
| Momentum vs. energy — key distinction | Momentum is ALWAYS conserved in an isolated system, for any type of collision. Kinetic energy is only conserved in elastic collisions. Don't assume both are conserved by default. |
| Vector nature | Always assign a positive direction and keep track of signs. Two objects moving toward each other have momenta with opposite signs, which can partially or fully cancel. |

---

## Summary

Momentum (p = mv) and impulse (J = FΔt) are connected by the impulse-momentum theorem: impulse equals the change in momentum. In any isolated system, total momentum is conserved regardless of what kind of collision occurs — elastic, inelastic, or explosive — but kinetic energy is only conserved in elastic collisions. The most important habit to build is defining a positive direction before assigning signs to velocities, since momentum problems are almost always vector problems in disguise.
