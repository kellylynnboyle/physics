## Practice Problems

1. A 0.5 kg ball moving at 8 m/s is struck by a bat and reverses direction, leaving at 12 m/s. Find the impulse delivered by the bat.
2. A 900 kg car moving at 20 m/s rear-ends a stationary 900 kg car, and they lock together. Find their common velocity after the crash, and the fraction of kinetic energy lost.
3. A 60 kg skater at rest pushes off a 90 kg skater, also at rest. If the 60 kg skater moves backward at 3 m/s, find the 90 kg skater's velocity.
4. Compare the average force on a falling egg landing on concrete (Δt ≈ 0.002 s) vs. landing on a pillow (Δt ≈ 0.5 s) for the same impulse.
5. A 2 kg cart at +4 m/s collides elastically with a stationary 2 kg cart. Predict the outcome using both momentum AND kinetic energy conservation, then check it matches the equal-mass elastic collision rule (velocities exchange).
6. A rocket in deep space ejects fuel to accelerate. Explain the process using momentum conservation instead of Newton's 3rd Law.

## Real-World Applications

- **Vehicle safety:** Crumple zones and airbags extend collision time to reduce force (impulse-momentum theorem)
- **Sports:** Follow-through in golf/baseball swings increases contact time to maximize impulse transferred to the ball
- **Rocket propulsion:** Conservation of momentum explains thrust without needing something to "push against"
- **Ballistic pendulum:** Classic lab/demonstration for measuring bullet speed using perfectly inelastic collision physics
- **Packaging design:** Bubble wrap and foam padding increase Δt during drops to lower peak impact force
