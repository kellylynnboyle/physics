# Cornell Notes: Momentum

**Name:** ____________  **Date:** ________  **Period:** ____
**Essential question:** How can we predict the outcome of a collision, and how do we reduce the force in one?

| Cue / Question (write after class) | Notes (write during class) |
|---|---|
| What is momentum? | p = mv, "mass in motion." Vector (same direction as v), unit kg·m/s. |
| What is impulse? | J = FΔt, the push times how long it acts. Unit N·s = kg·m/s. Area under a force-time graph. |
| Impulse-momentum theorem? | FΔt = Δp = mv_f − mv_i. A force acting over time changes momentum. |
| Why do airbags/crumple zones help? | Δp is fixed by the crash. Longer Δt → smaller average force F = Δp/Δt. |
| Why does bouncing give more impulse? | Reversing direction: Δp = m(v) − m(−v) = 2mv, compared with mv for stopping. |
| Conservation of momentum? | In an isolated system (no net external force), total p before = total p after. |
| Perfectly inelastic collision? | Objects stick: m₁v₁ + m₂v₂ = (m₁ + m₂)v′. KE is lost to heat, sound, deformation. |
| Elastic collision? | Both p and KE are conserved (objects bounce, little deformation). |
| What about explosions and recoil? | System starts at rest, total p = 0, so m₁v₁ = −m₂v₂. Lighter piece moves faster. |
| Problem-solving steps? | 1) Choose + direction. 2) Write p before and after with signs. 3) Set equal. 4) Solve. 5) Check KE to classify. |

**Worked example.** 2 kg at 6 m/s hits a 4 kg at rest and they stick: 12 = 6v′ → v′ = 2 m/s. KE before = 36 J, after = 12 J, so 24 J lost.
