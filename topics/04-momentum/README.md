# Unit 4: Momentum

**Standards:** HS-PS2-2 (use mathematical representations to support the claim that total momentum of a system is conserved when there is no net force); HS-PS2-3 (apply scientific and engineering ideas to design a device that minimizes the force on a macroscopic object during a collision).
**Science & Engineering Practices:** Using mathematics; constructing explanations and designing solutions.
**Suggested pacing:** 2 to 2.5 weeks

## Learning objectives
Students will be able to…
- [ ] **M1.** Define momentum (p = mv) and impulse (J = FΔt) and state their units and vector nature.
- [ ] **M2.** Apply the impulse-momentum theorem, FΔt = Δp, to collisions and safety situations.
- [ ] **M3.** Apply conservation of momentum to one-dimensional collisions and explosions/recoil.
- [ ] **M4.** Classify collisions as elastic, inelastic, or perfectly inelastic by comparing kinetic energy before and after.
- [ ] **M5.** Explain why momentum is conserved in an isolated system and what counts as an external force.
- [ ] **M6.** Design, build, and test a device (e.g., egg-drop or cart bumper) that minimizes collision force, and justify it with FΔt = Δp.

## Key concepts and equations
| Concept | Equation / idea |
|---|---|
| Momentum | p = mv (vector, kg·m/s) |
| Impulse | J = F_avg Δt = Δp = mv_f − mv_i |
| Conservation | Σp_before = Σp_after (no net external force) |
| Perfectly inelastic | m₁v₁ + m₂v₂ = (m₁ + m₂)v′ |
| Elastic | momentum **and** KE conserved |
| Recoil | 0 = m₁v₁ + m₂v₂ (from rest) |
| Force and time | Same Δp: longer Δt → smaller F |

## Most-missed ideas
1. Momentum is a vector. Opposite directions cancel.
2. Momentum is always conserved in an isolated system; kinetic energy is not.
3. Airbags change the time, not the momentum change.
4. Bouncing gives a bigger impulse than sticking (Δp ≈ 2mv).
5. Forgetting direction (sign) in recoil and head-on problems.
6. Forgetting to add both masses after a stick-together collision.

## Lab ideas
- **Dynamics-cart collisions:** Measure velocities before and after elastic (magnets) and inelastic (Velcro) collisions; compute Σp and ΣKE.
- **Egg-drop or cart-bumper design challenge (HS-PS2-3):** Constraints, criteria, test, redesign. Use a force sensor or video to compare impact times.
- **Impulse with a force sensor:** Find the area under the F–t graph and compare with Δp.

## Real-world applications
- Car safety: airbags, crumple zones, seat belts, helmets.
- Sports: follow-through in golf/baseball, catching a ball by "giving" with the hands, boxing gloves.
- Rockets and jets: momentum conservation in thrust.
- Accident reconstruction: using momentum to estimate pre-crash speeds.
- Billiards and Newton's cradle.

## Practice problems
1. A 0.145 kg baseball moving 40 m/s is hit back at 50 m/s. Find the impulse and the average force if contact lasts 1.0 ms.
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>Δp = 0.145(50 − (−40)) = 13.05 kg·m/s; F = 13.05/0.001 ≈ 13,000 N.</details>
2. A 1500 kg car moving at 20 m/s hits a 1000 kg car at rest and they stick. Find the speed afterward and the KE lost.
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>v′ = 30,000/2500 = 12 m/s; KE: 300,000 J → 180,000 J, so 120,000 J lost.</details>
3. A 0.5 kg cart moving at 4 m/s makes an elastic collision with an identical cart at rest. What are the final velocities?
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>For equal masses, they exchange velocities: first cart 0 m/s, second 4 m/s.</details>
4. A 500 kg cannon (not counting the shell) fires a 10 kg shell at 100 m/s. Find the cannon's recoil speed.
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>500v = 10(100) → v = 2 m/s backward.</details>
5. A 0.06 kg egg hits the floor at 5 m/s and stops. Compare the average force for a stop in 0.002 s (hard floor) and 0.1 s (foam pad).
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>Δp = 0.3 N·s. F = 150 N on the floor vs 3 N on the pad (50 times smaller).</details>

## Assessment criteria
| Level | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **Beginning (1)** | Defines p and J but cannot choose a direction or set up a conservation equation. |
| **Developing (2)** | Solves simple stick-together collisions; forgets signs or mixes elastic/inelastic ideas. |
| **Proficient (3)** | Uses sign conventions, applies FΔt = Δp and conservation correctly, and classifies collisions with KE evidence. Quiz score ≥ 80%. |
| **Advanced (4)** | Handles recoil and two-stage problems, and justifies a device design with data on force reduction and trade-offs. |
