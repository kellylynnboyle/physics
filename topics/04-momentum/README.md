# Unit 4 — Momentum

**Standards:** HS-PS2-2, HS-PS2-3 · **Time:** ~3 weeks · **Notes:** [cornell-notes.md](cornell-notes.md) · **Quiz:** `../../quizzes/index.html` (Momentum)

## Learning objectives

| Code | Objective | Level-3 evidence |
|---|---|---|
| M1 | Calculate momentum and impulse | p = mv and J = FΔt with units and direction |
| M2 | Apply the impulse–momentum theorem | Explains why longer stopping time lowers force |
| M3 | Apply conservation of momentum (collisions, explosions) | Defines the system and solves for a final velocity |
| M4 | Classify collisions | Uses KE before/after to classify elastic vs inelastic |
| M5 | Design to reduce collision force (HS-PS2-3) | Tests ≥ 2 designs, uses impulse reasoning |

## Key concepts checklist
- [ ] Momentum p = mv (vector, kg·m/s)
- [ ] Impulse J = F_avg Δt = Δp (N·s = kg·m/s); area under F–t graph
- [ ] Newton's 2nd law in momentum form: F_net = Δp/Δt
- [ ] System, internal vs external forces; closed/isolated system → total p conserved
- [ ] Law: Σp_before = Σp_after (in each direction; use signs)
- [ ] Collision types: elastic (KE conserved), inelastic (KE lost), perfectly inelastic (stick together)
- [ ] Explosions/recoil: total p = 0 stays 0
- [ ] Safety: increase Δt (airbags, crumple zones, padding) to decrease F for the same Δp
- [ ] Momentum is conserved in *all* isolated collisions; KE only in elastic ones

## Most-missed ideas
1. Forgetting direction: a ball bouncing back has Δp = 2mv, not 0.
2. Thinking KE is conserved in every collision.
3. Mixing impulse and force: same impulse can come from big F over short Δt or small F over long Δt.
4. Sign errors in 1-D collisions (choose + direction and stick with it).
5. Choosing the wrong system (include both objects!).

## Practice problems
1. Find the momentum of a 1500 kg car at 20 m/s. [30,000 kg·m/s]
2. A 0.15 kg ball approaches a bat at 20 m/s and leaves at 20 m/s the other way. Find the impulse. [6.0 N·s, opposite to initial]
3. A 2 kg cart at 3 m/s collides and sticks with a 1 kg cart at rest. Final v? [2 m/s]
4. A 4 kg rifle fires a 0.010 kg bullet at 400 m/s. Recoil speed? [1 m/s]
5. A 1 kg cart at 4 m/s hits an identical stationary cart elastically. Final velocities? [0 and 4 m/s]
6. A 70 kg driver stops from 15 m/s. Compare force if stopping time is 0.05 s vs 0.5 s. [21,000 N vs 2,100 N]
7. A force of 10 N acts for 3 s on a 5 kg object at rest. Final speed? [6 m/s]
8. *Challenge:* Two skaters push off from rest (60 kg and 40 kg). If the lighter moves 3 m/s, how fast does the heavier move? [2 m/s opposite]

## Lab ideas
- **Collision carts:** use motion sensors/video to verify Σp before = after for elastic and sticky collisions; compute KE lost.
- **Egg-drop / crumple-zone design (HS-PS2-3):** criteria, constraints, two test iterations, force-time reasoning.
- **Force plate or phone sensor:** jump-landing with stiff vs bent knees.

## Real-world applications
- Car safety: airbags, crumple zones, seat belts, helmets.
- Sports: follow-through in golf/baseball, boxing gloves, catching a ball by "giving".
- Space: rocket propulsion, satellite maneuvers, spacecraft docking.
- Forensics: crash reconstruction from skid marks and post-crash velocities.
