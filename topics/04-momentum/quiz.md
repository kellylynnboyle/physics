# Momentum: Most-Missed Questions
Take the [interactive version](../../site/index.html#momentum) for instant feedback. The "Misconception" line names the trap each question targets.

### 1. A 60 kg runner moves at 5.0 m/s. A 0.010 kg bullet moves at 800 m/s. Which has more momentum?
- A. The bullet, because it is much faster
- B. The runner
- C. They are equal
- D. Cannot be determined

### 2. A baseball catcher moves her glove backward while catching a fast ball. Why does this reduce the sting?
- A. It reduces the ball's momentum
- B. It increases the time of the stop, so the average force is smaller
- C. It reduces the impulse
- D. It makes the ball lose mass

### 3. In which type of collision is total momentum NOT conserved (for an isolated system)?
- A. Elastic
- B. Inelastic
- C. Perfectly inelastic
- D. None of them

### 4. A ball hits a wall and sticks. An identical ball hits the same wall at the same speed and bounces back with nearly the same speed. Which receives the larger impulse?
- A. The one that sticks
- B. The one that bounces
- C. Same impulse
- D. Neither: the wall doesn't move

### 5. A 5.0 kg rifle fires a 0.010 kg bullet at 400 m/s. Compare the recoil speed of the rifle to the bullet.
- A. The rifle moves at 400 m/s too
- B. The rifle moves much slower in the opposite direction (≈0.8 m/s)
- C. The rifle moves faster than the bullet
- D. The rifle does not move

### 6. Two identical carts approach each other with equal speed and stick together on collision. What is their velocity right after?
- A. Same speed as before
- B. Half the speed
- C. Zero
- D. Twice the speed

### 7. A 1500 kg car at 10 m/s hits and sticks to a 1000 kg car at rest. What is their combined velocity?
- A. 10 m/s
- B. 6.0 m/s
- C. 4.0 m/s
- D. 5.0 m/s

### 8. The area under a force–time graph represents…
- A. Work
- B. Impulse
- C. Power
- D. Acceleration

---

## Answer key
1. **B** — *Misconception: Speed alone determines momentum.* Runner: 60 × 5 = 300 kg·m/s. Bullet: 0.010 × 800 = 8 kg·m/s. Mass matters as much as velocity.
2. **B** — *Misconception: Reducing force requires reducing impulse.* Impulse (Δp) is the same either way. Since F = Δp/Δt, a longer stopping time means a smaller average force.
3. **D** — *Misconception: Inelastic collisions lose momentum.* Momentum is conserved in every collision of an isolated system. Only kinetic energy is lost in inelastic collisions.
4. **B** — *Misconception: Stopping is the maximum change.* Sticking: Δp = 0 − mv = −mv. Bouncing: Δp = −mv − mv = −2mv. The bounce changes momentum twice as much, so the impulse is twice as large.
5. **B** — *Misconception: Equal forces mean equal speeds.* Total momentum starts at zero, so m_rifle·v_rifle = −m_bullet·v_bullet → v = −(0.010 × 400)/5.0 = −0.8 m/s.
6. **C** — *Misconception: Forgetting momentum is a vector.* Momenta are equal and opposite, so the total is zero. After sticking, (2m)v′ = 0 → v′ = 0. Kinetic energy has been lost.
7. **B** — *Misconception: Dividing by one mass instead of the total.* p before = 1500 × 10 = 15,000. After: (2500)v′ = 15,000 → v′ = 6.0 m/s.
8. **B** — *Misconception: Confusing F–t area with F–x area.* Force × time = impulse (change in momentum). Force × distance would be work.
