# 5. Work & Energy
*Conservation of energy and its transformations*

**Big idea:** Energy is never created or destroyed. It is transferred by work (and heat) and transformed among kinetic, potential, and thermal forms.

## Key concepts to master
- [ ] **Work.** W = Fd cos θ, where θ is the angle between the force and the displacement. Unit: joule (J = N·m). Force perpendicular to motion does no work.
- [ ] **Kinetic energy.** KE = ½mv². Depends on the square of speed.
- [ ] **Gravitational potential energy.** PE_g = mgh, relative to a chosen reference height.
- [ ] **Elastic potential energy.** PE_s = ½kx² for a spring with constant k stretched or compressed by x.
- [ ] **Work–energy theorem.** W_net = ΔKE = ½mv_f² − ½mv_i².
- [ ] **Conservation of energy.** In an isolated system, total energy is constant: KE + PE + thermal = constant. With friction, mechanical energy decreases and thermal energy increases by the same amount.
- [ ] **Power.** P = W/t = Fv. Unit: watt (W = J/s). Same work in less time means more power.
- [ ] **Efficiency.** efficiency = useful energy out / total energy in. Always less than 100% in real devices.

## Practice problems
Try each problem before opening the answer.

1. A person pushes a crate 5.0 m with a 20 N horizontal force. Then a different person pulls it 5.0 m with a 20 N force at 60° above the horizontal. Find the work in each case.
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   Push: W = 20 × 5.0 = 100 J. Pull: W = 20 × 5.0 × cos 60° = 50 J.

   </details>

2. Find the kinetic energy of a 1000 kg car at 25 m/s. What happens to the KE if it speeds up to 50 m/s?
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   KE = ½(1000)(25)² = 3.1 × 10⁵ J (312,500 J). At 50 m/s it is 4× larger, 1.25 × 10⁶ J.

   </details>

3. A roller-coaster car starts from rest at the top of a 30 m hill. Ignoring friction, what is its speed at the bottom?
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   v = √(2gh) = √(2 × 9.8 × 30) ≈ 24 m/s.

   </details>

4. A 50 kg student climbs a 3.0 m staircase in 5.0 s. Find the work done against gravity and the average power.
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   W = mgh = 50 × 9.8 × 3.0 = 1.5 × 10³ J (1470 J). P = 1470/5.0 ≈ 290 W.

   </details>

5. A spring (k = 200 N/m) is compressed 0.10 m and used to launch a 0.050 kg block along a frictionless surface. Find the stored energy and launch speed.
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   PE = ½(200)(0.10)² = 1.0 J. v = √(2 × 1.0/0.050) ≈ 6.3 m/s.

   </details>

6. A 1000 kg car traveling 15 m/s brakes to a stop with a constant friction force of 7500 N. Find the stopping distance.
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   W = −fd = −ΔKE → d = ½(1000)(15²)/7500 = 15 m.

   </details>

## Real-world applications
- Renewable energy: hydroelectric dams convert gravitational PE to electricity; wind turbines convert KE.
- Transportation: regenerative braking in hybrid and electric vehicles recovers KE; speed limits relate to KE ∝ v².
- Amusement parks: roller coaster hill heights are set by energy conservation.
- Human body and food: Calories measure chemical energy; power output explains why sprinting can't be sustained.
