# 3. Newton's Laws
*Forces cause changes in motion*

**Big idea:** A net force on an object causes it to accelerate in proportion to the force and inversely to its mass; forces always come in equal-and-opposite pairs between two objects.

**Quick links:** [Cornell notes](cornell-notes.md) · [Quiz + answer key](quiz.md) · [Interactive quiz](../../site/index.html#newton)

## Key concepts to master
- [ ] **First law (inertia).** An object keeps its velocity (including rest) unless a nonzero net force acts. Mass measures inertia.
- [ ] **Second law.** a = F_net / m, or F_net = ma. Acceleration points in the direction of the net force.
- [ ] **Third law.** If A pushes on B with force F, B pushes on A with force −F. The pair acts on different objects, so they never cancel each other.
- [ ] **Mass vs. weight.** Mass (kg) is the amount of matter and does not change. Weight W = mg (N) is the gravitational force and depends on location.
- [ ] **Free-body diagrams.** Draw one object as a dot and every force acting ON it: weight, normal, friction, tension, applied push/pull.
- [ ] **Normal force.** Contact force perpendicular to a surface. It is not always equal to mg (elevators, inclines).
- [ ] **Friction.** Static friction matches the applied force up to f_s,max = μₛN. Kinetic friction f_k = μ_kN. Friction depends on N and the surfaces, not contact area.
- [ ] **Inclined planes.** Resolve weight into components: parallel to the incline mg sin θ and perpendicular mg cos θ.

## Practice problems
Try each problem before opening the answer.

1. A 1200 kg car experiences a net force of 3000 N. Find its acceleration.
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   a = F/m = 3000/1200 = 2.5 m/s².

   </details>

2. A 5.0 kg box is pushed with 20 N while friction exerts 8.0 N opposing the motion. Find the acceleration.
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   F_net = 20 − 8.0 = 12 N; a = 12/5.0 = 2.4 m/s².

   </details>

3. A 60 kg person stands in an elevator. Find the normal force (a) at rest and (b) while accelerating upward at 2.0 m/s².
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   (a) N = mg = 588 N. (b) N = m(g + a) = 60(11.8) = 708 N.

   </details>

4. A frictionless incline is 30° above the horizontal. Find the acceleration of a block released from rest on it.
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   a = g sin 30° = 4.9 m/s² down the slope.

   </details>

5. Two blocks (2.0 kg and 3.0 kg) touch on a frictionless table. A 10 N force pushes the 2.0 kg block into the 3.0 kg block. Find the acceleration and the contact force between the blocks.
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   a = 10/5.0 = 2.0 m/s². Contact force on the 3.0 kg block = (3.0)(2.0) = 6.0 N.

   </details>

6. A 10 kg sled is pulled at constant velocity by a 25 N horizontal rope. Find the friction force and μ_k.
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   Constant velocity ⇒ f = 25 N. N = mg = 98 N so μ_k = 25/98 ≈ 0.26.

   </details>

## Real-world applications
- Seat belts and airbags: inertia explains why passengers keep moving when a car stops suddenly.
- Rocket and jet propulsion: expelling gas backward pushes the rocket forward (third law).
- Elevator and roller-coaster design: apparent weight and rider comfort limits (g-forces).
- Tire tread and braking: static friction at the road determines stopping and cornering ability.
