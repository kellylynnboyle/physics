# Newton's Laws: Most-Missed Questions
Take the [interactive version](../../site/index.html#newton) for instant feedback. The "Misconception" line names the trap each question targets.

### 1. A hockey puck slides at constant velocity across frictionless ice. What is the net force on it?
- A. Forward, equal to its speed
- B. Zero
- C. Equal to its weight
- D. A small forward force to keep it going

### 2. A heavy truck collides head-on with a small car. During the collision, which exerts the larger force?
- A. The truck on the car
- B. The car on the truck
- C. They exert equal-magnitude forces on each other
- D. It depends on who was moving faster

### 3. A book rests on a table. What is the third-law partner of the Earth's gravitational pull on the book?
- A. The table's upward normal force on the book
- B. The book's gravitational pull on the Earth
- C. The book's push on the table
- D. Friction

### 4. An astronaut travels from Earth to the Moon. Which statement is correct?
- A. Her mass decreases
- B. Her weight decreases but her mass is unchanged
- C. Both mass and weight stay the same
- D. Her weight is unchanged but her mass decreases

### 5. A constant net force on a 2 kg cart gives it an acceleration of 3 m/s². What is the acceleration if the same net force acts on a 6 kg cart?
- A. 9 m/s²
- B. 3 m/s²
- C. 1 m/s²
- D. 0.5 m/s²

### 6. You stand on a scale in an elevator that is accelerating upward. The scale reading is…
- A. less than your weight
- B. equal to your weight
- C. greater than your weight
- D. zero

### 7. A 20 kg crate sits on a floor and you push it horizontally with 30 N, but it does not move. How large is the friction force?
- A. 0 N
- B. 30 N
- C. 196 N
- D. Cannot be determined

### 8. A block is pulled across a table at constant speed. If it is turned onto its smaller face (same weight, same surfaces), the kinetic friction force will…
- A. increase
- B. decrease
- C. stay the same
- D. become zero

---

## Answer key
1. **B** — *Misconception: Motion requires a force.* Constant velocity means zero acceleration, so the net force is zero (first law). No force is needed to keep an object moving.
2. **C** — *Misconception: Bigger or faster objects push harder in a collision.* Newton's third law: the forces are equal in magnitude and opposite in direction. The car has the larger acceleration because it has less mass (a = F/m).
3. **B** — *Misconception: Weight and normal force are a third-law pair.* A third-law pair is the same type of force acting on two different objects. Earth pulls book ⇔ book pulls Earth. The normal force balances weight, but it is a different interaction.
4. **B** — *Misconception: Mass and weight are the same thing.* Mass is the amount of matter and is constant. Weight = mg, and g on the Moon is about one-sixth of Earth's, so her weight is smaller.
5. **C** — *Misconception: Inverse relationship confusion.* F = (2)(3) = 6 N. With 6 kg: a = 6/6 = 1 m/s². Tripling the mass divides the acceleration by 3.
6. **C** — *Misconception: Normal force always equals mg.* Newton's second law: N − mg = ma with a upward, so N = m(g + a) > mg. The scale reads the normal force.
7. **B** — *Misconception: Friction always equals μN.* The crate is in equilibrium, so static friction exactly balances the push: 30 N. The μₛN value is only the maximum static friction.
8. **C** — *Misconception: Friction depends on contact area.* f_k = μ_kN depends on the surface properties and normal force, not area. N is unchanged, so friction is the same.
