# Topic 2: Projectile Motion

Projectile motion extends 1D kinematics to two dimensions by treating horizontal and vertical motion as two independent problems linked only by a shared time variable.

## Key concepts to master

- **Independence of horizontal and vertical motion** — the single biggest idea in this unit
- **Velocity components** — vₓ = v·cosθ, v_y = v·sinθ, for a launch angle θ above horizontal
- **Horizontal motion** — constant velocity (aₓ = 0, no air resistance), so x = vₓt
- **Vertical motion** — constant acceleration a_y = −g, so it's identical to 1D free-fall kinematics
- **Time of flight** — determined entirely by the vertical motion
- **Range** — R = (v² sin(2θ))/g for level launch and landing; maximum range at θ = 45°
- **Maximum height** — occurs when v_y = 0
- **Symmetric trajectory** — for level launch/landing, time up = time down, and impact speed = launch speed

## Learning objectives

By the end of this topic, students can:
1. Decompose an initial velocity vector into horizontal and vertical components
2. Explain why horizontal velocity stays constant while vertical velocity changes
3. Solve for time of flight, maximum height, and range for horizontal launches (launched parallel to the ground from a height)
4. Solve for time of flight, maximum height, and range for angled launches (launched at an angle from the ground)
5. Explain why 45° maximizes range for a given launch speed

## Practice problems

1. A ball rolls off a table 1.2 m high with a horizontal velocity of 3.0 m/s. How long is it in the air, and how far from the table does it land?
2. A football is kicked at 20 m/s at an angle of 37° above horizontal. Find the time of flight, maximum height, and range.
3. An arrow is shot horizontally from a height of 1.8 m at 45 m/s. How far does it travel before hitting the ground?
4. A projectile is launched at 30 m/s. At what two angles will it achieve the same range of 70 m? (Hint: complementary angles.)
5. A cannonball is fired at 50 m/s at 60° above horizontal from a cliff 20 m high. Find the total time of flight and the horizontal range. (This one mixes a height offset with an angled launch — set up vertical motion carefully.)

## Real-world applications

- **Sports** — basketball free throws, football punts, golf drives, and long jump all use launch-angle optimization
- **Ballistics and artillery** — historically, one of the first serious applications of projectile motion equations
- **Water fountains and sprinklers** — angle and speed determine spray pattern
- **Search-and-rescue airdrops** — calculating where to release supplies from a moving aircraft so they land on target (a horizontal-launch problem)
