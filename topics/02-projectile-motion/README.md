# Unit 2: Projectile Motion

**Standards:** HS-PS2-1 (use mathematical representations to support the claim that F_net = ma); builds on Kinematics.
**Science & Engineering Practices:** Using mathematics and computational thinking; developing and using models.
**Suggested pacing:** 1.5 to 2 weeks

## Learning objectives
Students will be able to…
- [ ] **P1.** Explain that horizontal and vertical motions are independent and why a projectile's horizontal velocity is constant.
- [ ] **P2.** Resolve an initial velocity into horizontal and vertical components using trigonometry.
- [ ] **P3.** Solve horizontally launched projectile problems (time from vertical motion, distance from horizontal motion).
- [ ] **P4.** Solve angled-launch problems for time of flight, maximum height, and range.
- [ ] **P5.** Predict how launch angle and speed change range, and identify the angle for maximum range on level ground.
- [ ] **P6.** Design and test a model (launcher or video analysis) that predicts a landing point, and evaluate its accuracy.

## Key concepts and equations
| Concept | Equation / idea |
|---|---|
| Components | vₓ = v cosθ, vᵧ = v sinθ |
| Horizontal motion | a = 0, x = vₓt |
| Vertical motion | a = −g, y = y₀ + vᵧt − ½gt², vᵧ = vᵧ₀ − gt |
| Time of flight (level) | t = 2vᵧ₀/g |
| Max height | H = vᵧ₀²/(2g) |
| Range (level) | R = v² sin(2θ)/g |
| At the peak | vᵧ = 0, vₓ unchanged, a = g down |
| Complementary angles | θ and 90° − θ give the same range |

## Most-missed ideas
1. A dropped and a horizontally launched object hit the ground at the same time.
2. Horizontal velocity stays constant (no horizontal force).
3. Acceleration at the top is still 9.8 m/s² downward, not zero.
4. Maximum range is at 45°, not 90°.
5. Sine and cosine get swapped when finding components.

## Lab ideas
- **Marble-ramp launch lab:** Roll a marble off a table, predict the landing point from table height and measured launch speed, then place a cup to catch it.
- **Water-stream or ball toss video:** Film a toss, use Tracker to plot x(t) and y(t), and show that x is linear and y is parabolic.
- **Angle-vs-range lab:** Use a launcher or the PhET sim; graph range against angle and find the best angle.

## Real-world applications
- Sports: free throws, field goals, long jump, golf, soccer free kicks.
- Emergency response: water from a fire hose, supply drops.
- Engineering: fountains, sprinkler design, and trajectory planning for spacecraft (as a first approximation).
- Safety and forensics: estimating speed from a vehicle's landing distance.

## Practice problems
1. A ball is kicked horizontally at 12 m/s from a 20 m roof. Find the time in the air, the horizontal distance, and the speed on impact.
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>t = √(2·20/9.8) ≈ 2.02 s; x ≈ 24.2 m; vᵧ ≈ 19.8 m/s, so v = √(12² + 19.8²) ≈ 23.2 m/s.</details>
2. A soccer ball is kicked at 25 m/s at 40° above level ground. Find the time of flight, range, and maximum height.
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>vᵧ ≈ 16.1 m/s; t ≈ 3.28 s; R = 25² sin80°/9.8 ≈ 62.8 m; H ≈ 13.2 m.</details>
3. A football is thrown at 20 m/s at 35°. What is the range?
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>R = 400 sin70°/9.8 ≈ 38.4 m.</details>
4. At 30 m/s, what launch angle (below 45°) gives a 50 m range on level ground?
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>sin2θ = 50(9.8)/900 ≈ 0.544, so 2θ ≈ 33.0° and θ ≈ 16.5° (73.5° also works).</details>
5. A long jumper leaves the ground at 9 m/s at 20°. How long is the jump?
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>R = 81 sin40°/9.8 ≈ 5.3 m.</details>

## Assessment criteria
| Level | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **Beginning (1)** | Describes a parabola but cannot separate x and y motion. |
| **Developing (2)** | Finds components; solves horizontal launch with prompting; mixes up when to use which equation. |
| **Proficient (3)** | Independently solves horizontal and angled problems with a labeled diagram, units, and a table of x and y quantities. Quiz score ≥ 80%. |
| **Advanced (4)** | Solves non-level launches (different start/end heights), evaluates a model against data, and explains discrepancies (air resistance, measurement). |
