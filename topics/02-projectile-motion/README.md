# Unit 2 — Projectile Motion

**Standards:** foundation for HS-PS2-1 · **Time:** ~2 weeks · **Notes:** [cornell-notes.md](cornell-notes.md) · **Quiz:** `../../quizzes/index.html` (Projectile Motion)

## Learning objectives

| Code | Objective | Level-3 evidence |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | Explain independence of horizontal and vertical motion | Predicts that a dropped and a horizontally launched object land together |
| P2 | Resolve a velocity into components | Computes vₓ = v cosθ, v_y = v sinθ |
| P3 | Solve horizontal-launch problems | Finds fall time from height, then range |
| P4 | Solve angled-launch problems | Finds time of flight, max height, and range |
| P5 | Describe velocity and acceleration along the path | States vₓ constant, v_y = 0 at the top, a = 9.8 m/s² down everywhere |

## Key concepts checklist
- [ ] Projectile = only gravity acts (no air resistance, no propulsion after launch)
- [ ] Two independent 1-D motions: horizontal (constant velocity) and vertical (constant acceleration −g)
- [ ] Vector components via trigonometry (SOH-CAH-TOA)
- [ ] Time links the two directions — solve vertical first, then use t for horizontal
- [ ] Horizontal launch: v₀y = 0, t = √(2h/g), R = vₓt
- [ ] Angled launch (level ground): t = 2v₀sinθ/g · H = (v₀sinθ)²/(2g) · R = v₀² sin2θ / g
- [ ] Complementary angles give equal range; 45° maximizes range on level ground
- [ ] Symmetry: speed at landing = launch speed (same height)

## Most-missed ideas
1. Thinking a horizontally fired object falls later than a dropped one (same fall time!).
2. Thinking there is a horizontal force/acceleration keeping the projectile moving.
3. Thinking velocity or acceleration is zero at the top (only v_y = 0; a = g).
4. Using the full speed in the vertical equation instead of the component.
5. Mixing calculator degree/radian mode.

## Practice problems
1. A ball rolls off a 19.6 m-high cliff at 5 m/s. Time in air? Horizontal distance? [2.0 s; 10 m]
2. A soccer ball is kicked at 20 m/s at 30° above horizontal. Find vₓ, v_y, time of flight, max height, range. [17.3 m/s; 10 m/s; 2.04 s; 5.1 m; 35.3 m]
3. A stunt car leaves a 2.0 m-high ramp horizontally at 25 m/s. How far away does it land? [≈ 16 m]
4. At what angle(s) does a projectile launched at 25 m/s land 40 m away? (use R = v² sin2θ/g) [≈ 19.4° or 70.6°]
5. A quarterback throws at 20 m/s, 40°. How long is the ball in the air? [≈ 2.6 s]
6. *Conceptual:* Draw velocity vectors (components) at 5 points along a parabolic path.
7. *Challenge:* A monkey hangs from a branch and lets go when the dart is fired directly at it. Does the dart hit? Why?

## Lab ideas
- **Hit-the-target:** roll a ball off a table, measure v from timing/ramp, predict landing spot, place a cup, test.
- **Launcher angle vs range:** collect range at 15°–75°, graph, find the max, compare with theory.
- **Video analysis:** Track a basketball shot; separate x(t) and y(t); confirm vₓ constant.

## Real-world applications
- Sports: basketball arc, long jump, golf, archery, javelin.
- Emergency & safety: fire-hose streams, water-bomber drops, landing zones for supply drops.
- Engineering: fountains, irrigation, sprinkler design, ski-jump profiles.
- Military/space history context: ballistic trajectories (discuss responsibly), re-entry capsules.

## Assessment criteria
- **Quiz:** ≥ 80% on the Projectile set.
- **Unit test:** at least one horizontal-launch and one angled-launch problem with a component table (x | y).
- **Lab:** predicted vs measured range within 10% earns Level 3; percent-error analysis required.
- **Mastery check:** explain in a CER why two balls (dropped vs fired horizontally) land together.
