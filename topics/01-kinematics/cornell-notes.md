**Essential question:** How can we describe and predict motion using words, numbers, and graphs?

| Cue / Question (write after class) | Notes (write during class) |
|---|---|
| What is the difference between distance and displacement? | **Distance:** total path length (scalar, always ≥ 0). **Displacement:** straight-line change in position, Δx = x_f − x_i (vector, has direction). A full lap has distance 400 m but displacement 0. |
| Speed vs. velocity? | **Speed** = distance/time (scalar). **Velocity** = displacement/time (vector). Average velocity v̄ = Δx/Δt. |
| What is acceleration? | Rate of change of velocity: a = Δv/Δt, in m/s². It can mean speeding up, slowing down, or turning. |
| How do I tell speeding up from slowing down? | Same sign for v and a → speeding up. Opposite signs → slowing down. The sign alone doesn't tell you. |
| What do x–t graph features mean? | Slope = velocity. Straight line = constant v. Curving up = accelerating. Flat = at rest. |
| What do v–t graph features mean? | Slope = acceleration. Area between line and axis = displacement. Above axis = moving positive direction. |
| Which equations for constant acceleration? | v = v₀ + at;  x = x₀ + v₀t + ½at²;  v² = v₀² + 2aΔx. Pick the one that omits the quantity you don't have. |
| How does free fall work? | Only gravity acts. a = 9.8 m/s² downward for every object, regardless of mass (no air resistance). |
| Problem-solving steps? | 1) Sketch and choose a + direction. 2) List knowns/unknowns with units. 3) Pick an equation. 4) Solve algebraically first. 5) Plug in, check units and reasonableness. |
| Why does doubling speed quadruple stopping distance? | d = v₀²/(2a): distance depends on v². |

**Worked example.** A car brakes from 20 m/s at −5 m/s². Stopping distance? v² = v₀² + 2ad → 0 = 400 + 2(−5)d → d = 40 m. Time: t = (0 − 20)/(−5) = 4 s.
