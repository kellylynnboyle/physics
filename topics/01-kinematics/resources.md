## Practice Problems

1. A cyclist accelerates from 3 m/s to 11 m/s in 4 seconds. Find the acceleration and the distance covered.
2. A stone is dropped from a 45 m cliff. How long does it take to hit the ground, and what is its impact velocity?
3. Sketch the x-t, v-t, and a-t graphs for a car that speeds up, then travels at constant velocity, then brakes to a stop.
4. A train decelerates from 30 m/s to rest over 200 m. Find its acceleration.
5. Two cars start 500 m apart and drive toward each other, one at 20 m/s and one at 30 m/s. When and where do they meet?
6. Given only a v-t graph (triangle shape rising then falling to zero), calculate total displacement using the area of the shapes.

*(Answer keys can be generated by your instructor or checked using the kinematic equations in the Cornell notes.)*

## Real-World Applications

- **Traffic safety:** Stopping-distance calculations (reaction distance + braking distance) used in driver's ed and road design
- **Sports analytics:** Sprinter acceleration curves (100 m dash split times)
- **Aerospace:** Runway length requirements based on required takeoff acceleration
- **Elevators & roller coasters:** Comfort limits are set by acceleration, not speed
- **Traffic/red-light cameras:** Use average velocity over a measured distance to estimate speed
