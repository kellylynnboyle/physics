# Cornell Notes — Kinematics

**Topic:** Kinematics (1D motion) | **Unit:** 1 | **Course:** 11th Grade Physics

---

## Notes

| Cue / Question | Notes |
|---|---|
| What is kinematics? | The description of motion (position, velocity, acceleration, time) **without** asking what causes it. Causes of motion are dynamics (Newton's Laws — Unit 3). |
| Distance vs. displacement | **Distance** = total path length traveled (scalar, always positive). **Displacement** (Δx) = straight-line change in position, `x_f − x_i` (vector, can be negative). Distance ≥ \|displacement\|. |
| Speed vs. velocity | **Speed** = distance / time (scalar). **Velocity** = displacement / time (vector — has direction). Average velocity `v_avg = Δx / Δt`. |
| Instantaneous vs. average velocity | Average velocity is over an interval; instantaneous velocity is the value at one instant — the slope of the tangent line on an x-t graph at that point. |
| Acceleration | Rate of change of velocity: `a = Δv / Δt`. Vector. Units: m/s². Nonzero acceleration means velocity is changing in magnitude, direction, or both. |
| Sign conventions | Positive/negative signs indicate **direction**, not "fast" or "slow." An object can have negative velocity and positive acceleration — this means it's slowing down while moving in the negative direction. |
| Speeding up vs. slowing down | If velocity and acceleration have the **same sign**, the object speeds up. If they have **opposite signs**, the object slows down. This is true regardless of which direction is "positive." |
| Motion graphs — position vs. time (x-t) | Slope = velocity. Steeper slope = faster. Flat line = at rest. Curved line = accelerating. |
| Motion graphs — velocity vs. time (v-t) | Slope = acceleration. Area under the curve = displacement. A v-t graph is **not** a picture of the path taken. |
| Motion graphs — acceleration vs. time (a-t) | Area under the curve = change in velocity (Δv). |
| The Big Four kinematic equations (constant acceleration only) | 1) `v = v₀ + at` 2) `Δx = v₀t + ½at²` 3) `v² = v₀² + 2aΔx` 4) `Δx = ½(v₀ + v)t` |
| Choosing an equation | List your knowns and unknowns first. Pick the equation missing the variable you don't have/need. |
| Free fall | Special case of constant acceleration where `a = -g = -9.8 m/s²` (taking up as positive). Applies to any object in the air with negligible air resistance, regardless of mass. |
| At the top of a vertical throw | Velocity = 0, but **acceleration is still -9.8 m/s²** — it never becomes zero during free fall. |
| Units & significant figures | Always convert to base SI units (m, s, m/s, m/s²) before plugging into equations. Report answers with the correct number of sig figs. |

---

## Summary

Kinematics describes *how* something moves using position, velocity, and acceleration — all of which are vectors except speed and distance. The four constant-acceleration equations let you solve for any unknown as long as acceleration is constant, which includes free fall (a = -9.8 m/s²). Graph slope and area are the two most powerful tools: slope of x-t gives velocity, slope of v-t gives acceleration, and area under v-t gives displacement. The single most common source of errors is sign confusion — always define a positive direction first and stick to it.
