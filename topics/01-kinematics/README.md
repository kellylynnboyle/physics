# Unit 1 — Kinematics

**Standards:** foundation for HS-PS2-1 · **Time:** ~4 weeks · **Notes:** [cornell-notes.md](cornell-notes.md) · **Quiz:** `../../quizzes/index.html` (Kinematics)

## Learning objectives (students will be able to…)

| Code | Objective | Level-3 evidence |
|---|---|---|
| K1 | Distinguish distance vs displacement and speed vs velocity | Computes both for a round trip; explains why velocity can be 0 while speed is not |
| K2 | Interpret x–t and v–t graphs | Finds velocity from x–t slope; acceleration from v–t slope; displacement from v–t area |
| K3 | Define and calculate acceleration, including sign | Predicts speeding up vs slowing down from signs of v and a |
| K4 | Use the constant-acceleration equations | Chooses the right equation from the known/unknown list |
| K5 | Model free fall | Computes time, speed, and drop distance with g = 9.8 m/s² |

## Key concepts checklist
- [ ] Reference frame, origin, and positive direction
- [ ] Scalar vs vector: distance, speed vs displacement, velocity, acceleration
- [ ] Average velocity = Δx / Δt; instantaneous velocity = slope of tangent
- [ ] Acceleration = Δv / Δt (units m/s²); direction matters
- [ ] Graph reading: slope and area on x–t, v–t, a–t
- [ ] Equations (constant a): v = v₀ + at · Δx = v₀t + ½at² · v² = v₀² + 2aΔx · Δx = ½(v₀+v)t
- [ ] Free fall: a = −g = −9.8 m/s² (down), regardless of mass; at the top v = 0 but a ≠ 0
- [ ] Motion maps / strobe diagrams

## Most-missed ideas (reteach these)
1. Using d = vt when velocity is **changing** (use average velocity or ½at²).
2. Velocity and acceleration are not the same thing — zero velocity does not mean zero acceleration.
3. Sign of acceleration ≠ "speeding up". Same sign as velocity → speeding up; opposite → slowing down.
4. Area under a v–t graph is displacement (not slope).
5. Distance vs displacement over a round trip.

## Practice problems (answers in brackets)
1. A runner completes one 400 m lap in 80 s. Find average speed and average velocity. [5 m/s; 0]
2. A car speeds up from 5 m/s to 25 m/s in 8 s. Find a and the distance covered. [2.5 m/s²; 120 m]
3. A driver brakes from 20 m/s with a = −5 m/s². How far to stop? How long? [40 m; 4 s]
4. A rock is dropped from rest from a cliff and hits the water in 3.0 s. Height? Impact speed? [44.1 m; 29.4 m/s]
5. A ball is thrown straight up at 14.7 m/s. Time to the top? Max height? Time in air? [1.5 s; 11.0 m; 3.0 s]
6. Sketch v–t and a–t graphs for a car that accelerates, cruises, then brakes to rest.
7. From the v–t graph given by your teacher, find displacement using area (trapezoids).
8. *Challenge:* Two cars start 100 m apart moving toward each other at 15 m/s and 10 m/s. When and where do they meet? [4 s; 60 m from the faster car's start]

## Lab ideas
- **Constant-velocity buggy:** collect x–t data, fit a line, interpret slope. Predict where it will be at t = 10 s, then test.
- **Ramp acceleration:** cart down an incline (motion sensor or phone video + Tracker); show x ∝ t² and v–t is linear.
- **Reaction-time ruler drop:** d = ½gt² to compute reaction time; compare class data.

## Real-world applications
- Traffic safety: stopping distance vs speed (distance grows with v²); yellow-light timing.
- Sports analytics: sprint acceleration, 0–60 mph car tests, swim-start analysis.
- Aviation/space: takeoff roll length, rocket ascent graphs.
- Phones/wearables: accelerometer data and step counting.
