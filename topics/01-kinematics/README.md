# Topic 1: Kinematics

> The study of motion — position, velocity, and acceleration — without regard to what causes it.

**Files in this module:** [`cornell-notes.md`](./cornell-notes.md) · [`quiz.html`](./quiz.html) (open in any browser)

## Key Concepts to Master

- **Position, distance, and displacement** — distance is the total path length (scalar); displacement is the straight-line change in position, including direction (vector).
- **Speed vs. velocity** — speed is how fast (scalar); velocity is speed *with direction* (vector). Average velocity = displacement / time; average speed = distance / time.
- **Acceleration** — the rate of change of velocity (vector). Constant-magnitude acceleration does not mean constant speed.
- **Uniformly accelerated motion (UAM)** — motion where acceleration is constant; this is the regime the four kinematic equations describe.
- **Free fall** — motion under gravity alone (a = -9.8 m/s², or -g), independent of mass when air resistance is ignored.
- **Graphical analysis** — reading and constructing position-time, velocity-time, and acceleration-time graphs:
  - slope of an x–t graph = velocity
  - slope of a v–t graph = acceleration
  - area under a v–t graph = displacement
  - area under an a–t graph = change in velocity
- **Sign conventions** — choosing one positive direction and applying it consistently to position, velocity, and acceleration.
- **Vectors vs. scalars** — distance/speed are scalars; displacement/velocity/acceleration are vectors and require direction.

## Key Equations (constant acceleration only)

| Equation | Solves for | Missing variable |
|---|---|---|
| `v = v₀ + at` | final velocity | displacement |
| `x = x₀ + v₀t + ½at²` | position | final velocity |
| `v² = v₀² + 2a(x − x₀)` | final velocity | time |
| `x = x₀ + ½(v₀ + v)t` | position | acceleration |

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this module, students will be able to:

1. Distinguish distance from displacement and speed from velocity in written and graphical problems.
2. Select and correctly apply the appropriate kinematic equation given three known variables.
3. Interpret and sketch position-time, velocity-time, and acceleration-time graphs for the same motion, and convert between them.
4. Solve one-dimensional free-fall problems, including objects thrown upward.
5. Justify a sign convention and apply it consistently through a multi-step problem.
6. Calculate average velocity/speed and instantaneous velocity, and explain the difference.

## Most Often Missed Ideas (misconceptions to target)

1. **"Acceleration is zero at the top of a toss."** For a ball thrown straight up, velocity is zero at the peak — acceleration is still -g the entire flight.
2. **"Negative acceleration always means slowing down."** It means slowing down only if the object moves in the positive direction; if moving in the negative direction, negative acceleration means *speeding up*.
3. **"Heavier objects fall faster."** In free fall (no air resistance), all objects accelerate at the same rate, g.
4. **Confusing slope and area on graphs** — e.g., reading the slope of a v–t graph as displacement instead of acceleration.
5. **Assuming distance = displacement** whenever the path isn't a straight line to the final point (e.g., there-and-back trips).
6. **Forgetting "starts from rest" means v₀ = 0**, or conversely assuming v₀ = 0 when it isn't stated.
7. **Mixing up which kinematic equation to use** because the "missing variable" wasn't identified first.

## Real-World Applications

- Reaction time and stopping distance in vehicle safety (why speed limits and following distances matter).
- Sprint timing and analysis in track and field.
- Elevator and roller-coaster acceleration/deceleration comfort limits.
- Airbag and seatbelt design (change in velocity over a controlled time/distance).
- Rocket launch trajectories during the vertical-ascent phase.

## Practice Problems

1. A car accelerates uniformly from rest to 26.8 m/s (60 mph) in 6.0 s. Find its acceleration and the distance traveled.
2. A ball is thrown straight up at 15 m/s. Find its maximum height, the time to reach it, and its velocity when it returns to the throower's hand.
3. A train decelerates from 40 m/s to rest over 500 m. Find its acceleration and the time taken.
4. Sketch the v–t and a–t graphs for a skydiver who free-falls for 10 s, then opens a parachute and decelerates to constant terminal velocity over the next 5 s.
5. Two runners start 100 m apart, running toward each other at 4 m/s and 6 m/s. How long until they meet, and where?
