# Topic 1: Kinematics

Kinematics is the description of motion — position, velocity, and acceleration — without worrying about what *causes* it (that's Newton's Laws, Topic 3). Everything else in this project builds on getting kinematics solid first.

## Key concepts to master

- **Distance vs. displacement** — distance is a scalar (total path length); displacement is a vector (straight-line change in position, can be negative)
- **Speed vs. velocity** — same scalar/vector distinction, applied to rate of change of position
- **Acceleration** — rate of change of *velocity*, not speed; can point opposite to velocity (deceleration) or perpendicular to it (later, in projectile motion)
- **Motion graphs**
  - Position-time (x-t): slope = velocity
  - Velocity-time (v-t): slope = acceleration; area under curve = displacement
  - Acceleration-time (a-t): area under curve = change in velocity
- **The four kinematic equations** (constant acceleration only):
  1. v = v₀ + at
  2. Δx = v₀t + ½at²
  3. v² = v₀² + 2aΔx
  4. Δx = ½(v₀ + v)t
- **Free fall** — constant acceleration a = −g = −9.8 m/s² (choosing up as positive), same equations apply
- **Sign conventions** — picking a positive direction once and sticking with it for the whole problem

## Learning objectives

By the end of this topic, students can:
1. Calculate displacement, average velocity, and average acceleration from data or a scenario
2. Extract velocity from the slope of an x-t graph and acceleration from the slope of a v-t graph
3. Extract displacement from the area under a v-t graph
4. Select and apply the correct kinematic equation given three known variables
5. Solve free-fall problems for time, velocity, or height

## Practice problems

1. A car accelerates from rest at 3.0 m/s² for 6.0 s. Find its final velocity and the distance traveled.
2. A ball is thrown straight up at 20 m/s. How long until it returns to the thrower's hand? What is its maximum height?
3. A train traveling at 30 m/s brakes at a constant −2.0 m/s². How far does it travel before stopping?
4. From a v-t graph showing velocity going from 0 to 10 m/s in 5 s, then constant at 10 m/s for 5 s, then to 0 over 2 s: find the total displacement and the acceleration in each phase.
5. A rock is dropped from a 45 m cliff. How long does it take to hit the ground, and what is its impact velocity?

## Real-world applications

- **Stopping distance** for cars (reaction time + braking distance) — used in driver's ed and traffic safety design
- **Sprint starts** in track & field — analyzing 100 m dash splits with kinematics
- **Elevators and rockets** — constant-acceleration phases during launch/ascent
- **Skydiving before terminal velocity** — free fall as an idealized (no air resistance) approximation
