# Unit 1: Kinematics

**Standards:** Supports HS-PS2-1 (analyze data to support Newton's 2nd law). Kinematics is the math language for the motion data used in PS2-1.
**Science & Engineering Practices:** Analyzing and interpreting data; using mathematics and computational thinking.
**Suggested pacing:** 2 to 2.5 weeks

## Learning objectives
Students will be able to…
- [ ] **K1.** Distinguish distance/displacement and speed/velocity, and explain why velocity and displacement are vectors.
- [ ] **K2.** Calculate average velocity and acceleration from data, a table, or a graph.
- [ ] **K3.** Construct and interpret position-time and velocity-time graphs (slope = rate, area under v–t = displacement).
- [ ] **K4.** Apply the constant-acceleration equations to solve one-dimensional problems, including free fall.
- [ ] **K5.** Determine from the signs of v and a whether an object is speeding up or slowing down.
- [ ] **K6.** Collect motion data in a lab, graph it, and use the graph to find g with a stated uncertainty.

## Key concepts and equations
| Concept | Equation / idea |
|---|---|
| Displacement | Δx = x_f − x_i (a vector, can be negative) |
| Average velocity | v̄ = Δx / Δt |
| Acceleration | a = Δv / Δt |
| Velocity update | v = v₀ + at |
| Position update | x = x₀ + v₀t + ½at² |
| Time-free relation | v² = v₀² + 2aΔx |
| Free fall | a = −9.8 m/s² (down is negative), same for all masses without air resistance |
| Graph rules | x–t slope = velocity; v–t slope = acceleration; v–t area = displacement |

## Most-missed ideas (misconceptions to confront)
1. Zero velocity means zero acceleration (false at the top of a throw).
2. Negative acceleration always means slowing down (false; compare signs of v and a).
3. Distance and displacement are the same (false for round trips).
4. Stopping distance is proportional to speed (it is proportional to speed squared).
5. Heavier objects fall faster (false without air resistance).

## Lab ideas
- **Buggy or cart motion:** Collect x–t data with a motion sensor or stopwatch plus meter tape; make x–t and v–t graphs.
- **Free-fall g lab:** Drop a ball through photogates, or video it and use Tracker. Compare your g with 9.8 m/s² and discuss sources of error.
- **Walk the graph:** Students walk to match a given x–t graph using a motion sensor.

## Real-world applications
- Traffic safety: stopping distance, yellow-light timing, speed limits near schools.
- Sports analytics: sprint start acceleration, 40-yard dash splits.
- Aviation and rocketry: takeoff run length, launch acceleration.
- Phone sensors and GPS: velocity from position data.

## Practice problems (answers hidden under each)
1. A sprinter goes from 0 to 10 m/s in 2.5 s. Find the acceleration and distance covered.
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>a = 4 m/s²; d = ½(4)(2.5²) = 12.5 m.</details>
2. A train slows uniformly from 30 m/s to 10 m/s over 400 m. Find a and the time.
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>a = (10² − 30²)/(2·400) = −1 m/s²; t = (10 − 30)/(−1) = 20 s.</details>
3. A ball dropped from a bridge hits the water in 3.0 s. How high is the bridge and how fast is the ball going at impact?
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>h = ½(9.8)(3²) = 44.1 m; v = 9.8 × 3 = 29.4 m/s.</details>
4. A ball is thrown straight up at 14.7 m/s. Find the time to the top, the maximum height, and the total time in the air.
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>t_up = 1.5 s; h = 14.7²/(2·9.8) ≈ 11.0 m; total = 3.0 s.</details>
5. A v–t graph rises from 0 to 8 m/s over 4 s, then stays at 8 m/s for 3 s. Find the displacement.
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>½(4)(8) + 8(3) = 16 + 24 = 40 m.</details>

## Assessment criteria
| Level | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **Beginning (1)** | Identifies terms but mixes up distance/displacement; cannot read slope from a graph. |
| **Developing (2)** | Solves single-step problems; graph reading is inconsistent; signs/units often wrong. |
| **Proficient (3)** | Chooses the correct equation, shows units and signs, and interprets x–t and v–t graphs correctly. Quiz score ≥ 80%. |
| **Advanced (4)** | Solves multi-stage problems, converts between graph types, and evaluates lab uncertainty in g. |
