# Cornell Notes: Newton's Laws

**Name:** ____________  **Date:** ________  **Period:** ____
**Essential question:** How do forces determine the motion of an object?

| Cue / Question (write after class) | Notes (write during class) |
|---|---|
| What is a force? | A push or pull, a vector measured in newtons (1 N = 1 kg·m/s²). |
| What does the 1st law say? | An object keeps its velocity (at rest or constant velocity) unless a net force acts. **Inertia** = tendency to resist change in motion; mass measures inertia. |
| What does the 2nd law say? | ΣF = ma. Acceleration is proportional to net force and inversely proportional to mass, in the direction of the net force. |
| What does the 3rd law say? | If A pushes B, B pushes A equally and oppositely. The pair acts on **different objects**, so they never cancel each other. |
| Mass vs. weight? | Mass (kg) is the amount of matter and doesn't change. Weight W = mg (N) depends on location. |
| What is the normal force? | The perpendicular push of a surface. Found from ΣF = ma. In an elevator accelerating up: N = m(g + a). |
| How do I draw a free-body diagram? | 1) Dot for the object. 2) Draw only forces acting **on** it (weight, normal, friction, tension, applied). 3) Label each. 4) Choose axes (tilt for inclines). 5) Never draw "force of motion" or acceleration as a force. |
| Friction rules? | Kinetic: f = μ_k N (opposes sliding). Static: f ≤ μ_s N (adjusts to prevent sliding). |
| Incline? | Weight components: along slope mg sinθ; into slope mg cosθ. Frictionless: a = g sinθ. |
| Law of gravitation? | F = Gm₁m₂/r². Double the distance → ¼ the force. |
| Problem-solving steps? | FBD → axes → ΣFₓ = maₓ and ΣFᵧ = maᵧ → solve → check units/direction. |

**Worked example.** 10 kg block, 30 N push, kinetic friction 20 N. F_net = 30 − 20 = 10 N; a = 10/10 = 1 m/s².

**Sketch area:** Draw FBDs for (a) a book at rest on a table, (b) a crate pushed at constant speed, (c) a block sliding down an incline.
