# Unit 3 — Newton's Laws

**Standards:** HS-PS2-1 · **Time:** ~4 weeks · **Notes:** [cornell-notes.md](cornell-notes.md) · **Quiz:** `../../quizzes/index.html` (Newton's Laws)

## Learning objectives

| Code | Objective | Level-3 evidence |
|---|---|---|
| N1 | State and apply the first law (inertia, equilibrium) | Explains constant velocity ⇒ net force 0, with no "force of motion" |
| N2 | Draw free-body diagrams and find net force | Correct force arrows, labeled by *agent*, with net force direction |
| N3 | Apply F_net = ma, including weight, normal force, friction | Solves elevator, incline, and friction problems |
| N4 | Identify third-law pairs | Names both forces, objects, and shows they act on different objects |
| N5 | Analyze data supporting F_net = ma (HS-PS2-1) | Graphs a vs F (slope = 1/m) or a vs 1/m and writes a CER |

## Key concepts checklist
- [ ] Force as a vector interaction (N); net force = vector sum
- [ ] Inertia: mass is the measure of inertia (kg)
- [ ] First law: net force 0 ⇔ constant velocity (rest included)
- [ ] Second law: a = F_net / m; direction of a = direction of F_net
- [ ] Third law: forces come in equal and opposite pairs acting on *different* objects
- [ ] Weight W = mg (a force) vs mass (a property); apparent weight in an accelerating elevator
- [ ] Normal force (perpendicular to surface; not always equal to mg)
- [ ] Friction: static (≤ μₛN) and kinetic (f = μₖN); friction opposes relative motion
- [ ] Inclines: components mg sinθ (along) and mg cosθ (perpendicular)
- [ ] Tension in ropes/pulleys; Atwood machine
- [ ] Air resistance and terminal velocity (net force 0 → constant velocity)

## Most-missed ideas
1. "A force is needed to keep an object moving" — wrong; force changes velocity.
2. Third-law pairs: normal force and weight are **not** a pair (different interactions).
3. Equal and opposite forces **don't** cancel across two objects.
4. Weight vs mass; apparent weight in elevators.
5. Net force with several forces: draw and add, don't just use the biggest force.
6. Friction direction and using μN when the object is not moving (static).

## Practice problems
1. A 1200 kg car has a net force of 3000 N. Find a. [2.5 m/s²]
2. A 2 kg block is pushed with 10 N right and 4 N left (frictionless). Find a. [3 m/s² right]
3. Find the mass, and weight on Earth and on the Moon (g = 1.6 m/s²), of a 60 kg student. [60 kg; 588 N; 96 N]
4. A 70 kg person stands in an elevator accelerating up at 2.0 m/s². Find the normal force. [826 N]
5. A 20 kg crate is pushed at constant speed on a floor with μₖ = 0.30. Find the push. [58.8 N]
6. A 5 kg box on a frictionless 30° ramp: find acceleration along the ramp. [4.9 m/s²]
7. Atwood machine: 3 kg and 2 kg masses over a pulley. Find a and the tension. [1.96 m/s²; 23.5 N]
8. Draw FBDs for: book on a table; a skydiver at terminal velocity; a hanging lamp.
9. *Challenge:* A horse pulls a cart. By the third law the cart pulls back equally, so why does it move? (Identify forces on each object.)

## Lab ideas
- **F vs a (HS-PS2-1 data lab):** cart pulled by hanging mass; vary F with m_total constant; graph a vs F; slope = 1/m.
- **a vs m:** constant force, vary mass; graph a vs 1/m.
- **Friction coefficient:** measure μₛ and μₖ with a spring scale or incline-angle method.
- **Elevator in a phone:** use phone accelerometer in an elevator; relate to apparent weight.

## Real-world applications
- Seat belts, headrests, and inertia in car crashes.
- Rocket propulsion (third law), jet engines, swimming, walking.
- Elevators, roller coasters, g-forces on pilots.
- Tire/road friction, ABS brakes, stopping on wet roads.
- Skydiving and parachutes (terminal velocity).

## Assessment criteria
- **Quiz:** ≥ 80% on the Newton's Laws set.
- **FBD check:** every force arrow labeled "force by X on Y"; at least 90% correct on 5 diagrams.
- **Lab/CER:** data table + graph with slope interpretation; CER tied to F_net = ma; percent error on mass from slope.
- **Unit test:** includes an elevator, an incline with friction, and a third-law pair identification.
