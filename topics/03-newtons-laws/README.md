# Topic 3: Newton's Laws of Motion

Newton's Laws explain *why* objects move the way kinematics describes. This is usually the topic where free-body diagrams become essential — get comfortable drawing them here, because every later topic depends on it.

## Key concepts to master

- **Newton's 1st Law (Inertia)** — an object at rest stays at rest, and an object in motion stays in motion at constant velocity, unless acted on by a net (unbalanced) force
- **Newton's 2nd Law** — ΣF = ma (net force equals mass times acceleration); acceleration is proportional to net force and inversely proportional to mass
- **Newton's 3rd Law** — for every action force, there is an equal and opposite reaction force, acting on the *other* object (third-law pairs never cancel because they act on different objects)
- **Free-body diagrams (FBDs)** — isolating one object and drawing every force acting *on* it (gravity, normal, tension, friction, applied force)
- **Weight vs. mass** — weight = mg (a force, in newtons); mass is a scalar quantity (in kg) that doesn't change with location
- **Normal force** — the support force perpendicular to a surface; not always equal to weight (e.g., on an incline, or with an additional vertical force)
- **Friction** — static friction (f_s ≤ μ_s N) prevents motion from starting; kinetic friction (f_k = μ_k N) opposes motion already happening
- **Systems of objects** — connected objects (via string/pulley) share the same magnitude of acceleration; solve with a system equation or object-by-object

## Learning objectives

By the end of this topic, students can:
1. State all three of Newton's Laws and give a real-world example of each
2. Draw a complete, correct free-body diagram for an object on a flat surface, an incline, or connected by a string/pulley
3. Apply ΣF = ma to solve for unknown force, mass, or acceleration, including on an incline (decomposing gravity into components)
4. Solve two-object systems (e.g., blocks connected by a string, one on a table and one hanging)
5. Correctly identify a Newton's-third-law pair versus a balanced pair of forces on a single object

## Practice problems

1. A 5.0 kg box is pushed with a horizontal force of 20 N across a frictionless floor. Find its acceleration.
2. The same 5.0 kg box now experiences friction, with μ_k = 0.3. Find the acceleration with the same 20 N applied force.
3. A 10 kg block sits on a 30° frictionless incline. Find its acceleration down the incline.
4. Two blocks (3 kg and 5 kg) are connected by a string over a frictionless pulley, with the 5 kg block hanging off a table edge. Find the acceleration of the system and the tension in the string.
5. A person weighs 700 N on Earth. What is their mass? What would they weigh on a planet with half of Earth's gravitational acceleration?
6. Explain, using Newton's third law, why a rifle "kicks back" (recoils) when fired.

## Real-world applications

- **Seatbelts and airbags** — Newton's 1st law (your body keeps moving forward in a crash unless a force stops it)
- **Rocket propulsion** — Newton's 3rd law (expelling exhaust gas backward pushes the rocket forward)
- **Car traction and tires** — static vs. kinetic friction determines braking and acceleration limits
- **Elevators** — apparent weight changes (normal force) during acceleration up/down
- **Tug-of-war and walking** — friction with the ground as the "engine" of forward motion (reaction force from pushing backward on the ground)
