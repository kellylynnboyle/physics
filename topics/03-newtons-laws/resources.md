## Practice Problems

1. A 5 kg box is pushed with 20 N across a floor with μ_k = 0.3. Find its acceleration.
2. Draw the FBD for a book resting on a table, then for the same book being pushed sideways at constant velocity.
3. A 1200 kg car accelerates from 0 to 25 m/s in 8 seconds. Find the net force required.
4. A 50 kg skier is on a 20° slope with μ_k = 0.1. Find the skier's acceleration down the slope.
5. Two blocks (3 kg and 5 kg) are connected by a string over a frictionless pulley (Atwood machine). Find the system's acceleration and the string tension.
6. Explain, using Newton's 3rd Law, how a rocket can accelerate in the vacuum of space with nothing to "push off of."

## Real-World Applications

- **Vehicle safety:** Seatbelts and airbags counteract inertia (1st Law) during sudden stops
- **Rocketry:** Thrust from expelled exhaust gas (3rd Law) propels rockets even in a vacuum
- **Elevators:** Apparent weight changes (normal force) during acceleration/deceleration
- **Sports:** Friction coefficients matter in track shoe design, tire treads, and ice skate blades
- **Engineering:** Structural load calculations rely on force balance (equilibrium) in beams and bridges
