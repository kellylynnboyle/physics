# Unit 1: Kinematics

**Suggested pacing:** about 3 weeks · **Standards link:** foundation for HS-PS2-1 (see [docs/standards-alignment.md](../docs/standards-alignment.md))

## Learning objectives

Students will be able to:

- [ ] **K1.** Distinguish distance from displacement and speed from velocity, and identify which are scalars and which are vectors.
- [ ] **K2.** Calculate average velocity and average acceleration from data tables and graphs.
- [ ] **K3.** Construct and interpret position–time, velocity–time and acceleration–time graphs (slope and area).
- [ ] **K4.** Select and apply the constant-acceleration equations to solve one-dimensional problems.
- [ ] **K5.** Model free fall as constant acceleration (g = 9.8 m/s²) and explain why mass does not affect it when air resistance is negligible.
- [ ] **K6.** Collect and analyze motion data (motion sensor or video analysis) to support a claim about an object's acceleration.

## Key concepts

| Concept | What students must be able to say or do |
|---|---|
| Position, distance, displacement | Displacement is the change in position (a vector with direction). Distance is path length (a scalar). |
| Speed vs. velocity | Velocity = Δx / Δt, with direction. Speed is its magnitude (for instantaneous values). |
| Acceleration | a = Δv / Δt. Any change in speed *or direction* is an acceleration. |
| Sign conventions | Positive or negative only gives direction. Speeding up or slowing down depends on whether v and a point the same way. |
| Graphs | x–t slope = velocity. v–t slope = acceleration. v–t area = displacement. |
| Constant-acceleration equations | v = v₀ + at · Δx = v₀t + ½at² · v² = v₀² + 2aΔx · Δx = ½(v₀ + v)t |
| Free fall | a = g = 9.8 m/s² downward at every point, including the top of a throw. |

**Equation-selection habit:** list knowns (x, v₀, v, a, t), mark the unknown, and choose the equation that has no missing variable.

## Cornell notes

Print this table, or have students copy the **Cue** column and fill the **Notes** column from class. The Summary is completed by the student at the end.

| Cue / Questions | Notes |
|---|---|
| What is the difference between distance and displacement? | Distance: total path length, scalar. Displacement: Δx = x_final − x_initial, vector. Example: a full lap has displacement 0. |
| What is velocity? How is it different from speed? | v = Δx/Δt, includes direction (sign). Speed = how fast, no direction. |
| What is acceleration? | a = Δv/Δt, units m/s². Changes in speed **or** direction. |
| When does an object speed up or slow down? | Speeds up: v and a same sign. Slows down: v and a opposite signs. |
| What does the slope of a position–time graph show? Of a velocity–time graph? | x–t slope = velocity. v–t slope = acceleration. |
| What does the area under a v–t graph show? | Displacement. Split into rectangles and triangles. |
| Which equation do I use? | Write knowns and unknown, pick the equation missing the variable you don't have. |
| What is free fall? | Only gravity acts. a = 9.8 m/s² down. Velocity changes by 9.8 m/s each second. |
| Do heavier objects fall faster? | Not without air resistance. Same acceleration regardless of mass. |
| Sketch: v–t graph for a ball thrown up and caught | Straight line sloping down with slope −9.8 m/s²: crosses zero at the peak. |

**Summary (student writes 3–4 sentences):** How do position, velocity and acceleration relate to each other, and how can graphs show all three?

## Most-missed questions

The [interactive quiz](../quiz/index.html) covers these ten, in order of how often students struggle:

1. Acceleration at the top of a throw is not zero.
2. Distance vs. displacement on a closed path.
3. Average speed is total distance over total time (not the mean of two speeds).
4. Slope of a v–t graph is acceleration.
5. Area under a v–t graph is displacement.
6. Negative acceleration does not always mean slowing down.
7. v = gt in free fall.
8. d = ½gt² (time is squared).
9. Stopping distance with v² = v₀² + 2aΔx.
10. A horizontal x–t line means at rest.

## Practice problems

<details>
<summary>Problems (answers below)</summary>

1. A cyclist speeds up from 2 m/s to 12 m/s in 5 s. Find her acceleration and the distance covered.
2. A car traveling at 25 m/s brakes uniformly to rest in 50 m. Find the acceleration and the stopping time.
3. A stone dropped from a bridge hits the water after 3.0 s. How high is the bridge and how fast is the stone going on impact?
4. A ball is thrown straight up at 14.7 m/s. Find the maximum height and the time to return to the thrower's hand.
5. A v–t graph shows 0 → 8 m/s over 0–4 s, constant 8 m/s from 4–10 s, then 8 → 0 m/s from 10–12 s. Find the total displacement.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. a = 10/5 = **2 m/s²**; Δx = ½(2 + 12)(5) = **35 m**.
2. a = (0 − 25²)/(2·50) = **−6.25 m/s²**; t = 25/6.25 = **4.0 s**.
3. h = ½(9.8)(3.0)² = **44.1 m**; v = 9.8 × 3.0 = **29.4 m/s**.
4. h = 14.7²/(2·9.8) ≈ **11.0 m**; t = 2(14.7/9.8) = **3.0 s**.
5. ½(4)(8) + (6)(8) + ½(2)(8) = 16 + 48 + 8 = **72 m**.

</details>

## Lab and hands-on ideas

- **Motion-sensor walk-the-graph:** match a given x–t or v–t graph by walking; then predict and verify.
- **Ramp and cart:** measure acceleration at three ramp angles and plot a vs. sin θ (links to Unit 3).
- **Free-fall timing:** drop two objects of different mass (e.g., a marble and a heavier steel ball) and compare fall times; then compare with a sheet of paper crumpled vs. flat to discuss air resistance.
- **Video analysis:** film a dropped ball with a phone and use a free tool such as Tracker to extract g.

## Real-world applications

- **Traffic safety:** stopping distance grows with the *square* of speed, which is why speed limits drop near schools.
- **Accident reconstruction:** investigators use skid-mark length and v² = 2ad to estimate pre-braking speed.
- **Sports analytics:** sprint split times show acceleration phases; velocity–time graphs show where a runner tops out.
- **Elevators and roller coasters:** designers limit acceleration and jerk for comfort and safety.

## Assessment criteria

| Level | Evidence for objectives K1–K6 |
|---|---|
| **4 Advanced** | Solves multi-stage problems (e.g., two-phase motion), moves fluently between graph, equation and verbal representations, and critiques a data set or graph with errors. |
| **3 Proficient** | Chooses correct equations and units, interprets slope and area correctly, and explains free fall using g. Quiz ≥ 75%. |
| **2 Developing** | Handles one-step problems and simple graphs; makes sign or equation-selection errors on multi-step problems. Quiz 60–74%. |
| **1 Beginning** | Confuses speed/velocity/acceleration; cannot yet read slope or area. Quiz < 60%. |
