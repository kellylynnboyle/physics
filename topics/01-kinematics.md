# Unit 1: Kinematics (Describing Motion)

**NGSS link:** Foundation for HS-PS2-1 (analyze data to support Newton's second law). Kinematics is not its own NGSS performance expectation, but students need it to analyze motion data (SEP 4: Analyzing and Interpreting Data; CCC: Patterns, Cause and Effect). Math: CCSS.MP.4, HSN.Q.A.1.

**Pacing suggestion:** 2.5 weeks

## Learning objectives
Students will be able to:
- [ ] **K1** Distinguish distance/displacement and speed/velocity, and identify which are vectors.
- [ ] **K2** Calculate average velocity and acceleration from data, a graph or a motion description.
- [ ] **K3** Interpret position–time and velocity–time graphs (slope, area, shape).
- [ ] **K4** Apply the constant-acceleration equations to solve multi-step problems.
- [ ] **K5** Model free fall (a = g = 9.8 m/s² downward, ≈10 for estimates) and explain why mass does not matter.
- [ ] **K6** Collect motion data in a lab and make a claim supported by evidence.

## Key concepts
| Concept | Definition | Symbol / Equation | Unit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Position | Location relative to an origin | x | m |
| Displacement | Change in position (vector) | Δx = x_f − x_i | m |
| Distance | Total path length (scalar) | d | m |
| Velocity | Rate of change of position (vector) | v = Δx/Δt | m/s |
| Speed | Rate of distance traveled (scalar) | s = d/t | m/s |
| Acceleration | Rate of change of velocity (vector) | a = Δv/Δt | m/s² |
| Constant-acceleration set | | v = v₀ + at; Δx = v₀t + ½at²; v² = v₀² + 2aΔx | |
| Free fall | Motion with only gravity acting | a = −g | m/s² |

## Cornell notes

**Header:** Unit 1 Kinematics · Name ______ · Date ______ · Period ____
**Essential question:** *How can we describe and predict motion using words, graphs and equations?*

| Cue (write questions/keywords here after class) | Notes (write during class) |
|---|---|
| What is a vector vs. a scalar? | **Scalar** = magnitude only (distance, speed, time). **Vector** = magnitude + direction (displacement, velocity, acceleration). Choose a positive direction and keep signs consistent. |
| Distance vs. displacement? | Distance = total path. Displacement = final − initial position, straight line. Round trip: displacement = 0, distance ≠ 0. |
| Speed vs. velocity? | Speed = distance/time (scalar). Velocity = displacement/time (vector). Average speed ≠ average of two speeds; use total distance ÷ total time. |
| What is acceleration? | Any change in velocity: speeding up, slowing down **or turning**. a = Δv/Δt. If **a** and **v** point the same way → speeding up; opposite → slowing down. |
| Reading position–time graphs | Slope = velocity. Straight line = constant v. Curve = acceleration. Flat line = at rest. Steeper = faster. |
| Reading velocity–time graphs | Slope = acceleration. **Area under the curve = displacement.** Line crossing zero = direction reversal. |
| The 3 big equations | v = v₀ + at (no Δx) · Δx = v₀t + ½at² (no v) · v² = v₀² + 2aΔx (no t). Pick the one missing the variable you don't have or need. |
| Free fall | Ignore air: a = 9.8 m/s² down for everything, going up **and** down. At the top v = 0 but a ≠ 0. Time up = time down (same level). |
| Problem-solving routine | **GUESS**: **G**iven, **U**nknown, **E**quation, **S**ubstitute (with units), **S**anity check. |

## Most-missed ideas (these appear in the quiz)
1. Acceleration direction when slowing down.
2. Zero velocity ≠ zero acceleration (top of a throw).
3. Average speed over two equal-distance legs.
4. Slope vs. area on graphs.
5. Heavy objects do not fall faster in a vacuum.

## Practice problems (answers in brackets)
1. A runner goes 100 m east in 12 s, then 40 m west in 8 s. Find total distance, displacement, average speed, average velocity. *[140 m; 60 m east; 7 m/s; 3 m/s east]*
2. A car goes from 5 m/s to 25 m/s in 4 s. Find a and the distance covered. *[5 m/s²; 60 m]*
3. A cyclist brakes uniformly from 12 m/s to rest in 3 s. Find a and stopping distance. *[−4 m/s²; 18 m]*
4. A stone is dropped from a bridge and hits water in 2.5 s (g = 9.8). Bridge height and impact speed? *[≈30.6 m; 24.5 m/s]*
5. Sketch the v–t graph for a ball thrown up and caught at the same height. Where is the displacement zero? *[Straight line sloping down through v = 0 at the top; positive area = negative area]*
6. **Challenge:** A police car starts from rest at 3 m/s² when a speeder passes at 24 m/s. When and where does it catch up? *[16 s; 384 m]*

## Lab / hands-on
- **Buggy lab:** measure position vs. time for a battery buggy at two speeds; plot and compare slopes.
- **Ramp and cart:** photogates or phone video; show constant acceleration with v–t graph.
- **Reaction-time ruler drop:** use d = ½gt² to find reaction time; class data set.

## Real-world connections
- Traffic safety: stopping distance and speed limits (reaction distance + braking distance).
- Sports analytics: sprint splits, acceleration phase in a 100 m dash.
- GPS/navigation: distance vs. displacement vs. ETA.
- California: Bay Area BART train speed–time profile; freeway on-ramp merging.
- Amusement parks: free-fall drop towers (g's felt).

|---|---|
| 4 – Mastery | Correctly solves multi-step problems with graphs and equations, with signs and units. Explains reasoning in words. Spots errors in a flawed solution. |
| 3 – Proficient | Correct setup and solution with at most one minor sign or unit slip. Interprets graphs correctly. |
| 2 – Developing | Chooses correct concepts but the equation selection or algebra is inconsistent. Confuses slope and area occasionally. |
| 1 – Beginning | Confuses vector/scalar or velocity/acceleration; needs scaffolding. |
