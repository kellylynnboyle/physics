## Key concepts

- **Position (x), displacement (Δx = x_f − x_i)** — vector, has direction; **distance** is a scalar path length
- **Speed** (scalar) vs **velocity** (vector): v = Δx / Δt
- **Acceleration**: a = Δv / Δt — a vector; speeding up or slowing down depends on whether v and a point the same way
- **Constant-acceleration equations**
  - v = v₀ + at
  - Δx = v₀t + ½at²
  - v² = v₀² + 2aΔx
  - Δx = ½(v₀ + v)t
- **Graphs:** slope of x–t = velocity · slope of v–t = acceleration · area under v–t = displacement
- **Free fall:** only gravity acts; a = 9.8 m/s² downward everywhere, including the peak

## Cornell notes

| Cue (question / keyword) | Notes |
|--------------------------|-------|
| Distance vs displacement? | Distance = total path length (scalar). Displacement = straight-line change in position with direction (vector). A full lap: distance = 400 m, displacement = 0. |
| Speed vs velocity? | Speed = distance/time (scalar). Velocity = displacement/time (vector). |
| What is acceleration? | Rate of change of **velocity**: a = Δv/Δt, units m/s². Can be nonzero while v = 0 (top of a throw). |
| When is an object speeding up? | v and a have the **same sign**. Opposite signs → slowing down. |
| x–t graph meaning | Slope = velocity. Flat = at rest. Steeper = faster. Curve = accelerating. |
| v–t graph meaning | Slope = acceleration. Area = displacement. Below the axis = moving in the negative direction. |
| Which equation do I use? | List knowns (v₀, v, a, Δx, t). Pick the equation that has the unknown and **lacks** the quantity you don't have. |
| Free fall | a = −9.8 m/s² the whole flight. Up speed at launch = down speed at same height. Heavier objects fall at the same rate (no air). |
| Why does falling distance grow? | Δx = ½gt² — distance ∝ t². Double the time → 4× the distance. |

## Practice problems

1. A runner jogs 3 km east then 1 km west in 20 min. Find distance, displacement, average speed, average velocity. *(4 km; 2 km E; 12 km/h; 6 km/h E)*
2. A car accelerates from 5 m/s to 25 m/s in 4 s. Find a and Δx. *(5 m/s²; 60 m)*
3. A train brakes from 30 m/s at −3 m/s². How far does it travel before stopping? *(150 m)*
4. A stone is dropped from a bridge and hits the water in 3.0 s. How high is the bridge, and how fast is the stone going? *(44 m; 29 m/s)*
5. Sketch v–t and a–t graphs for a ball thrown straight up and caught at the same height. *(v–t: straight line, slope −9.8, crosses 0 at the peak; a–t: constant −9.8)*
6. Challenge: A car at 20 m/s sees a barrier 50 m ahead. Reaction time 0.5 s, braking a = −8 m/s². Does it stop in time? *(10 m + 25 m = 35 m → yes)*

## Real-world applications

- Traffic safety: stopping distance ∝ v² (why highway speeds are dangerous)
- Sports analytics: sprint acceleration curves, radar speed guns
- Aviation: takeoff runway length from v² = v₀² + 2aΔx
- Phone sensors: accelerometers measure a to count steps and detect falls
