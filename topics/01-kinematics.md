# Unit 1: Kinematics

**Pacing:** ~3 weeks · **Quiz:** [Kinematics](../quiz/index.html) · **Standards support:** HS-PS2-1 (foundation: describing motion mathematically)

> Kinematics describes *how* things move (position, velocity, acceleration) without asking *why*. Forces come in Unit 3.

## Learning objectives

- [ ] **K1.** Distinguish distance vs. displacement and speed vs. velocity, and say which are vectors.
- [ ] **K2.** Calculate average velocity and average acceleration from data or a description.
- [ ] **K3.** Interpret and sketch position-time, velocity-time and acceleration-time graphs (slope and area meaning).
- [ ] **K4.** Choose and apply the constant-acceleration equations to solve one-dimensional problems.
- [ ] **K5.** Model free fall with a = g ≈ 9.8 m/s² (use 10 m/s² for mental math) and explain why mass does not matter without air resistance.
- [ ] **K6.** Design and run a lab that measures acceleration and report results with uncertainty.

## Key concepts and equations

| Concept | Meaning | Equation / unit |
|---|---|---|
| Position (x) | Location relative to an origin | m |
| Displacement (Δx) | Net change in position, a vector | Δx = x_f − x_i |
| Distance | Total path length, a scalar | m |
| Velocity (v) | Rate of change of position, a vector | v = Δx / Δt (m/s) |
| Speed | Rate of distance travelled, a scalar | s = d / t |
| Acceleration (a) | Rate of change of velocity, a vector | a = Δv / Δt (m/s²) |
| Constant-a equation 1 | No displacement | v = v₀ + at |
| Constant-a equation 2 | No final velocity | Δx = v₀t + ½at² |
| Constant-a equation 3 | No time | v² = v₀² + 2aΔx |
| Constant-a equation 4 | No acceleration | Δx = ½(v₀ + v)t |
| Free fall | Only gravity acts | a = −g (taking up as +) |

**Graph reading rules**

| Graph | Slope means | Area means |
|---|---|---|
| Position-time | velocity | (not used) |
| Velocity-time | acceleration | displacement |
| Acceleration-time | (jerk, not used) | change in velocity |

## Cornell notes

| Cue / Questions | Notes |
|---|---|
| What is the difference between distance and displacement? | **Distance** = total path (scalar). **Displacement** = straight-line change in position with direction (vector). A full lap: distance = lap length, displacement = 0. |
| What is the difference between speed and velocity? | Speed = distance / time (scalar). Velocity = displacement / time (vector, has direction). |
| What is acceleration? | Any change in velocity: speeding up, slowing down **or** turning. Units m/s². Positive/negative only gives direction. |
| When does an object speed up or slow down? | v and a same sign → speeds up. v and a opposite sign → slows down. |
| What does the slope of x-t mean? | Velocity. Steeper = faster. Flat = at rest. Curved = changing velocity. |
| What does the area under v-t mean? | Displacement. Area below the axis counts as negative displacement. |
| How do I pick a kinematic equation? | List knowns (v₀, v, a, Δx, t). Find the variable that is neither known nor wanted and use the equation that leaves it out. |
| What is free fall? | Motion under gravity alone. a = 9.8 m/s² downward everywhere, including the top of a throw (where v = 0 momentarily). |
| Does mass change fall time? | Not without air resistance. Both accelerate at g. |

**Summary (write your own, 2–3 sentences):** _Kinematics uses displacement, velocity and acceleration to describe motion. Graph slopes and areas link the three quantities. For constant acceleration, four equations connect them, and free fall is the special case a = g._

## Common misconceptions (the most-missed ideas)

1. Zero velocity means zero acceleration. *(Not at the top of a throw.)*
2. Negative acceleration always means slowing down. *(Depends on the sign of velocity.)*
3. Distance and displacement are the same.
4. Average speed is the mean of two speeds. *(Use total distance / total time.)*
5. Heavier objects fall faster.
6. A curved x-t graph shows the path the object took.

## Practice problems

Use g = 10 m/s² unless told otherwise.

| # | Problem | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A car starts from rest and accelerates at 3 m/s² for 8 s. Find its final speed and the distance covered. | 24 m/s; 96 m |
| 2 | A runner completes a 400 m lap in 80 s. Find average speed and average velocity. | 5 m/s; 0 m/s |
| 3 | A car going 20 m/s brakes uniformly to a stop in 40 m. Find acceleration and stopping time. | −5 m/s²; 4 s |
| 4 | A ball is dropped from a 45 m building. How long does it fall, and how fast is it going at impact? | 3 s; 30 m/s |
| 5 | A ball is thrown upward at 20 m/s. How high does it go, and when does it return to the thrower's hand? | 20 m; 4 s |
| 6 | You drive 60 km at 30 km/h and 60 km at 60 km/h. Find the average speed. | 40 km/h |
| 7 | *Graph:* A v-t graph rises linearly from 0 to 12 m/s in 4 s, stays level for 6 s, then falls to 0 in 2 s. Find the total displacement and the acceleration in each stage. | 108 m (24 + 72 + 12); +3, 0, −6 m/s² |
| 8 | *Challenge:* A cheetah at 30 m/s is 60 m behind a gazelle running at 20 m/s. When does it catch the gazelle if both keep constant speed? | 6 s |

## Lab and activity ideas

- **Moving Man (PhET):** match given graphs by walking; predict the graph before running the sim.
- **Constant-acceleration cart:** ramp, cart and motion sensor or phone video analysis; plot x-t and v-t and compare the slope to a computed value.
- **Reaction-time ruler drop:** use d = ½gt² to compute your reaction time.
- **Free-fall timing:** drop objects of different masses from the same height, record with slow-motion video.

## Real-world connections

- **Traffic safety:** stopping distance grows with v², which is why speed limits drop near schools.
- **Sports analytics:** sprint start acceleration, 40-yard dash splits.
- **Autonomous vehicles:** braking and following-distance algorithms use these same equations.
- **Aviation:** takeoff roll length from required speed and engine acceleration.
- **Forensics:** reconstructing speed from skid marks.
