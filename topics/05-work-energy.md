# Unit 5 — Work & Energy
**NGSS:** HS-PS3-1, HS-PS3-2, HS-PS3-3 · **Pacing:** ~3 weeks

## Learning objectives — "I can…"
- [ ] Calculate work W = Fd cosθ and identify when work is zero
- [ ] Calculate KE, gravitational PE, and elastic PE
- [ ] Apply conservation of mechanical energy and the work–energy theorem
- [ ] Account for non-conservative work (friction → thermal energy)
- [ ] Calculate power and efficiency
- [ ] Use energy bar charts and build/refine an energy-conversion device

## Key concepts & equations
| Concept | Equation |
|---|---|
| Work | W = Fd cosθ (J = N·m) |
| Kinetic energy | KE = ½mv² |
| Gravitational PE | PE_g = mgh |
| Elastic PE | PE_s = ½kx² |
| Work–energy theorem | W_net = ΔKE |
| Conservation | KE₁ + PE₁ + W_nc = KE₂ + PE₂ |
| Power | P = W/t = Fv (W = J/s) |
| Efficiency | useful output ÷ input energy |

## Cornell notes
| Cue | Notes |
|---|---|
| When is work done on an object? | When a force has a component along the displacement. Carrying a box horizontally at constant v: force is up, motion is sideways → W = 0. |
| How much work is done pulling 20 N at 60° over 10 m? | W = 20·10·cos60° = **100 J**. |
| What happens to KE if speed doubles? | ×4 (KE ∝ v²). Stopping distance quadruples too. |
| How do I get PE? | PE = mgh relative to a chosen zero level: 2 kg at 5 m → 98 J. |
| Does a heavier object fall to a higher speed? | No. mgh = ½mv² → v = √(2gh); mass cancels. |
| Where does the "lost" energy go? | Friction/air drag convert mechanical energy to thermal energy; total energy is still conserved. |
| What is the work–energy theorem? | Net work = change in KE. 1000 kg car, 10→20 m/s: ΔKE = ½(1000)(400−100) = 150,000 J. |
| Difference between power and energy? | Power = rate. 600 J in 3 s = 200 W. A kWh is energy, not power. |
| How do I use energy bar charts? | Draw bars for KE, PE, thermal at start and end; total height must match (including thermal). |

**Summary:** Energy is transferred by work and transformed among forms (kinetic, potential, thermal) but total energy never disappears. Use conservation of energy to find speeds and heights without needing forces at every instant.

## Worked examples
1. **Coaster.** Rest at 20 m, frictionless, speed at ground: v = √(2·9.8·20) = **19.8 m/s**.
2. **Work with friction.** A 5 kg block slides at 3 m/s on a rough floor with f = 10 N. KE₁ = ½(5)(9) = 22.5 J. Work–energy: −f·d = 0 − 22.5 → **d = 2.25 m** to stop; the 22.5 J becomes thermal energy.
3. **Power.** Lift 50 kg up 10 m in 20 s: W = 4900 J → **245 W**.
4. **Efficiency.** Motor uses 500 J to give 350 J useful → **70%**.

## Practice problems
1. Find the KE of a 0.5 kg ball at 12 m/s. [36 J]
2. A 70 kg climber ascends 300 m. PE gain? [205,800 J]
3. A spring k = 200 N/m is compressed 0.1 m. PE stored? Launch speed of a 0.1 kg cart? [1 J; 4.5 m/s]
4. A 1500 kg car brakes from 25 m/s to rest in 50 m. Average braking force? [9375 N]
5. A pendulum bob (0.2 kg) is released from 0.5 m height. Speed at bottom? [3.1 m/s]
6. A 100 W bulb runs 10 hours. Energy in kWh and cost at $0.30/kWh? [1 kWh; $0.30]
