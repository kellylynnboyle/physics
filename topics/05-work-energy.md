## Key concepts

- **Work** W = F·d·cosθ (J); only the force component **along** the displacement does work
- **Kinetic energy** KE = ½mv² (∝ v²)
- **Gravitational PE** PE_g = mgh (relative to a chosen zero level)
- **Elastic PE** PE_s = ½kx² (∝ x²)
- **Work–energy theorem:** W_net = ΔKE
- **Conservation of energy:** total energy of a closed system is constant; with friction, mechanical energy becomes thermal energy
- **Power** P = W/t = Fv (W)
- **Energy accounting:** use energy bar charts: initial energy + work in = final energy + thermal

## Most-missed questions

1. Work is zero for a horizontal carry at constant height
2. KE depends on v² (doubling v quadruples KE)
3. Speed after a fall is independent of mass (but KE isn't)
4. Spring PE depends on x²
5. Energy at the midpoint of a frictionless drop (half PE converted)
6. Power = work ÷ time vs "power = work"
7. Work–energy theorem calculation of speed from net work

## Practice problems

1. A 20 kg crate is pushed 5 m with 40 N at 30° above horizontal. Work? *(173 J)*
2. A 1500 kg car travels at 25 m/s. KE? Doubling the speed does what? *(468,750 J; ×4)*
3. A 2 kg ball is dropped from 19.6 m. Speed at the ground? *(19.6 m/s)*
4. A 3 kg block slides down a frictionless 4 m high ramp. Speed at the bottom? *(8.85 m/s)*
5. A spring (k = 200 N/m) is compressed 0.30 m. Stored energy? *(9 J)*
6. A 60 kg student climbs a 4 m staircase in 5 s. Power? *(≈ 470 W)*
7. Challenge: A 1.0 kg block slides 5 m down a 30° rough incline from rest and reaches 5 m/s. How much energy is lost to friction? *(PE = 24.5 J, KE = 12.5 J → 12 J)*

## Real-world applications

- Roller coasters and skate parks: PE ↔ KE transfers
- Hybrid/electric vehicles: regenerative braking recovers KE
- Power plants and solar/wind: energy conversion devices and efficiency
- Fitness and nutrition: work, power, and food Calories
- Hydroelectric dams: gravitational PE → electrical energy
