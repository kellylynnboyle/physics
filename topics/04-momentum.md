# Unit 4 – Momentum

NGSS: HS-PS2-2, HS-PS2-3 · Objectives M1–M4 (see [standards-and-assessment.md](../standards-and-assessment.md))

## Key concepts
- Momentum p = mv (vector)
- Impulse J = FΔt = Δp
- Conservation of momentum in isolated systems
- Elastic, inelastic, and perfectly inelastic collisions; explosions/recoil

## Cornell notes

| Cue / Question | Notes |
|---|---|
| What is momentum and what are its units? | p = mv, kg·m/s, a **vector** (direction = velocity direction). Ex: 1000 kg at 20 m/s: 20,000 kg·m/s. |
| What is impulse? | J = FΔt = Δp = m(v_f − v_i), units N·s = kg·m/s. Impulse is the change in momentum. Ex: 0.5 kg ball bouncing from +4 to −4 m/s: Δp = 0.5(−4 − 4) = −4 kg·m/s (magnitude 4 N·s). |
| Why do airbags and crumple zones reduce injury? | Same Δp, but longer Δt means smaller average force: F = Δp/Δt. Also: bending knees when landing, catching a ball by "giving". |
| When is momentum conserved? | When no net **external** force acts on the system: total p before = total p after. True for every collision type (and explosions) if the system is isolated. |
| How do I solve a perfectly inelastic collision? | Objects stick: m₁v₁ + m₂v₂ = (m₁ + m₂)v. Ex: 1200 kg at 10 m/s hits a stationary 800 kg: v = 12000/2000 = 6 m/s. Two 1 kg carts at ±2 m/s: total p = 0, so v_f = 0. |
| What is conserved in elastic vs inelastic collisions? | **Momentum**: both. **Kinetic energy**: only in elastic collisions; in inelastic collisions some KE becomes heat, sound, deformation. |
| How do I handle an explosion or recoil? | Start with total p = 0, so parts have equal and opposite momentum. Ex: 4 kg gun fires a 0.02 kg bullet at 500 m/s: 0.02(500) = 4v → gun recoils at 2.5 m/s. |
| How do I handle direction? | Choose a positive direction; objects moving the other way get negative velocity. Check that the sign of the answer makes sense. |

**Memory tricks:** "p = mv, J = FΔt" · "Before = After" · "Longer time, smaller force" · "Momentum always, energy sometimes."

**Summary:** Momentum measures how hard it is to stop something. Impulse changes momentum. In an isolated system the total vector momentum stays constant, which lets us predict collision outcomes without knowing the forces.

## Practice problems
1. Average force 1500 N acts for 0.02 s. Impulse? **30 N·s**
2. 1200 kg car at 10 m/s hits a stationary 800 kg car and they lock together. Final velocity? **6 m/s**
3. A 4 kg gun fires a 0.02 kg bullet at 500 m/s. Recoil speed? **2.5 m/s**
4. A 0.15 kg ball moving 20 m/s is caught in 0.01 s; find average force. **300 N** (0.15 × 20 / 0.01)
5. Design a package that protects an egg dropped from 3 m; explain with impulse.

## Real-world applications
Car safety (airbags, crumple zones, seat belts, helmets) · sports (follow-through, catching) · rocket propulsion · billiards · crash reconstruction.
