## Key concepts

- **Momentum** p = mv (kg·m/s), a vector
- **Impulse** J = FΔt = Δp = m(v_f − v_i)
- **Conservation:** in a closed system with no net external force, total p before = total p after
- **Collisions**
  - Elastic: p and KE conserved
  - Inelastic: p conserved, KE lost
  - Perfectly inelastic: objects stick; m₁v₁ + m₂v₂ = (m₁ + m₂)v′
- **Explosions/recoil:** total p starts at 0 and stays 0 → pieces go opposite directions
- **Force–time graph:** area = impulse
- **Safety design:** same Δp, longer Δt → smaller average force (airbags, crumple zones, helmets, padding)

## Cornell notes

| Cue | Notes |
|-----|-------|
| Momentum | p = mv. Direction = velocity direction. Heavier or faster → more momentum. |
| Impulse | J = FΔt. Equals Δp. Units N·s = kg·m/s. |
| Impulse–momentum theorem | FΔt = mΔv. Same Δp, bigger Δt → smaller F. |
| Why airbags work | They stretch the stopping time, reducing average force on the passenger. |
| Conservation of momentum | Isolated system: Σp_before = Σp_after. Signs matter. |
| Perfectly inelastic | Stick together: m₁v₁ + m₂v₂ = (m₁+m₂)v′. KE is lost to heat, sound, deformation. |
| Elastic | Both p and KE conserved (very hard objects, e.g. billiard balls approx.). |
| Bounce vs stick | Bounce reverses velocity, so Δp is larger → larger impulse. |
| Recoil | Total p = 0 initially, so m₁v₁ = −m₂v₂ (opposite directions). |
| F–t graph | Area under curve = impulse. |

## Most-missed questions

1. Momentum is a vector — head-on equal collisions can give zero total momentum
2. Is kinetic energy conserved in a perfectly inelastic collision? (No)
3. Recoil speed of a gun / cannon
4. Why airbags reduce force (time, not momentum change)
5. Bounce vs stick: which has more impulse
6. Area under an F–t graph is impulse

## Practice problems

1. A 1000 kg car at 20 m/s and a 2000 kg truck at 10 m/s: compare momentum. *(Equal: 20,000 kg·m/s)*
2. A 0.15 kg baseball moving at −40 m/s is hit to +50 m/s. Find impulse and average force if contact is 2 ms. *(13.5 N·s; 6750 N)*
3. A 2 kg cart at 3 m/s hits a stationary 1 kg cart and sticks. Final velocity? *(2 m/s)*
4. A 60 kg skater pushes off a 40 kg skater, both at rest. The 40 kg skater moves 3 m/s. Speed of the 60 kg skater? *(2 m/s opposite)*
5. For problem 3, how much KE is lost? *(KE_i = 9 J, KE_f = 6 J → 3 J)*
6. Challenge: Design (sketch + calculations) a crumple zone that cuts the average force on a 1 kg egg from 3 m/s to rest by 5× compared to a rigid stop of 0.01 s. *(Δt = 0.05 s; F drops from 300 N to 60 N)*

## Real-world applications

- Automotive: airbags, seatbelts, crumple zones, guardrails
- Sports: follow-through, padded gloves, catching with "give", helmets
- Rockets and jets: momentum of expelled gas
- Forensics: reconstructing crashes from final velocities
