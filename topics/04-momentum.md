# Unit 4: Momentum

**Pacing:** ~3 weeks · **Quiz:** [Momentum](../quiz/index.html) · **Standards:** HS-PS2-2 (conservation of momentum), HS-PS2-3 (design to minimize force)

> Momentum is "mass in motion." In a system with no external net force, total momentum never changes. This is how we analyze collisions and explosions.

## Learning objectives

- [ ] **M1.** Calculate momentum (a vector) of single objects and of systems.
- [ ] **M2.** Relate impulse to change in momentum and to the area under a force-time graph.
- [ ] **M3.** Explain how increasing collision time reduces force (airbags, helmets, crumple zones).
- [ ] **M4.** Use conservation of momentum for inelastic, elastic and explosion/recoil problems (HS-PS2-2).
- [ ] **M5.** Classify collisions by what is conserved and compute the kinetic energy lost.
- [ ] **M6.** Use data from a cart or video experiment to show momentum is conserved within uncertainty.
- [ ] **M7.** Evaluate a protective design using impulse and force-time data (HS-PS2-3).

## Key concepts and equations

| Concept | Equation / statement |
|---|---|
| Momentum | p = mv (kg·m/s), a vector |
| Impulse | J = FΔt = Δp = mv_f − mv_i (N·s) |
| Newton's 2nd law (momentum form) | F_net = Δp / Δt |
| Conservation of momentum | If F_ext,net = 0: Σp_before = Σp_after |
| Perfectly inelastic collision | m₁v₁ + m₂v₂ = (m₁ + m₂)v′ (objects stick) |
| Elastic collision | Momentum **and** kinetic energy conserved; equal masses swap velocities (1-D, one at rest) |
| Explosion / recoil | Starting from rest: 0 = m₁v₁ + m₂v₂ |
| Kinetic energy lost | ΔKE = KE_after − KE_before (negative for inelastic) |

## Cornell notes

| Cue / Questions | Notes |
|---|---|
| What is momentum? | p = mv. More mass or more speed means more momentum. A vector: direction matters (use + / −). |
| What is impulse? | A force acting for a time: J = FΔt. It equals the change in momentum. |
| How does an airbag work? | Same Δp, longer Δt, so smaller average force F = Δp/Δt. |
| Why is bouncing a larger impulse than sticking? | The velocity reverses: Δp = m(v_f − v_i) is bigger if v_f is opposite. |
| When is momentum conserved? | For a system with no external net force. Internal forces (3rd-law pairs) cancel. |
| Is kinetic energy conserved too? | Only in elastic collisions. In inelastic collisions KE becomes heat, sound and deformation. |
| How do I set up a collision problem? | Draw before/after sketches, choose + direction, write Σp_before = Σp_after, solve. |
| What about recoil? | Total p starts at 0, so the pieces have equal and opposite momenta. The lighter piece moves faster. |
| What does the F-t graph show? | Area under the curve = impulse. |

**Summary (write your own):** _Impulse changes momentum. In an isolated system, total momentum is conserved in every collision; kinetic energy is conserved only in elastic ones. Longer contact times mean smaller forces._

## Common misconceptions

1. Momentum and kinetic energy are the same thing, or are always conserved together.
2. Ignoring direction (momentum is a vector).
3. Faster means more momentum regardless of mass.
4. Safety devices reduce the change in momentum. *(They extend the time.)*
5. Equal and opposite forces in a collision mean equal changes in velocity. *(Equal Δp, not equal Δv.)*
6. Using the wrong mass after objects stick together.

## Practice problems

| # | Problem | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Find the momentum of a 1000 kg car moving at 20 m/s. | 20 000 kg·m/s |
| 2 | A 0.145 kg baseball arrives at 40 m/s and leaves at 50 m/s in the opposite direction. Find the impulse, and the average force if contact lasts 2 ms. | 13.05 N·s; 6525 N |
| 3 | A 2 kg cart at 6 m/s sticks to a 4 kg cart at rest. Find the final speed and the kinetic energy lost. | 2 m/s; 24 J (36 J → 12 J) |
| 4 | A 60 kg skater at rest throws a 3 kg ball at 10 m/s. Find the skater's recoil speed. (Skater mass is 60 kg after the throw.) | 0.5 m/s opposite |
| 5 | 1 kg cart at 3 m/s hits an identical stationary cart elastically. Find the final velocities. | 0 and 3 m/s |
| 6 | A 1200 kg car stops from 15 m/s in 0.1 s on a wall, versus 0.5 s with a crumple zone. Compare average forces. | 180 kN vs 36 kN |
| 7 | A 50 g egg falls and hits the floor at 4 m/s. Find the impulse to stop it, and force with 0.01 s vs 0.1 s. | 0.2 N·s; 20 N vs 2 N |
| 8 | A 3 kg cart at 2 m/s moves right and collides head-on with a 1 kg cart at 4 m/s moving left, and they stick. Find v′. | 0.5 m/s right (6 − 4 = 2 over 4 kg) |
| 9 | *Challenge:* A 0.01 kg bullet at 400 m/s embeds in a 1.99 kg block hanging on strings. What height does it rise? (g = 10) | v′ = 2 m/s, h = 0.2 m |

## Lab and activity ideas

- **Collision Lab (PhET):** try elastic and inelastic collisions with different masses.
- **Dynamics carts with photogates:** measure before/after velocities, calculate Σp and KE.
- **Crumple-zone challenge (HS-PS2-3):** drop a loaded cart or egg with different padding and graph force vs. time (force sensor) or compare stopping distance.
- **Impulse with a force plate or phone:** jump and measure the impulse and the height reached.

## Real-world connections

- **Vehicle safety:** airbags, crumple zones, seat belts, and padded dashboards.
- **Sports:** follow-through in a golf swing, catching a ball by "giving" with your hands, helmets.
- **Rockets:** thrust comes from momentum conservation with exhaust.
- **Accident reconstruction:** collision speeds from post-crash motion.
- **Particle physics:** conservation of momentum identifies unseen particles.
