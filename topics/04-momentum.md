# Unit 4: Momentum

**Suggested pacing:** about 3 weeks · **Standards:** HS-PS2-2 (conservation of momentum), HS-PS2-3 (design a device that minimizes collision force). See [docs/standards-alignment.md](../docs/standards-alignment.md).

## Learning objectives

Students will be able to:

- [ ] **M1.** Define momentum (p = mv) as a vector and calculate it.
- [ ] **M2.** Relate impulse to change in momentum (FΔt = Δp) and to the force–time graph.
- [ ] **M3.** Use mathematical representations to show that total momentum of a system is conserved when there is no net external force (HS-PS2-2).
- [ ] **M4.** Solve one-dimensional collision and explosion/recoil problems.
- [ ] **M5.** Classify collisions as elastic, inelastic or perfectly inelastic using kinetic energy.
- [ ] **M6.** Design, test and refine a device that reduces the force on an object during a collision (HS-PS2-3).

## Key concepts

| Concept | What students must be able to say or do |
|---|---|
| Momentum | p = mv, units kg·m/s, vector (sign = direction). |
| Impulse | J = FΔt = Δp. Area under a force–time graph. |
| Force and time trade-off | Same Δp: longer collision time ⇒ smaller average force. |
| Conservation of momentum | If net external force is zero, p_total before = p_total after. |
| Newton's third law link | During a collision, forces are equal and opposite for the same time, so the momentum changes are equal and opposite. |
| Collision types | Elastic: KE conserved. Inelastic: KE lost. Perfectly inelastic: objects stick. Momentum is conserved in all of them. |
| Recoil / explosions | Starts at rest ⇒ total momentum zero ⇒ pieces move in opposite directions. |
| Momentum vs. kinetic energy | p ∝ v; KE ∝ v². Different quantities with different units and behavior. |

## Cornell notes

| Cue / Questions | Notes |
|---|---|
| What is momentum? | "Mass in motion." p = mv. Vector. Units kg·m/s. |
| What is impulse? | J = F_avg·Δt = Δp = m(v_f − v_i). Units N·s = kg·m/s. |
| Why do airbags and crumple zones help? | Same Δp, longer Δt ⇒ smaller force. |
| What does the area under an F–t graph show? | Impulse (change in momentum). |
| When is momentum conserved? | Closed system with no net external force. Total before = total after. |
| How do I solve a collision? | Pick a positive direction. Write m₁v₁ + m₂v₂ = m₁v₁′ + m₂v₂′. Solve. Keep signs. |
| What is a perfectly inelastic collision? | Objects stick: m₁v₁ + m₂v₂ = (m₁ + m₂)v′. Max KE loss. |
| What is an elastic collision? | Momentum **and** KE conserved. Two equal masses head-on swap velocities. |
| Why does a bounce give more impulse than sticking? | Δp is bigger: v reverses (|Δp| = 2mv) instead of going to zero (mv). |
| What happens in a recoil? | Start at rest, total p = 0, so the pieces' momenta cancel (gun and bullet, skater and ball). |

**Summary (student writes 3–4 sentences):** Explain why total momentum is conserved in a collision, using Newton's third law, and give a real-world example.

## Practice problems

<details>
<summary>Problems (answers below)</summary>

1. A 0.145 kg baseball arrives at 40 m/s and leaves in the opposite direction at 50 m/s. The bat is in contact for 0.002 s. Find the impulse and average force.
2. A 1500 kg car moving at 20 m/s hits and sticks to a stationary 1000 kg car. Find the final speed and the kinetic energy lost.
3. A 60 kg skater at rest pushes off a 40 kg skater, who moves away at 3 m/s. How fast does the first skater move, and in what direction?
4. A 1 kg cart moving at 4 m/s collides elastically head-on with a 1 kg cart at rest. Find both final velocities.
5. A 0.06 kg egg hits the ground at 6 m/s and stops. Compare the average force if it stops in 0.01 s (hard floor) and 0.1 s (padded). Ignore gravity during the collision.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. Δp = 0.145(50 − (−40)) = **13.05 kg·m/s**; F = 13.05/0.002 ≈ **6,525 N**.
2. v′ = 1500(20)/2500 = **12 m/s**. KE before = 300,000 J; after = 180,000 J; lost = **120,000 J**.
3. 0 = 40(3) + 60v ⇒ v = **−2 m/s**: 2 m/s in the opposite direction.
4. Equal masses swap: first cart **0 m/s**, second cart **4 m/s**.
5. Δp = 0.06 × 6 = 0.36 kg·m/s. Hard: F = 0.36/0.01 = **36 N**. Padded: F = 0.36/0.1 = **3.6 N** (ten times smaller).

</details>

## Lab and hands-on ideas

- **Collision carts with motion sensors (HS-PS2-2):** compare total momentum before and after for sticking and bouncing carts; compute the percent difference and the KE change.
- **Egg-drop or crash-cart challenge (HS-PS2-3):** design a device with constraints on mass, cost and size; measure impact force with a force sensor or accelerometer app; iterate.
- **Impulse with a force sensor:** bounce vs. catch a ball and integrate the F–t graph.
- **PhET:** *Collision Lab* (set elasticity from 0% to 100%).

## Real-world applications

- **Vehicle safety:** airbags, crumple zones, seat belts and padded dashboards lengthen the collision time.
- **Sports:** follow-through in golf and baseball, catching a fast ball by "giving" with the hands, boxing gloves, helmets.
- **Spaceflight:** rockets gain momentum by expelling exhaust; satellites maneuver with thrusters.
- **Forensics:** estimating pre-collision speeds from post-collision motion.
- **Packaging:** foam inserts lengthen impact time for fragile items.
