# Unit 4 — Momentum & Impulse
**NGSS:** HS-PS2-2, HS-PS2-3 · **Pacing:** ~3 weeks

## Learning objectives — "I can…"
- [ ] Calculate momentum p = mv (vector) and impulse J = FΔt
- [ ] Apply impulse–momentum theorem: FΔt = Δp
- [ ] Use conservation of momentum for collisions and explosions
- [ ] Classify collisions: elastic, inelastic, perfectly inelastic
- [ ] Design and evaluate a device that reduces collision force (HS-PS2-3)

## Key concepts & equations
| Concept | Rule |
|---|---|
| Momentum | p = mv (kg·m/s), direction of v |
| Impulse | J = FΔt = Δp = m(v_f − v_i) (N·s = kg·m/s) |
| Conservation | If ΣF_ext = 0: p_total before = p_total after |
| Perfectly inelastic | m₁v₁ + m₂v₂ = (m₁+m₂)v_f |
| Elastic | momentum **and** kinetic energy conserved |
| Force reduction | Same Δp, longer Δt → smaller F |

## Cornell notes
| Cue | Notes |
|---|---|
| What is momentum and what are its units? | "Mass in motion": p = mv, kg·m/s, a vector. 1000 kg at 20 m/s → 20,000 kg·m/s. |
| What is impulse? | Force × time of contact = change in momentum. 50 N for 0.2 s → 10 N·s. |
| Why do airbags and crumple zones help? | Same Δp, but longer stopping time → smaller average force. |
| Is momentum conserved in all collisions? | Yes, in an isolated system. Kinetic energy is conserved **only** in elastic collisions. |
| What is the change in momentum when a ball bounces back? | Direction flips: 0.5 kg at 10 m/s in, 10 m/s out → Δp = 0.5(10 − (−10)) = 10 kg·m/s (not 0). |
| How do I solve a sticking collision? | Choose + direction, p_before = sum, divide by total mass. 2 kg @ 6 m/s hits 4 kg at rest → (12)/(6) = 2 m/s. |
| What happens in an explosion/recoil? | Starting at rest, total p = 0, so pieces move opposite with equal-size momenta. |
| What do I do with signs? | Pick right = +. Leftward velocities are negative. Add vectors, don't add speeds. |

**Summary:** Momentum measures how hard an object is to stop. Forces over time change momentum (impulse); with no external net force, a system's total momentum stays constant. Collisions differ in energy but never in momentum.

## Worked examples
1. **Inelastic.** 2 kg at 6 m/s + 4 kg at rest, stick: v = **2 m/s**. KE before 36 J, after 12 J → 24 J lost to deformation/heat.
2. **Recoil.** 50 kg skater throws 5 kg ball at 4 m/s: 0 = 5(4) + 50v → **v = −0.4 m/s**.
3. **Impulse.** 3 kg cart, 12 N for 2 s from rest: J = 24 N·s → **v = 8 m/s**.
4. **Egg drop.** Stopping in 0.01 s vs. 0.1 s → 10× less force with padding.

## Practice problems
1. Compare momentum of a 0.145 kg baseball at 40 m/s and a 70 kg runner at 5 m/s. [5.8 vs 350 kg·m/s]
2. A 0.15 kg ball hits a bat at 30 m/s and leaves opposite at 40 m/s in 0.002 s. Average force? [5250 N]
3. 1500 kg car (10 m/s) hits stationary 1000 kg car; lock together. Final speed? [6 m/s]
4. A 60 kg astronaut at rest throws a 2 kg wrench at 6 m/s. Her speed? [0.2 m/s opposite]
5. Two carts 1 kg at +3 and 1 kg at −1 collide elastically (equal masses). Final velocities? [−1 m/s and +3 m/s (exchange)]
6. Explain how a rolling-with-the-punch boxer reduces damage. [increase Δt]
