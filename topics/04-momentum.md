# 4. Momentum

**NGSS link:** **HS-PS2-2** (use mathematical representations to support that total momentum is conserved in a system of two interacting objects) and **HS-PS2-3** (design a device that minimizes force during a collision). Crosscutting: *Systems and System Models*.
**Suggested pacing:** 2.5 weeks · **Lab:** Dynamics carts collisions (elastic vs. sticky); **Design project:** egg-drop or crumple-zone cart.

## Learning objectives
- [ ] M1. Define momentum and impulse and give their units.
- [ ] M2. Use the impulse–momentum theorem, FΔt = Δp, to explain safety devices.
- [ ] M3. Apply conservation of momentum to collisions and explosions in 1-D.
- [ ] M4. Classify collisions as elastic, inelastic, or perfectly inelastic using kinetic energy.
- [ ] M5. Design, test, and refine a device that reduces collision force, and justify it with data.

## Key concepts
| Concept | Meaning | Formula |
|---|---|---|
| Momentum | Mass in motion (vector) | p = mv (kg·m/s) |
| Impulse | Force applied over time | J = FΔt (N·s) |
| Impulse–momentum theorem | Impulse changes momentum | FΔt = Δp = m(v_f − v_i) |
| Conservation of momentum | Isolated system: total p constant | Σp_before = Σp_after |
| Perfectly inelastic | Objects stick together | m₁v₁ + m₂v₂ = (m₁ + m₂)v′ |
| Elastic | KE also conserved | ½Σmv² same before and after |

## Cornell notes
| Cues / questions | Notes |
|---|---|
| What is momentum? | p = mv; direction of v. Hard to stop = large p. |
| What is impulse? | Force × time; equals the change in momentum. Area under an F–t graph. |
| Why do airbags help? | Same Δp, longer Δt → smaller average force. |
| When is momentum conserved? | When no net external force acts on the system (collisions are usually fast enough). |
| Steps for a collision problem | Pick a positive direction → write Σp before → Σp after → solve. Signs matter. |
| Explosions / recoil | Start at rest, total p = 0, so the pieces' momenta cancel. |
| Is KE conserved? | Only in elastic collisions. Inelastic: KE → heat, sound, deformation. Momentum is **always** conserved. |
| Bouncing vs. sticking | Bouncing gives a bigger Δp (reverses direction), hence a bigger impulse. |

**Summary:** _Momentum is conserved when I choose the system correctly. Impulse tells me how a force over time changes momentum. Making the collision time longer lowers the force._

## Practice problems
1. Compare p of a 0.15 kg baseball at 40 m/s and a 70 kg runner at 5 m/s. *(6 vs. 350 kg·m/s)*
2. A 0.145 kg ball arrives at 35 m/s and leaves at 45 m/s the other way. Find the impulse. *(11.6 N·s)*
3. If the bat contact lasts 1.2 ms in #2, what is the average force? *(≈ 9,700 N)*
4. A 3 kg cart at 4 m/s hits a 2 kg cart at rest and they stick. Final speed and KE lost? *(2.4 m/s; 9.6 J)*
5. A 1500 kg car going 20 m/s east hits a 1000 kg car going 10 m/s west; they lock. Final velocity? *(8 m/s east)*
6. A 60 kg astronaut pushes off a 120 kg satellite, and the satellite moves away at 0.5 m/s. How fast does the astronaut move the other way? *(1.0 m/s)*
7. **Design project:** Build a crumple-zone cart. Success metric: peak force (from an accelerometer or phone sensor) under 20 N on a 1 m/s impact. Document 3 iterations.
8. **Challenge:** A 0.02 kg bullet at 300 m/s embeds in a 2 kg block hanging as a ballistic pendulum. How high does the block swing? *(≈ 0.45 m; v = 2.97 m/s just after impact)*
