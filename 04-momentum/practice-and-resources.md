# Topic 4: Momentum — Practice, Resources & Applications

## Practice Problems

1. A `0.15 kg` baseball traveling at `40 m/s` is hit by a bat and leaves at `50 m/s` in the exact opposite direction. If the bat is in contact with the ball for `0.7 ms (0.0007 s)`, find the average force exerted by the bat.
2. A `1000 kg` car traveling at `15 m/s` collides with a stationary `1500 kg` car, and they lock together (perfectly inelastic). Find their common velocity immediately after the collision.
3. A `0.02 kg` bullet traveling at `400 m/s` embeds in a stationary `2 kg` wooden block resting on a frictionless surface. Find the block+bullet's velocity right after impact. What fraction of the initial kinetic energy was lost?
4. Two ice skaters, `50 kg` and `70 kg`, push off from rest against each other. If the `50 kg` skater moves away at `3 m/s`, what is the `70 kg` skater's velocity?
5. A `0.5 kg` ball moving at `6 m/s` collides elastically head-on with a stationary `0.5 kg` ball. Predict (without heavy calculation, using the fact that equal-mass elastic collisions exchange velocities) the velocity of each ball after the collision.
6. A `60 kg` astronaut at rest in space throws a `1 kg` tool away at `8 m/s`. Find the astronaut's resulting velocity.
7. In a car crash test, a `1200 kg` car decelerates from `18 m/s` to `0` in `0.15 s` with airbags, versus `0.03 s` without. Compare the average force experienced in each case.

### Answer Key
1. `Δp = m(v_f − v_i) = 0.15(−50 − 40) = −13.5 kg·m/s` (taking initial direction as positive); `F = Δp/Δt = −13.5/0.0007 ≈ −19,286 N` — about 19.3 kN opposing the ball's original direction.
2. Momentum conservation: `1000(15) + 1500(0) = (1000+1500)v_f → v_f = 15000/2500 = 6 m/s`
3. `0.02(400) = (2.02)v_f → v_f ≈ 3.96 m/s`. `KE_i = ½(0.02)(400²) = 1600 J`. `KE_f = ½(2.02)(3.96²) ≈ 15.8 J`. Fraction lost `≈ (1600−15.8)/1600 ≈ 99%` — almost all kinetic energy converts to heat/deformation, characteristic of a highly inelastic collision.
4. Momentum conservation from rest: `0 = 50(3) + 70(v₂) → v₂ = −150/70 ≈ −2.14 m/s` (opposite direction to the 50 kg skater)
5. In an equal-mass elastic head-on collision, the moving ball stops (`0 m/s`) and the initially-stationary ball moves off at the original speed (`6 m/s`) — velocities fully exchange.
6. `0 = 60(v_astro) + 1(8) → v_astro = −8/60 ≈ −0.133 m/s` (recoils opposite to the thrown tool)
7. `Δp = 1200(0−18) = −21,600 kg·m/s` in both cases (same mass and speed change). With airbags: `F = 21600/0.15 = 144,000 N`. Without: `F = 21600/0.03 = 720,000 N` — five times greater peak force without the airbag's extended stopping time.

- **The Physics Classroom** — [Momentum and Collisions tutorial](https://www.physicsclassroom.com/class/momentum) — thorough treatment including the "Momentum Conservation Principle" walkthroughs.

- **MythBusters / real crash-test footage** (search reputable sources like IIHS — Insurance Institute for Highway Safety) — good real footage for discussing crumple zones and impulse.

## Real-World Applications
- **Crumple zones and airbags**: extend collision time to reduce peak force on occupants (impulse-momentum theorem in action).
- **Rocket propulsion**: expelling mass (exhaust) backward at high speed gives the rocket forward momentum — conservation of momentum in an "explosion" rather than a collision.
- **Newton's cradle**: a classic demo of momentum and (near-)elastic collision transfer through a chain of objects.
- **Sports**: boxing gloves increase impact time to reduce force on the head compared to a bare-knuckle punch of the same momentum change; follow-through in swings (bat, racket) extends contact time to increase impulse delivered to the ball.
