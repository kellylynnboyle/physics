## Practice problems

1. A 0.15 kg baseball is thrown at 30 m/s and caught, coming to rest in 0.02 s. Find the impulse and the average force on the catcher's glove.
2. A 1200 kg car traveling at 15 m/s collides with a stationary 1500 kg car, and they lock together. Find their common velocity after the collision. Is this elastic or inelastic? How do you know?
3. A 0.5 kg cart moving at 4 m/s collides elastically with an identical stationary 0.5 kg cart. If the first cart stops completely, what is the second cart's velocity? (Elastic, equal-mass collisions transfer all velocity.)
4. A 60 kg astronaut at rest in space throws a 2 kg tool at 8 m/s away from the space station. Find the astronaut's resulting velocity. (This is an "explosion"-type problem.)
5. A 1000 kg car moving at 20 m/s (east) collides head-on with a 1500 kg car moving at 15 m/s (west). If they stick together, find their velocity after impact — remember to assign opposite signs to opposite directions.
6. Compare stopping a 1000 kg car from 20 m/s to 0 in (a) 5 seconds using brakes, versus (b) 0.1 seconds by hitting a wall. Calculate the average force in each case and explain the result using the impulse-momentum theorem.
7. **Conceptual:** Two skaters push off from each other on frictionless ice, starting at rest. Explain why they must move apart with momenta that are equal in magnitude but opposite in sign, even if they have very different masses.
8. **Conceptual:** In a car crash test, is kinetic energy conserved? Explain what happens to the "missing" kinetic energy in terms of crumple zones.

## Real-world applications

- **Airbags and crumple zones** — direct application of the impulse-momentum theorem: extending the collision time reduces the peak force on occupants for the same total momentum change.
- **Rocket propulsion** — an "explosion"-type momentum problem: expelling mass (exhaust) backward gives the rocket forward momentum, conserving total momentum of the rocket+exhaust system.
- **Billiards/pool** — nearly elastic collisions used to teach and verify momentum conservation in physics labs.
- **Ballistic pendulum** — a classic perfectly-inelastic-collision device historically used to measure bullet speeds by measuring how high a block swings after absorbing the bullet.
- **Boxing/martial arts padding and "rolling with a punch"** — increasing contact/follow-through time to reduce peak force, same physics as airbags.

## Suggested student task

Find a video (sports collision, crash test, or a lab collision cart demo)
and identify: was the collision closer to elastic, inelastic, or perfectly
inelastic? Justify your answer in 2–3 sentences using what happens to the
objects' shapes and relative motion after the collision.
