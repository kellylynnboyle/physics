## Practice Problems

1. A 1200 kg car traveling at 25 m/s comes to a stop in 4 seconds during braking. Find the impulse and the average braking force.
2. A 0.15 kg baseball traveling at 40 m/s is hit by a bat and leaves at 50 m/s in the opposite direction. Find the impulse delivered by the bat, and the average force if contact time was 0.002 s.
3. A 60 kg ice skater at rest pushes off a 40 kg skater, also at rest. If the 40 kg skater moves off at 3 m/s, find the velocity of the 60 kg skater (conservation of momentum in an "explosion").
4. Two train cars (5000 kg moving at 4 m/s, and 3000 kg at rest) collide and couple together (perfectly inelastic). Find their common final velocity.
5. A 0.5 kg ball moving at 6 m/s collides elastically head-on with a stationary 0.5 kg ball. What are the final velocities? (Hint: for equal masses in a 1D elastic collision, they exchange velocities.)
6. A firework at rest explodes into two pieces of equal mass. One piece flies off at 20 m/s. What is the velocity of the other piece?
7. A 2 kg cart moving at 3 m/s in the +x direction collides with a 1 kg cart moving at 4 m/s in the +y direction, and they stick together. Find the final velocity vector (magnitude and direction) — this requires conserving momentum separately in x and y.
8. Explain, using the impulse-momentum theorem, why a boxer "rolling with the punch" (moving their head back on impact) reduces injury compared to holding still.

## Real-World Applications

- **Car safety engineering:** crumple zones and airbags increase the collision time Δt, which reduces the peak force F for the same Δp (impulse-momentum theorem) — this is the core physics behind modern automotive safety
- **Sports:** "following through" in swinging a bat, racket, or golf club increases contact time and impulse delivered to the ball; landing with bent knees in gymnastics/basketball increases Δt to reduce force on joints
- **Rocket propulsion:** rockets conserve momentum by expelling mass (exhaust) in one direction to gain momentum in the other — this is the same conservation law used in the "explosion" problems above
- **Ballistic pendulum / forensics:** perfectly inelastic collision analysis is used to determine bullet speeds from the resulting swing of a block that catches the bullet
