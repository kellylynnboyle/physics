## 1. Momentum

| Cues / Questions | Notes |
|---|---|
| **What is momentum?** | Mass in motion: **p = mv**, in kg·m/s. It is a vector that points the same way as the velocity. |
| **How do I calculate the momentum of a 0.145 kg baseball moving at 40 m/s?** | p = mv = (0.145)(40) = **5.8 kg·m/s** in the direction it is moving. |
| **Why does the sign of momentum matter?** | Momentum has direction. Choose one direction as positive; anything moving the other way has negative momentum. Two identical carts moving toward each other at the same speed have a total momentum of **zero**. |
| **How is momentum different from kinetic energy?** | Momentum (mv) is a vector and goes with v. Kinetic energy (½mv²) is a scalar and goes with v². Doubling the speed doubles the momentum but quadruples the kinetic energy. |

> **IXL practice (goal: SmartScore 80+):** Physics **G.1** Conservation of momentum (the first problems build on p = mv)

## 2. Impulse

| Cues / Questions | Notes |
|---|---|
| **What is impulse?** | Force multiplied by the time it acts: **J = FΔt**, in N·s. On a force-time graph, impulse is the area under the curve. |
| **What does the impulse-momentum theorem say?** | Impulse equals change in momentum: **FΔt = Δp = m(v_f − v_i)**. |
| **A 50 N force acts for 0.20 s on a 0.50 kg ball at rest. How fast does it end up moving?** | Impulse = (50)(0.20) = 10 N·s = Δp. v_f = Δp ÷ m = 10 ÷ 0.50 = **20 m/s**. |
| **How do airbags and crumple zones reduce injury?** | The change in momentum is fixed (the car's speed down to zero). Lengthening the stopping time makes the force smaller: **bigger Δt, smaller F**. They do not reduce the impulse.<br>Worked: 1000 kg car, 20 m/s to 0, Δp = 20,000 kg·m/s. In 0.10 s: F = 200,000 N. In 0.50 s: F = **40,000 N**. |
| **Which receives the larger impulse: a ball that bounces off a wall or one that sticks?** | The one that **bounces**. Sticking: +mv to 0. Bouncing: +mv to −mv, up to twice the change.<br>Worked: 0.50 kg ball, +6.0 m/s to −4.0 m/s → Δp = 0.50(−4.0 − 6.0) = **−5.0 kg·m/s**. |

> **IXL practice:** no IXL Physics skill covers impulse alone. Use The Physics Classroom Concept Builders on impulse and momentum change.

## 3. Conservation of momentum

| Cues / Questions | Notes |
|---|---|
| **What does the law of conservation of momentum say?** | If no net external force acts on a system, its **total momentum before an interaction equals its total momentum after**. |
| **What is a system, and what makes a force "external"?** | The system is the set of objects you choose to track (for example, both carts). Forces between objects inside the system are internal; forces from outside (friction from the track, a hand) are external. |
| **Why is momentum conserved in a collision?** | By the third law the two objects push on each other with equal, opposite forces for the same time. So their impulses, and their changes in momentum, are equal and opposite and add to zero. |
| **A truck and a car collide. Which has the larger change in momentum?** | **Neither;** the changes are equal in size and opposite in direction. The car has the larger change in *velocity* because its mass is smaller. |
| **What steps solve a conservation problem?** | (1) Sketch before and after. (2) Choose a positive direction. (3) Write total p before = total p after. (4) Solve, then check the sign and size of the answer. |

> **IXL practice:** Physics **G.1** Conservation of momentum

## 4. Collisions and explosions

| Cues / Questions | Notes |
|---|---|
| **What are the types of collision?** | **Elastic:** objects bounce apart; momentum and kinetic energy both conserved. **Inelastic:** momentum conserved, some kinetic energy becomes thermal energy, sound, and deformation. **Perfectly inelastic:** the objects stick together (largest kinetic energy loss). |
| **Is momentum conserved in an inelastic collision?** | **Yes.** Momentum is conserved in every collision with no net external force. Only kinetic energy is not. |
| **A 2000 kg truck at 10 m/s hits a 1000 kg car at rest and they lock together. How fast do they move?** | Before: (2000)(10) + 0 = 20,000 kg·m/s. After: (3000)v′.<br>v′ = 20,000 ÷ 3000 = **6.7 m/s** forward.<br>Kinetic energy: 100,000 J before, 66,700 J after; about 33,300 J became thermal energy, sound, and bent metal. |
| **A 60 kg skater at rest throws a 2.0 kg ball forward at 15 m/s. What happens to the skater?** | Total momentum starts at zero and must stay zero: 0 = (2.0)(15) + (60)v → v = **−0.50 m/s** (backward). This is recoil, the same idea as a cannon or a rocket. |
| **After a cannon fires, how do the momenta of cannon and ball compare?** | **Equal in size, opposite in direction.** The cannon is slower only because it has more mass. |

> **IXL practice:** Physics **G.1** Conservation of momentum

## 5. Labs and engineering

| Cues / Questions | Notes |
|---|---|
| **How does a cart-collision lab test conservation of momentum?** | Measure each cart's mass and its velocity before and after the collision. Calculate total momentum before and after (with signs) and compare using percent difference. Friction is an external force, so expect a small loss. |
| **What makes a good collision-safety design?** | It increases the stopping time or distance (crumpling, padding, suspension) so that the force stays below what the object can survive, while meeting the limits on size, mass, and materials. Test, record what failed, change one feature, and test again. |

## Memory tricks

- **p = mv: "mass in motion."**
- **Impulse changes momentum: FΔt = mΔv.**
- **Bigger time, smaller force** (airbags, bent knees, soft hands).
- **Before = after:** total momentum, with signs, when no outside force acts.
- **Momentum is always conserved in collisions; kinetic energy only in elastic ones.**

## Summary

Momentum is mass times velocity and has direction. A force acting for a time delivers an impulse that changes momentum, which is why spreading a stop over a longer time reduces the force. Because interacting objects push on each other with equal and opposite forces for the same time, their momentum changes cancel, and the total momentum of a system with no net external force stays constant through any collision or explosion. Kinetic energy is conserved only in elastic collisions.
