# Cornell Notes — Momentum

## Page 1: Momentum & Impulse

| Cue | Notes |
|---|---|
| Momentum | p = mv. Vector — direction matters, same direction as velocity. |
| Impulse-momentum theorem | J = FΔt = Δp. Impulse delivered = change in momentum. |
| Impulse from a graph | Area under a force-vs-time graph = impulse. |
| Why airbags/crumple zones work | Δp (momentum change to stop) is fixed by mass and speed change. Since J = FΔt is fixed, a LONGER Δt means a SMALLER average force F. |

## Page 2: Conservation of Momentum

| Cue | Notes |
|---|---|
| When is momentum conserved? | When net EXTERNAL force on the system is ~zero (true during most collisions, since internal collision forces dominate over the short time). |
| Does this require an elastic collision? | NO — momentum conservation applies to BOTH elastic and inelastic collisions. |
| Sign convention | Pick a positive direction; velocities opposing it are negative. This is the #1 source of errors in 1D collision problems. |

## Page 3: Types of Collisions

| Cue | Notes |
|---|---|
| Elastic collision | Momentum conserved AND kinetic energy conserved. (Common in idealized/atomic-scale collisions.) |
| Inelastic collision | Momentum conserved, kinetic energy NOT conserved (lost to heat/sound/deformation). |
| Perfectly inelastic | Special case of inelastic — objects stick together, share one final velocity. Easiest to set up: m₁v₁ + m₂v₂ = (m₁+m₂)v_f. |
| Quick check | After solving any collision problem, ask: "Does KE before = KE after?" If not, it's inelastic — and that's fine, as long as momentum still balances. |
