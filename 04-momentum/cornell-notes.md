# Cornell Notes — Unit 4: Momentum

> **How to use:** Fill in the **Notes** column during class/reading, write **Cues** afterward,
> and summarize in your own words at the bottom.

| Cues / Questions | Notes |
|---|---|
| Define momentum | `p = mv`. A vector quantity — has both magnitude and direction, same direction as velocity. Units: kg·m/s. |
| What is impulse, and how does it relate to momentum? | `J = FΔt`. The impulse-momentum theorem states `J = Δp` — the impulse applied to an object equals its change in momentum. A small force over a long time can produce the same impulse as a large force over a short time. |
| State the law of conservation of momentum | In a closed/isolated system (no net external force), total momentum before an interaction equals total momentum after: `p_total,before = p_total,after`. |
| What does "closed/isolated system" mean here? | No external, unbalanced forces act on the system from outside (friction with the ground, air resistance, etc. are usually ignored or treated as negligible during the brief collision). |
| What's conserved in an elastic collision? | Both momentum AND kinetic energy are conserved. Objects bounce off each other without permanent deformation or heat loss. |
| What's conserved in an inelastic collision? | Only momentum is conserved. Kinetic energy is NOT conserved — some is transformed into heat, sound, or deformation of the objects. |
| What is a perfectly inelastic collision? | The extreme case of inelastic: the objects stick together and move with one common final velocity. Maximum kinetic energy is lost (though momentum is still conserved). |
| Does each individual object's momentum stay the same during a collision? | No — individual objects' momenta change (that's the whole point of a collision). It's the TOTAL momentum of the system that stays constant. |
| Why do signs matter in 1-D momentum problems? | Momentum is a vector. Objects moving in opposite directions have momenta with opposite signs, and you must add them algebraically (not just add magnitudes) to find total momentum. |
| How does impulse explain safety equipment (airbags, crumple zones, padding)? | For a given change in momentum (`Δp` is fixed by the crash), stretching out the time of impact (`Δt`) reduces the average force (`F = Δp/Δt`) needed, since `F·Δt` stays constant. |

## Quick Reference

| Quantity | Formula | Conserved in isolated system? |
|---|---|---|
| Momentum | `p = mv` | Always (elastic and inelastic) |
| Impulse | `J = FΔt = Δp` | — |
| Kinetic Energy | `KE = ½mv²` | Only in elastic collisions |

## Common Mistakes to Flag in Your Own Work

- [ ] Did I forget to assign signs to velocities moving in opposite directions?
- [ ] Did I assume kinetic energy is conserved in every collision (it's only elastic ones)?
- [ ] Did I apply conservation of momentum to a system with an unbalanced external force acting on it?
- [ ] Did I mix up an individual object's momentum change with the total system's (constant) momentum?
- [ ] Did I use the impulse-momentum theorem correctly, i.e., `FΔt = Δp`, not `F = p`?
