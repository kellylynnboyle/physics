# Cornell Notes — Momentum

**Topic:** Impulse, Collisions, and Conservation of Momentum | **Date:** ___________ | **Name:** ___________

| Cues / Questions | Notes |
|---|---|
| What is momentum? | p = mv. It's a **vector** — direction matters just as much as magnitude. A 2 kg ball moving left has different momentum than the same ball moving right at the same speed. |
| What is impulse, and how does it relate to momentum? | J = FΔt = Δp. Impulse is the change in momentum, caused by a force acting over a time interval. If force isn't constant, impulse = area under an F–t graph. |
| Why do airbags and crumple zones work? | They increase Δt (the time over which the person/car stops). Since Δp is fixed (you're stopping from some speed to zero), a bigger Δt means a smaller average force — F = Δp/Δt. |
| What's always conserved in a collision (isolated system)? | **Total momentum**, always — as long as there's no external net force. This is true for elastic, inelastic, AND explosions. |
| What's conserved only in ELASTIC collisions? | **Kinetic energy.** In inelastic collisions, some KE converts to heat, sound, or deformation — momentum is still conserved, but KE is not. |
| What does "perfectly inelastic" mean? | The objects stick together after colliding and move with one shared final velocity. Momentum is conserved: m₁v₁ + m₂v₂ = (m₁+m₂)v_f. |
| How do explosions fit conservation of momentum? | If an object starts at rest, total initial momentum = 0. After it splits, the pieces' momenta must sum to zero — meaning they have EQUAL magnitude and OPPOSITE direction momenta, not necessarily equal speeds (that depends on their masses). |
| How do I handle a 2D collision? | Apply conservation of momentum **separately** to the x-direction and the y-direction — treat each axis as its own 1D conservation problem, just like projectile motion splits x and y. |
| Common sign mistake? | Forgetting to assign a positive direction and treating a collision where one object bounces backward as if its momentum stayed positive — bouncing back means its momentum flips sign. |

## Summary

Momentum (p = mv) is a vector quantity, and the impulse-momentum theorem
(J = FΔt = Δp) explains why extending collision time reduces force — the
basis for airbags and crumple zones. Total momentum of an isolated system is
**always** conserved, whether the collision is elastic, inelastic, or an
explosion — but kinetic energy is only conserved in elastic collisions.
The most common mistakes are treating momentum as a scalar (dropping
direction/sign) and assuming kinetic energy is conserved in every
collision. For 2D problems, split momentum conservation into independent
x- and y-component equations, the same way projectile motion is split.
