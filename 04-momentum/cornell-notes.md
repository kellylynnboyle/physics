## Cues / Questions | Notes

| Cues / Questions | Notes |
|---|---|
| What is momentum, and what kind of quantity is it? | `p = mv`. It's a **vector** — direction matters. Two objects moving toward each other have momenta with opposite signs. |
| What is impulse? | `J = FΔt` — force applied over a time interval. Same units as momentum (kg·m/s = N·s). |
| What does the impulse-momentum theorem say? | `J = Δp`, so `FΔt = mΔv`. For a *fixed* change in momentum, a longer time (Δt) means a smaller required force (F), and vice versa. |
| Why do airbags/crumple zones/catching "with give" work? | They increase the time (Δt) over which momentum changes, which — for the same Δp — reduces the peak force on the person, per the impulse-momentum theorem. |
| When is total momentum conserved? | Whenever the **net external force** on a system is zero (an "isolated" or "closed" system). Forces objects exert *on each other* (internal forces) never change the total — they always come in equal-and-opposite pairs (Newton's 3rd law) that cancel in the total sum. |
| What's the #1 confused pair in this unit? | Momentum is conserved in **all** collisions (elastic or not), as long as the system is isolated. Kinetic energy is conserved **only** in elastic collisions. Don't assume KE is conserved just because momentum is. |
| Elastic vs. inelastic vs. perfectly inelastic — what's conserved in each? | **Elastic:** momentum AND KE both conserved (objects bounce apart). **Inelastic:** momentum conserved, KE decreases (converted to heat/sound/deformation). **Perfectly inelastic:** momentum conserved, KE loss is maximum, objects move together afterward with one shared final velocity. |
| How do I solve a perfectly inelastic collision? | Treat the stuck-together objects as one combined mass: `m₁v₁ + m₂v₂ = (m₁ + m₂)v_final`, solve for `v_final`. |
| What about explosions (objects starting together, flying apart)? | Same conservation law, run in reverse: total momentum before (often zero, if starting at rest) equals total momentum after — the pieces fly apart with momenta that sum back to the original total. |
| How do I handle 2D collisions? | Conserve momentum **separately** along the x-axis and y-axis — treat each direction as its own 1D conservation problem, exactly like 2D forces in Unit 3. |

## Summary

Momentum (`p = mv`) and impulse (`J = FΔt = Δp`) connect force, time, and
motion change — a small force over a long time can produce the same
momentum change as a large force over a short time, which is why
"increasing contact time" is a recurring real-world safety strategy.
Conservation of momentum holds for any isolated system regardless of
collision type, but conservation of kinetic energy does **not** — that
distinction (elastic vs. inelastic) is the single highest-value idea to
walk away from this unit with, since mixing it up is the most common
error on momentum assessments.
