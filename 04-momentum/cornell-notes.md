**Learning targets:** I can calculate momentum and impulse. I can use conservation of momentum to predict what happens in a collision.

**Essential question:** Why does making a collision last longer make it safer?

**How to use these notes:** cover the Notes column, answer each cue aloud, then check.

| Cues / Questions | Notes |
|---|---|
| **1. Momentum** | |
| What is momentum and what are its units? | **p = mv**, mass times velocity, in kg·m/s. It is a **vector** that points the same way as the velocity. |
| How can a slow truck have more momentum than a fast car? | Momentum depends on both mass and velocity. A 10,000 kg truck at 2 m/s (20,000 kg·m/s) has more than a 1000 kg car at 15 m/s (15,000 kg·m/s). |
| **2. Impulse** | |
| What is impulse? | **J = FΔt**, force times the time it acts, in N·s. It equals the area under a force-time graph. |
| What does the impulse-momentum theorem say? | **FΔt = Δp = mΔv.** The impulse on an object equals its change in momentum. |
| A 0.5 kg ball at rest is hit with an average force of 50 N for 0.2 s. How fast does it leave? | Impulse = 50 N × 0.2 s = 10 N·s = Δp.<br>Δv = Δp ÷ m = 10 ÷ 0.5 = **20 m/s**. |
| Why do airbags reduce injuries? | The change in momentum is the same with or without the airbag. The airbag makes the stopping **time longer**, so the **force is smaller** (F = Δp ÷ Δt). The same idea explains crumple zones, padded floors, and bending your knees when you land. |
| Which gets the bigger impulse from a wall: a ball that bounces back or clay that sticks? (Same mass and speed.) | The **bouncing ball**. Clay: Δp = 0 − mv, size mv. Ball: Δp = (−mv) − mv, size up to **2mv**, because its direction reverses.<br>Example: 0.5 kg at 10 m/s in, 10 m/s out: Δp = 0.5(−10 − 10) = −10 kg·m/s, not zero. |
| **3. Conservation of momentum** | |
| When is momentum conserved? | When the **net outside force on the system is zero**. The total momentum of all the objects together stays the same before and after a collision or explosion. |
| Why is it conserved? | Newton's third law: during the collision the objects push on each other with equal and opposite forces for the same time, so their momentum changes are equal and opposite and add to zero. |
| A 2 kg cart at 3 m/s hits and sticks to a 1 kg cart at rest. What is the final velocity? | Before: (2)(3) + (1)(0) = 6 kg·m/s.<br>After: (2 + 1)v′ = 6 → v′ = **2 m/s**.<br>Sense check: slower than 3 m/s because the same momentum is shared by more mass. |
| An 80 kg astronaut at rest throws a 2 kg wrench at 8 m/s. What happens to the astronaut? | Total momentum starts at zero and stays zero.<br>0 = (2)(8) + (80)v → v = −0.2 m/s. The astronaut moves at **0.2 m/s in the opposite direction**. |
| **4. Types of collisions** | |
| What is the difference between elastic and inelastic collisions? | **Elastic**: momentum and kinetic energy are both conserved (objects bounce apart with no lasting deformation).<br>**Inelastic**: momentum is conserved, some kinetic energy becomes thermal energy, sound, and deformation.<br>**Perfectly inelastic**: the objects stick together; the largest kinetic energy change. |
| Is momentum conserved even when kinetic energy is not? | **Yes.** Momentum is conserved in all collision types as long as no net outside force acts. Total *energy* is also conserved; it just changes form. |
| **5. Labs and measurement** | |
| In the cart collision lab, what do you compare? | Total momentum before (m₁v₁ + m₂v₂) with total momentum after. Record direction with signs. A difference under about 10% is consistent with conservation given friction and timing error. |

**Memory tricks**

- "Momentum is mass in motion."
- Same Δp, more time, less force.
- Draw a before picture and an after picture, with a velocity arrow on every object, before writing the equation.

**Summary**

Momentum (p = mv) is a vector that measures how hard it is to stop a moving object. A force acting over time delivers an impulse that changes momentum, so lengthening the stopping time lowers the force. Because the forces between colliding objects are equal and opposite, the total momentum of a system does not change during a collision or explosion, even when kinetic energy is transformed into other forms.
