# Project Outline & Master Checklist — 11th Grade Mechanics

This is the instructor/student-facing checklist for the whole project. Use it
to track progress unit by unit, confirm learning objectives are met, and see
how each unit is assessed.

## How to track progress

Copy this file (or the tables in it) per student, or use it as a class
tracker. Check off each row as it's completed. `[ ]` → `[x]`.

---

## Unit 1 — Kinematics

**Learning objectives** — the student can:
- [ ] Distinguish distance/displacement and speed/velocity, with correct sign conventions
- [ ] Calculate average velocity and average acceleration from data or a graph
- [ ] Apply the four constant-acceleration ("kinematic") equations to solve for an unknown
- [ ] Read position-time, velocity-time, and acceleration-time graphs (slope = rate, area = accumulated change)
- [ ] Solve 1-D free-fall problems, including "thrown upward" scenarios

**Assessment criteria**
- [ ] Cornell notes completed and self-checked against the summary
- [ ] Quiz score ≥ 80% on `01-kinematics/quiz.html` (retake until met)
- [ ] At least 5 of the practice problems in `01-kinematics/README.md` solved with work shown
- [ ] Can explain, in one sentence, why constant velocity means zero acceleration

---

## Unit 2 — Projectile Motion

**Learning objectives** — the student can:
- [ ] Explain why horizontal and vertical motion are independent
- [ ] Decompose an initial velocity into x- and y-components using launch angle
- [ ] Calculate time of flight, maximum height, and range for level-ground launches
- [ ] Solve projectiles launched from a height (e.g., off a cliff or table)
- [ ] Explain why complementary launch angles (e.g., 30° and 60°) give the same range

**Assessment criteria**
- [ ] Cornell notes completed and self-checked against the summary
- [ ] Quiz score ≥ 80% on `02-projectile-motion/quiz.html`
- [ ] At least 5 practice problems solved with work shown, including one "launched from a height" problem
- [ ] Can sketch x(t), y(t), vx(t), and vy(t) for a projectile launched at an angle

---

## Unit 3 — Newton's Laws of Motion

**Learning objectives** — the student can:
- [ ] State all three of Newton's laws in their own words
- [ ] Draw a correct free-body diagram for an object in a given scenario
- [ ] Apply F = ma to solve for acceleration, force, or mass in multi-force systems
- [ ] Correctly identify Newton's-third-law force pairs (same type of force, opposite objects)
- [ ] Distinguish mass from weight and explain how normal force can differ from weight
- [ ] Apply static vs. kinetic friction correctly, including on inclines

**Assessment criteria**
- [ ] Cornell notes completed and self-checked against the summary
- [ ] Quiz score ≥ 80% on `03-newtons-laws/quiz.html`
- [ ] At least 5 practice problems solved with a free-body diagram for each
- [ ] Can correctly identify the third-law pair for a given force and explain why it is *not* the pair students usually guess

---

## Unit 4 — Momentum & Collisions

**Learning objectives** — the student can:
- [ ] Calculate momentum (vector) and impulse, and relate them via the impulse-momentum theorem
- [ ] Apply conservation of momentum to isolated 1-D collision systems
- [ ] Distinguish elastic, inelastic, and perfectly inelastic collisions and know what is/isn't conserved in each
- [ ] Correctly assign signs to velocities/momenta based on direction in 1-D problems
- [ ] Explain why momentum can be conserved even when kinetic energy is not

**Assessment criteria**
- [ ] Cornell notes completed and self-checked against the summary
- [ ] Quiz score ≥ 80% on `04-momentum/quiz.html`
- [ ] At least 5 practice problems solved, including one elastic and one perfectly inelastic collision
- [ ] Can justify, with an equation, why momentum is conserved but KE is not in an inelastic collision

---

## Unit 5 — Work & Energy

**Learning objectives** — the student can:
- [ ] Calculate work done by a constant force, including cases where W = 0
- [ ] Apply the work-energy theorem to relate net work to change in kinetic energy
- [ ] Calculate gravitational PE, elastic PE, and apply conservation of mechanical energy
- [ ] Identify when non-conservative forces (friction, applied forces) remove/add mechanical energy
- [ ] Calculate power and distinguish it from work/energy

**Assessment criteria**
- [ ] Cornell notes completed and self-checked against the summary
- [ ] Quiz score ≥ 80% on `05-work-and-energy/quiz.html`
- [ ] At least 5 practice problems solved, including one with friction (non-conservative force) present
- [ ] Can explain in one sentence the difference between energy, work, and power (and their units)

## Full project completion checklist

- [ ] All 5 units' Cornell notes completed
