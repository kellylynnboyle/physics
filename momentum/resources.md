# Momentum — Practice Problems, Resources & Applications

## Practice problems

### Level 1 — Direct substitution
1. A 1200 kg car travels at 20 m/s. Find its momentum.
2. A 0.5 kg ball at rest is struck by a bat and leaves with a velocity of
   30 m/s. If the bat was in contact for 0.01 s, find the impulse
   delivered and the average force exerted by the bat.
3. A 60 kg skater at rest pushes off a 90 kg skater, also at rest. If the
   60 kg skater moves away at 3 m/s, find the velocity of the 90 kg
   skater.

### Level 2 — Multi-step / choose your equation
4. A 1000 kg car moving at 15 m/s east collides head-on with a 1500 kg
   car moving at 10 m/s west, and they stick together. Find their common
   velocity after the collision (magnitude and direction).
5. A 0.15 kg baseball moving at 40 m/s is hit back the way it came at
   50 m/s by a bat, with contact lasting 0.007 s. Find the impulse and
   the average force on the ball. (Careful with signs.)
6. Two carts on a frictionless track: Cart A (2 kg, 4 m/s) collides
   elastically with Cart B (2 kg, at rest). Using conservation of
   momentum and conservation of kinetic energy, find both carts'
   velocities after the collision.

### Level 3 — Recoil / graph-based
7. A 4 kg rifle fires a 0.02 kg bullet at 300 m/s. Find the recoil
   velocity of the rifle. Explain why the rifle's speed is so much
   smaller than the bullet's even though their momenta are equal in
   magnitude.
8. A force-time graph shows a force rising linearly from 0 N to 800 N
   over 0.02 s, then dropping linearly back to 0 N over the next 0.02 s
   (a triangular shape). Find the impulse delivered, and the change in
   velocity of a 2 kg object initially at rest.

### Challenge
9. A 1500 kg car moving at 12 m/s rear-ends a 1000 kg car moving at
   5 m/s in the same direction, and the bumpers lock (perfectly
   inelastic). (a) Find their common velocity after impact. (b) Compute
   the total kinetic energy before and after, and confirm kinetic energy
   was lost. (c) Explain in words where that energy went.

or the quiz in `quiz.html`.)*

## Real-world applications

- **Car crash safety — crumple zones and airbags**: both extend the
  collision time Δt for the same required Δp, lowering the average force
  on occupants (impulse-momentum theorem).
- **Rocket propulsion**: expelling exhaust mass backward at high speed
  gives the rocket forward momentum, conserving total momentum of the
  rocket-plus-exhaust system — the same principle as recoil.
- **Ballistics/recoil**: a fired gun recoils backward with momentum
  equal in magnitude to the bullet's forward momentum, but at a much
  smaller speed because of its much larger mass.
- **Sports**: billiards (elastic-like collisions transferring momentum
  between balls), bowling (pin momentum transfer), boxing/catching a
  ball with "give" (extending contact time to reduce force, the same
  idea as airbags).
- **Jet engines**: accelerate intake air backward, producing forward
  thrust by momentum conservation, similar in principle to rockets.

## Study tips for the most commonly missed question types

- **Momentum is a vector** — in any head-on problem, pick a positive
  direction first and give every velocity moving the other way a
  negative sign before you plug into an equation, not just its
  magnitude.
- **"Conserved" doesn't automatically mean kinetic energy too** —
  momentum is conserved in every isolated-system collision; kinetic
  energy is conserved only in elastic collisions. Check which the
  question is actually asking about before choosing an equation.
- **Don't forget the resting object's zero momentum** — a stationary
  target still belongs in the "before" total, as p = 0, not as a term
  you leave out.
- **Equal and opposite momenta ≠ equal and opposite velocities** — true
  only when masses are equal; otherwise the lighter piece moves faster.
