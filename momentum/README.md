# Unit 2: Momentum

The study of how mass and velocity combine into a conserved quantity —
momentum — and how that lets us analyze collisions and explosions without
needing to know the details of the forces involved.

## Key concepts students need to master

- **Momentum, p = mv** — a vector, with the same direction as velocity.
  Units: kg·m/s. Two objects can have equal speed but different momentum
  (different mass), or equal momentum but different speed.
- **Impulse, J = FΔt = Δp** (impulse-momentum theorem) — a net force
  applied over a time interval changes an object's momentum by exactly
  that impulse. For a fixed Δp, a longer Δt means a smaller average
  force, and vice versa.
- **Conservation of momentum** — for an isolated system (no net external
  force), total momentum before an interaction equals total momentum
  after: p_before(total) = p_after(total). This holds for *every*
  collision and explosion, elastic or not.
- **Elastic collisions** — kinetic energy IS conserved (in addition to
  momentum). Objects bounce apart; no energy lost to heat/sound/
  deformation.
- **Inelastic collisions** — kinetic energy is NOT conserved (some
  converts to heat, sound, or deformation), but momentum is still
  conserved. This includes most real-world collisions.
- **Perfectly inelastic collisions** — a special case of inelastic:
  objects stick together and move with one common final velocity.
  m₁v₁ + m₂v₂ = (m₁ + m₂)v_f.
- **Explosions/recoil** — the reverse of a perfectly inelastic collision:
  objects start together (often at rest, p_total = 0) and fly apart. The
  pieces must have equal and opposite momenta, not necessarily equal and
  opposite velocities (that only holds if masses are equal).
- **Momentum is a vector** — direction and sign matter. In 1D problems,
  pick a positive direction and assign velocities their correct sign,
  especially for objects moving toward each other.
- **Extending to 2D** — momentum conservation applies separately to each
  perpendicular component (p_x and p_y each conserved independently);
  covered briefly, using vector components rather than calculus.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this unit, students will be able to:

1. Calculate momentum and impulse given mass, velocity, force, and time,
   with correct units and direction.
2. Apply the impulse-momentum theorem to relate force, time, and change
   in momentum (e.g., explain why airbags and padding reduce injury by
   increasing collision time).
3. Apply conservation of momentum to solve 1D collision problems, both
   elastic and inelastic.
4. Distinguish elastic from inelastic collisions by comparing kinetic
   energy before and after.
5. Solve recoil/explosion problems using conservation of momentum,
   correctly relating momentum (not velocity) of the two pieces.
6. Correctly assign signs/directions to velocities in a collision
   problem, especially for head-on collisions.

## Assessment criteria

- **Quiz target:** ≥ 80% on `quiz.html`, with correct reasoning on the
  sign-convention and momentum-vs-kinetic-energy-conservation questions
  specifically (these are the most commonly missed).
- **Problem sets:** Full given/find/equation/solve/units setup shown, not
  just a final number, and explicit sign choices stated before plugging
  in values.
- **Mastery check:** Given a word problem describing a collision with no
  diagram, the student assigns a sign convention, correctly identifies
  whether momentum alone or momentum and kinetic energy are conserved,
  and sets up the equation without prompting.
