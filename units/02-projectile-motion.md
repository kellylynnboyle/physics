# Unit 2: Projectile Motion

**NGSS:** HS-PS2-1 | **Pacing:** ~3 weeks | **Prereq:** Unit 1, vector components

## Learning objectives
- [ ] **P1** Resolve a launch velocity into horizontal and vertical components.
- [ ] **P2** Explain that horizontal and vertical motions are independent.
- [ ] **P3** Solve horizontally launched projectile problems (time, range, impact velocity).
- [ ] **P4** Solve angled-launch problems (max height, time of flight, range).
- [ ] **P5** Analyze trajectory data from a lab or video.

## Key concepts and equations
No air resistance; only gravity acts, downward.

| Direction | Motion | Equations |
|---|---|---|
| Horizontal | constant velocity (a = 0) | x = vₓt |
| Vertical | constant acceleration (a = −g) | y = v_y0 t − ½gt², v_y = v_y0 − gt |
| Components | launch speed v at angle θ | vₓ = v cosθ, v_y0 = v sinθ |
| Range (level ground) | | R = v² sin2θ / g |
| Time in air (level ground) | | T = 2v sinθ / g |

## Cornell notes

| Cue | Notes |
|---|---|
| Why can we treat x and y separately? | Gravity acts only vertically, so horizontal velocity is unaffected. Time links the two. |
| What is the horizontal acceleration of a projectile? | Zero (ignoring air). vₓ stays constant the whole flight. |
| What is vertical velocity at the peak? | Zero. Acceleration is still 9.8 m/s² down. |
| Which hits first: dropped ball or ball fired horizontally from the same height? | Same time. Same vertical motion. |
| How do I split a launch velocity? | vₓ = v cosθ, v_y = v sinθ. Check: v = √(vₓ² + v_y²). |
| Which angle gives maximum range? | 45° on level ground. Complementary angles (30°/60°) give equal range. |
| How do I solve a projectile problem? | 1) Draw path. 2) Make an x column and a y column. 3) Solve y for time. 4) Use time in x. |
| How is speed at impact found? | Combine vₓ and v_y with Pythagoras; angle = tan⁻¹(v_y/vₓ). |

**Summary:** A projectile is two independent motions: constant velocity sideways, free fall vertically. Time of flight connects them.

## Common misconceptions
- A horizontal force is needed to keep the ball moving sideways (false).
- A faster horizontal launch falls more slowly (false, same fall time).
- Acceleration is zero at the peak (false).

## Practice problems
1. A ball rolls off a 19.6 m high table at 20 m/s. Time in air and range? **Ans: 2.0 s; 40 m**
2. A kicker launches a ball at 25 m/s at 37°. Find vₓ and v_y₀ (sin 37° ≈ 0.60, cos 37° ≈ 0.80). **Ans: 20 m/s; 15 m/s**
3. For problem 2, time of flight and range. **Ans: T = 2(15)/9.8 = 3.1 s; R = 20 × 3.06 ≈ 61 m**
4. Max height in problem 2. **Ans: v_y²/2g = 225/19.6 ≈ 11.5 m**
5. A stone is thrown horizontally from a 45 m cliff and lands 60 m out. Initial speed? **Ans: t = 3.03 s; v = 19.8 m/s**
6. Impact velocity for problem 1. **Ans: v_y = 19.6 m/s down; speed = √(400 + 384) ≈ 28 m/s at 44° below horizontal**

- **Lab:** Marble launch off a ramp; predict landing spot, then test (target challenge).

- Firefighting hose arcs; artillery and airplane drops; fountains.
- **Project:** Design a catapult hitting a target at a given distance; justify with calculations.
