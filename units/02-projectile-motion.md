# Unit 2 — Projectile Motion
**NGSS:** HS-PS2-1 (vector analysis) · **Time:** ~3 weeks · **Prereq:** Unit 1

## Learning objectives
- **P1** Treat horizontal and vertical motion independently; gravity acts only vertically.
- **P2** Solve horizontal-launch problems (time from vertical drop, range from vₓ·t).
- **P3** Resolve an angled launch into components; find time of flight, max height, and range.

## Key concepts
| Concept | Equation |
|---|---|
| Components | vₓ = v cosθ, v_y = v sinθ |
| Horizontal (a = 0) | x = vₓt |
| Vertical (a = −g) | y = y₀ + v_y t − ½gt², v_y = v_y0 − gt |
| Level-ground flight time | t = 2v_y0/g |
| Max height | h = v_y0²/(2g) |
| Range (level ground) | R = v²sin(2θ)/g; max at 45° (no air resistance) |
Assumptions: no air resistance; g constant; flat Earth over short range. Trajectory is a parabola.

| **Summary** | |

*Hints:* x and y are independent; aₓ = 0, a_y = −g always; both land together; use the vertical drop for t; complementary angles (e.g., 30°/60°) match, 45° max; at the peak speed = vₓ, not zero.

## Most-missed questions
1. Believing the horizontally launched ball stays up longer than a dropped one.
2. Velocity at the peak is **not** zero (only v_y is).
3. Acceleration at the peak is still g downward.
4. Using the total speed in x = vt instead of vₓ.
5. Forgetting the trig step (cos for x, sin for y) or using degrees-vs-radians mode errors.
6. Believing a heavier projectile travels farther (ignoring air resistance, mass is irrelevant).

## Practice problems
1. A ball rolls off a 1.2 m table at 3.0 m/s. Time in the air and landing distance? *(0.49 s; 1.5 m)*
2. A soccer ball is kicked at 20 m/s, 30° above horizontal. Time of flight, max height, range? *(2.04 s; 5.1 m; 35.3 m)*
3. A cliff diver leaves a 25 m cliff horizontally at 2.0 m/s. How far from the base does she land? *(4.5 m)*
4. At what speed must a ball be thrown horizontally from 1.5 m to land 12 m away? *(21.7 m/s)*
5. A cannon fires at 40 m/s at 45°. What is the speed at the peak? *(28.3 m/s)*
6. Show that a 30° and a 60° launch at the same speed have equal range.

## Real-world applications
Free throws and field goals, water fountains, long jump technique, firefighting nozzles, artillery/rocket ballistics, video-game physics, dropping supplies from aircraft.
**Lab idea:** Launch a marble off a ramp from a table; predict the landing spot from the measured exit speed, then place a cup to test it ("the target challenge").

## Assessment criteria
Level 3: separates x and y in a table; correct trig; units; reasonable answers. Level 4: solves non-level-ground launches or explains the effect of air resistance.
