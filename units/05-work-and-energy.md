# Unit 5: Work & Energy

**NGSS link:** HS-PS3-1 (computational model of energy changes), HS-PS3-2 (energy as motion of particles or position in fields), HS-PS3-3 (device converting energy), HS-PS3-4 (thermal equilibrium; connects to the second-semester units).
**Suggested pacing:** 3 weeks. **Prerequisites:** Units 1-4.

## Learning objectives (I can...)
- [ ] E1. Calculate work (W = Fd cos θ) and identify when work is zero, positive, or negative.
- [ ] E2. Calculate kinetic energy (½mv²), gravitational PE (mgh), and elastic PE (½kx²).
- [ ] E3. Apply the work-energy theorem: W_net = ΔKE.
- [ ] E4. Use conservation of mechanical energy with and without friction (energy bar charts).
- [ ] E5. Calculate power and efficiency (P = W/t).
- [ ] E6. Design and evaluate a device that converts energy (HS-PS3-3), reporting efficiency.

## Key concepts
| Concept | Essentials |
|---|---|
| Work | W = F d cos θ (joules); only the force component along displacement counts |
| Kinetic energy | KE = ½mv² |
| Potential energy | PE_g = mgh; PE_s = ½kx² |
| Work-energy theorem | W_net = ΔKE |
| Conservation | E_total constant; friction converts to thermal energy |
| Power | P = W/t = Fv (watts) |

## Cornell notes (fill-in format)
| Cue / Questions | Notes |
|---|---|
| When is work done? | A force acts through a displacement with a component along it. |
| When is work zero? | Force ⟂ displacement (carrying a box horizontally), or no displacement. |
| Why does doubling speed quadruple KE? | KE ∝ v². |
| Does the path matter for gravitational PE? | No, only the change in height. |
| What happens to energy with friction? | Mechanical energy transforms into thermal energy; total energy still conserved. |
| Work vs power? | Work = energy transferred; power = rate of transfer. |

## Practice problems
1. A 20 N force pulls a sled 15 m at 30° above horizontal. Find the work. *(≈ 260 J)*
2. A 1000 kg car going 20 m/s brakes to rest with μ = 0.8 friction. Find the stopping distance. *(≈ 25.5 m)*
3. A 0.5 kg ball is released from a 2 m ramp top (frictionless). Find speed at the bottom. *(≈ 6.3 m/s)*
4. A 60 kg student climbs stairs 4 m high in 5 s. Find the power. *(≈ 470 W)*
5. Challenge: A spring (k = 200 N/m) is compressed 0.1 m and launches a 0.05 kg cart. Find its speed. *(≈ 6.3 m/s)*

## Real-world applications
Regenerative braking in EVs, hydroelectric dams, roller coasters, stopping distance and speed limits, food Calories vs joules, solar and battery efficiency.
