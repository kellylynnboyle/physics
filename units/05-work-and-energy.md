## Cornell notes

| Cue / Questions | Notes |
|---|---|
| Work | W = Fd cosθ (joules). Only the force component along displacement does work. Perpendicular force (e.g., carrying a box level, circular motion) ⇒ W = 0. |
| Sign of work | Positive: adds energy to object; negative: removes (friction, braking). |
| Kinetic energy | KE = ½mv². Scalar. **Doubling v quadruples KE.** |
| Work–energy theorem | W_net = ΔKE. |
| Gravitational PE | PE = mgh (relative to chosen zero level). Only the height *change* matters. |
| Elastic PE | PE = ½kx² (spring constant k, stretch x). |
| Conservation of energy | In a closed system E_total is constant. If only conservative forces: KE + PE = constant ⇒ mgh = ½mv² (drop from rest). v = √(2gh), independent of mass and path. |
| Friction | Non-conservative: mechanical energy → thermal. Energy is not lost, just not recoverable as KE/PE. W_f = −fd. |
| Power | P = W/t = Fv (watts, 1 W = 1 J/s). |
| Efficiency | η = useful output / total input (always < 100%). |
| System models | Draw an energy bar chart / flow diagram before and after; choose system so energy flows are clear (HS-PS3-1,-2). |

## Practice problems
**Set A – Concepts**
1. Energy bar charts for a ball dropped, a ball thrown up, and a block sliding to a stop.
2. Does a satellite in circular orbit have work done on it by gravity? Why/why not?

**Set B – Calculations**
3. 20 N pushes a crate 5.0 m. Work? (100 J)
4. KE of a 2.0 kg object at 3.0 m/s? (9.0 J)
5. PE of 10 kg lifted 5.0 m? (490 J)
6. Ball dropped from 5.0 m: speed at bottom? (≈9.9 m/s)
7. A 600 J job takes 3.0 s. Power? (200 W)
8. Car (1000 kg) goes 10→20 m/s. Work done by engine? (150,000 J)

**Set C – Challenge**
9. A 2.0 kg block slides from rest down a 3.0 m-high ramp; speed at bottom is 6.0 m/s. Energy lost to friction? (≈22.8 J)
10. A 0.5 kg ball compresses a spring (k = 200 N/m) 0.10 m and is released horizontally on a frictionless surface. Speed? (2.0 m/s)
