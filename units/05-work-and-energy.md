# Unit 5: Work & Energy

**Standards:** HS-PS3-1 (computational model of energy changes in a system) · HS-PS3-2 (energy as motion of particles/objects or stored in fields/positions) · HS-PS3-3 (design a device that converts one form of energy into another) · Practices: Developing and Using Models, Using Mathematics, Designing Solutions · Crosscutting Concepts: Energy and Matter, Systems and System Models
**Suggested pacing:** 4 weeks · **Prerequisites:** Units 1–4 · **Quiz:** [`quizzes/index.html`](../quizzes/index.html) → Work & Energy

This unit also sets up Semester 2: electric potential energy, wave energy and nuclear energy all build on conservation of energy.

## 1. Key concepts to master
| Concept | Student-friendly statement |
|---|---|
| Work | W = Fd cos θ (joules). Only the force component along the motion does work. |
| Kinetic energy | KE = ½mv². |
| Gravitational PE | PE_g = mgh (relative to a chosen reference level). |
| Elastic PE | PE_s = ½kx². |
| Work–energy theorem | W_net = ΔKE. |
| Conservation of energy | In an isolated system total energy is constant; energy transforms and transfers, never disappears. Friction converts mechanical energy to thermal energy. |
| Power | P = W/t = Fv (watts). |
| Efficiency | Useful energy out ÷ energy in; always < 100% in real devices. |

## 2. Learning objectives
- **E1** Calculate work done by constant forces, including at an angle and when work is zero or negative.
- **E2** Calculate KE, PE_g and PE_s, and explain how they depend on mass, speed, height and stretch.
- **E3** Apply the work–energy theorem and conservation of mechanical energy to solve problems.
- **E4** Create energy bar charts / system diagrams that account for energy transfer, including thermal energy from friction.
- **E5** Compute power and efficiency for machines and everyday devices.
- **E6** Design and evaluate a device that converts one form of energy to another (HS-PS3-3), identifying where energy is "lost" to the surroundings.

## 3. Cornell notes

**Title:** Work and Energy · **Essential question:** *How does energy move and change form while the total stays the same?*

| Cue | Notes |
|---|---|
| What counts as work? | Force that acts through a displacement: W = Fd cos θ. Carrying a box level: no work by the lifting force (θ = 90°). Friction work is negative. |
| Kinetic energy? | KE = ½mv². Doubling v → KE ×4. |
| Potential energy? | Gravity: mgh. Spring: ½kx². Stored energy due to position/configuration. |
| Work–energy theorem? | W_net = KE_f − KE_i. Use it for stopping distance and braking. |
| Conservation of energy? | KE_i + PE_i + W_other = KE_f + PE_f. With no friction: KE + PE = constant. Mass cancels in free-fall/pendulum/roller-coaster speed. |
| Where did the energy go? | Friction → thermal energy; sound; deformation. Total is still conserved. |
| Power? | P = W/t; 1 W = 1 J/s. Horsepower ≈ 746 W. |
| Efficiency? | Efficiency = useful output / input × 100%. |

## 5. Practice problems
1. A 20 kg wagon is pulled 15 m with a 40 N rope force at 30° above the horizontal. Work by the rope? [≈ 520 J]
2. How much does the KE of a 1500 kg car change speeding from 10 m/s to 30 m/s? [600,000 J]
3. A 0.5 kg ball is thrown straight up at 12 m/s. Max height using energy? [≈ 7.3 m]
4. A 60 kg skier starts from rest 25 m above the bottom of a hill; 4000 J is lost to friction. Speed at the bottom? [mgh = 14,700 J; ½mv² = 14,700 − 4000 = 10,700 J → v ≈ 19 m/s]
5. A spring (k = 200 N/m) is compressed 0.15 m and launches a 0.10 kg cart on a frictionless track. Speed? [≈ 6.7 m/s]
6. A student lifts a 10 kg box 1.5 m in 2.0 s. Power output? [≈ 74 W]
7. A motor draws 500 W and lifts a 40 kg load 6.0 m in 10 s. Efficiency? [≈ 47%]
8. Draw energy bar charts (KE, PE_g, thermal) for a ball dropped, bouncing to a lower height. [rubric: total bar height constant; thermal increases]
9. *Challenge:* A 1000 kg car going 25 m/s brakes with constant friction 7000 N. Stopping distance, then compare with a 35 m/s start. [≈ 44.6 m; ≈ 87 m (about 2× for 1.4× speed)]

## 6. Real-world applications
- Roller coasters and ski jumps: potential ↔ kinetic conversions
- Hybrid/electric vehicles and regenerative braking
- Hydroelectric dams, wind turbines, solar panels (HS-PS3-3)
- Food Calories vs. joules; power consumption of household devices
- Stopping distance and speed limits (KE ∝ v²)

## 7. Lab / performance task
**Energy conservation lab:** Release a cart or ball from several heights on a ramp/loop track. Measure speed with photogates, compare ½mv² with mgh, and quantify the percent of energy dissipated. **Design challenge (HS-PS3-3):** Build a device (e.g., Rube Goldberg chain, solar oven, rubber-band car, hand-crank generator) that converts one form of energy into another; produce an energy-flow diagram and efficiency estimate.

## 8. Assessment criteria
| Level | Description |
|---|---|
| 4 – Advanced | Builds a quantitative energy model with friction/spring/gravity; evaluates efficiency data and proposes improvements. |
| 3 – Proficient (≥ 80%) | Calculates work, KE, PE, power; applies conservation and the work–energy theorem with correct units. |
| 2 – Developing | Uses formulas with prompts; confuses work with force or power with energy. |
| 1 – Beginning | Cannot identify energy forms or transformations. |
