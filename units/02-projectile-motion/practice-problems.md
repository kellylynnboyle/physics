# Practice Problems: Projectile Motion

Use g = 9.8 m/s², ignore air resistance, launches from level ground unless stated. Answer key at the bottom.

## Set A: Components and concepts (P1, P2)

1. Resolve a 40 m/s velocity at 30° above horizontal into components.
2. A ball has components vₓ = 12 m/s and v_y = 9.0 m/s. Find its speed and direction.
3. State the horizontal and vertical acceleration of a baseball (a) just after leaving the bat, (b) at the top of its flight, (c) just before landing.
4. A cannonball is fired horizontally while a second is dropped from the same height at the same instant. Which lands first? Explain.

## Set B: Horizontal launch (P3)

5. A ball leaves a 1.8 m high table at 12 m/s. How long is it in the air, and how far from the table does it land?
6. A projectile fired horizontally at 15 m/s from a cliff lands 30 m from the base. How tall is the cliff?
7. A plane flying level at 100 m/s drops a package from 500 m. How far horizontally does the package travel before hitting the ground?

## Set C: Angled launch (P4)

8. A soccer ball is kicked at 20 m/s at 30° above horizontal. Find the time of flight, maximum height, and range.
9. A football is thrown at 25 m/s at 40°. Find the range.
10. A ball is launched at 25° and lands at a certain range. What other launch angle (same speed) gives the same range?

## Set D: Challenge (extending)

11. A kicker sends a football at 22 m/s at 35°. A 3.0 m high crossbar is 40 m away. Does it clear the bar? (Hint: find the time to reach x = 40 m, then the height.)
12. A basketball is released from 2.0 m height at 8.0 m/s and 50°. The rim is 3.05 m high and 4.0 m away horizontally. Is the shot on target? (Compute y at x = 4.0 m.)
13. Explain, in terms of components, why throwing a ball at 45° from a cliff top does **not** give the longest range.

## Answer key

1. vₓ = 40 cos 30° = **34.6 m/s**; v_y = 40 sin 30° = **20 m/s**.
2. v = √(144 + 81) = **15 m/s**; θ = tan⁻¹(9/12) = **37° above horizontal**.
3. (a) aₓ = 0, a_y = 9.8 m/s² down; (b) same; (c) same. Acceleration never changes.
4. **Same time.** Both start with v_y₀ = 0 and have the same vertical acceleration, so the vertical motion is identical.
5. t = √(2 × 1.8/9.8) = **0.61 s**; x = 12 × 0.61 = **7.3 m**.
6. t = 30/15 = 2.0 s; h = ½(9.8)(4.0) = **19.6 m (about 20 m)**.
7. t = √(2 × 500/9.8) = 10.1 s; x = 100 × 10.1 = **1.0 × 10³ m** (about 1010 m).
8. v_y₀ = 10 m/s, vₓ = 17.3 m/s. t = 2(10)/9.8 = **2.04 s**; H = 100/19.6 = **5.1 m**; R = 17.3 × 2.04 = **35 m**.
9. R = 25² sin 80°/9.8 = 625 × 0.985/9.8 = **63 m**.
10. Complementary angle: **65°**.
11. vₓ = 22 cos 35° = 18.02 m/s; v_y₀ = 22 sin 35° = 12.62 m/s. t = 40/18.02 = 2.22 s. y = 12.62(2.22) − 4.9(2.22)² = 28.0 − 24.1 = **3.9 m** > 3.0 m, so it **clears the bar** (on the way down; the peak is at 1.29 s).
12. vₓ = 8 cos 50° = 5.14 m/s; v_y₀ = 8 sin 50° = 6.13 m/s. t = 4.0/5.14 = 0.778 s. y = 2.0 + 6.13(0.778) − 4.9(0.778)² = 2.0 + 4.77 − 2.97 = **3.80 m**. The ball is descending (the peak is at 0.63 s) but is still about 0.75 m above rim height when it reaches x = 4.0 m, so it passes over the rim: the shot is **long**, not on target.
13. With a launch point above the landing level, the extra fall time favors a flatter launch. The optimum is below 45° because lowering the angle trades some hang time for a larger horizontal component.
