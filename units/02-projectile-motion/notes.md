# Unit 2: Projectile Motion

**NGSS:** HS-PS2-1 (math and models) | **Quiz:** `../../quizzes/index.html` → Projectile Motion | **Pacing:** ~2 weeks

## Learning objectives (I can...)
1. Resolve a vector into horizontal and vertical components.
2. Explain that horizontal and vertical motions are independent for a projectile (no air resistance).
3. Calculate time of flight, maximum height, and range for horizontal and angled launches.
4. Predict where a projectile lands, then test the prediction in a lab and calculate percent error.

## Key concepts
- **Independence of x and y:** x: a = 0, constant vₓ. y: a = −9.8 m/s² (free fall).
- **Components:** vₓ = v cosθ, v_y = v sinθ.
- **Horizontal launch from height h:** t = √(2h/g), range R = vₓt.
- **Angled launch, same landing height:** t = 2v_y/g, R = vₓt = v² sin(2θ)/g, max height H = v_y²/(2g).
- **Range facts:** max at 45°; complementary angles (30° and 60°) give equal range; doubling launch speed quadruples range.
- **At the peak:** v_y = 0, vₓ ≠ 0, a = 9.8 m/s² down.

## Cornell notes

| Cue | Notes |
|---|---|
| What does "independent" mean for projectile motion? | Horizontal and vertical motion don't affect each other. Time connects them, so solve each direction and share t. |
| What is the horizontal acceleration of a projectile? | Zero (ignoring air). Horizontal velocity stays constant. |
| What is the vertical acceleration? | 9.8 m/s² downward the whole flight, including the peak. |
| A ball rolls off a table while another is dropped. Which lands first? | They land together; both start with v_y = 0 and fall the same height. |
| How do I split a launch velocity? | vₓ = v cosθ, v_y = v sinθ (θ above horizontal). 20 m/s at 30°: vₓ = 17.3 m/s, v_y = 10.0 m/s. |
| What is the velocity at the top of the path? | Purely horizontal (= vₓ). Not zero unless launched straight up. |
| How do I find time of flight? | Use the vertical motion only. Same-height launch: t = 2v_y/g. Horizontal launch: t = √(2h/g). |
| How do I find range? | R = vₓ · t, using the t from the vertical part. |
| Which launch angle gives maximum range? | 45° (same launch and landing height). Angles that add to 90° give equal range. |
| Worked example: ball leaves a 20 m cliff horizontally at 10 m/s. Where does it land? | y: 20 = ½(9.8)t² → t = √(40/9.8) = 2.02 s. x: R = 10(2.02) = 20.2 m. Sense check: fall time for 20 m is about 2 s ✔. |
| Worked example: launch with v_y = 14.7 m/s. Time in air? | Rise time = 14.7/9.8 = 1.5 s; total = 3.0 s (same landing height). H = 14.7²/(2·9.8) = 11.0 m. |

## Most-missed questions
1. Believing a horizontally thrown ball takes longer to land than a dropped one.
2. Saying velocity or acceleration is zero at the top.
3. Putting a horizontal force on the projectile ("the throw keeps pushing it").
4. Using the full launch speed instead of the component in the equation.
5. Thinking doubling speed doubles range (it quadruples it).
6. Mixing sin and cos when resolving components.

## Practice problems
1. Resolve 30 m/s at 40°. [vₓ = 23.0 m/s, v_y = 19.3 m/s]
2. A ball is kicked horizontally at 8.0 m/s from a 1.25 m wall. Time? Range? [0.505 s; 4.0 m]
3. A projectile is launched at 25 m/s at 53°. Flight time? [4.1 s, using v_y = 20 m/s]
4. For the launch in problem 3, range? [about 61 m using vₓ = 15 m/s × 4.08 s]
5. A cannonball fired at 45° has range 100 m. Range if speed is tripled? [900 m]
6. Which has the longer flight time at the same speed: 30° or 60°? [60°]
7. Draw velocity vectors at five points along a parabola. [vₓ same length; v_y shrinks, zero at top, then grows downward]
8. At what angle are two launches equal in range to a 25° launch? [65°]

## Labs
- **Launch-range lab:** use a spring launcher or ramp-launch. Measure launch speed with photogates, *predict* the landing spot at a given height with carbon paper, then test. Report percent error and sources of error (air drag, measuring angle).
- **Video analysis:** film a toss, use Tracker to plot x vs t (straight line) and y vs t (parabola).

## Assessment criteria
- **Proficient (3):** draws a diagram with components, solves y first to get t, then x, includes units, and states whether the result is reasonable.
- **Evidence:** quiz ≥ 80%, lab prediction within 10% of measured range, test items on time of flight and range correct.
