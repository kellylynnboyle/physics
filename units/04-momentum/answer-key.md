# Unit 4: Momentum — Answer Key (instructors)

## Quiz

| # | Answer | Why | Mistake this question targets | Objective |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **C.** They are equal | They push on each other with equal forces for the same time, so the impulses, and therefore the momentum changes, are equal in size and opposite in direction. | Confusing change in momentum with change in velocity. The car's velocity changes more. | |
| 2 | **B.** The ball | The ball's velocity reverses, so its change in velocity (and momentum) is nearly twice as large as the clay's, which only stops. | Thinking that stopping completely is the largest possible change. | |
| 3 | **C.** It increases the stopping time, which reduces the force | The change in momentum and the impulse are the same with or without the airbag. Spreading that impulse over a longer time makes the force smaller. | Saying the airbag reduces the impulse or the momentum change. | |
| 4 | **B.** Momentum is conserved; kinetic energy is not | Momentum is conserved in every collision without a net outside force. In a sticking collision some kinetic energy becomes heat, sound, and deformation. | Thinking momentum is lost when energy is lost. | |
| 5 | **C.** 2 m/s | Before: 2 × 3 = 6 kg·m/s. After: 3 kg × v = 6, so v = 2 m/s. | Averaging the two speeds without weighting by mass. | |
| 6 | **A.** Zero | The total was zero before and no outside force acted, so it is zero after. Their momenta are equal in size and opposite in direction. | Adding the two momenta as if they pointed the same way. | |
| 7 | **C.** 4 kg·m/s | Take toward the wall as positive: Δp = 0.5 × (−4 − 4) = −4 kg·m/s. The speed is unchanged but the direction reversed. | Ignoring direction and getting zero. | |
| 8 | **B.** 6 m/s | Impulse = 10 × 3 = 30 N·s = change in momentum. vf = 30 / 5 = 6 m/s. | Reporting the impulse (30) as the speed. | |
| 9 | **B.** The same size momentum and less speed | Momentum is conserved, so the two momenta are equal and opposite. The cannon's much larger mass means a much smaller speed. | Thinking the slower object must have less momentum. | |
| 10 | **B.** The stopping time is longer, so the force is smaller | The egg loses the same momentum either way. Stretching out the time of the catch lowers the force on the shell. | Saying the momentum change or impulse is reduced. | |

## Practice problems

1. Find the momentum of a 70 kg sprinter running at 9.0 m/s.  
   **Answer:** p = 70 × 9.0 = 630 kg·m/s in the direction of the run.
2. A 0.060 kg tennis ball arrives at 30 m/s and leaves the racket at 40 m/s in the opposite direction. Find the change in momentum. If contact lasts 0.0050 s, find the average force.  
   **Answer:** Δp = 0.060 × (−40 − 30) = −4.2 kg·m/s; F = 4.2 / 0.0050 = 840 N.
3. A 1500 kg car going 12 m/s stops in 4.0 s. Find the braking force.  
   **Answer:** Δp = 1500 × 12 = 18 000 kg·m/s; F = 18 000 / 4.0 = 4500 N opposite the motion.
4. A 3.0 kg cart moving at 4.0 m/s sticks to a 1.0 kg cart moving the same way at 2.0 m/s. Find their final velocity.  
   **Answer:** Before: 12 + 2 = 14 kg·m/s. After: 4.0·v. v = 3.5 m/s.
5. A 4.0 kg rifle fires a 0.010 kg bullet at 400 m/s. Find the recoil velocity of the rifle.  
   **Answer:** 0 = 0.010 × 400 + 4.0·v, so v = −1.0 m/s (backward).
6. Explain, using impulse, why a gymnastics mat is safer to land on than a wood floor.  
   **Answer:** The change in momentum is the same, but the mat lengthens the stopping time, so the average force is smaller.
