# Practice Problems: Momentum

Define the system and choose a positive direction in every problem. Answer key at the bottom.

## Set A: Concepts (M1, M2, M5)

1. Which has more momentum: a 1000 kg car at 2 m/s or a 0.010 kg bullet at 800 m/s? Which has more kinetic energy?
2. Explain why bending your knees when you land from a jump reduces the force on your legs.
3. A ball hits a wall at 4.0 m/s. Compare the impulse if it (a) sticks to the wall and (b) rebounds at 4.0 m/s.

## Set B: Impulse (M2, M5)

4. A 0.15 kg baseball is traveling at 40 m/s toward the batter. After a 0.0020 s contact, it leaves the bat at 50 m/s in the opposite direction. Find the impulse and the average force.
5. A 70 kg crash-test dummy goes from 15 m/s to rest. Find the average force if the stopping time is (a) 0.010 s (hitting the dashboard) and (b) 0.10 s (airbag).
6. A constant 20 N force acts on a 4.0 kg cart for 3.0 s. Find the change in velocity.

## Set C: Conservation (M3, M4)

7. A 1500 kg car moving at 20 m/s collides with a stationary 1000 kg car and they stick together. Find the final velocity and the KE lost.
8. A 2.0 kg cart at 3.0 m/s east collides head-on with a 1.0 kg cart at 4.0 m/s west and they stick. Find the final velocity.
9. A 2.0 kg ball at 5.0 m/s collides elastically with a stationary 2.0 kg ball. Find both velocities after.
10. A 60 kg skater and a 40 kg skater, both initially at rest, push apart. The 60 kg skater moves at 2.0 m/s. Find the speed of the other skater.
11. A 0.010 kg bullet moving at 400 m/s is fired from a 4.0 kg rifle. Find the recoil velocity of the rifle.

## Set D: Challenge (extending)

12. A 0.050 kg bullet is fired at 300 m/s into a 2.0 kg wooden block that is initially at rest on a frictionless surface and stays embedded. (a) Find the final speed. (b) How much KE is lost?
13. Design analysis: A force sensor records a peak of 900 N for your egg-drop device in a 0.020 s impact. You redesign and the peak drops to 300 N. If the impulse is the same, estimate the new impact time and explain which design change might have done this.

## Answer key

1. Car: p = 2000 kg·m/s. Bullet: p = 8.0 kg·m/s. **Car has more momentum.** KE car = ½(1000)(4) = 2000 J. KE bullet = ½(0.010)(640000) = 3200 J. **Bullet has more KE** (KE ∝ v²).
2. Bending your knees increases the stopping time, so for the same change in momentum (same landing speed, mass) the average force is smaller (F = Δp/Δt).
3. (a) Δp = 0 − m(4) → magnitude 4m. (b) Δp = m(−4) − m(4) → magnitude **8m**: twice the impulse for the rebound.
4. Take toward the batter as negative: Δp = 0.15[(+50) − (−40)] = 0.15(90) = **13.5 N·s**. F = 13.5/0.0020 = **6750 N** (about 6.8 × 10³ N).
5. Δp = 70(15) = 1050 kg·m/s in magnitude. (a) F = 1050/0.010 = **1.05 × 10⁵ N**. (b) F = 1050/0.10 = **1.05 × 10⁴ N**. Ten times smaller.
6. J = 20(3.0) = 60 N·s. Δv = 60/4.0 = **15 m/s**.
7. p = 1500(20) = 30000. v′ = 30000/2500 = **12 m/s**. KE_before = ½(1500)(400) = 300000 J. KE_after = ½(2500)(144) = 180000 J. **120 kJ lost** (1.2 × 10⁵ J).
8. East +: p = 2(3) + 1(−4) = 2. v′ = 2/3 = **0.67 m/s east**.
9. Equal masses, elastic, target at rest: the moving ball stops (**0 m/s**) and the other moves at **5.0 m/s**.
10. 60(2.0) = 40v → v = **3.0 m/s** in the opposite direction.
11. 0.010(400) = 4.0v → v = **1.0 m/s** backward (rifle).
12. (a) 0.050(300) = 15 = 2.05v → v = **7.3 m/s**. (b) KE_before = ½(0.050)(90000) = 2250 J. KE_after = ½(2.05)(7.32²) = 54.9 J. **About 2200 J lost** (97.6%).
13. Impulse same → F_peak × Δt (roughly, for the same-shaped pulse) is constant. 900 × 0.020 = 18 N·s; new time ≈ 18/300 = **0.060 s**, three times longer. Likely changes: a thicker/softer crush layer, longer crumple distance, or a design that deforms progressively. (The real pulse shape matters: a flatter, more constant-force pulse gives the lowest peak.)
