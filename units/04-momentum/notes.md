# Unit 4: Momentum and Impulse

**NGSS:** HS-PS2-2, HS-PS2-3 | **Quiz:** `../../quizzes/index.html` → Momentum | **Pacing:** ~3 weeks

## Learning objectives (I can...)
1. Define momentum (p = mv) and impulse (J = FΔt) and state that both are vectors.
2. Use the impulse-momentum theorem: FΔt = Δp.
3. Apply conservation of momentum to collisions and explosions in one dimension.
4. Classify collisions as elastic, inelastic, or perfectly inelastic using kinetic energy.
5. Design and evaluate a device that reduces collision force (HS-PS2-3).

## Key concepts
- **Momentum:** p = mv, unit kg·m/s, direction of velocity.
- **Impulse:** J = F_net Δt = Δp = mv_f − mv_i. Area under an F-t graph = impulse.
- **Conservation:** if the net external force on a system is zero, total p is constant: Σp_before = Σp_after.
- **Collision types:** elastic (momentum and KE conserved), inelastic (momentum conserved, KE lost), perfectly inelastic (objects stick, maximum KE lost). Momentum is conserved in all of them in an isolated system.
- **Reducing force:** same Δp over longer Δt → smaller average force (airbags, crumple zones, bending knees).

## Cornell notes

| Cue | Notes |
|---|---|
| What is momentum? | "Mass in motion": p = mv. Vector. Unit kg·m/s. 1000 kg car at 20 m/s: 20,000 kg·m/s. |
| What is impulse? | A force acting over a time: J = FΔt. Unit N·s = kg·m/s. It equals the change in momentum. |
| State the impulse-momentum theorem. | FΔt = Δp = m(v_f − v_i). Pay attention to signs: a bounce reverses direction, so Δp is bigger than for stopping. |
| Why do airbags and crumple zones help? | Same Δp, longer Δt, so average force F = Δp/Δt is smaller. Impulse stays the same; force drops. |
| Why does a ball that bounces get a bigger impulse than one that sticks? | Sticking: Δp = 0 − mv. Bouncing back: Δp = −mv − mv = −2mv (about twice as large). |
| When is momentum conserved? | When no net external force acts on the system. Internal forces (the collision itself) are equal and opposite, so they cancel in the total. |
| Elastic vs inelastic? | Elastic: KE same before and after. Inelastic: KE lost (sound, heat, deformation). Perfectly inelastic: objects stick together. Both conserve momentum. |
| How do I set up a collision problem? | 1) Sketch before/after. 2) Choose + direction. 3) Write Σp_before = Σp_after. 4) Solve. 5) Compare KE to classify. |
| Worked example: 2 kg at 3 m/s hits a 1 kg at rest and they stick | p_before = 2(3) = 6. After: (3 kg)v = 6 → v = 2 m/s. KE: 9 J → 6 J, so inelastic. |
| Worked example: 0.15 kg ball at 40 m/s stopped in 0.010 s | Δp = 0.15(0) − 0.15(40) = −6.0 kg·m/s. F = −6.0/0.010 = −600 N (opposite the motion). |
| Worked example: cannon 1000 kg fires a 10 kg ball at 100 m/s | Total p = 0 before. 0 = 10(100) + 1000 v → v = −1.0 m/s (recoil backward). |
| Two equal carts approach head-on at equal speeds. Total momentum? | Opposite signs cancel: p_total = 0. Momentum is a vector. |

## Most-missed questions
1. Forgetting that momentum is a vector (direction/sign).
2. Saying kinetic energy is conserved in every collision.
3. Believing the heavier object receives a larger force in a collision (third law: equal).
4. Thinking airbags reduce the momentum change (they reduce force by lengthening time).
5. Mixing up impulse and force.
6. Not treating bounce as Δp = 2mv.

## Practice problems
1. Momentum of an 80 kg runner at 5 m/s? [400 kg·m/s]
2. A 1500 kg car going 10 m/s is stopped by a 3000 N force. Time? [5.0 s]
3. A 0.50 kg ball moving 6 m/s hits a wall and rebounds at 4 m/s. Impulse? [5.0 N·s away from the wall]
4. A 4 kg cart at 5 m/s collides and sticks to a 6 kg cart at rest. Final v? [2.0 m/s]
5. In problem 4, KE lost? [50 J − 20 J = 30 J]
6. A 60 kg skater at rest pushes off a 40 kg skater; the 40 kg skater moves at 3 m/s. The 60 kg skater's speed? [2 m/s opposite]
7. Why does catching a ball with your hands moving backward hurt less? [Longer Δt, smaller F]
8. Sketch an F-t graph with peak force 100 N lasting 0.2 s as a triangle. Impulse? [10 N·s]

## Labs and design challenge
- **Collision carts:** use two dynamics carts with photogates or motion sensors for elastic (magnets/bumpers) and sticky (Velcro) collisions. Compute p and KE before and after.
- **Egg-drop / crumple-zone challenge (HS-PS2-3):** design a device to protect a raw egg from a 3 m drop. Constraints: mass and materials limits. Record criteria, tradeoffs, test results, and a revision.

- Airbags, seat belts, helmets, crumple zones, guardrails.

- Sports: follow-through in a bat or golf swing; catching and boxing technique.
- Car-accident reconstruction from skid marks and final positions.

## Assessment criteria
- **Proficient (3):** states which system is isolated, uses signed velocities, shows Σp before = after, classifies the collision with an energy check, and explains force reduction by Δt.
- **Evidence:** quiz ≥ 80%, collision lab shows momentum conserved within 10%, design challenge documented with test and revision.
