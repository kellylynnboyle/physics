# Unit 1 — Kinematics

**Standards:** Foundation for HS-PS2-1 (analyze data to support the claim that Newton's second law describes the mathematical relationship among net force, mass, and acceleration). Science practices: analyzing data, using mathematics.

## Learning objectives
Students can:
1. Distinguish distance/displacement and speed/velocity, scalar vs. vector.
2. Calculate average velocity and acceleration from data, and interpret the sign of each.
3. Read and sketch position–time, velocity–time, and acceleration–time graphs; use slope and area.
4. Apply the constant-acceleration equations to solve for unknowns.
5. Model free fall as constant acceleration of 9.8 m/s² downward.

## Key concepts and formulas

| Concept | Definition / formula | Units |
|---|---|---|
| Displacement | Δx = x_f − x_i (vector) | m |
| Average velocity | v̄ = Δx / Δt | m/s |
| Average speed | total distance / Δt (scalar) | m/s |
| Acceleration | a = Δv / Δt | m/s² |
| Constant-a equations | v = v₀ + at; Δx = v₀t + ½at²; v² = v₀² + 2aΔx; Δx = ½(v₀+v)t | |
| Free fall | a = −g = −9.8 m/s² (up positive) | m/s² |
| Graph rules | slope of x–t = velocity; slope of v–t = acceleration; area under v–t = displacement | |

## Cornell notes

| Cues (write questions here) | Notes |
|---|---|
| What is the difference between distance and displacement? | Distance = path length (scalar). Displacement = straight line from start to end with direction (vector). Round trip: displacement = 0. |
| Speed vs. velocity? | Speed = distance/time. Velocity = displacement/time and has direction. |
| What does acceleration mean? | Any change in velocity: speeding up, slowing down, or turning. Constant velocity means a = 0 no matter how fast. |
| When is an object speeding up? | When v and a point the same direction (same sign). Slowing down when opposite signs. |
| How do I read an x–t graph? | Steep slope = fast. Flat = at rest. Curve = accelerating. Negative slope = moving in negative direction. |
| How do I read a v–t graph? | Slope = a. Area between graph and axis = displacement. Crossing the axis = changing direction. |
| What is special about free fall? | Only gravity acts: a = 9.8 m/s² down for rising, falling, and at the top. Mass does not matter without air drag. |
| How to choose an equation? | List knowns (v₀, v, a, Δx, t) and the unknown; pick the equation missing the variable you neither know nor want. |

**Summary (write in your own words, 3 sentences):** _Kinematics describes motion using displacement, velocity, and acceleration. Graph slopes and areas connect the three quantities. For constant acceleration, four equations let me solve any one unknown from three knowns._

## Common misconceptions (most-missed quiz topics)
- "Fast means accelerating." No: constant velocity has zero acceleration.
- "Zero velocity means zero acceleration." No: at the top of a throw, v = 0 but a = −9.8 m/s².
- "Negative acceleration means slowing down." Depends on the sign of velocity.
- "Average velocity of a round trip is the average speed." Displacement is zero, so average velocity is zero.
- "Heavy objects fall faster." Without air resistance, all fall with the same acceleration.

## Practice problems (answers in brackets)
1. A student walks 40 m east, then 30 m west in 35 s. Find distance, displacement, average speed, average velocity. [70 m; 10 m east; 2.0 m/s; 0.29 m/s east]
2. A car goes from 10 m/s to 25 m/s in 5.0 s. Find a and the distance covered. [3.0 m/s²; 87.5 m]
3. A cyclist brakes from 12 m/s to rest over 24 m. Find a. [−3.0 m/s²]
4. A rock is dropped from a 45 m cliff. How long to land, and how fast? [3.03 s; 29.7 m/s]
5. A ball is thrown straight up at 15 m/s. Max height and total time in air? [11.5 m; 3.06 s]
6. Sketch v–t and a–t graphs for an x–t graph that is a parabola opening upward. [v–t: straight line sloping up; a–t: positive constant]
7. From a v–t graph that rises from 0 to 8 m/s in 4 s then stays constant for 6 s, find total displacement. [16 m + 48 m = 64 m]
8. Challenge: A train at 30 m/s passes a stopped car which starts accelerating at 3 m/s² at that instant. When does the car catch the train? [20 s]

## Real-world applications
- Vehicle safety: stopping distance grows with the *square* of speed (v² = 2aΔx), the basis of speed limits near schools.
- Sports analytics: sprint start times, split speeds, pitch speed from video.
- Traffic engineering: yellow-light timing uses reaction time plus braking distance.
- Spaceflight: launch and landing burns apply the same equations with changing a.

## Lab idea: Motion graphs
Use a cart on a track with a motion sensor or a phone video analyzed in Tracker (free). Produce x–t, v–t, a–t graphs for (a) constant velocity and (b) a cart on a slight incline. Use slope to measure a, compare with Δv/Δt, and discuss sources of error.
