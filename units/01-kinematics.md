# Unit 1: Kinematics

**Standards:** HS-PS2-1 (analyze data to support Newton's second law; kinematic description is the foundation) · Science & Engineering Practices: Analyzing and Interpreting Data, Using Mathematics · Crosscutting Concept: Cause and Effect
**Suggested pacing:** 3 weeks · **Quiz:** [`quizzes/index.html`](../quizzes/index.html) → Kinematics

## 1. Key concepts to master
| Concept | Student-friendly statement |
|---|---|
| Scalar vs. vector | Distance and speed have size only; displacement and velocity also have direction. |
| Position, displacement | Displacement Δx = x_f − x_i (a straight-line change, not the path length). |
| Velocity | v = Δx/Δt. Slope of a position–time graph. |
| Acceleration | a = Δv/Δt. Slope of a velocity–time graph. Points opposite to velocity when slowing down. |
| Constant-acceleration equations | v = v₀ + at · Δx = v₀t + ½at² · v² = v₀² + 2aΔx |
| Free fall | Near Earth's surface a = 9.8 m/s² downward for every object when air resistance is negligible. |
| Graph interpretation | x–t slope = v; v–t slope = a; v–t area = Δx. |

## 2. Learning objectives (students can…)
- **K1** Distinguish distance/displacement and speed/velocity, and compute each from a scenario.
- **K2** Construct and interpret position–time and velocity–time graphs, including slope and area.
- **K3** Choose and apply the correct constant-acceleration equation from a list of knowns.
- **K4** Explain why free-fall acceleration does not depend on mass and solve free-fall problems.
- **K5** Collect motion data (motion sensor, video analysis or stopwatch), graph it and describe the motion in words and equations.

## 3. Cornell notes (blank-cue version to hand out; instructors keep the filled version)

**Title:** Kinematics — Describing Motion · **Date:** ____ · **Essential question:** *How can we describe motion with words, graphs and equations?*

| Cue column (students write questions / keywords) | Notes column |
|---|---|
| What's the difference between distance and displacement? | Distance = path length (scalar). Displacement = Δx, straight line from start to finish (vector). Lap around a track: distance 400 m, displacement 0. |
| How do I get velocity from a graph? | v = slope of x–t. Steeper = faster. Flat = at rest. Negative slope = moving in the negative direction. |
| What is acceleration really? | Rate of change of VELOCITY: a = Δv/Δt (m/s²). Speeding up if a and v point the same way; slowing down if opposite. |
| Which equation do I use? | List knowns (v₀, v, a, Δx, t), find the missing one, pick the equation that has your 3 knowns + 1 unknown. |
| Free fall? | a = −9.8 m/s² (down) all the way up, at the top and on the way down. v = 0 at the top, a ≠ 0. |
| Area under v–t? | Displacement. Trapezoid/triangle/rectangle areas. |

## 5. Practice problems (answers in brackets, for instructors; remove before handing out)
1. A cyclist rides 3.0 km east, then 4.0 km north. Find the distance and the magnitude of displacement. [7.0 km; 5.0 km]
2. A car goes from 10 m/s to 25 m/s in 5.0 s. Find a and the distance traveled. [3.0 m/s²; 87.5 m]
3. A sprinter accelerates from rest at 2.5 m/s² for 4.0 s. Final velocity and distance? [10 m/s; 20 m]
4. A stone is dropped from a 80 m cliff. Time to land and impact speed? (g = 9.8) [≈4.0 s; ≈40 m/s]
5. A ball is thrown straight up at 14 m/s. Max height and total time in the air? [10 m; ≈2.9 s]
6. Sketch the v–t graph for a car that speeds up, cruises, then brakes to a stop; shade the region that equals total displacement. [rubric: trapezoid, labeled axes with units]
7. *Challenge:* A train slows from 30 m/s to a stop with a = −1.5 m/s². Does it stop before a crossing 320 m ahead? [d = 300 m; yes, with 20 m to spare]

## 6. Real-world applications
- Traffic-safety design: stopping distance vs. speed limits and yellow-light timing
- Sports analytics: sprint start acceleration, high-jump hang time
- Spacecraft launch and landing profiles, elevator ride comfort
- Accident reconstruction from skid marks

## 7. Lab / performance task
**Motion-sensor lab:** Walk a motion to match given x–t and v–t graphs, then design and record a "story" graph for a classmate to interpret. Alternative: **free-fall timing** with phone slow-motion video, using the video to measure *g* and report percent error.

## 8. Assessment criteria
| Level | Description |
|---|---|
| 4 – Advanced | Moves flexibly among graphs, equations and words; solves multi-stage problems (e.g., two-car chase); explains graph-area reasoning without prompting. |
| 3 – Proficient (≥ 80%) | Correctly applies equations with units and sign conventions; interprets slope and area on graphs. |
| 2 – Developing | Computes with prompts; frequently confuses velocity/acceleration or sign. |
| 1 – Beginning | Needs one-on-one support to identify knowns/unknowns. |
