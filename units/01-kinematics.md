# Unit 1 — Kinematics

**Standards:** Foundational to NGSS HS-PS2-1 (quantitative description of motion, prerequisite to relating force and motion). [CDE NGSS reference](https://www.cde.ca.gov/pd/ca/sc/ngssstandards.asp)

Kinematics describes **how** something moves — position, velocity, and
acceleration over time — without yet asking **why** (that's Newton's Laws,
Unit 3).

## Key concepts

- Position, distance, and displacement (scalar vs. vector)
- Speed vs. velocity (scalar vs. vector)
- Acceleration and its direction relative to velocity
- The four constant-acceleration ("big four") kinematics equations
- Graphical analysis: position-time and velocity-time graphs
- Free fall (g = 9.8 m/s²)
- Significant figures, units, and reference frames

## Cornell notes

| Cue | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Distance vs. Displacement** | Distance = total path length traveled (scalar, always positive). Displacement = straight-line change in position (vector, can be +, −, or 0). *Example:* walk 5 m east then 5 m west → distance = 10 m, displacement = 0 m. |
| **Speed vs. Velocity** | Speed = distance / time (scalar). Velocity = displacement / time (vector, has direction). |
| **Acceleration** | a = Δv / Δt (m/s²). Direction matters: acceleration in the *same* direction as velocity speeds an object up; acceleration *opposite* velocity slows it down — regardless of positive/negative sign convention. |
| **The Big Four Equations** (constant a) | v = v₀ + at  ·  Δx = v₀t + ½at²  ·  v² = v₀² + 2aΔx  ·  Δx = ½(v₀ + v)t |
| **Graphs** | On an x-t graph: slope = velocity. On a v-t graph: slope = acceleration, and the area under the curve = displacement. A curved x-t line means velocity is changing (acceleration is present). |
| **Free Fall** | Near Earth's surface, all objects accelerate downward at g = 9.8 m/s², independent of mass (ignoring air resistance). |

**Summary:** Master the scalar/vector distinction first — it's the root of
most kinematics errors. Then practice reading slope and area off motion
graphs until it's automatic; a huge share of test points live there.

## Practice problems

1. A cyclist accelerates from 2 m/s to 14 m/s in 6 s. Find the acceleration and the distance traveled.
2. A ball is thrown straight up at 20 m/s. Find the time to reach maximum height and the maximum height reached.
3. A car's position-time graph shows a straight line from (0 s, 0 m) to (4 s, 40 m), then a horizontal segment to (8 s, 40 m). Describe the motion in each segment and find the average velocity over the full 8 s.
4. An object decelerates from 30 m/s to rest over 100 m. Find the acceleration (use v² = v₀² + 2aΔx).
