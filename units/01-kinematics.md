# Unit 1: Kinematics

**NGSS:** HS-PS2-1 (foundation) | **Pacing:** ~4 weeks

## Learning objectives
- [ ] **K1** Distinguish distance/displacement and speed/velocity (scalar vs vector).
- [ ] **K2** Calculate average velocity and acceleration from data.
- [ ] **K3** Interpret position-time and velocity-time graphs (slope, area).
- [ ] **K4** Choose and apply the constant-acceleration equations.
- [ ] **K5** Describe free fall (a = 9.8 m/s² downward) including at the top of a throw.
- [ ] **K6** Collect motion data in a lab and use it to find acceleration.

## Key concepts and equations
| Idea | Equation |
|---|---|
| Average velocity | v = Δx / Δt |
| Acceleration | a = Δv / Δt |
| Velocity after time t | v = v₀ + at |
| Displacement | Δx = v₀t + ½at² |
| No-time equation | v² = v₀² + 2aΔx |
| Average-velocity form | Δx = ½(v₀ + v)t |

Graph rules: slope of x-t = velocity; slope of v-t = acceleration; area under v-t = displacement.

## Cornell notes

| Cue (answer aloud) | Notes |
|---|---|
| What is the difference between distance and displacement? | Distance = path length (scalar). Displacement = straight line from start to end with direction (vector). A full lap: distance 400 m, displacement 0. |
| What does the slope of a position-time graph tell you? | Velocity. Steeper = faster; negative slope = moving backward; flat = at rest. |
| What does a curved x-t graph mean? | Velocity is changing, so the object accelerates. |
| What do slope and area of a v-t graph tell you? | Slope = acceleration. Area = displacement (area below the axis counts negative). |
| When does an object speed up? | When velocity and acceleration have the **same sign**. Opposite signs = slowing down. |
| What is the acceleration of a ball at the top of its flight? | 9.8 m/s² down. Velocity is 0 for an instant, acceleration is not. |
| How do I pick a kinematics equation? | List knowns (v₀, v, a, Δx, t). Pick the equation missing the unknown you do not need. |
| What is free fall? | Only gravity acts. a = −9.8 m/s² (up positive). Mass does not matter without air resistance. |

**Summary (write your own, then compare):** Motion is described by position, velocity, and acceleration. Graphs and the five constant-acceleration equations connect them. Direction (sign) matters for velocity and acceleration.

## Common misconceptions (feed the quiz)
- Acceleration is zero when velocity is zero (false, e.g. top of a throw).
- Negative acceleration always means slowing down (false, depends on velocity's sign).
- Average speed is the average of two speeds (false unless times are equal).

## Practice problems
1. A car accelerates from rest at 3.0 m/s² for 6.0 s. Find final velocity and distance. **Ans: 18 m/s; 54 m**
2. A runner goes 100 m east then 40 m west in 20 s. Find distance, displacement, average speed, average velocity. **Ans: 140 m; 60 m east; 7.0 m/s; 3.0 m/s east**
3. A train slows from 30 m/s to 10 m/s over 200 m. Find acceleration. **Ans: −2.0 m/s²** (v² = v₀² + 2aΔx: 100 = 900 + 400a)
4. A rock is dropped from a cliff and hits the water in 3.0 s. How high is the cliff? **Ans: 44 m** (½·9.8·9 = 44.1)
5. A ball is thrown up at 19.6 m/s. Time to peak, max height? **Ans: 2.0 s; 19.6 m**
6. From a v-t graph: v rises 0 to 8 m/s in 4 s, then constant for 6 s. Total displacement? **Ans: 16 + 48 = 64 m**

## Real-world applications and labs
- **Lab:** Cart on a ramp with a motion sensor or phone video + Tracker. Plot x-t, v-t; find a from the slope.
- Car safety: stopping distance grows with v² (double the speed, four times the braking distance).
- Traffic lights and yellow-light timing, sports timing (0-60 mph, 100 m dash splits).
- **Mini-project:** record your walk to school/bus stop with a phone app; build graphs and narrate them.
