## Learning Objectives
Students will be able to:

- [ ] Distinguish distance/displacement and speed/velocity, and identify which are vectors.
- [ ] Calculate average velocity and acceleration from data, tables, and graphs.
- [ ] Interpret position–time and velocity–time graphs (slope and area).
- [ ] Apply the constant-acceleration equations to solve one-dimensional problems, including free fall (g = 9.8 m/s²).
- [ ] Use sign conventions to describe direction of velocity and acceleration.

## Key Concepts
- Position, distance, displacement
- Speed vs. velocity
- Acceleration (rate of change of velocity)
- Graphs: slope of x–t = velocity; slope of v–t = acceleration; area under v–t = displacement
- Equations: v = v₀ + at; Δx = v₀t + ½at²; v² = v₀² + 2aΔx
- Free fall and g = 9.8 m/s²
- Reference frames and sign conventions

## Cornell Notes — Kinematics: Describing Motion

| Cue / Question | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Distance vs. displacement?** | Distance = total path length (scalar, ≥ 0). Displacement = Δx = x_final − x_initial (vector, has direction, can be 0 or negative). |
| **Speed vs. velocity?** | Speed = distance/time (scalar). Velocity = displacement/time (vector). A lap around a track: average velocity = 0, average speed ≠ 0. |
| **What is acceleration?** | a = Δv/Δt (m/s²). Any change in velocity — speeding up, slowing down, OR turning. Sign of a alone does not tell you speeding up or slowing down: compare signs of v and a. |
| **How do I read motion graphs?** | x–t: slope = velocity. v–t: slope = acceleration, area = displacement. a–t: area = change in velocity. Flat x–t line = at rest; flat v–t line = constant velocity. |
| **Which equation do I use?** | List knowns (v₀, v, a, Δx, t). Pick the equation missing the unknown you do not need: v = v₀+at (no Δx); Δx = v₀t+½at² (no v); v² = v₀²+2aΔx (no t). Valid ONLY for constant a. |
| **Free fall?** | Ignoring air resistance, every object accelerates at g = 9.8 m/s² downward, regardless of mass. At the top of a toss v = 0 but a is still 9.8 m/s² down. |

**Summary (write in your own words):** Kinematics describes motion without asking what causes it. Position, velocity, and acceleration are linked by slopes and areas on graphs and by three constant-acceleration equations. Always define a positive direction and track signs.

## Practice Problems

1. A car speeds up from 0 to 20 m/s in 5.0 s. Find its acceleration and the distance covered.
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>a = 4.0 m/s²; Δx = ½at² = 50 m.</details>
2. A stone is dropped from rest from a bridge and hits the water in 3.0 s. How high is the bridge, and how fast is the stone going on impact?
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>Δx = ½(9.8)(3²) ≈ 44 m; v = 9.8×3 ≈ 29 m/s.</details>
3. A runner goes 100 m east then 40 m west in 20 s. Find distance, displacement, average speed, and average velocity.
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>140 m; 60 m east; 7.0 m/s; 3.0 m/s east.</details>
4. Sketch the v–t graph for a ball thrown straight up and caught at the same height. What is the slope? What is the total area?
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>A straight line sloping down with slope −9.8 m/s² (crossing zero at the top); total signed area = 0 (displacement 0).</details>
5. A train moving at 30 m/s brakes uniformly at −2.0 m/s². How far does it travel before stopping?
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>Δx = (0 − 30²)/(2×−2.0) = 225 m.</details>

## Most-Missed Questions (interactive version in `site/index.html`)

1. A car accelerates uniformly from rest to 20 m/s in 5 s. How far does it travel in those 5 s?  
   A) 20 m; B) 50 m; C) 100 m; D) 4 m  
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>**B** — Average velocity is (0+20)/2 = 10 m/s, so Δx = 10 × 5 = 50 m. Using v × t = 100 m wrongly assumes the car was at top speed the entire time.</details>
2. A ball is thrown straight up. At the very top of its path, which is true?  
   A) v = 0 and a = 0; B) v = 0 and a = 9.8 m/s² downward; C) v = 9.8 m/s and a = 0; D) v = 0 and a = 9.8 m/s² upward  
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>**B** — Gravity acts the entire flight. Velocity is momentarily zero, but it is still changing, so a is 9.8 m/s² down.</details>
3. The area under a velocity–time graph represents:  
   A) Acceleration; B) Displacement; C) Speed; D) Force  
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>**B** — Area = v × t = displacement. The slope (not the area) of a v–t graph is acceleration.</details>
4. A runner completes one full lap of a 400 m track. Her displacement is:  
   A) 400 m; B) 200 m; C) 0 m; D) Cannot be determined  
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>**C** — Displacement compares end position to start position. She finished where she started, so Δx = 0 even though distance = 400 m.</details>
5. A cart has velocity −3 m/s and acceleration −2 m/s². The cart is:  
   A) Slowing down; B) Speeding up; C) At rest; D) Moving at constant speed  
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>**B** — v and a have the same sign (both negative), so speed increases. Negative acceleration does NOT automatically mean slowing down.</details>
6. A rock is dropped from rest. How far does it fall in 2.0 s? (g = 9.8 m/s²)  
   A) 9.8 m; B) 19.6 m; C) 39.2 m; D) 4.9 m  
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>**B** — Δx = ½gt² = ½(9.8)(2²) = 19.6 m. Falling distance grows with t², not t.</details>
7. A driver travels 60 km at 60 km/h and then another 60 km at 40 km/h. What is the average speed for the whole trip?  
   A) 50 km/h; B) 48 km/h; C) 52 km/h; D) 100 km/h  
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>**B** — Total distance 120 km; total time 1 h + 1.5 h = 2.5 h; 120/2.5 = 48 km/h. Averaging the two speeds only works if equal TIMES are spent at each speed.</details>
8. Ignoring air resistance, a 10 kg bowling ball and a 1 kg ball are dropped from the same height. Which hits the ground first?  
   A) The 10 kg ball; B) The 1 kg ball; C) They land together; D) Depends on the shape only  
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>**C** — Both accelerate at g regardless of mass. Mass cancels when comparing gravitational force and inertia.</details>
