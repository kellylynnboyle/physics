# Unit 1: Kinematics

**Standards:** supports HS-PS2-1 (data analysis and mathematics practices). **Essential question:** How can we describe motion precisely with words, graphs and equations, and use that description to predict where an object will be?

## Learning objectives
- [ ] K1 Distinguish distance/displacement and speed/velocity.
- [ ] K2 Define acceleration and predict speeding up, slowing down or turning from the signs of v and a.
- [ ] K3 Read and sketch position-time and velocity-time graphs; use slope and area.
- [ ] K4 Solve constant-acceleration problems with the four kinematic equations.
- [ ] K5 Model free fall with a = 9.8 m/s^2 downward.
- [ ] K6 Collect motion data and fit a graph or equation.

## Key concepts and equations
| Concept | Definition / equation | Unit | Type |
|---|---|---|---|
| Distance d | Total path length | m | scalar |
| Displacement Δx | x_final - x_initial | m | vector |
| Speed | distance / time | m/s | scalar |
| Velocity | v = Δx / Δt | m/s | vector |
| Acceleration | a = Δv / Δt | m/s^2 | vector |
| Kinematic 1 | v = v0 + a t | | no Δx |
| Kinematic 2 | Δx = v0 t + ½ a t^2 | | no v |
| Kinematic 3 | v^2 = v0^2 + 2 a Δx | | no t |
| Kinematic 4 | Δx = ½ (v + v0) t | | no a |
| Free fall | a = -9.8 m/s^2 (up positive) | | air resistance ignored |

Graph rules: on x-t, **slope = velocity**. On v-t, **slope = acceleration** and **area under the curve = displacement**.

## Cornell notes
| Cues / Questions | Notes |
|---|---|
| What is the difference between distance and displacement? | **Distance** is the length of the path (scalar). **Displacement** is the straight-line change in position with direction (vector). A runner who finishes a 400 m lap has distance 400 m and displacement 0. |
| What is the difference between speed and velocity? | Speed = distance/time (no direction). **Velocity** = displacement/time (has direction). Constant speed around a curve is still changing velocity. |
| What does acceleration measure, and what are its units? | Rate of change of **velocity**: a = Δv/Δt, in m/s^2. A change in speed *or* direction is an acceleration. |
| When does an object speed up, and when does it slow down? | Speeds up when v and a have the **same sign**; slows down when they have **opposite signs**. Negative a does not always mean slowing down. |
| What does the slope of a position-time graph tell you? | Velocity. Steeper = faster; horizontal = at rest; negative slope = moving in the negative direction. |
| What do slope and area mean on a velocity-time graph? | Slope = acceleration. Area between the line and the axis = displacement (area below the axis is negative). Horizontal line = constant velocity, a = 0. |
| Which kinematic equation do I choose? | List the 5 variables (Δx, v0, v, a, t). Pick the equation that has the three known values and the one unknown, and leaves out the variable you do not have. |
| What is true at the top of a free-fall path? | v = 0 for an instant but a is still 9.8 m/s^2 downward. Up and down trips take equal time and the object returns with the same speed. |
| Worked: a car goes 5 → 25 m/s in 4 s. Find a and the distance. | a = Δv/Δt = (25-5)/4 = **5 m/s^2**. Δx = ½(v + v0)t = ½(30)(4) = **60 m**. Sense check: average speed 15 m/s × 4 s = 60 m. |
| Worked: a rock is dropped and hits the water in 3.0 s. How high is the bridge, and how fast is it moving at impact? | Down is positive, v0 = 0, a = 9.8. Δx = ½ a t^2 = ½(9.8)(9) = **44 m**. v = a t = 9.8 × 3.0 = **29 m/s**. |

**Memory tricks:** "**GUESS**": Given, Unknown, Equation, Substitute, Solve (and check units). "**Slope-Area**": on v-t graph slope gives a, area gives Δx. "**Same sign speeds up**" for v and a.

**Summary (write your own, then compare):** Kinematics describes motion without asking what causes it. Position, velocity and acceleration are linked by slope and area on graphs and by four equations that hold when acceleration is constant. Always set a positive direction and keep signs consistent.

## Practice problems (answers at the end)
1. A runner covers 100 m in 12.5 s. Find the average speed.
2. A car moving at 30 m/s brakes uniformly at 6.0 m/s^2. Find the stopping time and distance.
3. A ball is thrown straight up at 14.7 m/s. Find the time to the top, the maximum height, and the total time in the air.
4. A v-t graph rises from 0 to 10 m/s in 5 s, stays at 10 m/s for 10 s, then falls to 0 in 5 s. Find the total displacement and the acceleration in each stage.
5. A cyclist's position is x = 2 + 3t (m, s). Find the velocity and the position at t = 4 s.
6. A student says "the ball at the top of its flight has zero acceleration." Explain the error.

**Answers:** (1) 8.0 m/s. (2) t = 5.0 s; Δx = v^2/(2a) = 900/12 = 75 m. (3) t_up = 1.5 s; H = v^2/(2g) = 216.09/19.6 ≈ 11 m; total 3.0 s. (4) ½(5)(10) + (10)(10) + ½(5)(10) = 150 m; a = +2, 0, -2 m/s^2. (5) v = 3 m/s; x = 14 m. (6) Velocity is zero at the top but gravity still acts, so a = 9.8 m/s^2 downward.

## Real-world applications
- Stopping distance and speed limits near schools (distance grows with the square of speed).
- Sports analytics: sprint start acceleration, GPS and speed tracking.
- Traffic engineering: yellow-light timing, ramp merge lengths.
- Elevators and rides: smooth acceleration limits for comfort.
- Spacecraft launches and landings use the same equations with changing acceleration.

## Lab ideas
- **Motion detector walk:** match a given x-t graph; then predict the v-t graph.
- **Cart on a ramp:** measure a for several angles; graph a vs sin(θ).
- **Reaction-time ruler drop:** use Δx = ½ g t^2 to find reaction time.
- **Video analysis:** record a dropped ball and measure g with Tracker.
