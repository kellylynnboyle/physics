# Unit 1: Kinematics

**NGSS link:** HS-PS2-1 (analyze data to support F = ma; motion graphs). Kinematics is the prerequisite toolkit for the dynamics standards.
**Suggested pacing:** 2-3 weeks

## Learning objectives (I can...)
- [ ] K1. Distinguish distance/displacement and speed/velocity; use vectors and sign conventions.
- [ ] K2. Define acceleration as change in velocity over time and predict its sign from motion.
- [ ] K3. Interpret and draw x-t, v-t, and a-t graphs (slope and area meanings).
- [ ] K4. Solve constant-acceleration problems with the kinematic equations.
- [ ] K5. Model free fall (a = 9.8 m/s² down) and explain why mass does not matter without air resistance.
- [ ] K6. Collect and analyze motion data (cart/ramp or video analysis) and report uncertainty.

## Key concepts
| Concept | Essentials |
|---|---|
| Position, displacement | Δx = x_f − x_i, a vector |
| Velocity | v = Δx/Δt; slope of x-t |
| Acceleration | a = Δv/Δt; slope of v-t |
| Kinematic equations | v = v₀ + at; Δx = v₀t + ½at²; v² = v₀² + 2aΔx |
| Free fall | a = −9.8 m/s² (down), at all points of the flight |

## Cornell notes (fill-in format)
| Cue / Questions | Notes |
|---|---|
| What is the difference between distance and displacement? | Distance: total path length (scalar). Displacement: change in position, with direction (vector). |
| What does the slope of an x-t graph tell me? | Velocity. Steeper = faster; negative slope = moving in the negative direction. |
| What does the area under a v-t graph tell me? | Displacement (areas below the axis are negative). |
| When do v and a point the same way? | Speeding up: same direction. Slowing down: opposite directions. |
| Which equation do I use? | List knowns (x, v₀, v, a, t). Pick the equation that has the unknown and omits the one you don't have. |
| What is special about free fall? | a = 9.8 m/s² downward the whole time, including at the top where v = 0. |
| **Summary (write in your own words)** | _Students: 3-4 sentences linking graphs, equations, and the meaning of acceleration._ |

## Practice problems
1. A cyclist goes from 4 m/s to 10 m/s in 3 s. Find a and the distance covered. *(2 m/s², 21 m)*
2. A stone is dropped from a 45 m bridge (use 9.8 m/s²). Find the fall time and impact speed. *(3.0 s, 30 m/s)*
3. Sketch the v-t and a-t graphs for a ball tossed up and caught at the same height.
4. A car brakes from 25 m/s with a = −5 m/s². Find the stopping distance. *(62.5 m)*
5. Challenge: Two cars start 100 m apart and drive toward each other; write position equations and find when they meet.

## Real-world applications
- Traffic safety: stopping distance and following distance, speed limits.
- Sports: sprint starts, 0-60 mph car specs, elevator comfort limits.
- Data from phones: accelerometer apps (phyphox) show a(t) for a real ride.

## Lab
