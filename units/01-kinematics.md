# Unit 1 — Kinematics
**NGSS:** HS-PS2-1 (motion data), math practices · **Time:** ~4 weeks

## Learning objectives
- **K1** Distinguish distance/displacement and speed/velocity (scalar vs. vector).
- **K2** Interpret position–time and velocity–time graphs (slope, area).
- **K3** Choose and apply the constant-acceleration equations.
- **K4** Analyze free fall (g ≈ 9.8 m/s², downward, independent of mass without air resistance).

## Key concepts
| Concept | Definition | Equation / unit |
|---|---|---|
| Displacement | Change in position (vector) | Δx = x_f − x_i (m) |
| Average velocity | Displacement per time | v = Δx/Δt (m/s) |
| Acceleration | Rate of change of velocity (vector) | a = Δv/Δt (m/s²) |
| Constant-a equations | | v = v₀ + at · Δx = v₀t + ½at² · v² = v₀² + 2aΔx · Δx = ½(v₀+v)t |
| Graphs | x–t slope = velocity; v–t slope = acceleration; v–t area = displacement | |

*Answer key hints:* displacement is net change with direction; slope of x–t = velocity (curve → changing velocity); area under v–t = displacement; same signs → speeding up; no t → v² = v₀² + 2aΔx; no Δx → v = v₀ + at; at the top v = 0 but a = −9.8 m/s².

## Most-missed questions (also in the interactive quiz)
1. **Zero velocity ≠ zero acceleration** (top of the toss). Misconception: acceleration is "how fast it's moving."
2. **Sign of acceleration vs. speeding up.** Speeding up/slowing down depends on whether v and a point the same way.
3. **Reading v–t graphs:** slope is acceleration, area is displacement; a line crossing zero means a direction change, not a stop in acceleration.
4. **Average speed for a round trip** is total distance ÷ total time, not the average of two speeds.
5. **Heavier objects fall faster** (not without air resistance).
6. **Unit slips:** km/h → m/s (÷3.6).

## Practice problems
1. A car goes from 0 to 27 m/s in 6.0 s. Find a and the distance traveled. *(4.5 m/s²; 81 m)*
2. A ball is thrown straight up at 20 m/s. Max height? Time to return? *(20.4 m; 4.08 s)*
3. A cyclist rides 12 km at 24 km/h then 12 km at 12 km/h. Average speed? *(16 km/h)*
4. A train brakes uniformly from 30 m/s to rest in 150 m. Find a and stopping time. *(−3.0 m/s²; 10 s)*
5. Sketch the v–t graph for a ball dropped, hitting the floor, and bouncing up (choose down as negative).
6. A stone dropped from a bridge hits the water in 2.5 s. Height and impact speed? *(30.6 m; 24.5 m/s)*

## Real-world applications
Traffic-light timing and stopping distances (reaction time + braking), runway length for aircraft, sprint start analysis, rideshare/GPS speed estimates, elevator ride comfort.
**Lab idea:** Buggy or cart motion with a ruler and phone timer; build x–t and v–t graphs, fit a line, and compare the slope to a stopwatch speed. Free-fall lab: measure g from drop heights.

## Assessment criteria
Level 3 (Proficient): correct sign conventions; every problem shows givens/equation/units; graphs have labeled axes and slope units; ≥80% on quiz. Level 4: derives or justifies equations; explains graph features in terms of forces.
