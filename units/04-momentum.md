## Key concepts and equations
| Idea | Equation |
|---|---|
| Momentum | p = mv (kg·m/s) |
| Impulse | J = FΔt = Δp = mv_f − mv_i |
| Conservation (isolated system) | Σp_before = Σp_after |
| Perfectly inelastic | m₁v₁ + m₂v₂ = (m₁ + m₂)v' |
| Kinetic energy | KE = ½mv² (conserved only in elastic collisions) |

## Cornell notes

| Cue | Notes |
|---|---|
| What is momentum? | Mass × velocity; a vector pointing with the velocity. |
| What is impulse? | Force × time. Equals change in momentum. Units N·s = kg·m/s. |
| Why do airbags help? | Same Δp, longer Δt, so smaller average force. |
| When is momentum conserved? | When no net external force acts on the system (collision forces are internal). |
| How do I set up a collision problem? | Pick a positive direction. Write p before and after for each object with signs. Set sums equal. |
| Elastic vs inelastic? | Momentum conserved in both. KE conserved only in elastic. Stick-together = perfectly inelastic (maximum KE loss). |
| What happens in a rebound? | Δp is larger than for stopping because velocity reverses: Δp = m(v_f − v_i) with v_f negative. |
| How does a rocket or recoil work? | Explosion in an isolated system: total p stays 0, so pieces move in opposite directions with m₁v₁ = −m₂v₂. |

**Summary:** Momentum measures how hard it is to stop an object. Forces over time change it (impulse). In an isolated system total momentum stays constant, even when KE does not.

## Common misconceptions
- Momentum is lost in inelastic collisions (only KE is).
- Momentum is a scalar, so opposite motions add.
- A rebound gives the same impulse as stopping (it gives more).
- The bigger object exerts more force in a collision (equal and opposite).

## Practice problems
1. Momentum of a 1500 kg car at 20 m/s? **Ans: 30 000 kg·m/s**
2. The car stops in 4.0 s. Average force? **Ans: 7 500 N** (30 000/4)
3. A 2 kg cart at 6 m/s hits and sticks to a 4 kg cart at rest. Final speed and KE lost? **Ans: 2 m/s; KE 36 J → 12 J, lost 24 J**
4. A 0.50 kg ball hits a wall at 4.0 m/s and bounces back at 4.0 m/s. Impulse magnitude? **Ans: 4.0 N·s**
5. A 60 kg skater at rest throws a 3.0 kg ball at 10 m/s. Skater's recoil speed? **Ans: 0.50 m/s opposite**
6. Two 1 kg carts head toward each other at 3 m/s and stick. Final velocity? **Ans: 0**
7. An egg stops in 0.002 s on concrete vs 0.05 s on a pillow, Δp = 0.1 kg·m/s. Compare forces. **Ans: 50 N vs 2 N**
