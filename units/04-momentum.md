# Unit 4 — Momentum
**NGSS:** HS-PS2-2, HS-PS2-3 · **Time:** ~3 weeks · **Prereq:** Unit 3

## Learning objectives
- **M1** Calculate momentum and impulse; relate impulse to a change in momentum.
- **M2** Apply conservation of momentum to collisions and explosions (1-D).
- **M3** Design and evaluate a device that reduces collision force by increasing contact time.

## Key concepts
| Concept | Equation |
|---|---|
| Momentum | p = mv (kg·m/s, vector) |
| Impulse | J = FΔt = Δp |
| Conservation | If ΣF_ext = 0: Σp_before = Σp_after |
| Elastic | momentum and KE conserved |
| Inelastic | momentum conserved, KE lost; perfectly inelastic = stick together |
| Force and time | F_avg = Δp/Δt → longer Δt, smaller F |

*Hints:* p is a vector, KE is not; impulse = area under F–t; same Δp but longer time → smaller force; system with no external net force; KE is lost in inelastic collisions but momentum is not; choose + direction first.

## Most-missed questions
1. Momentum is conserved in every collision — but KE isn't (inelastic).
2. A bounce gives a larger impulse than a stick for the same incoming speed.
3. Equal and opposite forces → equal changes in momentum, different changes in velocity.
4. Treating momentum as a scalar (sign errors).
5. Confusing impulse (FΔt) with force.
6. Believing a bigger object always "wins" a collision (forces are equal).

## Practice problems
1. A 0.145 kg baseball at 40 m/s is caught and stopped in 0.010 s. Average force? *(580 N)*
2. A 1200 kg car at 10 m/s hits a stationary 800 kg car and they stick. Final speed? *(6.0 m/s)*
3. A 2 kg cart at 3 m/s hits a 1 kg cart at rest elastically. Final velocities? *(1 m/s; 4 m/s)*
4. A 60 kg skater pushes off a 40 kg skater, both initially at rest. The 60 kg skater moves at 2 m/s. Speed of the other? *(3 m/s opposite)*
5. A 0.5 kg ball hits a wall at 6 m/s and rebounds at 4 m/s. Impulse? *(5 N·s away from the wall)*
6. Explain with F = Δp/Δt why you bend your knees when landing.

## Real-world applications
Airbags, helmets, crumple zones, catching a ball, rocket thrust, sports collisions, billiards, forensic crash reconstruction.
**Project (HS-PS2-3):** Design an egg-drop or cart-crash device with constraints (mass, materials, budget). Measure drop height, stopping time, and estimate average force; iterate twice and justify with F = Δp/Δt.

## Assessment criteria
Level 3: states system and sign convention; conserves p correctly; units. Level 4: classifies collisions by KE change and evaluates a design with a quantitative force estimate.
