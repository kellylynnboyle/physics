# Unit 4 — Momentum

**Standards:**
- HS-PS2-2 Use mathematical representations to support the claim that the total momentum of a system of objects is conserved when there is no net force on the system.
- HS-PS2-3 Apply scientific and engineering ideas to design, evaluate, and refine a device that minimizes the force on a macroscopic object during a collision.
- Connects to HS-ETS1-2, HS-ETS1-3 (design and trade-offs).

## Learning objectives
Students can:
1. Calculate momentum (p = mv) and treat it as a vector.
2. Relate impulse to change in momentum (FΔt = Δp) and interpret force–time graphs.
3. Apply conservation of momentum to elastic, inelastic, and explosion/recoil problems.
4. Classify collisions by what is conserved (momentum always; kinetic energy only if elastic).
5. Design and evaluate a device that reduces collision force by increasing contact time.

## Key concepts and formulas

| Concept | Formula |
|---|---|
| Momentum | p = mv (kg·m/s) |
| Impulse | J = FΔt = Δp = m(v_f − v_i) |
| Area under F–t graph | impulse |
| Conservation (isolated system) | Σp_before = Σp_after |
| Perfectly inelastic | m₁v₁ + m₂v₂ = (m₁+m₂)v′ |
| Explosion / recoil | 0 = m₁v₁ + m₂v₂ ⇒ opposite momenta |
| Elastic collision | momentum and KE both conserved |

## Cornell notes

| Cues | Notes |
|---|---|
| What is momentum? | "Mass in motion." p = mv, a vector pointing along velocity. Same p can come from heavy/slow or light/fast. |
| What is impulse? | Force × time of contact. It equals the change in momentum. |
| Why do airbags work? | Same Δp, longer Δt ⇒ smaller average force. Also crumple zones, helmets, catching a ball by giving with it. |
| When is momentum conserved? | When no net external force acts on the system. Internal forces (collision forces) are equal and opposite and cancel. |
| How do I set up a collision problem? | 1) Pick a positive direction. 2) Write p before for each object with signs. 3) Write p after. 4) Set sums equal. |
| Types of collisions? | Elastic: bounce, KE conserved. Inelastic: some KE becomes heat/sound/deformation. Perfectly inelastic: stick together, most KE lost. |
| Bounce vs. stick? | Bouncing reverses velocity, so Δp is bigger (up to 2mv) than stopping (mv). Bounce = bigger impulse. |
| What about recoil? | Start at rest ⇒ total p = 0. Thrown object and thrower get equal and opposite momentum. |

**Summary:** _Momentum is mass times velocity. Impulse changes momentum, so extending the collision time reduces force. In an isolated system total momentum is conserved in every collision, even when kinetic energy is not._

## Common misconceptions
- "Momentum and kinetic energy are the same thing."
- "Kinetic energy is conserved in all collisions."
- Dropping the signs (momentum is a vector).
- "The bigger object exerts a bigger force in a collision." (Equal.)
- "A bouncing ball receives less impulse than a sticking one."

## Practice problems
1. Compare momentum: 1000 kg car at 20 m/s vs. 2000 kg car at 10 m/s. [both 20,000 kg·m/s]
2. A 0.15 kg ball hits a bat at 40 m/s and leaves at 50 m/s the other way, contact 2 ms. Find impulse and average force. [13.5 N·s; 6750 N]
3. A 2 kg cart at 4 m/s hits and sticks to a 2 kg cart at rest. Final speed? KE lost? [2 m/s; 16 J − 8 J = 8 J]
4. A 3 kg cart at 2 m/s collides head-on with a 1 kg cart at −4 m/s and they stick. [v′ = 0.5 m/s]
5. A 50 kg skater at rest throws a 5 kg ball at 10 m/s. Recoil speed? [1 m/s]
6. A 1200 kg car at 15 m/s stops in 0.1 s (wall) vs. 0.5 s (crumple zone). Average forces? [180,000 N; 36,000 N]
7. A 4 kg object at 3 m/s hits a stationary 2 kg object. After, the 4 kg has 1 m/s forward. Find the other speed. [4 m/s]. Is this elastic? [KE before 18 J; after 2 + 16 = 18 J — yes]
8. Design task (HS-PS2-3): Build a package that protects a raw egg from a 2 m drop using ≤ 30 g of tape and 10 sheets of paper. Estimate impact force reduction from extended stopping time.

## Real-world applications
- Vehicle safety: airbags, crumple zones, seat belts, guardrails.
- Sports: follow-through in golf/tennis, boxing gloves, catching with "soft hands", helmets.
- Space and rockets: thrust via momentum exchange, satellite docking.
- Forensics: reconstructing collisions from skid marks and final positions.
- Medicine: head-impact thresholds in concussion research.

## Lab idea: Collision carts
Use two dynamics carts with velcro (inelastic) and magnets/springs (elastic). Measure masses and speeds before/after with photogates or video. Compute total p and total KE before/after; compare percent difference; explain any loss.
