# Unit 4: Momentum and Impulse

## NGSS Learning Goals
- HS-PS2-2: Use mathematical representations to support the claim that the total momentum of a system of objects is conserved when there is no net force on the system.
- HS-PS2-3: Apply scientific and engineering ideas to design, evaluate, and refine a device that minimizes the force on a macroscopic object during a collision.
- Science & Engineering Practices: Using Mathematics; Constructing Explanations and Designing Solutions
- Crosscutting Concepts: Systems and System Models; Cause and Effect

## Learning Objectives
Students will be able to:

- [ ] Calculate momentum (p = mv) and recognize it as a vector.
- [ ] Relate impulse to change in momentum (FΔt = Δp) and interpret force–time graphs.
- [ ] Apply conservation of momentum to elastic, inelastic, and explosion/recoil problems.
- [ ] Classify collisions by whether kinetic energy is conserved.
- [ ] Design and evaluate a device that reduces collision force by increasing contact time (HS-PS2-3).

## Key Concepts
- Momentum p = mv (kg·m/s)
- Impulse J = FΔt = Δp
- Area under F–t graph = impulse
- Conservation of momentum for an isolated system
- Elastic, inelastic, perfectly inelastic collisions
- Explosions and recoil
- Crumple zones, airbags, and helmets

## Cornell Notes — Momentum and Impulse

| Cue / Question | Notes |
|---|---|
| **What is momentum?** | p = mv. A vector pointing in the direction of velocity. Heavier and/or faster objects have more momentum. Units kg·m/s. |
| **What is impulse?** | J = F_net Δt = Δp = m(v_f − v_i). Same Δp can come from a large force over a short time or a small force over a long time. |
| **Why airbags & crumple zones?** | They lengthen the collision time. Δp is fixed by the crash, so F = Δp/Δt gets smaller — less injury. (Design target for HS-PS2-3.) |
| **Conservation of momentum?** | If net external force = 0: p_total before = p_total after. m₁v₁ + m₂v₂ = m₁v₁′ + m₂v₂′. Choose a positive direction and keep signs. |
| **Types of collisions?** | Elastic: momentum AND kinetic energy conserved. Inelastic: momentum conserved, KE lost (heat, sound, deformation). Perfectly inelastic: objects stick together, v_f = (m₁v₁+m₂v₂)/(m₁+m₂). |
| **Explosions / recoil?** | System starts at rest (p = 0), so the pieces' momenta cancel: m₁v₁ = −m₂v₂. The lighter piece gets the higher speed. |
| **Momentum vs. kinetic energy?** | Doubling v doubles p but quadruples KE (KE = p²/2m). KE is a scalar and is not always conserved; momentum is a vector and is conserved in isolated systems. |

**Summary (write in your own words):** Forces acting over time change momentum. In an isolated system, internal forces cannot change total momentum, so the vector sum before equals the sum after. Extending impact time lowers the force.

## Practice Problems

1. Find the momentum of a 1000 kg car moving at 20 m/s.
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>p = 20,000 kg·m/s.</details>
2. A 2 kg cart moving at 3 m/s sticks to a 1 kg cart at rest. Find their final speed.
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>v = (2×3)/(3) = 2 m/s.</details>
3. A 0.15 kg ball moving at 30 m/s is hit back at 40 m/s in the opposite direction. Find the impulse. If contact lasts 5 ms, what is the average force?
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>Δp = 0.15(40 − (−30)) = 10.5 N·s; F = 10.5/0.005 = 2100 N.</details>
4. A 1000 kg cannon fires a 10 kg shell at 100 m/s. Find the cannon's recoil velocity.
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>v = −(10×100)/1000 = −1 m/s (opposite to the shell).</details>
5. Why does a car crash at 20 m/s hurt less with an airbag, even though Δp is the same?
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>The airbag increases stopping time Δt, so average force F = Δp/Δt is smaller.</details>

## Most-Missed Questions (interactive version in `site/index.html`)

1. A 2 kg cart moving at 3 m/s collides and sticks to a 1 kg cart at rest. Their speed afterward is:  
   A) 1 m/s; B) 2 m/s; C) 3 m/s; D) 4.5 m/s  
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>**B** — Momentum before = 2 × 3 = 6 kg·m/s; after = (3 kg)v, so v = 2 m/s.</details>
2. Why do airbags reduce injury in a crash?  
   A) They reduce the change in momentum; B) They increase the stopping time, reducing the force; C) They increase the force but over a larger area only; D) They reduce the car's mass  
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>**B** — Δp is fixed by the crash. F = Δp/Δt, so a longer Δt gives a smaller average force.</details>
3. In a perfectly inelastic collision between two carts:  
   A) Momentum is conserved but kinetic energy is not; B) Both momentum and kinetic energy are conserved; C) Kinetic energy is conserved but momentum is not; D) Neither is conserved  
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>**A** — Momentum is conserved in any isolated collision. Kinetic energy is converted to heat, sound, and deformation when objects stick.</details>
4. Two identical carts move toward each other at equal speeds and stick together. After the collision they:  
   A) Move in the direction of either cart; B) Move at double speed; C) Stop; D) Move at half speed  
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>**C** — Momentum is a vector. The momenta are equal and opposite, so the total is zero and the combined cart stops.</details>
5. A ball hits a wall and bounces straight back with the same speed. Compared with sticking to the wall, the impulse on the ball is:  
   A) The same; B) Half as large; C) Twice as large; D) Zero  
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>**C** — Bouncing reverses momentum: Δp = m(v − (−v)) = 2mv. Sticking gives only Δp = mv.</details>
6. A 1000 kg cannon fires a 10 kg shell at 100 m/s. The cannon's recoil speed is:  
   A) 0.1 m/s; B) 1 m/s; C) 10 m/s; D) 100 m/s  
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>**B** — Total momentum starts at zero: 1000 v = −10 × 100, so |v| = 1 m/s.</details>
7. If a car's speed doubles, its momentum and kinetic energy change by factors of:  
   A) 2 and 2; B) 2 and 4; C) 4 and 2; D) 4 and 4  
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>**B** — p = mv scales with v, but KE = ½mv² scales with v².</details>
8. A constant 10 N force acts on a cart for 3 s. The impulse delivered is:  
   A) 3.3 N·s; B) 13 N·s; C) 30 N·s; D) 7 N·s  
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>**C** — J = FΔt = 10 × 3 = 30 N·s, which equals the area under the force–time graph.</details>
