# Unit 4: Momentum

**Standards:** HS-PS2-2 (use mathematical representations to support conservation of momentum in a system) · HS-PS2-3 (design a device that minimizes the force on a macroscopic object during a collision) · Practices: Using Mathematics, Designing Solutions · Crosscutting Concepts: Systems and System Models, Conservation
**Suggested pacing:** 3 weeks · **Prerequisite:** Unit 3 · **Quiz:** [`quizzes/index.html`](../quizzes/index.html) → Momentum

## 1. Key concepts to master
| Concept | Student-friendly statement |
|---|---|
| Momentum | p = mv (kg·m/s), a vector in the direction of velocity. |
| Impulse | J = F_avg·Δt = Δp. Impulse–momentum theorem. |
| Force and time | For a fixed Δp, a longer stopping time means a smaller average force. |
| Conservation of momentum | In an isolated system, total momentum before = total momentum after. |
| Collision types | Elastic: momentum and KE conserved. Inelastic: momentum conserved, KE lost. Perfectly inelastic: objects stick. |
| Explosions / recoil | Start at rest, total p = 0, so pieces move in opposite directions with equal and opposite momenta. |
| Newton's third law link | Equal and opposite forces for the same time give equal and opposite impulses, which is why p is conserved. |

## 2. Learning objectives
- **M1** Calculate momentum and its change (with direction/sign) for single objects.
- **M2** Apply the impulse–momentum theorem to find force, time or velocity change.
- **M3** Use conservation of momentum to predict outcomes of collisions and explosions in 1-D.
- **M4** Classify collisions and justify with kinetic-energy calculations.
- **M5** Design, test and refine a device that reduces collision force (e.g., egg-drop or crumple-zone cart) and justify the design using impulse.

## 3. Cornell notes

**Title:** Momentum and Collisions · **Essential question:** *What is conserved when objects interact, and how can we use that to predict motion?*

| Cue | Notes |
|---|---|
| What is momentum? | "Mass in motion": p = mv. Direction matters; choose + direction and use signs. |
| What is impulse? | J = FΔt = Δp = m(v_f − v_i). Area under an F–t graph. |
| Why airbags / crumple zones? | Same Δp, but larger Δt → smaller F. |
| Conservation rule? | Σp_before = Σp_after (isolated system, no external net force). m₁v₁ + m₂v₂ = m₁v₁′ + m₂v₂′ |
| Sticky collision? | m₁v₁ + m₂v₂ = (m₁ + m₂)v′. KE is lost. |
| Elastic collision? | Both momentum and KE are conserved (billiard balls, approximately). |
| Explosion or push-apart? | Total p stays 0: m₁v₁ = −m₂v₂. Lighter piece moves faster. |
| Bounce vs. stick? | Bouncing means a bigger Δp, so a bigger impulse. |

## 5. Practice problems
1. A 0.15 kg baseball moving at +40 m/s is hit and leaves at −50 m/s. Impulse? Contact time is 1.0 ms: find the average force. [−13.5 N·s; −13,500 N]
2. A 1000 kg car moving at 20 m/s stops in 0.50 s in a collision vs. 5.0 s braking. Compare average forces. [40,000 N vs 4,000 N]
3. A 3.0 kg cart at 4.0 m/s collides and sticks to a 1.0 kg cart at rest. Final speed and KE lost? [3.0 m/s; 6 J]
4. A 0.20 kg cart at +1.5 m/s hits a 0.30 kg cart at rest elastically. *(Challenge.)* [v₁′ = −0.30 m/s, v₂′ = +1.2 m/s]
5. A 50 kg skater throws a 2.0 kg ball at 10 m/s forward from rest. Recoil speed? [0.40 m/s backward]
6. A rocket expels 0.05 kg of gas per second at 2000 m/s. What force acts on the rocket? [100 N]
7. Explain with impulse why you bend your knees when landing. [longer Δt → smaller F for same Δp]
8. Compare p and KE for a 2 kg object at 3 m/s and a 6 kg object at 1 m/s. [p: 6 each, KE: 9 J vs 3 J]

## 6. Real-world applications
- Vehicle safety: airbags, seat belts, crumple zones, guardrail design
- Sports: follow-through, catching and boxing gloves, helmets
- Space: rocket propulsion and satellite maneuvers
- Forensics: reconstructing vehicle collisions
- Particle physics: conservation of momentum in collisions

## 7. Lab / performance task
**Collision cart lab:** Use dynamics carts with photogates or video to measure velocities before/after elastic (magnets/bumper) and perfectly inelastic (Velcro) collisions. Compute total p and KE before and after; discuss uncertainty. **Design challenge (HS-PS2-3):** Build a protective container or bumper cart that keeps a fragile object safe from a fixed-height drop or ramp collision. Document criteria, constraints, tests and redesign using force–time reasoning.
