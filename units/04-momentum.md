## Cornell notes

| Cue / Questions | Notes |
|---|---|
| Momentum | p = mv (kg·m/s), vector, direction of velocity. Choose + direction and keep signs. |
| Impulse | J = FΔt = Δp = mv_f − mv_i. Area under a F–t graph = impulse. |
| Reducing force | For the same Δp, longer Δt ⇒ smaller average F (airbags, crumple zones, bending knees, catching a ball). |
| Bounce vs. stop | Rebound reverses velocity, so |Δp| is larger than stopping ⇒ larger impulse. |
| Conservation | If net external force ≈ 0, total p is constant: Σp_before = Σp_after. Internal forces cancel (Newton's 3rd). |
| Perfectly inelastic | Objects stick: m₁v₁ + m₂v₂ = (m₁ + m₂)v′. Max KE lost (not zero). |
| Elastic | Momentum **and** KE conserved. Equal masses head-on: velocities swap. |
| Which is conserved? | Momentum: always (isolated). KE: only in elastic. Total energy: always (becomes heat, sound, deformation). |
| Recoil / explosions | Start at rest (p = 0) ⇒ pieces have equal and opposite momenta; lighter piece moves faster. |
| Newton's 2nd form | ΣF = Δp/Δt — force is rate of change of momentum. |

## Practice problems
**Set A – Concepts**
1. Why is it easier to catch a water balloon by moving your hands with it?
2. Two carts collide and stick: is momentum conserved? Is kinetic energy?

**Set B – Calculations**
3. Momentum of a 1500 kg car at 20 m/s? (30,000 kg·m/s)
4. A 0.50 kg ball moving 10 m/s is stopped in 0.050 s. Average force? (100 N)
5. 2.0 kg cart at 3.0 m/s hits stationary 1.0 kg cart; they stick. v′? (2.0 m/s)
6. 50 kg skater at rest throws a 5.0 kg ball at 4.0 m/s. Recoil speed? (0.40 m/s)
7. A 0.15 kg baseball moving 40 m/s is hit back at 50 m/s. Impulse? (13.5 N·s)

**Set C – Challenge**
8. A 1000 kg car at 15 m/s hits a stationary 500 kg car; they lock. Find v′ and % KE lost. (10 m/s, 33%)
9. Using a F–t graph (triangle, peak 800 N, base 0.02 s) find impulse and Δv for a 4 kg object. (8 N·s, 2 m/s)
