## Key concepts
| Concept | Essentials |
|---|---|
| Momentum | p = mv, kg·m/s, vector |
| Impulse | J = FΔt = Δp; area under F-t |
| Conservation | In an isolated system, Σp_before = Σp_after |
| Collision types | Elastic: KE kept. Inelastic: KE lost. Both conserve p. |
| Recoil | Equal and opposite momenta from rest |

## Cornell notes (fill-in format)
| Cue / Questions | Notes |
|---|---|
| What is momentum? | Mass in motion: p = mv, with direction. |
| What is impulse? | Force × time; it equals the change in momentum. |
| Why do airbags, crumple zones and padding help? | Same Δp, longer Δt, smaller average F. |
| When is momentum conserved? | When no net external force acts on the system. |
| Is KE conserved in a collision? | Only in elastic collisions. |
| How do I handle directions? | Pick +, assign signs to every velocity before computing. |
| **Summary** | _Students: use impulse and momentum to explain why you bend your knees when landing._ |

## Practice problems
1. Find p for a 0.145 kg baseball moving at 40 m/s. *(5.8 kg·m/s)*
2. A 0.15 kg ball moving at +20 m/s is hit back at −30 m/s, contact time 0.01 s. Find the average force. *(−750 N, magnitude 750 N)*
3. A 1200 kg car at 15 m/s hits a stationary 1800 kg car and they lock. Find the final speed and KE lost. *(6 m/s; 81 kJ)*
4. A 3 kg cart at 4 m/s collides elastically with an identical cart at rest. Predict the final speeds. *(0 and 4 m/s)*
5. Challenge: A cannon (800 kg) fires a 20 kg shell at 100 m/s. Find the recoil speed. *(2.5 m/s)*
