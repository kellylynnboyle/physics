# Unit 1: Kinematics — Answer Key (instructors)

## Quiz

| # | Answer | Why | Mistake this question targets | Objective |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **B.** 9.8 m/s² downward | Gravity does not switch off at the top. The velocity is zero for an instant, but it is still changing at 9.8 m/s² downward, which is why the ball starts moving down. | Mixing up zero velocity with zero acceleration. | |
| 2 | **B.** Speeding up | When velocity and acceleration have the same sign, the object speeds up. Negative acceleration only means slowing down when the velocity is positive. | Assuming negative acceleration always means slowing down. | |
| 3 | **C.** 0 m/s | She finishes where she started, so her displacement is 0 and average velocity = 0 / 100 s = 0 m/s. Her average speed is 4 m/s. | Using distance instead of displacement for velocity. | |
| 4 | **B.** Moving at constant velocity | The slope of a position-time graph is velocity. A straight line has one constant slope, so the velocity is constant. Speeding up would be a curve that gets steeper. | Reading a rising line as "getting faster". | |
| 5 | **B.** Moving at a constant 5 m/s | The graph shows velocity, and it stays at 5 m/s. A flat line means zero acceleration, not zero motion. At rest would be a line along v = 0. | Reading a velocity-time graph as if it were a position-time graph. | |
| 6 | **C.** They land at the same time | In free fall every object has the same acceleration, 9.8 m/s² downward, so both cover the same distance in the same time. | Believing heavier objects fall faster. | |
| 7 | **D.** Four times as far | From rest, d = ½·a·t². Distance depends on time squared, so doubling the time gives 2² = 4 times the distance. | Assuming distance grows in step with time during acceleration. | |
| 8 | **B.** Displacement | Area = velocity × time, which has units of m/s × s = m. That is displacement. The slope, not the area, gives acceleration. | Swapping slope and area. | |
| 9 | **B.** Yes, because its direction is changing | Velocity is a vector. A change in direction is a change in velocity, and any change in velocity is an acceleration. | Treating constant speed as constant velocity. | |
| 10 | **C.** 19.6 m/s | vf = vi + a·t = 0 + 9.8 × 2.0 = 19.6 m/s. The value 19.6 m also happens to be the distance fallen, from d = ½·a·t². | Using the distance equation or forgetting to multiply by time. | |

## Practice problems

1. A cyclist rides 3.0 km north, then 4.0 km south in 0.50 h. Find the distance, the displacement, the average speed, and the average velocity.  
   **Answer:** Distance 7.0 km; displacement 1.0 km south; average speed 14 km/h; average velocity 2.0 km/h south.
2. A car speeds up from 10 m/s to 30 m/s in 8.0 s. Find its acceleration and the distance it covers.  
   **Answer:** a = 2.5 m/s²; d = ½(10 + 30)(8.0) = 160 m.
3. A velocity-time graph is a flat line at 6 m/s for 5 s, then drops in a straight line to 0 over the next 3 s. Find the total displacement.  
   **Answer:** Rectangle 6 × 5 = 30 m plus triangle ½ × 3 × 6 = 9 m; total 39 m.
4. A stone is dropped from a bridge and hits the water 2.0 s later. How high is the bridge, and how fast is the stone moving at impact?  
   **Answer:** d = ½(9.8)(2.0)² = 19.6 m; vf = 19.6 m/s.
5. A ball is thrown straight up at 14.7 m/s. How long does it take to reach the top, and how high does it go?  
   **Answer:** t = 14.7 / 9.8 = 1.5 s; height = 14.7² / (2 × 9.8) = 11.0 m.
6. A plane must reach 60 m/s to take off and accelerates at 4.0 m/s² from rest. What is the shortest runway it can use?  
   **Answer:** d = vf² / (2a) = 3600 / 8.0 = 450 m.
