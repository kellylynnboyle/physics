# Unit 1: Kinematics (describing motion)

**NGSS:** HS-PS2-1 (data and math foundation) | **Quiz:** `../../quizzes/index.html` → Kinematics | **Pacing:** ~3 weeks

## Learning objectives (I can...)
1. Distinguish distance, displacement, speed, velocity, and acceleration, and state which are vectors.
2. Describe motion with motion maps, position-time (x-t) and velocity-time (v-t) graphs.
3. Calculate velocity and acceleration from data and from graph slopes; find displacement from v-t area.
4. Solve one-dimensional constant-acceleration problems, including free fall (g = 9.8 m/s² down).
5. Collect motion data in a lab and use a best-fit line to extract velocity or acceleration.

## Key concepts
- **Scalar vs vector:** distance and speed have size only; displacement, velocity, acceleration have size and direction.
- **Velocity** v = Δx / Δt. **Acceleration** a = Δv / Δt.
- **Graphs:** slope of x-t = velocity; slope of v-t = acceleration; area under v-t = displacement.
- **Constant-acceleration equations** (pick the one missing the unknown):
  - v = v₀ + at
  - Δx = v₀t + ½at²
  - v² = v₀² + 2aΔx
  - Δx = ½(v₀ + v)t
- **Free fall:** ignoring air, a = 9.8 m/s² downward for rising, falling, or at the top.
- **Sign convention:** choose + direction first; keep it for the whole problem.

## Cornell notes

| Cue (answer aloud from memory) | Notes |
|---|---|
| What is the difference between distance and displacement? | Distance = total path length (scalar). Displacement = straight-line change in position, with direction (vector). A full lap of a 400 m track: distance 400 m, displacement 0. |
| What is the difference between speed and velocity? | Speed = distance/time (scalar). Velocity = displacement/time (vector). Average speed is total distance ÷ total time, not the mean of two speeds. |
| What does acceleration mean, and what are its units? | Rate of change of velocity: a = Δv/Δt, m/s². It happens when speed *or* direction changes. |
| When is an object speeding up? | When v and a point the same direction (same sign). Slowing down when opposite signs. A negative a does not always mean slowing. |
| What does the slope of an x-t graph tell you? Of a v-t graph? | x-t slope = velocity (steeper = faster; negative slope = moving in − direction). v-t slope = acceleration. |
| What does the area under a v-t graph tell you? | Displacement. Example: v rises 0 → 10 m/s in 5 s, area = ½(5)(10) = 25 m. |
| How do you draw a motion map? | Dots at equal time intervals. Equal spacing = constant velocity; growing spacing = speeding up; shrinking = slowing. Arrows show v and a. |
| Which equation do I use? | List knowns (x, v₀, v, a, t). Choose the equation that lacks the variable you neither know nor want. |
| What is free fall? | Motion under gravity only. a = 9.8 m/s² down. At the top of a toss: v = 0 but a is still 9.8 down. Rise time = fall time (same height). |
| Worked example: car at 20 m/s brakes to rest in 5 s. a? distance? | Choose + = forward. a = (0 − 20)/5 = −4 m/s². Δx = ½(20 + 0)(5) = 50 m. Check: v² = 2aΔx → 400 = 2(4)(50) ✔. |
| Worked example: object dropped for 3 s. v? distance? | + down, v₀ = 0. v = 9.8(3) = 29.4 m/s. Δx = ½(9.8)(3²) = 44.1 m. |

## Most-missed questions (the quiz targets these)
1. Mixing up distance and displacement (the lap problem).
2. Thinking zero velocity means zero acceleration (top of a throw).
3. Reading "negative acceleration" as "slowing down".
4. Averaging two speeds instead of dividing total distance by total time (60 km at 30 km/h, then 60 km at 60 km/h → 40 km/h, not 45).
5. Using the slope when the area is needed (or vice versa).
6. Forgetting units or sign convention.

## Practice problems (answers in brackets)
1. A student walks 3 m east then 4 m north. Distance? Displacement? [7 m; 5 m at about 53° north of east]
2. A cyclist covers 120 m in 8.0 s at constant velocity. v? [15 m/s]
3. A car goes from 5 m/s to 25 m/s in 4.0 s. a? [5 m/s²]
4. A train starting from rest accelerates at 2.0 m/s² for 10 s. v? Δx? [20 m/s; 100 m]
5. A ball is thrown straight up at 14.7 m/s. Time to the top? Max height? [1.5 s; 11.0 m]
6. A stone falls from rest for 2.0 s. Distance fallen? [19.6 m]
7. Sketch the v-t graph for a ball thrown up and caught at the same height. [Straight line, slope −9.8 m/s², crossing zero at the top]
8. A runner jogs 100 m at 4 m/s then 100 m at 6 m/s. Average speed? [4.8 m/s, not 5]

## Labs
- **Constant-velocity buggy:** measure x at equal times, graph x vs t, slope = v. Variables: independent time; dependent position; control surface and battery.
- **Ramp acceleration:** cart down a ramp with motion sensor or Tracker video. Graph v vs t, slope = a; compare a to g sinθ later in Unit 3.

## Real-world applications
- Highway speed enforcement (average speed cameras use Δx/Δt).
- Stopping distance and safe following distance (v² = 2aΔx → doubling speed quadruples braking distance).
- Elevators and subway trains designed for comfortable acceleration limits.
- Sprint timing in track events; GPS/fitness watches.
