# Unit 5 — Work & Energy
**NGSS:** HS-PS3-1, HS-PS3-2, HS-PS3-3 · **Time:** ~3 weeks · **Prereq:** Units 1, 3

## Learning objectives
- **E1** Calculate work and power.
- **E2** Calculate KE, gravitational PE, elastic PE; apply the work–energy theorem.
- **E3** Apply conservation of energy, with and without friction.
- **E4** Design a device that converts energy and evaluate its efficiency.

## Key concepts
| Concept | Equation |
|---|---|
| Work | W = Fd cosθ (J = N·m); zero if F ⟂ displacement |
| Kinetic energy | KE = ½mv² |
| Gravitational PE | PE = mgh (relative to a chosen zero) |
| Elastic PE | PE = ½kx² |
| Work–energy theorem | W_net = ΔKE |
| Conservation | KE_i + PE_i + W_other = KE_f + PE_f (W_friction is negative) |
| Power | P = W/t = Fv (W = J/s) |
| Efficiency | useful output ÷ total input |

## Most-missed questions
1. Doubling speed doubles KE (it quadruples).
2. Holding a heavy object still does work on it (no displacement → no work on the object).
3. Energy is "used up" (it is transformed; total is conserved).
4. Mass matters for the speed at the bottom of a frictionless hill (it cancels).
5. Work vs. power confusion (two machines doing equal work in different times).
6. Using the path length instead of vertical height in PE.

## Practice problems
1. A 20 kg crate is pushed 5 m by 40 N at 0°. Work done? *(200 J)*
2. A 1000 kg car goes from 10 to 20 m/s. Work done by the engine (net)? *(150 kJ)*
3. A 2 kg ball drops from 5 m. Speed just before impact (no air resistance)? *(9.9 m/s)*
4. A 60 kg student climbs 3 m of stairs in 4 s. Power output? *(441 W)*
5. A 0.5 kg block compresses a spring (k = 200 N/m) by 0.1 m and is released on a frictionless surface. Speed? *(2.0 m/s)*
6. A 50 kg skier slides from rest down a 20 m high slope and reaches 15 m/s at the bottom. Energy lost to friction? *(4.2 kJ)*

## Real-world applications
Roller coasters, hydro-electric dams, regenerative braking in EVs, wind turbines, bike gearing, food Calories, energy-saving designs.
**Project (HS-PS3-3):** Build a device (e.g., rubber-band car, solar-powered motor, mini hydro turbine) that converts energy. Report input/output energy, efficiency, and one improvement.

## Assessment criteria
Level 3: identifies system and states start/end states; correct energy bookkeeping and units. Level 4: includes friction/thermal losses, uses bar charts or a spreadsheet model (HS-PS3-1) and evaluates efficiency.
