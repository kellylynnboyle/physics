## Key concepts and equations
| Idea | Equation |
|---|---|
| Work | W = Fd cosθ (joules) |
| Kinetic energy | KE = ½mv² |
| Gravitational PE | PE = mgh |
| Spring PE | PE = ½kx² |
| Work-energy theorem | W_net = ΔKE |
| Conservation | KE₁ + PE₁ + W_nc = KE₂ + PE₂ |
| Power | P = W/t = Fv (watts) |
| Efficiency | useful output / input × 100% |

## Cornell notes

| Cue | Notes |
|---|---|
| What is work? | Force along the displacement times distance. Force ⟂ motion does zero work (carrying a box level). |
| When is work negative? | Force opposite to motion, e.g. friction, or braking. |
| Why does doubling speed quadruple KE? | KE ∝ v². Stopping distance scales the same way. |
| State the work-energy theorem. | Net work = change in kinetic energy. |
| When is mechanical energy conserved? | Only gravity and springs do work (no friction, no drag). |
| How do I solve a roller-coaster problem? | Pick two points. Write KE + PE at each. Set equal (add friction loss if any). Mass cancels. |
| Difference between energy and power? | Energy is the amount (J). Power is the rate (W = J/s). 1 hp ≈ 746 W. |
| Where does the energy go with friction? | Into thermal energy of surfaces and surroundings. Total energy is conserved; mechanical energy is not. |
| What does an energy bar chart show? | Each form (KE, PE, thermal) at each moment; bars sum to a constant. |

**Summary:** Work transfers energy. Energy changes form but the total is conserved. Mechanical energy can be turned into thermal energy by friction. Power measures how fast.

## Common misconceptions
- Holding a heavy object still does work on it (no displacement, no work, though muscles use chemical energy).
- Energy is "used up" (it is transformed or dissipated).
- Heavier objects reach a higher speed at the bottom of a frictionless ramp (mass cancels).
- Doubling speed doubles KE.

## Practice problems
1. Work to lift a 2.0 kg book 3.0 m at constant speed? **Ans: 58.8 J**
2. A 50 N force pulls a sled 10 m with a rope at 60° above horizontal. Work? **Ans: 50 × 10 × 0.5 = 250 J**
3. Speed at the bottom of a frictionless 20 m drop from rest? **Ans: √(2 × 9.8 × 20) ≈ 19.8 m/s**
4. A 1000 kg car going 20 m/s brakes to rest. Work done by brakes and distance if F = 5000 N? **Ans: −200 000 J; 40 m**
5. A spring (k = 200 N/m) is compressed 0.10 m. Stored energy? **Ans: 1.0 J**
6. A 60 kg student climbs 4.0 m of stairs in 5.0 s. Power? **Ans: 60 × 9.8 × 4.0 / 5.0 = 470 W**
7. A motor takes in 800 J and lifts a load gaining 600 J of PE. Efficiency? **Ans: 75%**
8. Compare work to speed a car from 0 to 10 m/s vs from 10 to 20 m/s. **Ans: second takes 3× as much (½m·10² = 50m J vs ½m(20²−10²) = 150m J)**

## Real-world applications and labs
- **Lab (HS-PS3-1):** Cart or ball on a ramp: measure h and v at the bottom, compare mgh to ½mv², compute percent lost to friction.
- **Lab:** Spring and mass: graph F vs x, area gives energy; compare with ½kx².
- **Device challenge (HS-PS3-3):** Build a mousetrap car, rubber-band or solar vehicle, or marble roller coaster; document energy transformations and efficiency.
- Roller coasters, hydroelectric dams, hybrid-car regenerative braking, power ratings of appliances, food calories (1 Cal = 4184 J).
