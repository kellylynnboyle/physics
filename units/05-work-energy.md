# Unit 5 — Work & Energy

**Standards:** NGSS HS-PS3-1 — "Create a computational model to calculate the change in energy of one component in a system when the change in energy of the other component(s) and energy flows in and out of the system are known." HS-PS3-2 — relating energy to forces between objects. HS-PS3-3 — designing a device that converts one form of energy into another. [CDE NGSS reference](https://www.cde.ca.gov/pd/ca/sc/ngssstandards.asp)

## Key concepts

- Work: W = Fd·cosθ
- Work-energy theorem: W_net = ΔKE
- Kinetic energy: KE = ½mv²
- Gravitational potential energy: PEg = mgh
- Conservation of mechanical energy (no friction/non-conservative forces)
- Power: P = W/t = Fv
- Energy transformations and where "lost" energy goes

## Cornell notes

| Cue | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Work** | W = Fd·cosθ (Joules). Only the force component *parallel* to displacement does work. A perpendicular force (e.g., carrying a tray at constant height while walking) does zero work. |
| **Work-Energy Theorem** | W_net = ΔKE. The net work done on an object equals its change in kinetic energy. |
| **Kinetic Energy** | KE = ½mv² — depends on speed **squared**, so doubling speed quadruples KE. |
| **Gravitational PE** | PEg = mgh, measured relative to a chosen reference height (h = 0 is arbitrary — only ΔPE is physically meaningful). |
| **Conservation of Mechanical Energy** | If only conservative forces act (gravity, springs — no friction/air resistance), KE + PE = constant. Where PE decreases, KE increases by exactly the same amount, and vice versa. |
| **Power** | P = W/t = Fv (Watts). Power is about *how fast* work is done, not how much total work is done. |

**Summary:** Energy is never created or destroyed, only transformed or
transferred. When mechanical energy seems to "disappear" to friction, it
has become heat and sound — total energy is still conserved, even though
*mechanical* energy alone is not.

## Practice problems

1. A 20 kg box is pushed 8 m across a floor with a 50 N force applied at 30° above horizontal. Find the work done.
2. A 1000 kg roller coaster car starts at rest at the top of a 45 m frictionless hill. Find its speed at the bottom.
3. A 2 kg ball is dropped from 10 m. Using energy conservation, find its speed just before hitting the ground, and check it against a kinematics calculation.
4. A motor lifts a 500 N crate 6 m in 4 s. Find the power output of the motor.
5. A 0.2 kg spring-loaded dart is launched by a spring compressed 0.1 m with spring constant k = 250 N/m. Find its launch speed (elastic PE = ½kx²).

## Real-world applications

- Roller coaster design (height ↔ speed trade-offs, energy loss to track friction).
- Hydroelectric power (gravitational PE → kinetic → electrical energy).
- Regenerative braking in electric/hybrid vehicles (KE → stored electrical energy).
- Energy efficiency ratings for appliances and motors (useful work output vs. power input).
