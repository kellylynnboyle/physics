# Unit 3: Newton's Laws

**NGSS link:** HS-PS2-1 (analyze data to support F = ma), HS-PS2-4 (gravitational and electrostatic force models; Newton's law of gravitation).
**Suggested pacing:** 3 weeks. **Prerequisites:** Units 1-2, vectors.

## Learning objectives (I can...)
- [ ] N1. State and apply Newton's 1st law (inertia) to explain motion at constant velocity.
- [ ] N2. Draw free-body diagrams for objects in 1-D and on inclines.
- [ ] N3. Apply ΣF = ma, including mass-vs-weight, normal force, friction, tension.
- [ ] N4. Identify action-reaction pairs and explain why they never cancel.
- [ ] N5. Use Newton's law of gravitation qualitatively and quantitatively (F = Gm₁m₂/r²).
- [ ] N6. Analyze lab data to show a ∝ F and a ∝ 1/m.

## Key concepts
| Concept | Essentials |
|---|---|
| 1st law | No net force → constant velocity (at rest or moving uniformly) |
| 2nd law | ΣF = ma, a vector equation; unit: N = kg·m/s² |
| 3rd law | F_AB = −F_BA, on different objects, same type |
| Weight | W = mg (a force); mass is a measure of inertia |
| Friction | f_k = μ_k N; static friction adjusts up to μ_s N |
| Gravitation | F = G m₁m₂ / r² |

## Cornell notes (fill-in format)
| Cue / Questions | Notes |
|---|---|
| What does a net force of zero mean? | Constant velocity (not necessarily at rest). Equilibrium. |
| Steps for a 2nd-law problem? | 1) Sketch. 2) Free-body diagram. 3) Choose axes. 4) ΣF = ma in each direction. 5) Solve, check units. |
| What's the difference between mass and weight? | Mass: kg, constant. Weight: N, = mg, depends on location. |
| Why don't action-reaction forces cancel? | They act on different objects. |
| What does the scale read in an accelerating elevator? | The normal force N: N = m(g + a) going up with acceleration a. |
| What happens to F_g if distance doubles? | It becomes 1/4 (inverse-square). |
| **Summary** | _Students: explain all three laws using a car braking with a passenger._ |

## Practice problems
1. A 1500 kg car accelerates from rest to 20 m/s in 8 s. Find the net force. *(3750 N)*
2. A 5 kg block is pulled across a floor by a 30 N force; kinetic friction is 10 N. Find a. *(4 m/s²)*
3. A 70 kg person is in an elevator accelerating upward at 2 m/s². Find the scale reading. *(826 N)*
4. Two blocks (2 kg and 3 kg) touch on a frictionless table; a 10 N force pushes the 2 kg block. Find a and the contact force. *(2 m/s², 6 N)*
5. Challenge: A 10 kg box on a 30° incline with μ_k = 0.2: find a down the slope. *(≈ 3.2 m/s²)*

## Real-world applications
Seatbelts and headrests (inertia), rocket and jet propulsion (3rd law), elevator and roller-coaster feel, crash tests, why heavy and light objects fall together.

## Lab
**Atwood-style or cart + hanging mass:** vary F at constant m, then m at constant F; graph a vs F and a vs 1/m; interpret slope.
