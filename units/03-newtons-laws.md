# Unit 3: Newton's Laws

**Standards:** HS-PS2-1 (Newton's second law from data) · HS-PS2-4 (gravitational and electrostatic forces — gravity portion this semester) · Practices: Analyzing Data, Constructing Explanations · Crosscutting Concepts: Cause and Effect, Patterns
**Suggested pacing:** 4 weeks · **Prerequisites:** Units 1–2 · **Quiz:** [`quizzes/index.html`](../quizzes/index.html) → Newton's Laws

## 1. Key concepts to master
| Concept | Student-friendly statement |
|---|---|
| Force | A push or pull; a vector measured in newtons (1 N = 1 kg·m/s²). |
| 1st Law (inertia) | With zero net force, velocity stays constant (at rest or constant velocity). |
| 2nd Law | a = F_net / m; direction of a is the direction of the net force. |
| 3rd Law | Forces come in pairs acting on different objects, equal in size, opposite in direction, same type. |
| Weight vs. mass | Weight W = mg (a force, N); mass is amount of matter (kg). |
| Normal force & tension | Contact forces perpendicular to a surface / along a rope. |
| Friction | f ≤ μ_s N (static); f = μ_k N (kinetic). Opposes sliding. |
| Free-body diagrams | Draw all forces on ONE object; choose axes; sum forces along each axis. |
| Universal gravitation (intro) | F = Gm₁m₂/r²; ties g to Earth's mass and radius (HS-PS2-4). |

## 2. Learning objectives
- **N1** Draw accurate free-body diagrams for objects at rest, at constant velocity and accelerating.
- **N2** Use F_net = ma to solve 1-D and simple 2-D problems (inclines, pulleys, elevators).
- **N3** Identify third-law pairs and explain why they never cancel.
- **N4** Differentiate mass and weight, and compute normal force, friction and tension.
- **N5** Plan an investigation and use data (a vs. F, and a vs. m) to support the second law.
- **N6** Relate gravitational force to mass and distance qualitatively and quantitatively.

## 3. Cornell notes

**Title:** Newton's Laws of Motion · **Essential question:** *How do forces change (or not change) the motion of objects?*

| Cue | Notes |
|---|---|
| Law 1? Why do things keep moving? | Objects keep their velocity unless a net force acts. Inertia = resistance to change, measured by mass. "Constant velocity" ⇒ ΣF = 0. |
| Law 2? | ΣF = ma. Same force on a bigger mass → smaller acceleration. Graph a vs. F is a line with slope 1/m. |
| Law 3? | A on B ⇔ B on A. Equal and opposite, different objects, so they never cancel each other. |
| Mass vs. weight? | Mass: kg, constant. Weight: N, W = mg. 5 kg → 49 N on Earth, 8 N on Moon. |
| How to draw a FBD? | Dot for object; arrows from the dot for each force: weight (down), normal (⊥ surface), friction (parallel), tension (along rope), applied. Label each. |
| What does a scale read? | Normal force. In an elevator accelerating up: N = m(g + a). |
| Incline problems? | Tilt axes: parallel (mg sin θ) and perpendicular (mg cos θ). N = mg cos θ. |
| Friction? | Kinetic: f = μ_k N. Static friction adjusts up to μ_s N. |

## 5. Practice problems
1. A 1500 kg car accelerates at 2.0 m/s². Net force? [3000 N]
2. A 4.0 kg crate is pushed with 30 N against 10 N of kinetic friction. Acceleration? [5.0 m/s²]
3. A 60 kg student rides an elevator moving down and slowing at 1.5 m/s². Scale reading? [≈ 678 N]
4. A 10 kg block sits on a frictionless 30° incline. Acceleration down the slope and normal force? [4.9 m/s²; ≈ 85 N]
5. Two blocks (2 kg and 3 kg) are connected by a rope and pulled across a frictionless table by a 20 N force applied to the 3 kg block. Acceleration and rope tension? [4 m/s²; 8 N]
6. Name the third-law partner of each: (a) gravity on a falling apple, (b) a swimmer pushing water backward, (c) tires pushing the road. [apple pulls Earth; water pushes swimmer; road pushes tires forward]
7. A 2.0 kg cart is pulled with different forces and the accelerations are 0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0 m/s² for 1, 2, 3, 4 N. Graph a vs. F and find the slope and what it means. [slope 0.5 kg⁻¹ → m = 2 kg]
8. *Challenge:* Find g at an altitude equal to Earth's radius above the surface. [g/4 ≈ 2.45 m/s²]

## 6. Real-world applications
- Seat belts, crumple zones and head restraints (inertia)
- Rocket propulsion and swimming (third law)
- Elevator and amusement-ride design (apparent weight)
- Skid and anti-lock brake performance (friction)
- Orbits and satellite launch (gravitation)

## 7. Lab / performance task
**Newton's second-law lab:** Cart on a track with a hanging mass (or force sensor). Vary net force at constant total mass, then vary mass at constant force. Graph a vs. F and a vs. 1/m, write a claim–evidence–reasoning report. *(Directly addresses HS-PS2-1 data analysis.)* Optional: design and test a **friction coefficient** measurement on a ramp.

## 8. Assessment criteria
| Level | Description |
|---|---|
| 4 – Advanced | Solves multi-body, incline and friction problems with FBDs; designs an experiment to measure μ. |

| 2 – Developing | Draws FBDs with missing/extra forces (e.g., "force of motion"); occasional algebra errors. |
| 1 – Beginning | Cannot connect net force to acceleration. |
