# Unit 3 — Newton's Laws

**Standards:** NGSS HS-PS2-1 — "Analyze data to support the claim that Newton's second law of motion describes the mathematical relationship among the net force on a macroscopic object, its mass, and its acceleration." [CDE NGSS reference](https://www.cde.ca.gov/pd/ca/sc/ngssstandards.asp)

Newton's Laws connect force (the **why**) to the motion kinematics
describes (the **how**).

## Key concepts

- First Law (inertia)
- Second Law (ΣF = ma)
- Third Law (action-reaction pairs)
- Free body diagrams and net force
- Mass vs. weight
- Static vs. kinetic friction, normal force, tension

## Cornell notes

| Cue | Notes |
|---|---|
| **1st Law — Inertia** | An object at rest stays at rest, and an object in motion stays in motion at constant velocity, unless acted on by a net (unbalanced) external force. |
| **2nd Law** | ΣF = ma. Acceleration is proportional to net force and inversely proportional to mass; acceleration points in the same direction as the net force. |
| **3rd Law** | Every action force has an equal and opposite reaction force — acting on the *other* object, never the same one, so they can't cancel each other out for a single object's motion. |
| **Mass vs. Weight** | Mass (kg) = amount of matter, constant everywhere. Weight (N) = Fg = mg, depends on local gravity. |
| **Free Body Diagrams** | Draw every force acting *on* the object (gravity, normal, applied, friction, tension) as a vector from a single point; net force is the vector sum. |
| **Friction** | Static friction (not sliding) ranges from 0 up to μₛN; kinetic friction (sliding) ≈ μₖN, roughly constant once motion starts. μₛ > μₖ typically. |

**Summary:** Always start a problem by drawing the free body diagram and
identifying the net force — nearly every Newton's-Laws error traces back
to a missing or mis-paired force.

## Practice problems

1. A 5 kg box is pushed with 40 N of force across a floor with a friction force of 15 N. Find its acceleration.
2. Two blocks (3 kg and 7 kg) are connected by a string over a pulley (Atwood machine). Find the acceleration of the system and the tension in the string.
3. A 60 kg skater pushes off a wall with 90 N of force. Find their acceleration and identify the 3rd-law reaction force.
4. A 10 kg crate sits on a ramp inclined at 20°. Draw the free body diagram and find the component of gravity acting along the ramp surface.

## Real-world applications

- Seatbelt and airbag design (inertia during sudden stops).
- Rocket propulsion (3rd Law — expelling exhaust mass backward pushes the rocket forward).
- Traction and tire design (static vs. kinetic friction in braking and acceleration).
