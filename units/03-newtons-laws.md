# Unit 3 — Newton's Laws
**NGSS:** HS-PS2-1 · **Time:** ~4 weeks · **Prereq:** Units 1–2, vectors

## Learning objectives
- **N1** Draw a complete free-body diagram (FBD).
- **N2** Apply ΣF = ma in 1-D and on inclines, including friction.
- **N3** Identify Newton's third-law pairs.
- **N4** Explain inertia, equilibrium, weight vs. mass, and apparent weight.

## Key concepts
| Concept | Statement / equation |
|---|---|
| 1st law (inertia) | No net force → constant velocity (including at rest) |
| 2nd law | ΣF = ma (N = kg·m/s²) |
| 3rd law | F(A on B) = −F(B on A); equal, opposite, **on different objects** |
| Weight | W = mg (a force; mass is not) |
| Friction | f_s ≤ μ_s N; f_k = μ_k N; N is not always mg |
| Incline | Along: mg sinθ; perpendicular: N = mg cosθ |
| Equilibrium | ΣF = 0 (vector sum) |

*Hints:* FBD shows only forces on the object (not velocity, not "force of motion"); constant v → ΣF = 0; pairs act on different objects; weight = mg, normal force depends on the situation; tilt axes along the incline; scale reads N = m(g + a) with up positive.

## Most-missed questions
1. "A force is needed to keep an object moving" (Aristotelian idea).
2. Third-law pairs cancel (they act on different objects).
3. Heavier objects hit with a larger force in a collision of a truck and a bug — forces are equal.
4. Normal force always equals mg (false on inclines, in elevators, with extra pushes).
5. Friction depends on contact area / speed (to a first approximation it doesn't).
6. Net force is zero at the top of a toss (it isn't: gravity).

## Practice problems
1. A 1500 kg car accelerates at 2.0 m/s². Net force? *(3000 N)*
2. A 10 kg box is pushed with 50 N; μ_k = 0.30. Acceleration? *(2.1 m/s²)*
3. A 5 kg block slides down a frictionless 30° incline. Acceleration and normal force? *(4.9 m/s²; 42.4 N)*
4. A 70 kg person stands on a scale in an elevator accelerating up at 2 m/s². Reading? *(826 N)*
5. Draw FBDs for a book on a table, a skydiver at terminal velocity, and a hanging mass on a string.
6. Two blocks (2 kg and 3 kg) touch on a frictionless floor; 10 N pushes the 2 kg block. Acceleration and contact force? *(2 m/s²; 6 N)*

## Real-world applications
Seat belts and airbags, rocket and jet propulsion, elevators and amusement-park rides, car traction and ABS, skydiving, bridge and crane design.
**Lab idea:** Cart on a track with hanging mass; vary F with constant mass, then m with constant F; graph a vs. F and a vs. 1/m to verify ΣF = ma (CER report).

## Assessment criteria
Level 3: FBDs complete and labeled; ΣF equation set up per axis; correct units/direction. Level 4: multi-body or incline-with-friction problems; explains the reading on a scale in terms of net force.
