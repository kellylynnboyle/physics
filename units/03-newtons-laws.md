## Key concepts
Force as a vector · net force · inertia · mass vs. weight · normal force · friction (static vs. kinetic) · tension · action–reaction · apparent weight.

## Cornell notes

| Cue / Questions | Notes |
|---|---|
| 1st law (inertia) | An object keeps its velocity (rest or constant v) unless a **net** external force acts. Constant velocity ⇒ ΣF = 0. Mass measures inertia. |
| 2nd law | a = ΣF/m, i.e., ΣF = ma. Direction of a = direction of net force. Units: 1 N = 1 kg·m/s². |
| 3rd law | If A pushes on B, B pushes on A with equal magnitude, opposite direction. The two forces act on **different** objects, so they never cancel. |
| Mass vs. weight | Mass (kg) = amount of matter, constant everywhere. Weight W = mg (N) is gravitational force; changes with g (Moon g ≈ 1.6 m/s²). |
| Free-body diagram | Dot for object; arrows for forces acting **on** it only. Label each. Choose axes; sum components. |
| Normal force | Perpendicular contact force. Not always = mg (ramps, elevators, extra pushes). |
| Friction | Opposes sliding (or tendency). Kinetic f = μ_k N; static f ≤ μ_s N. Independent of contact area (model). |
| Inclines | Weight components: parallel mg sinθ, perpendicular mg cosθ. N = mg cosθ. |
| Apparent weight | Elevator accelerating up: N = m(g + a); down: N = m(g − a); free fall: N = 0. |
| Terminal velocity | Drag rises with speed until drag = weight, then ΣF = 0 and v is constant. |

## Practice problems
**Set A – Concepts**
1. Draw FBDs: book at rest on a table; box pushed at constant speed; skydiver at terminal velocity; block sliding down a frictionless ramp.
2. Identify the third-law partner of: Earth pulls on you; table pushes up on a book.

**Set B – Calculations**
3. Net force of 50 N on a 10 kg cart. Acceleration? (5 m/s²)
4. 5 kg block: 30 N right, 10 N left. a? (4 m/s²)
5. A 60 kg person: weight on Earth and Moon (g = 1.6)? (588 N, 96 N)
6. 20 kg crate pushed at constant speed on a floor with μ_k = 0.30. Push force? (≈59 N)
7. 70 kg person in an elevator accelerating up at 2 m/s². Scale reading? (≈826 N)

**Set C – Challenge**
8. Two blocks (3 kg and 2 kg) in contact on a frictionless floor, 25 N pushes the 3 kg block. Find a and the contact force between blocks. (5 m/s², 10 N)
9. A 10 kg block on a 30° frictionless incline: a and N? (4.9 m/s², ≈85 N)

## Real-world applications
Seat belts and headrests · rocket propulsion · car traction/ABS braking · elevators and amusement-park rides · skydiving · roller skating/ice · sports equipment friction (shoe soles, climbing chalk).
