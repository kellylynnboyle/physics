# Unit 3: Newton's Laws

**NGSS:** HS-PS2-1 | **Pacing:** ~4 weeks | **Prereq:** Units 1-2, vectors

## Learning objectives
- [ ] **N1** State and apply the first law (inertia, equilibrium).
- [ ] **N2** Use ΣF = ma to find acceleration, force, or mass.
- [ ] **N3** Identify third-law pairs and explain why they do not cancel.
- [ ] **N4** Draw correct free-body diagrams.
- [ ] **N5** Distinguish mass from weight; compute W = mg and apparent weight.
- [ ] **N6** Model friction (static and kinetic) with f = μN.
- [ ] **N7** Analyze data showing a ∝ F and a ∝ 1/m (HS-PS2-1 lab).

## Key concepts and equations
| Idea | Equation |
|---|---|
| Second law | ΣF = ma |
| Weight | F_g = mg (g = 9.8 N/kg) |
| Friction | f_k = μ_k N, f_s ≤ μ_s N |
| Inclined plane | along ramp: mg sinθ; perpendicular: N = mg cosθ |
| Third law | F(A on B) = −F(B on A) |

## Cornell notes

| Cue | Notes |
|---|---|
| What does the first law say? | No net force: object at rest stays at rest, moving stays at constant velocity. Constant velocity needs no net force. |
| What is inertia and what measures it? | Resistance to change in motion; measured by mass. |
| State the second law. | a = ΣF/m. Same direction as net force. 1 N = 1 kg·m/s². |
| What is a third-law pair? | Equal and opposite forces on **different objects** (same type). They never cancel because they act on different objects. |
| What is the third-law partner of an object's weight? | The object's gravitational pull on Earth, not the normal force. |
| How do I draw a free-body diagram? | Dot for the object; arrows from the dot for each force on it (gravity, normal, friction, applied, tension); label; no acceleration arrow. |
| Mass vs weight? | Mass: amount of matter (kg), same everywhere. Weight: gravitational force (N), changes with g. |
| What is friction's direction? | Opposes sliding (or tendency to slide). Static friction adjusts up to μ_s N; kinetic is constant. |
| Normal force always equals mg? | No. On a flat surface with no vertical forces, yes; on a ramp N = mg cosθ; in an accelerating elevator it changes. |
| Terminal velocity? | Air drag grows with speed until it equals weight; net force 0, so constant velocity. |

**Summary:** Forces cause changes in velocity. Find ΣF with a free-body diagram, then use a = ΣF/m. Forces always come in pairs on two objects.

## Common misconceptions
- A force is needed to keep an object moving at constant velocity.
- Heavier objects fall faster (without air resistance).
- Third-law pairs cancel; the larger object exerts a larger force.
- Normal force is always the reaction to weight.

## Practice problems
1. A 10 kg cart gets a net 30 N force. Acceleration? **Ans: 3.0 m/s²**
2. A 5.0 kg block is pushed with 20 N across a floor with f_k = 8 N. Acceleration? **Ans: (20−8)/5 = 2.4 m/s²**
3. Weight of a 70 kg person on Earth (g = 9.8) and on the Moon (g = 1.6)? **Ans: 686 N; 112 N; mass 70 kg in both**
4. A 60 kg person in an elevator accelerating up at 2.0 m/s². Normal force? **Ans: N = m(g+a) = 60 × 11.8 = 708 N**
5. A 20 kg box on a 30° frictionless ramp. Acceleration down the ramp? **Ans: g sin30° = 4.9 m/s²**
6. μ_k = 0.40 for a 25 kg crate. Horizontal force to slide it at constant velocity? **Ans: 0.40 × 25 × 9.8 = 98 N**
7. A truck and a fly collide. Compare the forces. **Ans: equal in magnitude; the fly's acceleration is far larger because its mass is tiny.**

## Real-world applications and labs
- **Lab (HS-PS2-1):** Dynamics cart with hanging mass. Vary force at constant total mass, then vary mass at constant force; graph a vs F and a vs 1/m.
- **Lab:** Measure μ_s and μ_k for shoes or wood by tilting a ramp.
- Seat belts and headrests (inertia), rocket launch (third law), elevators and roller coaster sensations, friction and tire grip.
- **Project:** Elevator ride with a phone accelerometer app; match readings to ΣF = ma.
