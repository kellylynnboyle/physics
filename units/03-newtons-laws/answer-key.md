# Unit 3: Newton's Laws — Answer Key (instructors)

## Quiz

| # | Answer | Why | Mistake this question targets | Objective |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **B.** Zero | Constant velocity means zero acceleration, so the net force is zero. No force is needed to keep an object moving. | Believing motion requires a force. | |
| 2 | **C.** The forces are equal in size | Third law: the two forces are equal and opposite. The car has the larger acceleration because its mass is smaller. | Thinking the bigger or faster object pushes harder. | |
| 3 | **C.** The book pulling up on Earth | Swap the objects: Earth pulls book, so book pulls Earth. The table's push is a different interaction that happens to be the same size. | Treating weight and normal force as an action-reaction pair. | |
| 4 | **B.** Tension is equal | Constant speed means zero acceleration and zero net force, so the upward tension equals the downward weight. | Thinking upward motion needs a net upward force. | |
| 5 | **A.** Gravity only | The hand's force stops at release. The ball keeps rising because of its inertia while gravity slows it. | Imagining the force of the throw stays with the ball. | |
| 6 | **B.** Mass stays the same; weight decreases | Mass is the amount of matter and does not change. Weight = m·g, and g on the Moon is about one-sixth of Earth's. | Using mass and weight as the same thing. | |
| 7 | **B.** Exactly 50 N | Constant speed means the net force is zero, so friction must exactly balance the 50 N push. | Thinking the push must beat friction for the box to keep moving. | |
| 8 | **C.** It stays the same | a = Fnet / m. Doubling both gives 2F / 2m = F / m, the same acceleration. | Tracking only one of the two changes. | |
| 9 | **B.** 5 m/s² right | Fnet = 14 − 4 = 10 N right. a = 10 / 2.0 = 5 m/s² right. Using 14 N alone gives the 7 m/s² wrong answer. | Using one force instead of the net force. | |
| 10 | **B.** The two forces act on different objects, so they do not cancel | Only forces on the same object can cancel. The cart accelerates if the forces on the cart (horse's pull minus friction) are unbalanced. | Cancelling an action-reaction pair. | |

Use the last column to tag each question with the objective ID you want it to count toward.

## Practice problems

1. Three forces act on a crate: 40 N right, 15 N left, and 10 N left. Find the net force and say whether the crate accelerates.  
   **Answer:** Fnet = 40 − 25 = 15 N right. Yes, it accelerates to the right.
2. What net force gives a 0.50 kg ball an acceleration of 12 m/s²?  
   **Answer:** Fnet = m·a = 0.50 × 12 = 6.0 N.
3. A 75 kg astronaut travels to a planet where g = 3.7 m/s². Find the astronaut's mass and weight there.  
   **Answer:** Mass 75 kg; weight = 75 × 3.7 = 278 N.
4. A 20 kg sled is pulled with 60 N across snow with μ = 0.10. Find the friction force and the acceleration.  
   **Answer:** Fnorm = 196 N; Ffrict = 19.6 N; Fnet = 40.4 N; a = 2.0 m/s².
5. A 1000 kg elevator accelerates upward at 1.5 m/s². Find the tension in its cable.  
   **Answer:** Ftens − m·g = m·a, so Ftens = 1000 × (9.8 + 1.5) = 11 300 N.
6. A swimmer pushes backward on the pool wall with 300 N. Describe the reaction force and explain why the swimmer moves but the wall does not.  
   **Answer:** The wall pushes the swimmer forward with 300 N. The swimmer's small mass gives a noticeable acceleration; the wall is fixed to the building and Earth, whose mass is enormous.
