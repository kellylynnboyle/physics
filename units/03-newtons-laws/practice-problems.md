# Practice Problems: Newton's Laws

Use g = 9.8 m/s². Start every problem with a free-body diagram. Answer key at the bottom.

## Set A: Concepts (N1, N2, N5)

1. A hockey puck slides at constant velocity on frictionless ice. What is the net force? Does anything push it forward?
2. Draw free-body diagrams for: (a) a book at rest on a table, (b) a box pushed at constant velocity on a rough floor, (c) a skydiver at terminal velocity, (d) a block sliding down a frictionless incline.
3. A book rests on a table. Name the third-law partner of (a) the Earth's pull on the book and (b) the table's push on the book.
4. A large truck collides head-on with a small car. Compare the force on the car with the force on the truck, and compare their accelerations.

## Set B: Second law (N3, N4)

5. A 1200 kg car experiences a net force of 3000 N. Find its acceleration.
6. A 70 kg student: find weight on Earth (g = 9.8) and on the Moon (g = 1.62), and mass on the Moon.
7. A 5.0 kg block is pulled with a horizontal force of 30 N; kinetic friction is 10 N. Find the acceleration.
8. A 10 kg block slides down a frictionless 30° incline. Find the acceleration and the normal force.
9. A 2.0 kg object hangs from a rope and accelerates upward at 1.5 m/s². Find the tension.
10. A 60 kg person stands on a scale in an elevator. Find the scale reading when the elevator accelerates (a) up at 2.0 m/s², (b) down at 2.0 m/s², (c) moves up at constant speed.

## Set C: Friction and drag (N6)

11. A 20 kg box on a floor has μ_s = 0.50 and μ_k = 0.30. (a) Find the friction force when you push with 40 N. (b) What push is needed to start it moving? (c) What push keeps it moving at constant speed?
12. An 80 kg skydiver reaches terminal velocity. What is the drag force? What is the acceleration?

## Set D: Data analysis and challenge (N7, extending)

13. A cart (total mass 0.50 kg) is pulled by hanging masses with these net forces and measured accelerations. Plot a vs. F and find the mass from the slope.

| F (N) | 0.10 | 0.20 | 0.30 | 0.40 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a (m/s²) | 0.21 | 0.39 | 0.61 | 0.80 |

14. Two blocks (3.0 kg and 2.0 kg) touch on a frictionless surface; a 10 N force pushes the 3.0 kg block toward the 2.0 kg block. Find the acceleration and the contact force between blocks.

## Answer key

1. **Zero.** Nothing pushes it forward; it simply keeps its velocity (inertia).
2. (a) weight ↓, normal ↑ (equal). (b) weight ↓, normal ↑, push →, friction ← (push = friction). (c) weight ↓, drag ↑ (equal). (d) weight ↓ and normal ⊥ surface (no friction).
3. (a) The book pulls up on the Earth. (b) The book pushes down on the table.
4. **Forces are equal** in magnitude and opposite. The car has the larger acceleration (a = F/m, smaller mass).
5. a = 3000/1200 = **2.5 m/s²**.
6. Weight on Earth = 70(9.8) = **686 N**; on the Moon = 70(1.62) = **113 N**; mass = **70 kg** on the Moon.
7. a = (30 − 10)/5.0 = **4.0 m/s²**.
8. a = g sin 30° = **4.9 m/s²**; N = mg cos 30° = 10(9.8)(0.866) = **85 N**.
9. T − mg = ma → T = 2.0(9.8 + 1.5) = **22.6 N**.
10. (a) N = 60(9.8 + 2.0) = **708 N**. (b) N = 60(9.8 − 2.0) = **468 N**. (c) N = 60(9.8) = **588 N**.
11. N = 196 N. (a) Maximum static friction = 0.50(196) = 98 N, and 40 N < 98 N, so the box doesn't move and **friction = 40 N**. (b) just over **98 N**. (c) f_k = 0.30(196) = **59 N**.
12. Drag = weight = 80(9.8) = **784 N**; a = **0**.
13. Slope of a vs. F ≈ (0.80 − 0.21)/(0.40 − 0.10) ≈ 1.97 (m/s²)/N, so m = 1/slope ≈ **0.51 kg**, consistent with 0.50 kg.
14. Whole system: a = 10/5.0 = **2.0 m/s²**. For the 2.0 kg block: F_contact = 2.0(2.0) = **4.0 N**.
