# Unit 5: Work, Energy, and Power

**NGSS:** HS-PS3-1, HS-PS3-2, HS-PS3-3 (and a first look at HS-PS3-4) | **Quiz:** `../../quizzes/index.html` → Work & Energy | **Pacing:** ~4 weeks

## Learning objectives (I can...)
1. Calculate work done by a constant force, including at an angle.
2. Calculate kinetic energy, gravitational potential energy, and elastic potential energy.
3. Apply the work-energy theorem and conservation of mechanical energy.
4. Use energy bar charts and system models to track energy transfers, including losses to thermal energy.
5. Calculate power and efficiency.
6. Design or refine a device that converts energy from one form to another (HS-PS3-3).

## Key concepts
- **Work:** W = Fd cosθ (joules). Only the force component along the displacement does work. Zero work if F ⟂ d (carrying a box level).
- **Kinetic energy:** KE = ½mv². Doubling speed → 4× KE.
- **Gravitational PE:** PE_g = mgh (relative to a chosen zero level).
- **Elastic PE:** PE_s = ½kx².
- **Work-energy theorem:** W_net = ΔKE.
- **Conservation:** in a closed system total energy is constant. If only conservative forces act, KE + PE is constant. Friction transfers mechanical energy to thermal energy (not lost, just not recoverable as motion).
- **Power:** P = W/t = Fv, unit watt (J/s). **Efficiency** = useful output / input.

## Cornell notes

| Cue | Notes |
|---|---|
| What is work in physics? | Energy transferred by a force through a displacement: W = Fd cosθ. No displacement, no work. Force perpendicular to motion, no work. |
| What are the units of work and energy? | Joule: 1 J = 1 N·m = 1 kg·m²/s². |
| When is work negative? | When the force opposes the motion (θ = 180°), e.g., friction or brakes. It removes energy. |
| What is kinetic energy and how does it scale? | KE = ½mv². Proportional to v²: 2× speed → 4× KE (and 4× braking distance). |
| What is gravitational potential energy? | PE = mgh, energy stored by position in Earth's field. Only changes in height matter; choose a zero level and stay consistent. |
| State the work-energy theorem. | Net work done on an object equals its change in kinetic energy: W_net = ½mv_f² − ½mv_i². |
| State conservation of energy. | Energy is never created or destroyed, only transferred or transformed. Without friction: ½mv² + mgh is constant. |
| Does mass matter for speed at the bottom of a frictionless slide? | No. mgh = ½mv² → v = √(2gh); mass cancels. |
| Where does "lost" energy go? | Friction and air drag convert mechanical energy into thermal energy (and sound). Total energy is still conserved. |
| What is power? | Rate of doing work: P = W/t. 1 W = 1 J/s. Also P = Fv. |
| Worked example: 2 kg dropped from 5 m | PE = 2(9.8)(5) = 98 J. At the ground KE = 98 J → v = √(2·98/2) = 9.9 m/s. |
| Worked example: 50 N force at 60° over 10 m | W = 50 × 10 × cos60° = 250 J. |
| Worked example: 1000 kg car at 20 m/s brakes with 5000 N | KE = ½(1000)(400) = 200,000 J. W = −F d → d = 200,000/5000 = 40 m. |
| Worked example: lift 60 kg by 3 m in 6 s | W = mgh = 60(9.8)(3) = 1764 J. P = 1764/6 = 294 W. |
| Worked example: spring k = 200 N/m compressed 0.10 m | PE = ½(200)(0.10²) = 1.0 J. |

## Most-missed questions
1. Believing you do work when carrying a heavy box horizontally at constant speed.
2. Forgetting the squared in KE (doubling speed doubles KE).
3. Saying the heavier object reaches higher speed on a frictionless slide.
4. Saying energy is "used up" instead of transformed.
5. Using mass instead of height change for PE comparisons, or choosing inconsistent zero levels.
6. Confusing work with power, or force with energy.
7. Ignoring the cos θ.

## Practice problems
1. W by a 30 N force pulling a sled 8 m at 0° to the ground. [240 J]
2. KE of a 0.145 kg baseball at 40 m/s. [116 J]
3. A 3 kg book is raised 1.5 m. PE gained? [44.1 J]
4. A 1.0 kg ball is thrown up at 10 m/s. Max height using energy? [5.1 m]
5. A roller-coaster car (500 kg) starts at rest at 30 m. Speed at 10 m (no friction)? [19.8 m/s]
6. A 70 kg student runs up stairs rising 4.0 m in 5.0 s. Average power? [549 W]
7. A motor takes in 500 J and does 350 J of useful work. Efficiency? [70%]
8. A 2.0 kg block slides 3.0 m on a surface with μ_k = 0.25 after being given 20 J of KE. Does it stop? [Friction work = 0.25(2.0)(9.8)(3.0) = 14.7 J < 20 J, so no; KE left = 5.3 J]
9. Draw energy bar charts for a ball tossed up (start, top, just before landing).

## Labs and design challenge
- **Ramp energy lab:** release a cart from several heights; measure speed with a photogate; graph v² vs h (slope = 2g if ideal). Discuss why measured slope is lower (friction).
- **Spring lab:** find k from F vs x; launch a cart and test ½kx² = ½mv².
- **Roller-coaster / energy-converter design (HS-PS3-3):** build a track or small device that converts stored energy into motion or electricity within constraints; calculate efficiency and refine.

## Real-world applications
- Roller coasters, ski jumps, and pendulums (PE ↔ KE).
- Hydroelectric dams and wind turbines (energy conversion).
- Hybrid and electric cars regenerative braking.
- Appliance wattage, electric bills (kWh), and human power output.
- Why braking distance grows with the square of speed.
