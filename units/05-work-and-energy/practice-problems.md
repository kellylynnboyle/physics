# Practice Problems: Work & Energy

Use g = 9.8 m/s². Draw an energy bar chart for problems 4-9 and 12. Answer key at the bottom.

## Set A: Work and power (E1, E6)

1. A 40 N force pulls a box 6.0 m along the floor. Find the work if the force is (a) horizontal, (b) at 30° above horizontal.
2. A student lifts a 12 kg box 1.5 m straight up at constant speed in 2.0 s. Find the work done and the power.
3. Does a waiter do work on a tray when he carries it horizontally at constant velocity across the room? Explain.

## Set B: Energy and conservation (E2, E3, E4)

4. A 1000 kg car is moving at 15 m/s. Find its KE. How much work is needed to speed it to 30 m/s?
5. A 50 kg skateboarder starts from rest at the top of a 3.0 m frictionless ramp. Find her speed at the bottom.
6. A spring (k = 200 N/m) is compressed 0.10 m and used to launch a 0.050 kg ball horizontally on a frictionless surface. Find the launch speed.
7. A pendulum bob is raised 0.20 m above its lowest point and released. Find its speed at the bottom.
8. A 500 kg roller-coaster car crests a 20 m hill at 2.0 m/s and then reaches the ground level. Find its speed at the bottom, ignoring friction.

## Set C: Friction and efficiency (E5, E6)

9. A 1200 kg car moving at 20 m/s brakes to a stop. The friction coefficient is 0.70. Find the stopping distance and the thermal energy produced.
10. A motor draws 800 J of electrical energy and lifts a 5.0 kg load 10 m. Find the efficiency.
11. A 2.0 kg block slides down a rough 3.0 m high ramp and reaches the bottom at 5.0 m/s. How much thermal energy was produced?

## Set D: Challenge (extending)

12. A 0.10 kg ball is dropped from 2.0 m onto a spring (k = 400 N/m) on the floor. Ignoring the spring's own height, find the maximum compression of the spring. (Include the compression distance in the fall.)
13. Show that doubling the speed of a car multiplies its stopping distance by 4 using the work-energy theorem.
14. Design question (HS-PS3-3): A hand-crank flashlight turns 60 J of mechanical work per minute into 12 J of light. Identify the energy conversions, calculate the efficiency, and propose one way to improve it.

## Answer key

1. (a) W = 40(6.0)cos 0° = **240 J**. (b) W = 40(6.0)cos 30° = **208 J**.
2. W = mgh = 12(9.8)(1.5) = **176 J** (about 180 J). P = 176/2.0 = **88 W**.
3. **No** work is done on the tray by the lifting force: it is vertical while displacement is horizontal (θ = 90°, cos 90° = 0). (He does do work internally with his body, but not on the tray in the physics sense.)
4. KE = ½(1000)(15)² = **112,500 J** (1.1 × 10⁵ J). At 30 m/s KE = 450,000 J, so W = **337,500 J** (3.4 × 10⁵ J).
5. v = √(2 × 9.8 × 3.0) = **7.7 m/s**.
6. ½kx² = ½(200)(0.10)² = 1.0 J. ½(0.050)v² = 1.0 → v = √40 = **6.3 m/s**.
7. v = √(2 × 9.8 × 0.20) = **2.0 m/s**.
8. ½mv_f² = ½mv_i² + mgh → v_f = √(2² + 2 × 9.8 × 20) = √396 = **19.9 m/s** (about 20 m/s).
9. KE = ½(1200)(400) = 240,000 J. f_k = μmg = 0.70(1200)(9.8) = 8232 N. d = 240000/8232 = **29 m**. Thermal energy = **2.4 × 10⁵ J** (if the car stops).
10. Useful = mgh = 5.0(9.8)(10) = 490 J. Efficiency = 490/800 = **61%**.
11. Initial PE = 2.0(9.8)(3.0) = 58.8 J. Final KE = ½(2.0)(25) = 25 J. Thermal = **33.8 J** (about 34 J).
12. Energy: mg(2.0 + x) = ½kx² → 0.98(2.0 + x) = 200x² → 200x² − 0.98x − 1.96 = 0 → x = [0.98 + √(0.96 + 1568)]/400 = (0.98 + 39.6)/400 = **0.10 m**.
13. W_friction = f d = ΔKE → d = mv²/(2f). With the same f and m, d ∝ v², so doubling v multiplies d by **4**.
14. Mechanical (hand) → electrical (generator) → light (LED) + thermal. Efficiency = 12/60 = **20%**. Improve by reducing friction in the gears, using a more efficient generator or LED, or adding a capacitor to store energy.
