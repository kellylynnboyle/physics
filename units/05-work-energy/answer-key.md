# Unit 5: Work & Energy — Answer Key (instructors)

## Quiz

| # | Answer | Why | Mistake this question targets | Objective |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **D.** 0 J | Work requires displacement. The barbell does not move, so W = F × 0 = 0 J, even though her muscles tire. | Equating effort or force with work. | |
| 2 | **D.** It becomes four times as large | KE = ½·m·v². Speed is squared, so 2² = 4 times the kinetic energy. | Treating kinetic energy as proportional to speed. | |
| 3 | **C.** The speeds are the same | Both lose the same potential energy, m·g·h, so both gain the same kinetic energy. The ramp ball takes longer but arrives with the same speed. | Confusing time taken with final speed. | |
| 4 | **C.** Four times the distance | The brakes must remove all the kinetic energy: F·d = ½·m·v². Double the speed means four times the energy and four times the distance. | Assuming stopping distance doubles when speed doubles. | |
| 5 | **A.** Zero | The supporting force is vertical and the displacement is horizontal. A force perpendicular to the motion does no work (cos 90° = 0). | Multiplying force by distance without checking directions. | |
| 6 | **B.** They have the same speed; the heavy ball has more kinetic energy | Speed from v = √(2·g·h) does not depend on mass. Kinetic energy, ½·m·v², does, so the heavier ball has more. | Thinking same speed means same energy, or that heavier falls faster. | |
| 7 | **B.** It became thermal energy in the box and floor | Friction transforms kinetic energy into thermal energy; the surfaces warm slightly. Energy is never destroyed. | Saying energy is "lost" or "used up". | |
| 8 | **C.** They do the same work; Ana has more power | Work = weight × height is the same for both. Power = work / time, so Ana, who takes half the time, has twice the power. | Mixing up work and power. | |
| 9 | **B.** At the lowest point | At the lowest point the potential energy is smallest, so the kinetic energy and the speed are greatest. At the ends the bob is momentarily at rest. | Placing maximum speed at the ends of the swing. | |
| 10 | **B.** 9.9 m/s | m·g·h = ½·m·v², so v = √(2 × 9.8 × 5.0) = √98 = 9.9 m/s. The 98 is the energy in joules, not the speed. | Stopping at the energy value or forgetting the square root. | |

## Practice problems

1. A worker pulls a crate 12 m with a 200 N rope angled 30° above the floor. How much work does the rope do?  
   **Answer:** W = 200 × 12 × cos 30° = 2080 J (about 2100 J).
2. Find the kinetic energy of a 0.145 kg baseball thrown at 40 m/s.  
   **Answer:** KE = ½ × 0.145 × 40² = 116 J.
3. A 50 kg diver stands on a 10 m platform. Find the potential energy relative to the water and the speed on reaching the water.  
   **Answer:** PE = 50 × 9.8 × 10 = 4900 J; v = √(2 × 9.8 × 10) = 14 m/s.
4. A 2.0 kg block slides down a 3.0 m high ramp from rest and reaches the bottom at 6.0 m/s. How much energy did friction transform to heat?  
   **Answer:** PE at top = 58.8 J; KE at bottom = 36 J; thermal energy = 22.8 J.
5. A motor lifts a 400 N load 5.0 m in 8.0 s. Find its power output.  
   **Answer:** W = 2000 J; P = 2000 / 8.0 = 250 W.
6. A car's speed rises from 15 m/s to 30 m/s. By what factor do its kinetic energy and its braking distance (same braking force) change?  
   **Answer:** Both increase by a factor of 4.
