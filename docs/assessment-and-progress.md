# Assessment Criteria and Progress Tracking

## Assessment plan

| Type | Purpose | Frequency | Weight (suggested) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Interactive quiz ([quiz/index.html](../quiz/index.html)) | Practice and diagnose misconceptions; self-check | Anytime, unlimited retakes | Not graded (formative) |
| Cornell notes check | Note-taking and review habits | Each unit | 10% |
| Practice problems and homework | Fluency | Each class | 15% |
| Lab or design challenge (claim–evidence–reasoning) | Science and engineering practices; NGSS performance expectations | 1 per unit | 25% |
| Unit test | Mastery of objectives | End of each unit | 40% |
| Reflection / error analysis | Metacognition | Each unit | 10% |

Adjust weights to match your department or school policy.

## Four-level mastery scale

| Level | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| **4 Advanced** | Applies concepts in new or multi-step contexts; explains reasoning with multiple representations; spots and corrects errors. |
| **3 Proficient** | Meets the objective: correct setup, equations, units and explanation in familiar contexts. |
| **2 Developing** | Partial understanding: correct on simple cases; errors in setup, signs, units or reasoning on harder ones. |
| **1 Beginning** | Major misconceptions or cannot start the task independently. |

**Proficiency target:** a score of 3 on every objective by the end of the unit. Any objective at 1 or 2 triggers a reteach and reassessment.

The quiz score maps to a suggested level (≥ 90% → 4, 75–89% → 3, 60–74% → 2, < 60% → 1) as a *practice indicator only*. The teacher determines the official level from tests, labs and problem work.

## Lab / design challenge rubric (claim–evidence–reasoning)

| Criterion | 4 | 3 | 2 | 1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Claim | Precise, testable, tied to the physics law | Clear and relevant | Vague or partly off-target | Missing |
| Evidence (data) | Complete, organized, graphed, with uncertainty | Complete and organized | Incomplete or messy | Missing |
| Reasoning | Connects data to law/equation and addresses alternative explanations | Connects data to law/equation | Restates data without connection | Missing |
| Error analysis | Identifies sources and their direction and size, proposes improvements | Identifies sources | Generic ("human error") | Missing |
| Design iteration (design tasks) | Multiple tested iterations with data-driven changes | One tested revision | Build without testing | Missing |

## Instructor notes

- **Tailoring the quizzes:** the "most-missed" questions are chosen from misconceptions widely documented in physics education research (for example, in force and motion concept inventories). After a unit, replace or reorder questions in `quiz/questions.js` using your own class's item analysis.
- **Retakes:** quiz questions and answer order are shuffled each attempt. Best score per topic is stored in the student's browser only; nothing is sent anywhere.
- **Reteach triggers:** if more than 30% of the class misses the same quiz item, use it as a bell-ringer discussion with a demo or simulation.
