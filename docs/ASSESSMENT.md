# Assessment Criteria

How progress is measured in every unit. Objectives and their evidence are listed in each unit plan (`units/<unit>/README.md`).

## Proficiency scale

Each learning objective is scored on this scale.

| Level | Name | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | Extending | Applies the idea to a new or combined situation without help, and explains why the method works. |
| 3 | Proficient | Solves standard problems correctly with units and a sense check; explains the concept in their own words. **This is the target.** |
| 2 | Developing | Solves problems with a worked example or hints; makes errors in setup, signs, or units; explanation is partly correct. |
| 1 | Beginning | Recalls terms or formulas but cannot yet apply them. |

A unit is complete when every objective is at level 3 or higher.

## Evidence collected in each unit

| Evidence | What it measures | Mastery criterion | Suggested weight |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cornell notes self-test | Recall of key ideas | Answers every cue aloud from memory on two different days | Completion |
| Interactive quiz (most-missed questions) | Conceptual understanding and misconceptions | 7 of 8 on the first try (80% or better) | 10% |
| Practice problems | Problem-solving method | 5 of 6 correct with knowns, equation, units, and sense check shown | 15% |
| IXL practice plan | Skill fluency | SmartScore 80+ on every listed skill | 10% |
| Lab report | Science practices | Level 3 or higher on the lab rubric below | 25% |
| Unit test | All objectives | 80% or better; any objective below level 3 is retaught and retested | 40% |

Weights are a starting point. Set them to match the course grading policy.

## Lab report rubric

| Criterion | 4 | 3 | 2 | 1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Question and variables | Testable question; independent, dependent, and controlled variables all identified and justified | Testable question; all three kinds of variables identified | Question stated; one kind of variable missing or wrong | Question unclear; variables not identified |
| Data | Repeated trials, organized table, units, appropriate significant figures, uncertainty noted | Repeated trials, organized table, units | Single trials or missing units | Data incomplete |
| Graph and analysis | Correct graph with best-fit line; slope or area interpreted with units and compared with the predicted value | Correct graph; slope or area calculated and interpreted | Graph has errors, or the calculation is not interpreted | No graph or analysis |
| Claim, evidence, reasoning | Claim answers the question; cites specific data; reasoning links the data to the physics principle and addresses sources of error | Claim, supporting data, and a correct physics principle | Claim with weak or missing evidence or reasoning | Claim only, or no claim |

## Engineering design rubric (Units 4 and 5)

| Criterion | 4 | 3 | 2 | 1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Criteria and constraints | Measurable criteria and all constraints stated; trade-offs discussed | Criteria and constraints stated | Some criteria or constraints missing | Not stated |
| Physics reasoning | Design choices justified with the governing equation (FΔt = Δp, or energy conservation) and estimated values | Design choices justified with the correct principle | Principle named but not connected to the design | No physics reasoning |
| Testing and refinement | Several tests; one variable changed at a time; improvement shown with data | At least one data-based improvement | Tested once with no revision | Not tested |

## Tracking progress

- Students record levels and checklist items in the [progress tracker](PROGRESS-TRACKER.md).
- After each quiz, students note the "common mistake" label of every question they missed. Those labels tell the teacher which misconception to reteach.
- Before the unit test, any objective still at level 1 or 2 gets a targeted review: reread that section of the Cornell notes, redo its worked examples, and repeat the matching IXL skill.
- Spaced review: redo the quiz for earlier units once every two to three weeks. The quiz saves each student's best first-try score in their browser.
