# Assessment Criteria and Progress Tracking

## 1. How mastery is judged
Every learning objective (K1, P2, N3, …) is scored on a four-level scale. Evidence can come from quizzes, practice problems, labs, or whiteboard explanations.

| Level | Label | What it looks like |
|-------|-------|--------------------|
| 4 | Extending | Correct and efficient, **and** explains why, transfers to an unfamiliar context, or catches an error in a worked example. |
| 3 | **Proficient (mastery)** | Correct setup, correct physics, correct units, in at least 4 of 5 problems. Minor arithmetic slips only. |
| 2 | Developing | Right idea but missing steps, wrong sign or direction, wrong equation, or units missing. Needs prompting. |
| 1 | Beginning | Cannot start independently, or a major misconception (see the quiz "Misconception" tags). |

**Mastery target:** level 3 or higher on every objective by the unit exam. Re-assessment is allowed: re-take the quiz and submit corrected practice problems with a short note on what was misunderstood.

## 2. Problem-solving rubric (for any numerical problem)
| Criterion | 2 pts | 1 pt | 0 pts |
|-----------|-------|------|-------|
| Diagram / free-body diagram / graph | Complete and labeled | Partial | None |
| Knowns, unknown, and equation | All stated, equation matches | Some missing | Missing |
| Substitution and algebra | Correct with units carried | Minor error | Major error |
| Answer, units, and significant figures | Correct with units | Unit or sig-fig error | Wrong |
| Reasonableness check | Explained | Mentioned | None |

## 3. Weighting (suggested; instructors may adjust)
| Component | Weight |
|-----------|--------|
| Unit exams | 30% |
| Quizzes (interactive quiz + in-class) | 15% |
| Labs and design projects (HS-PS2-3, HS-PS3-3) | 25% |
| Cornell notes (completeness + summary quality) | 10% |
| Practice problems / IXL | 10% |
| Participation and whiteboarding | 10% |

## 4. Cornell notes rubric (out of 10)
| Criterion | Points |
|-----------|--------|
| Cues written as questions or keywords | 2 |
| Notes accurate and complete (equations, units, diagrams) | 3 |
| Summary in student's own words (2–3 sentences) | 3 |
| Self-quiz completed (cover notes, answer cues) | 2 |

## 5. Quiz score interpretation
The interactive quizzes use the most-missed questions, so they are **harder than typical**.

| Score (of 8) | Interpretation | Next step |
|--------------|----------------|-----------|
| 7–8 | Strong grasp of common pitfalls | Move on; try extending problems |
| 5–6 | Mostly solid | Review the misconceptions you missed |
| 3–4 | Gaps | Redo Cornell self-test, re-watch/read, retake |
| 0–2 | Not yet | Meet with the instructor; start with the simulation |

## 8. Common misconceptions at a glance (from the quizzes)
| Unit | Most-missed ideas |
|------|-------------------|
| Kinematics | Distance vs. displacement; reading dot diagrams (counting dots instead of gaps, closer = faster); zero velocity ≠ zero acceleration; negative acceleration ≠ slowing; heavier objects fall faster |
| Projectile | Horizontal speed affects fall time; everything is zero at the top; a "force of the throw" continues; sin vs. cos |
| Newton | Motion requires a force; bigger object hits harder; weight and normal force are a third-law pair; friction depends on area |
| Momentum | Speed alone determines momentum; inelastic collisions lose momentum; bounce vs. stick impulse; ignoring direction |
| Energy | Effort = work; KE ∝ v; path affects final speed; energy is "used up"; work vs. power |
