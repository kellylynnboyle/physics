# Assessment and Progress Tracking

[Project home](../README.md)

This page defines how mastery is judged and recorded across the five Semester 1 units. Each unit's own page lists its numbered learning objectives (1.1, 1.2, … 5.6) and the evidence for each.

## Proficiency scale

Score every learning objective on this scale.

| Score | Level | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | Extends | Solves unfamiliar or multi-step problems, explains the reasoning, and can correct someone else's misconception. |
| 3 | Meets | Solves standard problems correctly with units and direction, and explains the idea in their own words. **This is the target.** |
| 2 | Approaching | Solves problems with a worked example or hints; explanations are partly correct or rely on memorized phrases. |
| 1 | Beginning | Cannot yet start the problem, or the answer shows the common misconception. |

A unit is **mastered** when every objective is at 3 or above. Any objective at 1 or 2 gets a reteach and a retest.

## Evidence for each unit

| Evidence | What counts as meeting the standard | When |
|---|---|---|
| Interactive quiz (pre) | Not graded. Used to find which misconceptions the class holds. | First day of unit |
| Cornell notes cue self-test | Answers every cue question aloud from memory. | Ongoing, checked by a partner |
| Practice problems | At least 6 of 8 correct with work shown (5 of 6 in Unit 2). | Mid-unit |
| IXL practice plan | SmartScore 80+ on every listed skill. | Before the unit test |
| Lab report | 3 or above on every row of the lab rubric below. | After each lab |
| Interactive quiz (post) | 80% or higher (8 of 10). | Day before the unit test |
| Unit test | 3 or above on each objective. | End of unit |

## Lab report rubric

Used for every lab. The Claim, Evidence, Reasoning (CER) rows match the NGSS science and engineering practices.

| Row | 4 | 3 | 2 | 1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Question and variables | States a testable question; names independent, dependent and controlled variables and why each control matters | States the question and all three kinds of variable | Variables partly identified or mixed up | Question or variables missing |
| Data and graph | Organized table with units and repeated trials; correct graph with labeled axes, units and best-fit line | Table and graph complete with units | Table or graph missing units, labels or trials | Data incomplete |
| Claim | Answers the question precisely and states the mathematical relationship | Answers the question | Claim is vague or only partly matches the data | No claim |
| Evidence | Quotes specific values (slope, percent error or percent difference) from the data | Refers to the data accurately | Refers to the data in general terms | No evidence |
| Reasoning | Connects evidence to the physics principle and explains sources of error and their effect | Connects evidence to the correct principle | Names a principle without connecting it | No reasoning |

## Engineering design rubric (Units 4 and 5)

For the egg-drop device (HS-PS2-3) and the energy-conversion device (HS-PS3-3).

| Row | 4 | 3 | 2 | 1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Criteria and constraints | Quantified criteria and constraints, with trade-offs discussed | Criteria and constraints clearly stated | Stated loosely | Missing |
| Physics explanation | Explains each design feature with the governing equation and estimated values | Explains the design with the correct principle | Principle named but not applied | No physics |
| Testing and data | Multiple tests with recorded measurements | Tested with recorded results | Tested once, results informal | Not tested |
| Refinement | Each change justified by test evidence; improvement measured | At least one evidence-based change | Changes made without evidence | No refinement |

## Using the quiz results

Every quiz question targets one named misconception (listed under "Most commonly missed ideas" on each unit page). After the pre-quiz, tally which questions most of the class missed and spend class time on those. A question a student misses on both the pre-quiz and the post-quiz marks the objective for reteaching.

The quizzes run entirely in the browser and do not send scores anywhere. A student's best score is kept only on their own device, so students report their result or show the results screen.
