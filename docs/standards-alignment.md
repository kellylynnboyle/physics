# Standards Alignment

Source: California NGSS, Physics & Earth Science course model
(<https://www.cde.ca.gov/pd/ca/sc/ngssstandards.asp>). The performance expectations (PEs)
below are the standard NGSS high-school physical-science PEs used in that model.
Wording is paraphrased — **verify exact text and the course-model assignment on the CDE
page** before publishing to families.

| PE | Paraphrase | Units |
|----|-----------|-------|
| HS-PS2-1 | Analyze data to support the claim that Newton's second law describes the mathematical relationship among net force, mass, and acceleration | 1, 2, 3 |
| HS-PS2-2 | Use mathematical representations to support the claim that total momentum of a system is conserved when there is no net external force | 4 |
| HS-PS2-3 | Design, evaluate, and refine a device that minimizes the force on a macroscopic object during a collision | 4 |
| HS-PS3-1 | Create a computational model to calculate the change in energy of one component of a system when the change in energy of the other components and energy flows are known | 5 |
| HS-PS3-2 | Develop and use models to illustrate that energy at the macroscopic scale can be accounted for as motion of particles, or energy stored in fields (position) | 5 |
| HS-PS3-3 | Design, build, and refine a device that converts one form of energy into another, within given constraints | 5 |

Semester 2 PEs (HS-PS2-4/5, HS-PS3-5, HS-PS4-x, HS-PS1-8, HS-ESS2-x/ESS1-x for plate tectonics and
earthquakes) are out of scope here.

## Science & Engineering Practices emphasized

- Analyzing and interpreting data (graphs, lab tables)
- Using mathematics and computational thinking (kinematic equations, conservation laws)
- Planning and carrying out investigations
- Constructing explanations / designing solutions (crumple-zone and energy-conversion devices)

## Crosscutting concepts emphasized

- Cause and effect (forces → changes in motion)
- Systems and system models (defining the system for momentum and energy accounting)
- Energy and matter (conservation, flows)
- Patterns (linear vs. quadratic relationships in graphs)
