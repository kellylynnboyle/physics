# NGSS Alignment (California, Physics & Earth Science course model)

Source: https://www.cde.ca.gov/pd/ca/sc/ngssstandards.asp

## Semester 1 performance expectations

| Code | Performance expectation (abbreviated) | Units | Evidence in this project |
|---|---|---|---|
| HS-PS2-1 | Analyze data to support the claim that Newton's 2nd law describes the relationship among net force, mass, and acceleration | 1, 2, 3 | Motion data graphs, cart lab, projectile and 2nd-law problems |
| HS-PS2-2 | Use mathematical representations to support the claim that the total momentum of a system is conserved when there is no net force | 4 | Collision lab, conservation problems |
| HS-PS2-3 | Design, evaluate, and refine a device that minimizes the force on a macroscopic object during a collision | 3, 4 | Egg-drop or cart-bumper design challenge |
| HS-PS2-4 | Use mathematical representations of Newton's law of gravitation and Coulomb's law to describe and predict forces | 3 (gravity); Sem 2 (electric) | Gravitation problems, Gravity Force Lab |
| HS-PS3-1 | Create a computational model to calculate the change in energy of one component in a system when the change in energy of the other components and the energy flows are known | 5 | Spreadsheet or code energy model |
| HS-PS3-2 | Develop and use models to illustrate that energy at the macroscopic scale can be accounted for as motion of particles/objects or energy stored in fields | 5 | Energy bar charts, skate-park and spring labs |
| HS-PS3-3 | Design, build, and refine a device that works within given constraints to convert one form of energy into another | 5 | Energy-converter design report |
| HS-PS3-4 | Plan and conduct an investigation of thermal energy transfer between components to show energy is conserved | 5 (intro), Sem 2 | Friction-to-thermal discussion; calorimetry later |

Kinematics (Unit 1) and Projectile Motion (Unit 2) are not stand-alone performance expectations in NGSS. They supply the mathematical tools students need for HS-PS2-1.

## Cross-cutting concepts used
Cause and effect; systems and system models; energy and matter (conservation); patterns; scale, proportion, and quantity.
