# Standards Alignment (California NGSS, HS Physics)

Source for the official wording: https://www.cde.ca.gov/pd/ca/sc/ngssstandards.asp. The statements below are paraphrased summaries; confirm against the CDE text, including clarification statements and assessment boundaries, before publishing to families.

## Performance expectations used in Semester 1

| Code | Paraphrased expectation | Units |
|---|---|---|
| **HS-PS2-1** | Analyze data to support the claim that Newton's second law describes the mathematical relationship among net force, mass, and acceleration | Kinematics, Projectile Motion, Newton's Laws |
| **HS-PS2-2** | Use mathematical representations to support the claim that the total momentum of a system of objects is conserved when there is no net force on the system | Momentum |
| **HS-PS2-3** | Apply scientific and engineering ideas to design, evaluate, and refine a device that minimizes the force on a macroscopic object during a collision | Momentum |
| **HS-PS3-1** | Create a computational model to calculate the change in energy of one component of a system when the change in energy of the other components and the energy flows in and out are known | Work & Energy |
| **HS-PS3-2** | Develop and use models to illustrate that energy at the macroscopic scale can be accounted for as a combination of energy associated with the motions of particles (objects) and energy associated with the relative positions of particles (objects) | Work & Energy |
| **HS-PS3-3** | Design, build, and refine a device that works within given constraints to convert one form of energy into another form of energy | Work & Energy |

**Note on Kinematics and Projectile Motion.** NGSS has no standalone performance expectation for these. They are the mathematical tools students need to meet HS-PS2-1 (acceleration is the quantity Newton's second law predicts), so they are taught as supporting content.

Semester 2 standards (HS-PS2-4, HS-PS2-5, HS-PS3-5, HS-PS4-x, HS-PS1-8, HS-ESS2-x, HS-ESS1-x) will be added with the second-semester project.

## Disciplinary core ideas (DCIs)

| DCI | Idea | Units |
|---|---|---|
| PS2.A Forces and Motion | Newton's second law, F = ma; momentum conservation; forces on interacting objects are equal and opposite | 3, 4 |
| PS3.A Definitions of Energy | Energy is a quantity conserved in a system; it can be transferred; energy at the macroscopic scale = motion + position | 5 |
| PS3.B Conservation of Energy and Energy Transfer | Total energy within a closed system is constant; energy flows in and out; mechanical energy lost to thermal energy | 5 |
| PS3.C Relationship Between Energy and Forces | Work is done when a force acts through a distance; energy stored in fields | 5 |
| ETS1.A/B/C Engineering design | Define criteria and constraints; evaluate solutions; optimize | 4 (HS-PS2-3), 5 (HS-PS3-3) |

## Crosscutting Concepts (CCCs)

- **Systems and System Models**: defining the system is the first step in momentum and energy problems.
- **Cause and Effect**: net force causes acceleration.
- **Patterns**: linear/quadratic relationships in motion graphs.
- **Energy and Matter**: conservation and transfer.
- **Stability and Change**: balanced vs. unbalanced forces.
- **Scale, Proportion, and Quantity**: how KE, PE, and stopping distance scale with speed, height, and mass.

## Math connections (CA CCSS-Math)

Algebraic manipulation of formulas (A-CED, A-REI), quadratic relationships (F-IF, A-REI.4), right-triangle trigonometry for vector components (G-SRT.6-8), vectors and their components (N-VM.1-3), reading and interpreting slopes and areas of graphs (F-IF.6, S-ID.6).
