# Standards Alignment (California NGSS)

Source of the learning goals: California NGSS, <https://www.cde.ca.gov/pd/ca/sc/ngssstandards.asp>.
Performance expectations below are paraphrased from memory of the NGSS text; **confirm exact wording on the CDE site before publishing to students.**

## Performance expectations covered in Semester 1

| Code | Performance expectation (paraphrased) | Units |
|---|---|---|
| HS-PS2-1 | Analyze data to support the claim that Newton's second law describes the relationship among net force, mass and acceleration of a macroscopic object. | 1, 2 (foundation), 3 (primary) |
| HS-PS2-2 | Use mathematical representations to support the claim that total momentum of a system is conserved when there is no net force on the system. | 4 |
| HS-PS2-3 | Apply science and engineering ideas to design, evaluate and refine a device that minimizes the force on a macroscopic object during a collision. | 4 |
| HS-PS3-1 | Create a computational model to calculate the change in energy of one component of a system when the changes in energy of the other components and the energy flows in and out are known. | 5 |
| HS-PS3-2 | Develop and use models to illustrate that energy at the macroscopic scale can be accounted for as motions of particles or energy stored in fields. | 5 |
| HS-PS3-3 | Design, build and refine a device that works within given constraints to convert one form of energy into another form. | 5 |
| HS-PS3-4 | Plan and conduct an investigation to show that thermal energy transfers lead to a more uniform energy distribution (extension via friction and energy losses). | 5 (extension) |

Kinematics and Projectile Motion are not performance expectations in themselves. They are the prerequisite mathematics and modeling skills for HS-PS2-1 and are therefore treated as foundation units.

## Science and engineering practices emphasized

| Practice | Where it shows up |
|---|---|
| Analyzing and interpreting data | Kinematics graphs, a = F/m lab |
| Using mathematics and computational thinking | Projectile components, collision conservation, energy spreadsheet model |
| Developing and using models | Free-body diagrams, energy bar charts |
| Constructing explanations / engaging in argument from evidence | Claim–evidence–reasoning lab reports |
| Designing solutions | Crash-force design challenge, energy conversion device |

## Crosscutting concepts emphasized

Cause and effect · Systems and system models · Energy and matter (conservation, flows) · Patterns · Stability and change

## Common Core connections

- CCSS.MATH.CONTENT.HSN-Q (units and quantities), HSF-IF (interpreting functions, graphs), HSG-SRT (right-triangle trigonometry for vector components).
- CCSS ELA/Literacy RST.11-12 and WHST.11-12 (reading technical text, writing lab arguments).
