# Standards, Learning Objectives and Assessment

## 1. Standards alignment (Semester 1)

Source: California NGSS, https://www.cde.ca.gov/pd/ca/sc/ngssstandards.asp. The performance expectations (PEs) below are the high school physical science PEs that Semester 1 mechanics supports. Verify wording and codes against the live page before distributing.

| PE | Summary (paraphrased) | Units |
|---|---|---|
| HS-PS2-1 | Analyze data to support the claim that Newton's second law describes the mathematical relationship among net force, mass and acceleration. | 1, 2, 3 |
| HS-PS2-2 | Use mathematical representations to support the claim that the total momentum of a system is conserved when there is no net external force. | 4 |
| HS-PS2-3 | Design, evaluate and refine a device that minimizes the force on a macroscopic object during a collision. | 3, 4 |
| HS-PS3-1 | Create a computational model to calculate the change in energy of one component of a system when the change in energy of the other components and the energy flows are known. | 5 |
| HS-PS3-2 | Develop and use models to illustrate that energy at the macroscopic scale can be accounted for as motion, or as energy stored in fields (position). | 5 |
| HS-PS3-3 | Design, build and refine a device that converts one form of energy into another within given constraints. | 5 |

Kinematics and projectile motion are not a PE on their own. They supply the motion data and the mathematical tools (Science and Engineering Practices: analyzing data, using mathematics) that HS-PS2-1 depends on.

Semester 2 (preview, to be built the same way): HS-PS2-4 and HS-PS2-5 (gravitational and electric forces, magnetism), HS-PS4-1 to HS-PS4-5 (waves and light), HS-ESS2-3 (plate tectonics and the Earth's interior), HS-PS1-8 (nuclear processes).

Cross-cutting concepts used throughout: Patterns, Cause and Effect, Systems and System Models, Energy and Matter (conservation), Scale/Proportion/Quantity.

## 2. Learning objectives by unit

Each objective is written "I can...". Use the ID when you track progress. Level: **K** = knowledge/recall, **A** = apply to a problem, **E** = explain, analyze or design.

### Unit 1: Kinematics
| ID | Objective | Level |
|---|---|---|
| K1 | Distinguish distance/displacement and speed/velocity, with units and vector or scalar status. | K |
| K2 | Define acceleration and predict whether an object speeds up, slows down or turns from signs of v and a. | E |
| K3 | Read and sketch position-time and velocity-time graphs; use slope and area. | A |
| K4 | Solve constant-acceleration problems with the four kinematic equations. | A |
| K5 | Model free fall with a = 9.8 m/s^2 downward, including the top of the path. | A |
| K6 | Collect motion data (motion detector or video) and use it to fit a graph or equation. | E |

### Unit 2: Projectile Motion
| ID | Objective | Level |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | Explain that horizontal and vertical motion are independent and describe each. | E |
| P2 | Resolve a launch velocity into components. | A |
| P3 | Solve horizontal-launch problems (time, range, impact velocity). | A |
| P4 | Solve angled-launch problems (peak height, time of flight, range). | A |
| P5 | Predict how angle and speed change range, and identify complementary angles. | E |
| P6 | Test a prediction experimentally and compare it with measured range, with sources of error. | E |

### Unit 3: Newton's Laws
| ID | Objective | Level |
|---|---|---|
| N1 | State and apply the first law (inertia, equilibrium). | E |
| N2 | Draw correct free-body diagrams. | A |
| N3 | Apply F_net = ma in one dimension, including friction, tension and incline components. | A |
| N4 | Identify third-law pairs and explain why they do not cancel. | E |
| N5 | Distinguish mass and weight; explain apparent weight in an accelerating elevator. | E |
| N6 | Use data to show that acceleration is proportional to net force and inversely proportional to mass (HS-PS2-1). | E |

### Unit 4: Momentum
| ID | Objective | Level |
|---|---|---|
| M1 | Calculate momentum and impulse; relate impulse to change in momentum. | A |
| M2 | Explain how extending collision time reduces force (airbags, crumple zones). | E |
| M3 | Apply conservation of momentum to collisions and explosions in 1-D. | A |
| M4 | Classify collisions as elastic, inelastic or perfectly inelastic using kinetic energy. | E |
| M5 | Design and test a device that protects an egg in a collision (HS-PS2-3). | E |

### Unit 5: Work and Energy
| ID | Objective | Level |
|---|---|---|
| W1 | Calculate work for forces parallel and at an angle to displacement. | A |
| W2 | Calculate kinetic, gravitational potential and elastic potential energy. | A |

| W4 | Apply conservation of mechanical energy and account for friction as thermal energy. | A |
| W5 | Calculate power and efficiency. | A |
| W6 | Build an energy bar chart or model for a system (HS-PS3-1, HS-PS3-2). | E |
| W7 | Design a device that converts one form of energy to another and evaluate its efficiency (HS-PS3-3). | E |

## 3. Assessment criteria

### Mastery levels (use for every objective)

| Level | Score | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 4 Extending | 90-100% | Solves new, multi-step problems; explains why; catches and corrects errors. |
| 3 Proficient | 80-89% | Solves standard problems with correct setup, units and sign; explains the main idea. |
| 2 Developing | 60-79% | Correct idea but errors in setup, signs or units; needs prompts. |
| 1 Beginning | below 60% | Cannot yet set up the problem or explain the concept. |

**Target: Level 3 on every objective before the unit test.** A student who is at Level 1 or 2 on an objective after the unit quiz returns to the Cornell cues and the missed-question review, then retakes.

### Problem-solving rubric (4 points per problem)

| Criterion | 1 pt |
|---|---|
| Diagram | Labeled sketch, free-body diagram or graph with a stated positive direction. |
| Given and unknown | Known values with units; the target quantity named. |
| Equation and substitution | Correct equation chosen and values substituted with units. |
| Answer | Correct number, units and sign, with a one-line sense check. |

### Lab report rubric (20 points)

| Criterion | 4 | 3 | 2 | 1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Question and hypothesis | Testable, with a reasoned prediction | Testable, prediction weakly reasoned | Vague | Missing |
| Data and graph | Organized table with units and uncertainty; correct graph | Minor labeling errors | Incomplete | Missing |
| Analysis | Uses graph or equation; compares with theory quantitatively | Quantitative with small errors | Qualitative only | Missing |
| Error and improvement | Specific sources, effect on result, a concrete fix | Sources named, effect unclear | Generic ("human error") | Missing |
| Conclusion (CER) | Claim, evidence and reasoning fully linked to the question | Mostly linked | Claim without evidence | Missing |

### Quiz and test expectations

- Interactive quiz (`quizzes/index.html`): practice and diagnostic. Score of 80% or higher on a unit marks the unit "quiz-ready". Missed questions are listed with explanations.
- Unit test blueprint (about 45 min):

| Part | Weight | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Concept / multiple choice | 30% | Misconceptions from the quiz bank, graph reading |
| Calculation problems | 40% | 3-4 problems scored with the problem-solving rubric |
| Explain / design | 20% | One CER or free-body-diagram explanation |
| Data analysis | 10% | Interpret a table or graph from a lab |

### Suggested grade weights (adjust locally)

Unit tests 35%, labs and projects 25%, quizzes and practice 15%, homework/notes completion 10%, semester capstone 15%.

## 5. Semester capstone (HS-PS2-3, HS-PS3-3)

Design, build and test a device that uses at least two units (for example: an egg-drop container using momentum and energy, a rubber-band or spring launcher using projectile motion and elastic energy, or a ramp-and-cart safety bumper using Newton's laws and momentum). Deliverables: design sketch, prediction with calculations, measured data, a CER conclusion, and one refinement.

Capstone rubric (24 points): design and constraints (4), physics prediction with correct calculations (8), data and analysis (6), refinement based on evidence (3), communication (3).
