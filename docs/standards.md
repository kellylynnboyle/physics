# Standards Alignment (NGSS - California)

Source: California Department of Education NGSS page - https://www.cde.ca.gov/pd/ca/sc/ngssstandards.asp
Standards below are the Physical Science performance expectations used as this course's learning goals. Wording is paraphrased from the NGSS HS-PS performance expectations; confirm against the CDE page before publishing.

| Standard | Performance expectation | Units |
|---|---|---|
| HS-PS2-1 | Analyze data to support the claim that Newton's second law of motion describes the mathematical relationship among the net force on a macroscopic object, its mass, and its acceleration. | 1. Kinematics, 2. Projectile Motion, 3. Newton's Laws |
| HS-PS2-2 | Use mathematical representations to support the claim that the total momentum of a system of objects is conserved when there is no net force on the system. | 4. Momentum |
| HS-PS2-3 | Apply scientific and engineering ideas to design, evaluate, and refine a device that minimizes the force on a macroscopic object during a collision. | 3. Newton's Laws, 4. Momentum |
| HS-PS3-1 | Create a computational model to calculate the change in the energy of one component in a system when the change in energy of the other component(s) and energy flows in and out of the system are known. | 5. Work & Energy |
| HS-PS3-2 | Develop and use models to illustrate that energy at the macroscopic scale can be accounted for as either motion of particles or energy stored in fields (position). | 5. Work & Energy |
| HS-PS3-3 | Design, build, and refine a device that works within given constraints to convert one form of energy into another form of energy. | 5. Work & Energy |

Kinematics and projectile motion are not stand-alone NGSS performance expectations; they are prerequisite skills for HS-PS2-1.
Semester 2 topics (electricity, magnetism, waves, light, plate tectonics, earthquakes, nuclear physics) will map to HS-PS2-4/5, HS-PS3-5, HS-PS4-1/3/5, HS-PS1-8, and HS-ESS2/ESS1 and are outside this project's scope.
