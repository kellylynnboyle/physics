# 5. Work & Energy

*Suggested pacing: ~3 weeks* · *Big idea:* Energy is transferred and transformed but total energy in a closed system is conserved. Work is the transfer of energy by a force.

## California NGSS performance expectations

- **HS-PS3-1** — Create a computational model to calculate the change in the energy of one component in a system when the change in energy of the other component(s) and the energy flows in and out of the system are known.
- **HS-PS3-2** — Develop and use models to illustrate that energy at the macroscopic scale can be accounted for as a combination of energy associated with the motions of particles (objects) and energy associated with the relative positions of particles (objects).
- **HS-PS3-3** — Design, build, and refine a device that works within given constraints to convert one form of energy into another form of energy.

- *Science & Engineering Practices:* Developing and using models; using computational thinking; designing solutions.
- *Crosscutting Concepts:* Energy and matter (conservation); systems and system models.

## Learning objectives (students can…)

- [ ] Calculate work for forces parallel and at an angle to displacement.
- [ ] Calculate KE, gravitational PE, and elastic PE and interpret energy bar charts.
- [ ] Apply the work–energy theorem and conservation of energy to solve problems (HS-PS3-1, HS-PS3-2).
- [ ] Account for dissipated energy and calculate power and efficiency.
- [ ] Design, test, and refine an energy-conversion device against constraints (HS-PS3-3).

## Key concepts

| Concept | What to master |
|---|---|
| **Work** | W = Fd cosθ (joules). Only the force component along the displacement does work. |
| **Kinetic energy** | KE = ½mv². Depends on v², so doubling speed quadruples KE. |
| **Gravitational PE** | PE_g = mgh, relative to a chosen zero level. |
| **Elastic PE** | PE_s = ½kx² for a spring. |
| **Work–energy theorem** | W_net = ΔKE. |
| **Conservation of energy** | KE + PE (+ thermal) stays constant in a closed system; with friction, mechanical energy becomes thermal energy. |
| **Power & efficiency** | P = W/t (watts). Efficiency = useful output / total input. |

**Equations:** `W = Fd cosθ` · `KE = ½mv²` · `PE_g = mgh` · `PE_s = ½kx²` · `W_net = ΔKE` · `E_i = E_f (closed system)` · `P = W/t = Fv` · `Efficiency = E_useful / E_input`

**Vocabulary:** work, joule, kinetic energy, potential energy, conservation of energy, power, watt, efficiency, thermal energy, dissipation

## Cornell Notes: Work & Energy

| Cue / Question | Notes |
|---|---|
| What is work? | Energy transferred by a force over a distance: W = Fd cosθ. If force ⟂ motion (carrying a box level) W = 0. No motion → no work. |
| Kinetic energy? | KE = ½mv². Energy of motion; v is squared, so 2× speed → 4× KE (and ~4× braking distance). |
| Potential energy? | Stored energy due to position or configuration: mgh (gravity), ½kx² (spring). Depends on a chosen zero point. |
| Work–energy theorem? | Net work = change in KE. A braking force does negative work and removes KE. |
| Conservation of energy? | Energy changes form but the total is constant. With no friction: mgh_i + ½mv_i² = mgh_f + ½mv_f². Path shape doesn't matter, only height change. |
| Where does 'lost' energy go? | Friction and air resistance convert mechanical energy to thermal energy (dissipated, not destroyed). Efficiency < 100%. |
| Power? | Rate of doing work: P = W/t, in watts (J/s). 1 hp ≈ 746 W. |

**Summary:** Work transfers energy. Energy is conserved: track KE, PE, and thermal energy before and after. Use energy methods when speed and height are asked, forces and time are not.

## Most-missed questions quiz (answer key)

*Take the interactive version in `site/index.html`.*

**1. You carry a heavy box horizontally across a room at constant velocity. How much work does your upward lifting force do on the box?**

- a. Zero  ✅
- b. Positive, equal to mg × distance
- c. Negative
- d. Equal to the box's KE

*Why:* The lifting force is perpendicular to the displacement (cos 90° = 0), so W = 0.  
*Common misconception:* Effort or holding something heavy equals work.

**2. A car doubles its speed from 15 m/s to 30 m/s. By what factor does its kinetic energy change?**

- a. 4  ✅
- b. 2
- c. 8
- d. 1/2

*Why:* KE ∝ v². (2)² = 4. Also the braking distance quadruples.  
*Common misconception:* KE is proportional to v.

**3. How much gravitational potential energy does a 2.0 kg object gain when lifted 5.0 m?**

- a. 98 J  ✅
- b. 10 J
- c. 49 J
- d. 9.8 J

*Why:* ΔPE = mgΔh = (2.0)(9.8)(5.0) = 98 J.  
*Common misconception:* Using weight in kg or leaving out g.

**4. A ball is dropped from 20 m. Ignoring air, how fast is it moving when it is 5 m above the ground?**

- a. About 17 m/s  ✅
- b. About 9.8 m/s
- c. About 20 m/s
- d. About 34 m/s

*Why:* Falls 15 m: mgh = ½mv² → v = √(2gh) = √(2 × 9.8 × 15) ≈ 17.1 m/s. Mass cancels.  
*Common misconception:* Using the 20 m or 5 m as the drop height.

**5. A 60 kg student climbs stairs 3.0 m high in 5.0 s. What power does the student develop?**

- a. About 350 W  ✅
- b. About 1760 W
- c. About 35 W
- d. About 3500 W

*Why:* W = mgh = 60 × 9.8 × 3.0 = 1764 J. P = W/t = 1764/5.0 ≈ 353 W.  
*Common misconception:* Confusing work (J) and power (W) or forgetting to divide by time.

**6. Two frictionless roller-coaster tracks drop the same vertical height: one is a steep straight drop and one is a long gentle curve. How do the speeds at the bottom compare?**

- a. Equal speeds  ✅
- b. Steep drop is faster
- c. Gentle curve is faster
- d. Can't be determined

*Why:* mgh converts into ½mv²; only the height change matters, not the path. (The steeper path arrives sooner, though.)  
*Common misconception:* A steeper path produces a higher final speed.

**7. A spring (k = 200 N/m) is compressed 0.10 m. How much elastic potential energy is stored?**

- a. 1.0 J  ✅
- b. 20 J
- c. 2.0 J
- d. 0.10 J

*Why:* PE = ½kx² = ½(200)(0.10)² = 1.0 J. Note the distance is squared.  
*Common misconception:* Using PE = kx.

**8. A machine takes in 500 J and produces 400 J of useful output. Where did the other 100 J go, and what is the efficiency?**

- a. Dissipated as thermal energy; 80%  ✅
- b. Destroyed; 80%
- c. Stored as potential energy; 125%
- d. Dissipated as thermal energy; 20%

*Why:* Energy is never destroyed; it spreads out as thermal energy (and sound). Efficiency = 400/500 = 80%.  
*Common misconception:* Energy is 'used up' or lost.

**9. A 1000 kg car speeds up from 10 m/s to 20 m/s. How much net work was done on it?**

- a. 150,000 J  ✅
- b. 100,000 J
- c. 50,000 J
- d. 200,000 J

*Why:* W_net = ΔKE = ½(1000)(20² − 10²) = ½(1000)(300) = 150,000 J.  
*Common misconception:* Subtracting speeds before squaring: ½m(20 − 10)².

## Practice problems

1. A 0.5 kg ball is thrown straight up at 12 m/s. How high does it rise?
   - *Answer:* h ≈ 7.35 m.
2. A 500 kg roller-coaster car starts at rest at 30 m. What is its speed at 10 m (frictionless)?
   - *Answer:* v ≈ 19.8 m/s.
3. A crane lifts an 800 kg load 15 m in 20 s. Find the work and power.
   - *Answer:* W ≈ 117,600 J; P ≈ 5,880 W.
4. A 1200 kg car traveling 25 m/s brakes to rest with a 6000 N friction force. Find the stopping distance.
   - *Answer:* KE = 375,000 J; d ≈ 62.5 m.
5. A spring (k = 400 N/m) is compressed 0.20 m and launches a 0.10 kg mass on a frictionless surface. Find the launch speed.
   - *Answer:* PE = 8.0 J; v ≈ 12.7 m/s.

## Labs & activities

- Pendulum or ramp lab: compare ΔPE to KE at the bottom; account for the difference.
- Stair-run power lab: measure mass, height, time and compute power.
- Energy-conversion design challenge (HS-PS3-3): build a device (e.g., rubber-band car, solar-powered vehicle) and measure efficiency.

## Real-world applications

- Roller coasters and skate parks (PE ↔ KE).
- Hybrid and electric vehicles; regenerative braking.
- Hydroelectric dams, wind turbines, and solar panels.
- Energy labels, appliance efficiency, and utility bills (kWh).
- Why speed limits on highways matter: KE ∝ v².
