# 1. Kinematics

*Suggested pacing: ~3 weeks* · *Big idea:* Motion can be described precisely with position, velocity, and acceleration, using graphs, tables, and equations.

## California NGSS performance expectations

- **HS-PS2-1** — Analyze data to support the claim that Newton's second law of motion describes the mathematical relationship among the net force on a macroscopic object, its mass, and its acceleration. (Kinematics supplies the acceleration data.)

- *Science & Engineering Practices:* Analyzing and interpreting data; using mathematics and computational thinking.
- *Crosscutting Concepts:* Patterns; cause and effect.

## Learning objectives (students can…)

- [ ] Distinguish distance/displacement and speed/velocity and compute each.
- [ ] Calculate acceleration from velocity and time data and interpret its sign.
- [ ] Construct and interpret x–t and v–t graphs, including slope and area.

## Key concepts

| Concept | What to master |
|---|---|
| **Position, distance, displacement** | Distance is a scalar path length; displacement is a vector from start to end (Δx = x_f − x_i). |
| **Speed vs. velocity** | Speed = distance/time (scalar). Velocity = displacement/time (vector, has direction). |
| **Acceleration** | a = Δv/Δt. Any change in speed OR direction is an acceleration. Units m/s². |
| **Motion graphs** | x–t slope = velocity. v–t slope = acceleration; v–t area = displacement. |
| **Constant-acceleration equations** | v = v₀ + at;  Δx = v₀t + ½at²;  v² = v₀² + 2aΔx;  Δx = ½(v₀+v)t. |
| **Free fall** | Near Earth's surface, ignoring air resistance, every object accelerates at g = 9.8 m/s² downward, regardless of mass. |

**Equations:** `v = Δx / Δt` · `a = Δv / Δt` · `v = v₀ + at` · `Δx = v₀t + ½at²` · `v² = v₀² + 2aΔx` · `g = 9.8 m/s² (down)`

**Vocabulary:** scalar, vector, displacement, velocity, acceleration, free fall, slope, reference frame

## Cornell Notes: Kinematics

| Cue / Question | Notes |
|---|---|
| What is the difference between distance and displacement? | Distance = total path length (scalar, never negative). Displacement = straight-line change in position with direction (vector). Run one lap of a track: distance = 400 m, displacement = 0. |
| Speed vs. velocity? | Speed = distance ÷ time (scalar). Velocity = displacement ÷ time (vector). Velocity can be negative (direction); speed cannot. |
| What is acceleration? | a = Δv/Δt. Speeding up, slowing down, OR turning all count. If v and a point the same way → speeding up; opposite ways → slowing down. |
| How do I read a graph? | x–t: slope = velocity. v–t: slope = acceleration, area under curve = displacement. Flat v–t line = constant velocity (a = 0). Straight sloped v–t line = constant acceleration. |
| Which equation do I pick? | List knowns (v₀, v, a, Δx, t). Choose the equation that has your unknown and omits the quantity you don't have. |
| How does free fall work? | a = −9.8 m/s² the whole flight (up, at the top, and down). At the top v = 0 but a is still 9.8 m/s² down. Mass does not matter without air resistance. |

**Summary:** Kinematics describes motion without asking why. Choose a positive direction, list knowns, pick the equation, and check units and sign. Graphs and equations are two views of the same motion.

## Most-missed questions quiz (answer key)

*Take the interactive version in `site/index.html`.*

**1. A ball is thrown straight up. At the very top of its flight, what are its velocity and acceleration (ignore air)?**

- a. v = 0, a = 9.8 m/s² downward  ✅
- b. v = 0, a = 0
- c. v = 9.8 m/s upward, a = 0
- d. v = 0, a = 9.8 m/s² upward

*Why:* Gravity acts the whole flight. At the top the velocity is momentarily zero but the velocity is still changing, so a = 9.8 m/s² down.  
*Common misconception:* Zero velocity means zero acceleration.

**2. A runner completes one 400 m lap in 80 s. What is the average velocity and average speed?**

- a. 0 m/s and 5 m/s  ✅
- b. 5 m/s and 5 m/s
- c. 0 m/s and 0 m/s
- d. 5 m/s and 0 m/s

*Why:* Displacement for a full lap is 0, so average velocity is 0. Average speed = 400 m / 80 s = 5 m/s.  
*Common misconception:* Speed and velocity are the same thing.

**3. A car's v–t graph rises uniformly from 0 to 10 m/s in 5 s, then stays at 10 m/s for 5 s. What is its total displacement?**

- a. 75 m  ✅
- b. 100 m
- c. 50 m
- d. 25 m

*Why:* Area under v–t: triangle ½(5)(10) = 25 m plus rectangle (5)(10) = 50 m → 75 m.  
*Common misconception:* Using v_final × total time instead of finding the area.

**4. A car starts from rest and accelerates at 3 m/s² for 4 s. How far does it travel?**

- a. 24 m  ✅
- b. 48 m
- c. 12 m
- d. 6 m

*Why:* Δx = v₀t + ½at² = 0 + ½(3)(4²) = 24 m.  
*Common misconception:* Forgetting the ½ or forgetting to square t.

**5. An object has negative velocity and negative acceleration. What is happening to its speed?**

- a. Speeding up  ✅
- b. Slowing down
- c. Constant speed
- d. Cannot tell without more information

*Why:* When velocity and acceleration have the same sign they point the same way, so speed increases.  
*Common misconception:* Negative acceleration always means slowing down.

**6. A rock is dropped from rest. Ignoring air resistance, how fast is it moving after 3.0 s?**

- a. 29.4 m/s  ✅
- b. 9.8 m/s
- c. 14.7 m/s
- d. 88.2 m/s

*Why:* v = v₀ + at = 0 + (9.8)(3.0) = 29.4 m/s.  
*Common misconception:* Mixing up v = gt with Δx = ½gt².

**7. A bowling ball and a tennis ball are dropped together from the same height in a vacuum. Which lands first?**

- a. They land at the same time  ✅
- b. The bowling ball
- c. The tennis ball
- d. It depends on the height only

*Why:* In free fall a = g for every mass. Heavier objects feel more gravity but also have more inertia, so the acceleration is the same.  
*Common misconception:* Heavier objects fall faster.

**8. A car moving at 20 m/s brakes with a constant acceleration of −5 m/s². How far does it travel before stopping?**

- a. 40 m  ✅
- b. 80 m
- c. 20 m
- d. 4 m

*Why:* v² = v₀² + 2aΔx → 0 = 400 + 2(−5)Δx → Δx = 40 m. Note: doubling speed would quadruple the stopping distance.  
*Common misconception:* Stopping distance grows linearly with speed (it grows with v²).

**9. Convert 72 km/h to m/s.**

- a. 20 m/s  ✅
- b. 259 m/s
- c. 7.2 m/s
- d. 72 m/s

*Why:* 72 km/h × (1000 m / 1 km) × (1 h / 3600 s) = 20 m/s (divide km/h by 3.6).  
*Common misconception:* Unit conversion errors (multiplying by 3.6 instead of dividing).

## Practice problems

1. A cyclist slows from 12 m/s to rest in 4.0 s. Find the acceleration and the stopping distance.
   - *Answer:* a = −3.0 m/s²; Δx = 24 m.
2. A hiker walks 40 m east then 30 m north in 50 s. Find distance, displacement, average speed, and average velocity.
   - *Answer:* Distance 70 m; displacement 50 m (37° N of E); avg speed 1.4 m/s; avg velocity 1.0 m/s.
3. A stone dropped from a bridge hits the water after 2.5 s. How high is the bridge and how fast is the stone going on impact?
   - *Answer:* h ≈ 30.6 m; v ≈ 24.5 m/s.
4. A car speeds up from 15 m/s to 25 m/s over 200 m. Find the acceleration and the time.
   - *Answer:* a = 1.0 m/s²; t = 10 s.
5. An object starts at x = 2 m, moves +3 m/s for 4 s, rests 3 s, then moves −2 m/s for 5 s. Find the final position and total distance traveled.
   - *Answer:* Final x = 4 m; total distance = 12 + 10 = 22 m.

## Labs & activities

- Constant-velocity buggy: collect position–time data, fit a line, interpret slope.
- Ramp & cart (or phone phyphox): measure acceleration; verify Δx = ½at².
- Reaction-time ruler drop: compute reaction time from d = ½gt².

## Real-world applications

- Following distance and stopping distance for new drivers (why speed matters quadratically).
- Speed cameras and average-speed zones; GPS speed vs. velocity.
- Track & field: reading a 100 m split-time table as acceleration, then constant velocity.
- Elevator ride graphs and amusement-park free-fall drops.
