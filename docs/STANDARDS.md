# Standards Map

Learning goals come from the Next Generation Science Standards for California Public Schools (CA NGSS), using the high school three-course model in which physics is taught together with Earth and space science ("Physics in the Universe").

- Standards: https://www.cde.ca.gov/pd/ca/sc/ngssstandards.asp
- Course model: California Science Framework, Chapter 7, at https://www.cde.ca.gov/ci/sc/cf/cascienceframework2016.asp

Check the wording below against the CDE documents before using it in a syllabus.

## Semester 1: performance expectations by unit

| Unit | Performance expectation | What students do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Kinematics, 2 Projectile Motion | Foundation for **HS-PS2-1** | Describe and measure motion (position, velocity, acceleration) so that the effect of forces can be analyzed in Unit 3. NGSS has no separate kinematics expectation. |
| 3 Newton's Laws | **HS-PS2-1** | Analyze data to support the claim that Newton's second law describes the mathematical relationship among the net force on a macroscopic object, its mass, and its acceleration. |
| 4 Momentum | **HS-PS2-2** | Use mathematical representations to support the claim that the total momentum of a system of objects is conserved when there is no net force on the system. |
| 4 Momentum | **HS-PS2-3** | Apply scientific and engineering ideas to design, evaluate, and refine a device that minimizes the force on a macroscopic object during a collision. |
| 4 Momentum, 5 Work & Energy | **HS-ETS1-2, HS-ETS1-3** | Break a design problem into smaller problems; evaluate a solution against criteria and trade-offs. |
| 5 Work & Energy | **HS-PS3-1** | Create a computational model to calculate the change in the energy of one component in a system when the change in energy of the other component(s) and energy flows in and out of the system are known. |
| 5 Work & Energy | **HS-PS3-2** | Develop and use models to illustrate that energy at the macroscopic scale can be accounted for as a combination of energy associated with the motions of particles (objects) and energy associated with the relative position of particles (objects). |
| 5 Work & Energy | **HS-PS3-3** | Design, build, and refine a device that works within given constraints to convert one form of energy into another form of energy. |

## Science and engineering practices used every unit

| Practice | Where it shows up |
|---|---|
| Planning and carrying out investigations | Unit labs: identify variables, control conditions, repeat trials |
| Analyzing and interpreting data | Motion graphs (Unit 1), force and acceleration graphs (Unit 3) |
| Using mathematics and computational thinking | Kinematic equations, vector components, conservation equations, spreadsheet energy model (Unit 5) |
| Developing and using models | Motion maps, free-body diagrams, before-and-after collision sketches |
| Constructing explanations and designing solutions | Egg drop or crash cushion (Unit 4), energy conversion device (Unit 5) |
| Engaging in argument from evidence | Claim, evidence, reasoning paragraphs in lab reports |

## Crosscutting concepts

Patterns (Unit 1), cause and effect (Units 2 and 3), systems and system models (Unit 4), energy and matter (Unit 5).
