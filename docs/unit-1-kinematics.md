# Unit 1: Kinematics

**Pacing:** 3 weeks  |  **NGSS:** HS-PS2-1

> **Essential question:** How can we describe and predict motion using words, graphs, and equations?

*Kinematics has no stand-alone NGSS performance expectation. It builds the motion vocabulary and graph/equation skills students need to analyze acceleration data for HS-PS2-1.*

## 1. Learning objectives

- [ ] Distinguish distance from displacement and speed from velocity, and give the sign (direction) of each.
- [ ] Calculate average velocity and acceleration from data tables and graphs.
- [ ] Interpret position-time, velocity-time, and acceleration-time graphs (slope and area).
- [ ] Select and apply the constant-acceleration equations to solve one-dimensional problems.
- [ ] Model free fall as constant acceleration of 9.8 m/s² downward, independent of mass (no air resistance).

## 2. Key concepts

| Concept | What students must master |
|---|---|
| **Position, distance, displacement** | Position is where an object is relative to an origin. Distance is the total path length (scalar). Displacement is the change in position, Δx = x_f − x_i (vector). |
| **Speed vs. velocity** | Speed = distance/time (scalar). Velocity = displacement/time (vector); its sign gives direction. |
| **Acceleration** | a = Δv/Δt. Any change in velocity (speeding up, slowing down, OR turning) is acceleration. Units: m/s². |
| **Graph meaning** | x-t slope = velocity. v-t slope = acceleration; v-t area = displacement. a-t area = change in velocity. |
| **Constant-acceleration equations** | v = v₀ + at;  Δx = v₀t + ½at²;  v² = v₀² + 2aΔx;  Δx = ½(v + v₀)t. |
| **Free fall** | Only gravity acts. a = −9.8 m/s² (down) the whole time, including at the top of a toss. |

**Key formulas:** `v̄ = Δx/Δt`  ·  `a = Δv/Δt`  ·  `v = v₀ + at`  ·  `Δx = v₀t + ½at²`  ·  `v² = v₀² + 2aΔx`

## 3. Cornell notes (cue column / notes column)

| Cues / Questions | Notes |
|---|---|
| What is the difference between distance and displacement? | Distance: total path traveled (scalar, always ≥ 0). Displacement: straight-line change in position with direction (vector). Walk 40 m east then 30 m west → distance 70 m, displacement 10 m east. |
| Speed vs. velocity? | Speed = distance ÷ time (no direction). Velocity = displacement ÷ time (has direction). Moving in a circle at constant speed = changing velocity. |
| Define acceleration. Sign rules? | a = Δv/Δt. If v and a have the SAME sign the object speeds up; OPPOSITE signs → slows down. Sign shows direction, not 'fast' or 'slow'. |
| How do I read an x-t graph? | Slope = velocity. Straight line = constant v. Curve = changing v. Flat = at rest. |
| How do I read a v-t graph? | Slope = acceleration. Area between line and axis = displacement (area below the axis counts negative). Crossing the axis = turning around. |
| Which kinematic equation do I use? | List knowns/unknown (x, v₀, v, a, t). Pick the equation that has the unknown and NO missing variable. 'No time given' → v² = v₀² + 2aΔx. |
| What is special about free fall? | a = 9.8 m/s² downward at every moment (rising, at the top, falling). v = 0 at the top but a is NOT 0. Mass doesn't matter without air resistance. |

**Summary (write in your own words):** Kinematics describes motion without asking what causes it. Vectors (displacement, velocity, acceleration) carry direction; graphs and four equations connect them. Free fall is the key example of constant acceleration.

## 4. Most-missed questions (self-quiz)

Try each question before opening the answer. The interactive version is in `site/index.html`.

**1. A hiker walks 40 m east, then 30 m west. What is her displacement?**

   - A. 70 m east
   - B. 10 m east
   - C. 10 m west
   - D. 70 m, no direction

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Displacement is net change in position: +40 − 30 = +10 m (east). 70 m is the distance.  
*Common mistake:* Confusing distance (scalar) with displacement (vector).

</details>

**2. A ball is tossed straight up. At the very top of its path, its velocity and acceleration are:**

   - A. v = 0, a = 0
   - B. v = 0, a = 9.8 m/s² down
   - C. v = 9.8 m/s up, a = 0
   - D. v = 0, a = 9.8 m/s² up

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Gravity acts the entire flight, so a = 9.8 m/s² downward even when v = 0 for an instant.  
*Common mistake:* Thinking zero velocity means zero acceleration.

</details>

**3. On a velocity-time graph, the slope of the line represents:**

   - A. displacement
   - B. velocity
   - C. acceleration
   - D. distance

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** slope = Δv/Δt = acceleration. Displacement is the AREA under the line.  
*Common mistake:* Mixing up what slope and area mean on different graphs.

</details>

**4. A cart has velocity −5 m/s and acceleration −2 m/s². The cart is:**

   - A. speeding up
   - B. slowing down
   - C. moving at constant speed
   - D. at rest

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** v and a have the same sign (both negative), so speed increases.  
*Common mistake:* 'Negative acceleration' does not automatically mean slowing down.

</details>

**5. Ignoring air resistance, a 10 kg ball and a 1 kg ball are dropped from the same height at the same time. The 10 kg ball:**

   - A. lands first
   - B. lands second
   - C. lands at the same time
   - D. can't be determined

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** Both have a = g. Mass cancels in free fall.  
*Common mistake:* Believing heavier objects fall faster.

</details>

**6. A car starts from rest and accelerates at 3 m/s² for 4 s. How far does it travel?**

   - A. 12 m
   - B. 24 m
   - C. 48 m
   - D. 6 m

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Δx = ½at² = ½(3)(16) = 24 m. (12 m = a·t is the final speed.)  
*Common mistake:* Forgetting the ½ and the t², or reporting speed instead of distance.

</details>

**7. A car drives at constant speed around a circular track. Its velocity is:**

   - A. constant
   - B. changing because the direction changes
   - C. zero
   - D. changing only if speed changes

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Velocity is a vector; changing direction is a change in velocity (acceleration).  
*Common mistake:* Treating velocity as only a number (speed).

</details>

**8. A driver travels 120 km at 60 km/h, then 120 km at 40 km/h. What is the average speed for the whole trip?**

   - A. 50 km/h
   - B. 48 km/h
   - C. 100 km/h
   - D. 40 km/h

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Total distance 240 km; times 2 h + 3 h = 5 h; 240/5 = 48 km/h. Averaging the speeds only works for equal TIMES.  
*Common mistake:* Averaging the two speeds instead of total distance ÷ total time.

</details>

**9. An object in free fall drops 4.9 m in its first second. How far has it dropped after 2 seconds (from the start)?**

   - A. 9.8 m
   - B. 14.7 m
   - C. 19.6 m
   - D. 4.9 m

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** Δx = ½gt² grows with t². Doubling t quadruples distance: 4 × 4.9 = 19.6 m.  
*Common mistake:* Assuming distance is proportional to time (linear).

</details>

**10. Which equation is the best choice for finding stopping distance when you know v₀, v = 0, and a, but NOT t?**

   - A. v = v₀ + at
   - B. Δx = v₀t + ½at²
   - C. v² = v₀² + 2aΔx
   - D. a = Δv/Δt

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** It contains Δx and no t, so it needs only the known quantities.  
*Common mistake:* Picking an equation that contains an unknown time.

</details>

## 5. Common misconceptions to watch for

- Velocity is zero ⇒ acceleration is zero (false at the top of a toss).
- Negative acceleration always means slowing down (depends on velocity's sign).
- Heavier objects fall faster (only with air resistance).
- Average speed over two legs = average of the two speeds (only if equal TIMES).

## 6. Practice problems

Show: knowns, unknown, equation, substitution with units, and a reasonableness check.

1. A runner covers 100 m east in 12.5 s. What is the average velocity?
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>8.0 m/s east</details>

2. A car goes from rest to 27 m/s in 9.0 s. Find a and the distance traveled.
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>a = 3.0 m/s²; Δx = ½(27)(9) = 121.5 m</details>

3. A ball is dropped from 78.4 m. How long to land and how fast is it going?
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>t = √(2·78.4/9.8) = 4.0 s; v = 9.8·4.0 = 39.2 m/s</details>

4. A car at 25 m/s brakes at 5.0 m/s². Stopping distance and time?
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>Δx = 25²/(2·5) = 62.5 m; t = 5.0 s</details>

5. A v-t graph rises from 0 to 10 m/s in 5 s, then stays at 10 m/s for 5 s. Total displacement?
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>25 m + 50 m = 75 m</details>

6. A ball is thrown straight up at 14.7 m/s. Time to the top and maximum height?
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>t = 1.5 s; h = 14.7²/(2·9.8) ≈ 11.0 m</details>

## 8. Real-world applications

- Traffic safety: stopping distance grows with the SQUARE of speed (v² = 2aΔx), which is why speed limits drop near schools.
- Sports analytics: sprint start acceleration and 40-yard dash splits.
- Aviation and rocketry: takeoff roll length and launch acceleration profiles.
- GPS/phone sensors: accelerometers integrate acceleration into velocity and position.
