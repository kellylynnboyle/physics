# How to Use the Cornell Notes in This Project

Every unit page has a two-column table: **Cues / Questions** on the left and **Notes** on the right. The left column is your self-test.

## The routine

1. **Read and annotate** the Notes column once during or after class. Bold terms are the vocabulary to know.
2. **Cover the Notes column.** Read each cue question and answer it aloud from memory.
3. **Check and mark misses.** Put a star next to every cue you missed.
4. **Redraw** each diagram and each worked example from memory.
5. **Repeat tomorrow, then again in a few days.** Recall spread over several days builds retention that rereading does not.
6. **Take the unit quiz** (`quizzes/index.html`). Aim for 80% or higher, then redo the questions you missed.

## Writing your own Cornell notes in class

- Draw a vertical line about one third from the left edge. Leave a 2-inch strip at the bottom for the summary.
- Take notes on the right during class.
- Within 24 hours, write cue *questions* (not topic labels) on the left. "What does the slope of a velocity-time graph tell you?" is a cue. "Slope" is not.
- Write a 2-3 sentence summary at the bottom in your own words.

## Sign conventions (use these every time)

- Choose a positive direction first, and say it. Right and up are usually positive.
- A quantity is negative only because it points opposite the positive direction. Negative acceleration does not always mean slowing down.
- Always write units. Check that the units of your answer match the quantity.
- Sense check every answer: is the size reasonable, and does the sign match the picture?
