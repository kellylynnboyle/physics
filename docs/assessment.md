# Assessment & Progress Tracking

## How progress is measured

| Component | Weight | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Concept quizzes (interactive, most-missed questions) | 20% | ≥ 80% = proficient; retakes after reviewing explanations |
| Cornell notes | 10% | Cues, notes, and a summary in student's own words |
| Labs & design challenges | 25% | CER (Claim–Evidence–Reasoning) write-up, data tables, graphs |
| Problem sets | 15% | Setup, units, signs, and reasoning shown |
| Unit test | 30% | Mix of conceptual, graph, and multi-step quantitative items |

## Four-level rubric (all topics)

| Level | Description |
|---|---|
| 4 – Mastery | Transfers ideas to new situations and explains/corrects misconceptions |
| 3 – Proficient | Solves standard problems correctly with reasoning |
| 2 – Developing | Partial understanding; needs prompts or makes frequent errors |
| 1 – Beginning | Core misconceptions; limited correct work |

## Standards coverage (Semester 1)

| NGSS PE | Topic(s) |
|---|---|
| **HS-PS2-1** — Analyze data to support the claim that Newton's second law of motion describes the mathematical relationship among the net force on a macroscopic object, its mass, and its acceleration. (Kinematics supplies the acceleration data.) | Kinematics, Projectile Motion, Newton's Laws |
| **HS-PS2-2** — Use mathematical representations to support the claim that the total momentum of a system of objects is conserved when there is no net force on the system. | Momentum |
| **HS-PS2-3** — Apply scientific and engineering ideas to design, evaluate, and refine a device that minimizes the force on a macroscopic object during a collision. | Momentum |
| **HS-PS2-4** — Use mathematical representations of Newton's Law of Gravitation and Coulomb's Law to describe and predict the gravitational and electrostatic forces between objects. | Newton's Laws |
| **HS-PS3-1** — Create a computational model to calculate the change in the energy of one component in a system when the change in energy of the other component(s) and the energy flows in and out of the system are known. | Work & Energy |
| **HS-PS3-2** — Develop and use models to illustrate that energy at the macroscopic scale can be accounted for as a combination of energy associated with the motions of particles (objects) and energy associated with the relative positions of particles (objects). | Work & Energy |
| **HS-PS3-3** — Design, build, and refine a device that works within given constraints to convert one form of energy into another form of energy. | Work & Energy |

*Semester 2 standards (electricity, magnetism, waves, light, Earth science and nuclear physics) are outside this semester-1 project.*
