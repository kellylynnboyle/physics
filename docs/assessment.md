# Assessment Plan and Progress Tracking

## Scale
- **4 Mastery** (>= 90%) - consistently accurate, can explain and transfer
- **3 Proficient** (75-89%) - meets the standard with minor errors
- **2 Developing** (60-74%) - partial understanding; reteach and retake
- **1 Beginning** (< 60%) - significant gaps; intervention

## Weighting (suggested)
| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Interactive quizzes and Cornell notes | 15% |
| Labs / design challenges (SEP evidence) | 25% |
| Practice and homework (IXL, problem sets) | 15% |
| Unit tests | 35% |
| Semester cumulative exam | 10% |

## Mastery rules
- Quiz pass mark is 75%. Missed items must be retaken (the site's *Retry missed* button) and corrected in notes.
- Retakes allowed once per unit after completing practice problems.
- Each lab includes a claim-evidence-reasoning (CER) conclusion.

## Objective-level tracking

### Unit 1: Kinematics
- U1.1 Distinguish distance/displacement and speed/velocity, and identify which are vectors.
- U1.2 Calculate average velocity and acceleration from data.
- U1.3 Interpret position-time and velocity-time graphs (slope and area meanings).
- U1.4 Apply the constant-acceleration equations to solve one-dimensional problems.
- U1.5 Model free fall with a = 9.8 m/s^2 downward and explain why mass does not matter (ignoring air).

### Unit 2: Projectile Motion
- U2.1 Resolve a velocity vector into horizontal and vertical components.
- U2.2 Explain that horizontal and vertical motions of a projectile are independent.
- U2.3 Calculate time of flight, maximum height, and range for horizontal and angled launches.
- U2.4 Predict how launch speed and angle change range and height.
- U2.5 Evaluate when the ignore-air-resistance model is reasonable.

### Unit 3: Newton's Laws
- U3.1 State Newton's three laws and give an example of each.
- U3.2 Draw free-body diagrams and find net force in one and two dimensions.
- U3.3 Use F_net = ma to calculate acceleration, force, or mass.
- U3.4 Distinguish mass from weight and compute normal and friction forces.
- U3.5 Identify action-reaction pairs and explain why they never cancel.
- U3.6 Analyse data (e.g. cart + hanging mass) to support the F_net = ma relationship (HS-PS2-1).

### Unit 4: Momentum
- U4.1 Calculate momentum as a vector and explain its units.
- U4.2 Relate impulse to change in momentum and to force and time.
- U4.3 Apply conservation of momentum to collisions and explosions in one dimension.
- U4.4 Classify collisions as elastic, inelastic, or perfectly inelastic.
- U4.5 Design/evaluate a device that reduces collision force by increasing contact time (HS-PS2-3).

### Unit 5: Work & Energy
- U5.1 Calculate work done by a constant force, including the angle between force and displacement.
- U5.2 Calculate kinetic, gravitational potential, and elastic potential energy.
- U5.3 Use the work-energy theorem and conservation of mechanical energy to solve problems.
- U5.4 Account for energy dissipated by friction as thermal energy.
- U5.5 Calculate power and efficiency.
- U5.6 Build a model/graph (e.g. bar chart) tracking energy transfers in a system (HS-PS3-1, HS-PS3-2).
- U5.7 Design and refine a device that converts energy under constraints (HS-PS3-3).
