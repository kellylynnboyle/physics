# Assessment Guide

How progress is measured in each unit, what counts as mastery, and a tracker for recording it.

## What is assessed in every unit

| Evidence | What it shows | Mastery mark |
|---|---|---|
| Cornell notes self-test | Recall of key ideas without looking | Answers every cue aloud with no more than two misses |
| Practice problems (6 per unit) | Problem-solving method | At least 5 of 6 correct with givens, equation, substitution, and units shown |
| IXL skills | Fluency on individual skills | SmartScore 80 or higher on each skill in the unit plan |
| Missed-question quiz (10 per unit) | Whether common misconceptions are gone | 8 of 10 or better, and can explain each missed question |
| Lab report or design report | Science and engineering practices | Level 3 or higher on the rubric below |
| Unit test | Overall understanding | 70% to pass; 85% or higher for mastery |

## Proficiency scale for learning objectives

Each "I can" objective in a unit outline is scored on this scale. An objective is **mastered at level 3 or higher**.

| Level | Name | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | Extending | Applies the idea correctly to an unfamiliar situation and explains the reasoning; can teach it to a classmate |
| 3 | Proficient | Solves standard problems correctly and independently, with method and units shown |
| 2 | Developing | Solves problems with notes or hints; makes errors in setup, signs, or units |
| 1 | Beginning | Recognizes the vocabulary but cannot start a problem alone |

## Lab report rubric

| Criterion | 4 | 3 | 2 | 1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Question and variables | Testable question; independent, dependent, and controlled variables all correct and justified | Question and all variables correct | One variable misidentified or controls missing | No clear question or variables |
| Data | Multiple trials, organized table, units, sensible precision | Organized table with units | Table incomplete or units missing | Data missing or unreadable |
| Graph and analysis | Correct graph, best-fit line, slope or area interpreted with units and linked to the physics | Correct graph and correct calculation | Graph or calculation has errors | No graph or analysis |
| Conclusion | Claim supported by data, compared with the accepted value or prediction (percent error), sources of error explained | Claim supported by data | Claim stated without evidence | No conclusion |

## Engineering design rubric (collision device, energy device)

| Criterion | 4 | 3 | 2 | 1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Criteria and constraints | Stated, measurable, and used to judge the design | Stated and measurable | Vague | Missing |
| Physics explanation | Explains the design with the correct relationship (FΔt = Δp, or energy conversions and efficiency) and numbers | Explains with the correct relationship | Names the idea without applying it | No physics |
| Testing and refinement | Changes one feature at a time, retests, and shows the improvement with data | Tests and makes one justified change | Tests once, no refinement | Not tested |
| Trade-offs | Compares at least two designs against the criteria | Notes one trade-off | Mentions trade-offs loosely | None |

## Suggested grade weighting

| Category | Weight |
|---|---|
| Unit tests | 40% |
| Labs and design reports | 30% |
| Quizzes, including the missed-question quizzes | 15% |
| Practice (Cornell notes, practice problems, IXL) | 15% |

This weighting is a starting point. Replace it with the department's policy if one exists.
