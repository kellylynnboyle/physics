# Assessment Criteria and Progress Tracking

## Proficiency scale

Every learning objective is scored on the same scale. Level 3 is the goal for all students.

| Level | Name | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | Extending | Solves unfamiliar, multi-step problems; explains the reasoning and corrects a common misconception for someone else. |
| 3 | Proficient (the goal) | Solves standard problems correctly with units and direction; explains the concept in their own words. |
| 2 | Developing | Solves routine problems with a formula sheet or hints; explanations are partly correct. |
| 1 | Beginning | Recognizes vocabulary and equations but cannot yet apply them without step-by-step help. |

## What counts as evidence

| Evidence | Mastery criterion | What it shows |
|---|---|---|
| Cornell notes self-test | Answers every cue aloud without looking | Recall of concepts and vocabulary |
| Practice problems | At least 80% correct with given values, equation, substitution, and units shown | Problem-solving method |
| Interactive quiz | 8/10 or better on a full attempt | Common misconceptions are resolved |
| IXL Practice Plan | SmartScore 80+ on each listed skill | Fluency |
| Lab report | Level 3 on the lab rubric | Data analysis and reasoning from evidence |
| Design project (Units 4 and 5) | Level 3 on the engineering rubric | Applying ideas to a real problem |
| Unit test | 80% or better, or Level 3 on each objective | Independent performance |

An objective is marked **met** when two different kinds of evidence are at Level 3 or the mastery criterion.

## Lab report rubric

| Criterion | 4 | 3 | 2 | 1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Question and variables | Variables and controls identified and justified | Independent, dependent, and controlled variables correct | One variable misidentified | Variables missing |
| Data and graph | Graph is correct, with best-fit line and slope interpreted with units | Labeled graph with correct axes, units, and best-fit line | Graph has missing labels or units | No graph or unusable data |
| Claim, evidence, reasoning | Claim supported by data and physics principle; limits discussed | Claim supported by specific data and the correct principle | Claim stated with weak or partial evidence | Claim missing or unsupported |
| Error analysis | Sources of error ranked, with a tested or specific improvement | Main sources of error named with their effect on results | "Human error" only | Not addressed |

## Engineering design rubric (HS-PS2-3, HS-PS3-3)

| Criterion | 4 | 3 | 2 | 1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Criteria and constraints | Prioritized, with trade-offs explained | Clearly stated and measurable | Vague or incomplete | Missing |
| Physics explanation | Quantitative use of impulse or energy to justify the design | Correct use of impulse-momentum or energy conservation to explain the design | Physics mentioned but misapplied | No physics connection |
| Testing and data | Multiple trials with a comparison between versions | Device tested with recorded data | Tested once, little data | Not tested |
| Refinement | Two or more refinements, each tied to data | At least one refinement based on test results | Changes made without reference to data | No refinement |

## Reading the results

- **Most objectives at 3 or 4:** move on; use Level 4 extension problems.
- **One or two objectives at 2:** redo the matching Cornell notes section and its quiz questions (each unit page lists which quiz questions go with each objective).
- **Several objectives at 1 or 2:** reteach with the PhET simulation for that unit, then repeat the practice problems before retaking the quiz.
