# Unit 4: Momentum

**Pacing:** 3 weeks  |  **NGSS:** HS-PS2-2, HS-PS2-3

> **Essential question:** How can we predict the outcome of a collision, and how do we make collisions safer?

*Direct match: conservation of momentum (HS-PS2-2) and engineering a collision-safety device (HS-PS2-3).*

## 1. Learning objectives

- [ ] Calculate momentum and recognize that it is a vector.
- [ ] Relate impulse to change in momentum (J = FΔt = Δp) and interpret F-t graphs.
- [ ] Apply conservation of momentum to elastic, inelastic, and explosion/recoil problems.
- [ ] Classify collisions and explain what is and isn't conserved.
- [ ] Design, test, and refine a device that reduces force during a collision (HS-PS2-3).

## 2. Key concepts

| Concept | What students must master |
|---|---|
| **Momentum** | p = mv (kg·m/s), a vector in the direction of velocity. |
| **Impulse** | J = FΔt = Δp = mv_f − mv_i. Area under a force–time graph equals impulse. |
| **Impulse–momentum & safety** | For the same Δp, longer contact time ⇒ smaller average force (airbags, crumple zones, padding). |
| **Conservation of momentum** | If net external force on a system is zero, total p before = total p after. |
| **Collision types** | Elastic: p and KE conserved. Inelastic: p conserved, KE lost. Perfectly inelastic: objects stick together (max KE loss). |
| **Explosions & recoil** | Starting from rest, total p = 0, so pieces move in opposite directions with equal-size momenta. |

**Key formulas:** `p = mv`  ·  `J = FΔt = Δp`  ·  `m₁v₁ + m₂v₂ = m₁v₁′ + m₂v₂′`  ·  `stuck together: v′ = (m₁v₁ + m₂v₂)/(m₁ + m₂)`

## 3. Cornell notes (cue column / notes column)

| Cues / Questions | Notes |
|---|---|
| What is momentum? | p = mv. Vector. A slow truck and a fast bullet can have similar momentum. Units kg·m/s. |
| What is impulse? | J = FΔt = Δp. A force acting over time changes momentum. Units N·s = kg·m/s. |
| Why do airbags work? | Same Δp, but longer Δt → smaller F (F = Δp/Δt). Applies to padding, helmets, crumple zones, bending knees when landing. |
| When is momentum conserved? | In an isolated system (net external force = 0), for ANY collision — elastic or inelastic. |
| Elastic vs. inelastic? | Elastic: KE conserved (billiard balls, approximately). Inelastic: KE converted to heat/sound/deformation. Perfectly inelastic: stick together. |
| How do I solve a collision? | 1) Choose + direction. 2) Write p_before = p_after with signs. 3) Solve for the unknown velocity. 4) Check KE if asked. |
| Recoil? | Start at rest: 0 = m₁v₁ + m₂v₂ → v₂ = −(m₁/m₂)v₁. The lighter object moves faster. |
| Bounce vs. stick? | A rebounding object has a bigger Δp (reverses direction) than one that stops, so it receives a bigger impulse. |

**Summary (write in your own words):** Momentum measures 'how hard it is to stop' a moving object. Forces change momentum over time (impulse). In an isolated system total momentum stays constant, which lets us predict collisions and recoil without knowing the forces.

## 4. Most-missed questions (self-quiz)

Try each question before opening the answer. The interactive version is in `site/index.html`.

**1. A 2 kg cart moves at 3 m/s. What is its momentum?**

   - A. 1.5 kg·m/s
   - B. 5 kg·m/s
   - C. 6 kg·m/s
   - D. 18 kg·m/s

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** p = mv = 2 × 3 = 6 kg·m/s.  
*Common mistake:* Using ½mv² (kinetic energy-like) or v/m.

</details>

**2. Two identical carts move toward each other at equal speeds on a frictionless track. The total momentum of the system is:**

   - A. twice one cart's momentum
   - B. zero
   - C. equal to one cart's momentum
   - D. cannot be determined

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Momentum is a vector. +p and −p add to zero.  
*Common mistake:* Adding magnitudes and ignoring direction.

</details>

**3. Why does an airbag reduce injury in a crash?**

   - A. It reduces the passenger's change in momentum
   - B. It increases the stopping time, reducing the average force
   - C. It reduces the passenger's mass
   - D. It increases the impulse

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Δp is fixed by the crash; F = Δp/Δt, so a larger Δt gives a smaller force.  
*Common mistake:* Believing airbags change the momentum change or 'absorb' force without time.

</details>

**4. A 1000 kg car moving at 10 m/s collides with and sticks to a stationary 1000 kg car. What is their velocity after the collision?**

   - A. 10 m/s
   - B. 5 m/s
   - C. 2.5 m/s
   - D. 20 m/s

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** 10 000 = 2000·v′ → v′ = 5 m/s.  
*Common mistake:* Forgetting that the combined mass is in the denominator.

</details>

**5. In an inelastic collision (no external forces), which quantity is conserved?**

   - A. only kinetic energy
   - B. only momentum
   - C. both momentum and kinetic energy
   - D. neither

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Momentum is conserved in all isolated collisions; KE is not conserved in inelastic ones.  
*Common mistake:* Thinking 'conserved' applies to energy only, or to both in every collision.

</details>

**6. Two identical balls hit a wall with the same speed. Ball A sticks; ball B bounces back at the same speed. Which gets the larger impulse from the wall?**

   - A. ball A
   - B. ball B
   - C. equal impulse
   - D. neither receives impulse

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** B's momentum changes from +mv to −mv (Δp = 2mv); A's changes by mv.  
*Common mistake:* Overlooking the sign change in the rebounding case.

</details>

**7. A 4.0 kg rifle fires a 0.010 kg bullet at 400 m/s. Ignoring other forces, the rifle's recoil speed is:**

   - A. 0.1 m/s
   - B. 1.0 m/s
   - C. 10 m/s
   - D. 400 m/s

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** 0 = (0.010)(400) + 4.0·v → |v| = 1.0 m/s.  
*Common mistake:* Forgetting that the recoil velocity is much smaller because of the larger mass.

</details>

**8. The area under a force-versus-time graph equals:**

   - A. the change in kinetic energy
   - B. the impulse (change in momentum)
   - C. the acceleration
   - D. the work done

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Area = ∫F dt = J = Δp.  
*Common mistake:* Confusing F-t area with F-x area (work).

</details>

**9. A truck and a small car collide head-on. Compared with the truck, the change in momentum of the car has:**

   - A. greater magnitude
   - B. smaller magnitude
   - C. equal magnitude
   - D. no relationship

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** Forces are equal and opposite for the same time, so impulses and Δp are equal in size. Car's Δv is larger since its mass is smaller.  
*Common mistake:* Confusing equal Δp with equal Δv.

</details>

**10. Two objects with the same momentum are stopped by equal-size constant forces. Compare the stopping times.**

   - A. the heavier takes longer
   - B. the lighter takes longer
   - C. the times are equal
   - D. not enough information

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** Δt = Δp/F, and both Δp and F are the same.  
*Common mistake:* Thinking mass or speed alone determines stopping time.

</details>

## 5. Common misconceptions to watch for

- Momentum and kinetic energy are the same / both are always conserved.
- Momentum is a scalar (direction and signs matter).
- Bigger force always means bigger change in momentum (time matters).
- In a collision, the heavier object feels more force (third law).

## 6. Practice problems

Show: knowns, unknown, equation, substitution with units, and a reasonableness check.

1. A 0.145 kg baseball moves at 40 m/s. What is its momentum?
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>5.8 kg·m/s</details>

2. A 50 N force acts on a 2.0 kg object for 0.20 s. Find the impulse and velocity change.
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>J = 10 N·s; Δv = 5.0 m/s</details>

3. A 2 kg cart at 3 m/s hits and sticks to a 1 kg cart at rest. Find the final velocity.
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>v′ = 6/3 = 2.0 m/s</details>

4. Two skaters at rest push apart: 60 kg and 40 kg. The 40 kg skater moves at 3 m/s. How fast does the other move?
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>2.0 m/s in the opposite direction</details>

5. A 0.060 kg egg moving 5.0 m/s stops in 0.010 s (hard floor) vs 0.10 s (padding). Compare the average forces.
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>30 N vs 3 N: 10× less force with padding</details>

6. A 1500 kg car moving 20 m/s east hits a 1000 kg car at rest; they stick. Find the final velocity and KE lost.
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>v′ = 12 m/s; KE lost = 300 kJ − 180 kJ = 120 kJ</details>

## 8. Real-world applications

- Vehicle safety: airbags, crumple zones, seat belts, guardrail crash cushions.
- Sports: follow-through in baseball/golf, catching a ball by 'giving' with your hands, helmets.
- Rocket propulsion: exhaust momentum balances rocket momentum.
- Forensics: crash reconstruction from skid marks and conservation of momentum.
