# Standards Map (CA NGSS)

Source: [NGSS for California Public Schools](https://www.cde.ca.gov/pd/ca/sc/ngssstandards.asp), high school physical science standards ([PDF](https://www.cde.ca.gov/pd/ca/sc/documents/ngsshsphyicalscidci.pdf)). Wording below follows the published performance expectations; confirm against the PDF before using it in official documents.

| Performance expectation | What students do | Where it is addressed |
|---|---|---|
| **HS-PS2-1** | Analyze data to support the claim that Newton's second law of motion describes the mathematical relationship among the net force on a macroscopic object, its mass, and its acceleration. | Unit 3 (assessed); Units 1–2 build the acceleration skills it needs |
| **HS-PS2-2** | Use mathematical representations to support the claim that the total momentum of a system of objects is conserved when there is no net force on the system. | Unit 4 |
| **HS-PS2-3** | Apply scientific and engineering ideas to design, evaluate, and refine a device that minimizes the force on a macroscopic object during a collision. | Unit 4 (egg-drop project) |
| **HS-PS3-1** | Create a computational model to calculate the change in the energy of one component in a system when the change in energy of the other component(s) and energy flows in and out of the system are known. | Unit 5 (spreadsheet model) |
| **HS-PS3-2** | Develop and use models to illustrate that energy at the macroscopic scale can be accounted for as a combination of energy associated with the motions of particles (objects) and energy associated with the relative positions of particles (objects). | Unit 5 (energy bar charts) |
| **HS-PS3-3** | Design, build, and refine a device that works within given constraints to convert one form of energy into another form of energy. | Unit 5 (energy conversion device) |
| **HS-ETS1-2, HS-ETS1-3** | Break a complex real-world problem into smaller problems that can be solved through engineering; evaluate a solution against prioritized criteria and trade-offs. | Units 4 and 5 (design projects) |

Kinematics and projectile motion are not separate NGSS performance expectations. They are taught first because students must be able to measure and describe acceleration before they can analyze data for HS-PS2-1. HS-PS2-4 (gravitational and electrostatic forces) is previewed in Unit 3 and belongs with the Semester 2 work on fields.

## Science and engineering practices emphasized

| Practice | Where students use it |
|---|---|
| Analyzing and interpreting data | Motion graphs (Unit 1), second-law lab (Unit 3), collision lab (Unit 4) |
| Using mathematics and computational thinking | Kinematic equations, projectile predictions, momentum and energy calculations, spreadsheet energy model |
| Developing and using models | Free-body diagrams, motion maps, energy bar charts |
| Planning and carrying out investigations | Every unit lab: identify independent, dependent, and controlled variables |
| Designing solutions | Egg-drop device (Unit 4), energy conversion device (Unit 5) |

## Objectives by unit

### Unit 1: Kinematics

- HS-PS2-1 (foundation: acceleration must be measured before it can be linked to net force)
- SEP: Analyzing and Interpreting Data
- SEP: Using Mathematics and Computational Thinking
- CCC: Patterns

| ID | Objective |
|---|---|
| K1 | Distinguish distance from displacement and speed from velocity, and calculate each from a description of a trip. |
| K2 | Calculate acceleration and explain what its sign means for an object speeding up or slowing down. |
| K3 | Interpret position-time and velocity-time graphs using slope and area. |
| K4 | Select and solve the correct kinematic equation for constant acceleration. |
| K5 | Solve free-fall problems and explain why all objects fall with the same acceleration. |

### Unit 2: Projectile Motion

- HS-PS2-1 (foundation: gravity as the only force gives a constant vertical acceleration)
- SEP: Using Mathematics and Computational Thinking
- SEP: Developing and Using Models
- CCC: Cause and Effect

| ID | Objective |
|---|---|
| P1 | Resolve a vector into components and combine components into a resultant with magnitude and direction. |
| P2 | Explain the independence of horizontal and vertical motion and identify gravity as the only force on a projectile. |
| P3 | Solve horizontally launched projectile problems for time, range, and impact speed. |
| P4 | Solve angle-launched projectile problems for time of flight, maximum height, and range. |
| P5 | Make a quantitative prediction of a landing point, test it, and explain the sources of error. |

### Unit 3: Newton's Laws

- HS-PS2-1: Analyze data to support the claim that Newton's second law describes the relationship among net force, mass, and acceleration
- HS-PS2-4 (preview: gravitational force)
- SEP: Analyzing and Interpreting Data
- CCC: Cause and Effect

| ID | Objective |
|---|---|
| N1 | Identify the forces on an object and draw a correctly labeled free-body diagram. |
| N2 | Use the first law to explain the motion of objects with balanced forces, including constant-velocity motion. |
| N3 | Apply F_net = ma to solve for acceleration, net force, or mass in one-dimensional problems. |
| N4 | Identify action-reaction pairs and explain why equal forces can give unequal accelerations. |
| N5 | Calculate weight, normal force, and friction, and distinguish mass from weight. |
| N6 | Analyze cart-and-hanging-mass data to support the claim that a = F_net/m (HS-PS2-1). |

### Unit 4: Momentum

- HS-PS2-2: Use mathematical representations to support the claim that the total momentum of a system is conserved when there is no net force on the system
- HS-PS2-3: Design, evaluate, and refine a device that minimizes the force on an object during a collision
- HS-ETS1-2, HS-ETS1-3 (engineering design)
- CCC: Systems and System Models

| ID | Objective |
|---|---|
| M1 | Calculate momentum and compare the momenta of different objects, including direction. |
| M2 | Apply the impulse-momentum theorem to find force, time, or change in velocity. |
| M3 | Explain how extending collision time reduces force, with real examples. |
| M4 | Use conservation of momentum to solve collision and explosion problems (HS-PS2-2). |
| M5 | Classify collisions as elastic or inelastic using kinetic energy. |
| M6 | Design, test, and refine a device that minimizes force in a collision (HS-PS2-3). |

### Unit 5: Work & Energy

- HS-PS3-1: Create a computational model to calculate the change in energy of one component of a system when the other changes and energy flows are known
- HS-PS3-2: Develop and use models showing that energy is a combination of energy of motion and energy of relative position
- HS-PS3-3: Design, build, and refine a device that converts one form of energy into another
- CCC: Energy and Matter

| ID | Objective |
|---|---|
| E1 | Calculate work done by a force at any angle and identify zero and negative work. |
| E2 | Calculate kinetic energy and apply the work-energy theorem. |
| E3 | Calculate gravitational and elastic potential energy. |
| E4 | Apply conservation of energy to solve problems with and without friction, using bar charts as models (HS-PS3-2). |
| E5 | Calculate power and distinguish it from work. |
| E6 | Build a spreadsheet model of energy changes (HS-PS3-1) and an energy conversion device (HS-PS3-3). |
