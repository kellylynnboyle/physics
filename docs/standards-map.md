# NGSS Standards Map

[Back to project outline](../README.md)

Source: [NGSS for California Public Schools, K–12](https://www.cde.ca.gov/pd/ca/sc/ngssstandards.asp) and [Grades 9–12 Physical Science by Disciplinary Core Idea (PDF)](https://www.cde.ca.gov/pd/ca/sc/documents/ngsshsphyicalscidci.pdf).

| Unit | Standard or practice | How the unit addresses it |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Kinematics | **HS-PS2-1** | Foundation. Students need position, velocity, and acceleration data skills before they can analyze force, mass, and acceleration. |
| 1. Kinematics | **SEP 4 / SEP 5** | Analyzing and interpreting data; using mathematics and computational thinking (motion graphs, kinematic equations). |
| 1. Kinematics | **CCC Patterns** | Slope and area patterns on motion graphs. |
| 2. Projectile Motion | **HS-PS2-1** | Foundation. Gravity is the only force on a projectile, so acceleration is vertical only. Sets up net force and acceleration in the next unit. |
| 2. Projectile Motion | **SEP 2 / SEP 5** | Developing and using models; using mathematics (vector components, two sets of kinematic equations). |
| 2. Projectile Motion | **CCC Cause and Effect** | A vertical force changes only the vertical motion. |
| 3. Newton's Laws | **HS-PS2-1** | Analyze data to support the claim that Newton's second law describes the mathematical relationship among net force, mass, and acceleration. |
| 3. Newton's Laws | **HS-PS2-4** | Partial. Weight as the gravitational force on an object near Earth (F = mg) prepares for the law of gravitation. |
| 3. Newton's Laws | **SEP 4 / CCC Cause and Effect** | Analyzing data; net force as the cause of acceleration. |
| 4. Momentum | **HS-PS2-2** | Use mathematical representations to support the claim that the total momentum of a system of objects is conserved when there is no net force on the system. |
| 4. Momentum | **HS-PS2-3** | Apply scientific and engineering ideas to design, evaluate, and refine a device that minimizes the force on a macroscopic object during a collision. |
| 4. Momentum | **HS-ETS1-2 / HS-ETS1-3** | Engineering design: break a problem into parts and evaluate solutions against criteria and constraints (egg-drop or crumple-zone project). |
| 5. Work & Energy | **HS-PS3-1** | Create a computational model to calculate the change in the energy of one component in a system when the change in energy of the other components and energy flows in and out of the system are known. |
| 5. Work & Energy | **HS-PS3-2** | Develop and use models to illustrate that energy at the macroscopic scale can be accounted for as a combination of energy of motion and energy of relative position. |
| 5. Work & Energy | **HS-PS3-3** | Design, build, and refine a device that works within given constraints to convert one form of energy into another form of energy. |

No high school performance expectation names kinematics or projectile motion directly. Units 1 and 2 build the motion and data skills that HS-PS2-1 depends on.

Second-semester standards (HS-PS2-4, HS-PS2-5, HS-PS3-5, HS-PS4, HS-ESS1, HS-ESS2, HS-PS1-8) are outside this project.
