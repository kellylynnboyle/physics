# Standards Map: California NGSS, Semester 1

Source: [NGSS for California Public Schools](https://www.cde.ca.gov/pd/ca/sc/ngssstandards.asp), high school physical science, as used in the Physics in the Universe (Physics & Earth Science) course model. Performance expectation wording below is taken from the NGSS HS-PS2 and HS-PS3 pages.

## Performance expectations by unit

| Performance expectation | What students do | Unit | Where it is assessed |
|---|---|---|---|
| **HS-PS2-1** | Analyze data to support the claim that Newton's second law of motion describes the mathematical relationship among the net force on a macroscopic object, its mass, and its acceleration. | 3 (built on 1 and 2) | Cart-and-hanging-mass lab report; Unit 3 test |
| **HS-PS2-2** | Use mathematical representations to support the claim that the total momentum of a system of objects is conserved when there is no net force on the system. | 4 | Cart-collision lab; Unit 4 test |
| **HS-PS2-3** | Apply science and engineering ideas to design, evaluate, and refine a device that minimizes the force on a macroscopic object during a collision. | 4 | Egg-drop (collision safety) design report |
| **HS-PS3-1** | Create a computational model to calculate the change in the energy of one component in a system when the change in energy of the other component(s) and energy flows in and out of the system are known. | 5 | Spreadsheet energy model |
| **HS-PS3-2** | Develop and use models to illustrate that energy at the macroscopic scale can be accounted for as a combination of energy associated with the motion of particles (objects) and energy associated with the relative positions of particles (objects). | 5 | Energy bar charts; ramp or pendulum lab |
| **HS-PS3-3** | Design, build, and refine a device that works within given constraints to convert one form of energy into another form of energy. | 5 | Marble coaster or Rube Goldberg device report |
| **HS-ETS1-2, HS-ETS1-3** | Break a design problem into smaller parts; evaluate a solution against criteria and trade-offs. | 4, 5 | Both design reports |

## Notes on Units 1 and 2

NGSS has no performance expectation for kinematics or projectile motion on their own. They are taught because HS-PS2-1 requires students to analyze "tables or graphs of position or velocity as a function of time," which is the content of Unit 1. The HS-PS2-1 assessment boundary is one-dimensional motion, so Unit 2 (projectile motion) goes beyond what the state assessment requires. It is in this course because the course description lists it. Instructors short on time can shorten Unit 2 without losing coverage of an assessed standard.

## Earth science connections

The course model integrates Earth and space science. Each unit outline lists one link under "Real-world applications," which sets up second-semester topics:

- Unit 1: earthquake location from P-wave and S-wave travel times
- Unit 2: volcanic projectiles and landslide debris
- Unit 3: friction on faults and plate motion
- Unit 4: asteroid impacts and planetary defense
- Unit 5: energy release in earthquakes and landslides
