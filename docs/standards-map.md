# Standards Map: CA NGSS

[Project home](../README.md)

The learning goals for this course are the California Next Generation Science Standards for high school, following the Physics and Earth Science course model (Physics in the Universe in the three-course model). Source: [NGSS for California Public Schools, K–12](https://www.cde.ca.gov/pd/ca/sc/ngssstandards.asp), specifically the [Grades 9–12 Physical Science standards (PDF)](https://www.cde.ca.gov/pd/ca/sc/documents/ngsshsphyicalscidci.pdf).

## Semester 1 performance expectations

| Standard | Students who demonstrate understanding can… | Units |
|---|---|---|
| HS-PS2-1 | Analyze data to support the claim that Newton's second law of motion describes the mathematical relationship among the net force on a macroscopic object, its mass, and its acceleration. | 1 (graphing and acceleration skills), 2 (two-dimensional case), **3** |
| HS-PS2-2 | Use mathematical representations to support the claim that the total momentum of a system of objects is conserved when there is no net force on the system. | **4** |
| HS-PS2-3 | Apply scientific and engineering ideas to design, evaluate, and refine a device that minimizes the force on a macroscopic object during a collision. | **4** |
| HS-PS3-1 | Create a computational model to calculate the change in the energy of one component in a system when the change in energy of the other component(s) and energy flows in and out of the system are known. | **5** |
| HS-PS3-2 | Develop and use models to illustrate that energy at the macroscopic scale can be accounted for as a combination of energy associated with the motions of particles (objects) and energy associated with the relative positions of particles (objects). | **5** |
| HS-PS3-3 | Design, build, and refine a device that works within given constraints to convert one form of energy into another form of energy. | **5** |

Bold marks the unit where the standard is formally assessed.

### Assessment boundaries to keep in mind

- **HS-PS2-1**: limited to one-dimensional motion and to macroscopic objects at non-relativistic speeds. Projectile motion (Unit 2) goes beyond this boundary; it is taught as course content and is not needed to meet the standard.
- **HS-PS2-2**: limited to systems of two macroscopic bodies moving in one dimension.
- **HS-PS2-3**: limited to qualitative evaluations and/or algebraic manipulations.
- **HS-PS3-1**: limited to basic algebraic expressions or computations and to systems of two or three components.
- **HS-PS3-3**: quantitative evaluation is limited to total output for a given input.

## Science and engineering practices by unit

| Practice | Where it is used |
|---|---|
| Analyzing and interpreting data | Unit 1 motion graphs; Unit 3 second-law lab |
| Using mathematics and computational thinking | Every unit; Unit 5 spreadsheet energy model |
| Developing and using models | Unit 2 trajectory predictions; Unit 3 free-body diagrams; Unit 5 energy bar charts |
| Planning and carrying out investigations | All labs |
| Constructing explanations and designing solutions | Unit 4 egg drop; Unit 5 energy-conversion device |
| Engaging in argument from evidence | Claim, Evidence, Reasoning in every lab report |
