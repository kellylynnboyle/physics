**Pacing:** 4 weeks  |  **NGSS:** HS-PS3-1, HS-PS3-2, HS-PS3-3

> **Essential question:** How is energy transferred and transformed, and why is the total always conserved?

*Matches HS-PS3-1 (computational energy model), HS-PS3-2 (kinetic and potential energy models), and HS-PS3-3 (device converting energy). HS-PS3-4 (thermal energy) is a stretch connection through friction.*

## 1. Learning objectives

- [ ] Account for energy lost to friction as thermal energy; compute power and efficiency.

## 2. Key concepts

| Concept | What students must master |
|---|---|
| **Work** | W = Fd cosθ (joules). Only the component of force along the displacement does work. Perpendicular forces do zero work. |
| **Kinetic energy** | KE = ½mv². Depends on the square of speed. |
| **Gravitational potential energy** | PE_g = mgh (relative to a chosen zero level). |
| **Elastic potential energy** | PE_s = ½kx². |
| **Work–energy theorem** | W_net = ΔKE. |
| **Conservation of energy** | Energy is never created or destroyed: KE + PE + thermal… = constant in an isolated system. Without friction, mechanical energy (KE + PE) is conserved. |
| **Power & efficiency** | P = W/t (watts). Efficiency = useful energy out ÷ total energy in. |

**Key formulas:** `W = Fd cosθ`  ·  `KE = ½mv²`  ·  `PE_g = mgh`  ·  `PE_s = ½kx²`  ·  `W_net = ΔKE`  ·  `P = W/t`  ·  `E_i + W_ext = E_f + Q`

## 3. Cornell notes (cue column / notes column)

| Cues / Questions | Notes |
|---|---|
| What is work in physics? | W = Fd cosθ. Force must cause displacement. Holding a box still or carrying it level at constant speed: zero work by the lifting force (θ = 90°). |
| What is kinetic energy? | KE = ½mv². Double speed → 4× KE; double mass → 2× KE. |
| What is gravitational PE? | PE = mgh. Depends on height above a reference only, not the path taken. |
| What is the work–energy theorem? | Net work = change in KE. Positive net work speeds up; negative net work slows down. |
| How do I use conservation of energy? | Draw before/after bar charts (LOL diagrams). KE₁ + PE₁ = KE₂ + PE₂ (+ losses). From height h: v = √(2gh). |
| Where does energy go with friction? | Into thermal energy of surfaces and surroundings. Total energy is still conserved; mechanical energy is not. |
| What is power? | Rate of doing work: P = W/t = Fv. Watt = J/s. A kilowatt-hour is a unit of ENERGY (3.6 MJ). |
| Efficiency? | Useful out ÷ total in. No device is 100% efficient because some energy always spreads as heat. |

**Summary (write in your own words):** Work transfers energy. Energy changes form (kinetic, potential, thermal…) but the total remains constant. Energy accounting with bar charts lets us solve speed and height problems without tracking forces in detail.

## 4. Most-missed questions (self-quiz)

Try each question before opening the answer. The interactive version is in `site/index.html`.

**1. You carry a heavy box horizontally across a room at constant speed. The work done by your upward lifting force on the box is:**

   - A. positive
   - B. negative
   - C. zero
   - D. equal to mgd

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** The lifting force is perpendicular to the displacement (cos90° = 0), so it does no work.  
*Common mistake:* Thinking effort or holding a weight equals physics work.

</details>

**2. A car doubles its speed. Its kinetic energy becomes:**

   - A. 2 times larger
   - B. 4 times larger
   - C. 8 times larger
   - D. the same

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** KE = ½mv² ∝ v².  
*Common mistake:* Assuming KE is proportional to speed.

</details>

**3. How much gravitational potential energy does a 2.0 kg book gain when lifted 3.0 m?**

   - A. 6 J
   - B. 19.6 J
   - C. 58.8 J
   - D. 117.6 J

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** mgh = 2.0 × 9.8 × 3.0 = 58.8 J.  
*Common mistake:* Dropping g from the calculation (or ½ from KE).

</details>

**4. A frictionless roller-coaster car starts from rest at 20 m high. Its speed at the bottom is about:**

   - A. 9.8 m/s
   - B. 14 m/s
   - C. 19.8 m/s
   - D. 39.2 m/s

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** v = √(2gh) = √(2·9.8·20) ≈ 19.8 m/s, independent of the track's shape.  
*Common mistake:* Believing a steeper or shorter path changes the final speed; or forgetting the square root.

</details>

**5. A machine does 600 J of work in 3.0 s. Its power output is:**

   - A. 1800 W
   - B. 200 W
   - C. 600 W
   - D. 0.005 W

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** P = W/t = 600/3 = 200 W.  
*Common mistake:* Multiplying instead of dividing.

</details>

**6. A 2 kg object starts at rest. 50 J of net work is done on it. Its kinetic energy is now:**

   - A. 25 J
   - B. 50 J
   - C. 100 J
   - D. 0 J

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** W_net = ΔKE = KE_f − 0 = 50 J.  
*Common mistake:* Re-applying ½ or using mass when the theorem already gives KE directly.

</details>

**7. A spring is stretched twice as far as before. The elastic potential energy stored is:**

   - A. twice as much
   - B. four times as much
   - C. half as much
   - D. the same

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** PE_s = ½kx² ∝ x².  
*Common mistake:* Assuming linear scaling for spring energy.

</details>

**8. A sliding box slows to a stop on a rough floor. Where did its kinetic energy go?**

   - A. It disappeared
   - B. It became thermal energy of the box and floor
   - C. It became potential energy
   - D. It was stored as momentum

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Friction converts mechanical energy into thermal energy; total energy is conserved.  
*Common mistake:* Believing energy can be lost or destroyed.

</details>

**9. A kilowatt-hour (kWh) is a unit of:**

   - A. power
   - B. force
   - C. energy
   - D. current

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** kW (power) × h (time) = energy. 1 kWh = 3.6 × 10⁶ J.  
*Common mistake:* Reading 'kilowatt' as the whole unit and missing the time factor.

</details>

**10. A 10 N force acts at 60° above the horizontal on a wagon that moves 5.0 m horizontally. The work done by the force is:**

   - A. 50 J
   - B. 43 J
   - C. 25 J
   - D. 0 J

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** W = Fd cosθ = 10 × 5 × cos60° = 25 J.  
*Common mistake:* Ignoring the angle (50 J) or using sin instead of cos (43 J).

</details>

## 5. Common misconceptions to watch for

- Any force applied over time does work (displacement and direction matter).
- Energy is 'used up' (it is transformed).
- KE is proportional to speed (it is proportional to speed squared).
- Power and energy are the same (power is a rate).

Show: knowns, unknown, equation, substitution with units, and a reasonableness check.

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>W = 120 J</details>

2. A 10 N force pulls a wagon 5.0 m at 60° above the horizontal. How much work is done?
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>W = 10·5·cos60° = 25 J</details>

3. A 1000 kg car moves at 20 m/s. What is its kinetic energy?
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>KE = 200 000 J</details>

4. A 0.50 kg ball is dropped from 10 m. Find its speed and KE at the ground (no air resistance).
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>v = 14 m/s; KE = 49 J</details>

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>W = 9800 J; P = 980 W</details>

6. A 50 kg skater starts at rest at height 4.0 m. At the bottom her speed is 7.0 m/s. How much energy was lost to friction?

## 8. Real-world applications

- Renewable energy: hydroelectric dams (PE → KE → electrical), wind turbines, and solar efficiency.
- Transportation: regenerative braking in hybrids/EVs recovers KE; fuel efficiency and drag.
- Roller coasters and skate parks: energy bar charts and top-hill height design.
- Household energy: reading the electric bill in kWh and comparing appliance power ratings.
