# Learning Objectives and Assessment Criteria

Use this file to track progress by objective, not just by unit grade. Each objective has an ID (K1, P2, ...) that is repeated in the unit outline, in the progress tracker, and in the `obj` tag of every quiz question.

## Mastery scale (use for every objective)

| Level | Label | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | **Extending** | Solves unfamiliar or multi-step problems, explains *why* in words, connects to a real-world case or a second representation (graph, equation, diagram) |
| 3 | **Proficient** | Solves standard problems correctly with units and a clear setup; explains the reasoning; **target for every objective** |
| 2 | **Developing** | Right idea, but errors in setup, units, signs, or vectors; or can calculate but cannot explain |
| 1 | **Beginning** | Needs significant help; common misconception still present |

Mastery threshold: **3 on every objective** by the end of the unit. Objectives scored 1-2 are re-assessed after targeted practice.

## Learning objectives

Students will be able to...

### Unit 1: Kinematics (HS-PS2-1, supporting)
- **K1** Distinguish distance/displacement and speed/velocity; compute average velocity from displacement and time.
- **K2** Define acceleration as change in velocity over time and use sign to describe speeding up and slowing down.
- **K3** Interpret position-time and velocity-time graphs (slope and area) and convert between them.
- **K4** Apply the constant-acceleration equations to solve problems in one dimension.
- **K5** Analyze free fall (g = 9.8 m/s²), including the motion at the top of a toss.
- **K6** Collect and analyze motion data (e.g., cart, video, or motion sensor) and report a result with units.

### Unit 2: Projectile Motion (HS-PS2-1, supporting)
- **P1** Resolve a velocity vector into horizontal and vertical components.
- **P2** Explain that horizontal and vertical motions are independent and state the acceleration in each direction.
- **P3** Solve horizontal-launch problems (time from vertical motion, range from horizontal motion).
- **P4** Solve angled-launch problems: time of flight, maximum height, range.
- **P5** Predict and test a landing point in a lab; account for sources of error.

### Unit 3: Newton's Laws (HS-PS2-1)
- **N1** State and apply Newton's first law (inertia, equilibrium, constant velocity).
- **N2** Draw free-body diagrams for objects at rest, at constant velocity, and accelerating.
- **N3** Use ΣF = ma to solve one-dimensional and incline problems.
- **N4** Distinguish mass from weight; calculate weight and apparent weight.
- **N5** Identify third-law pairs correctly (different objects, equal magnitude, opposite direction).
- **N6** Model friction (static vs. kinetic) and air resistance, including terminal velocity.
- **N7** Analyze data from an investigation to support the claim that a ∝ F_net and a ∝ 1/m.

### Unit 4: Momentum (HS-PS2-2, HS-PS2-3)
- **M1** Calculate momentum as a vector and compare momenta of different objects.
- **M2** Relate impulse to change in momentum and to force × time.
- **M3** Apply conservation of momentum to collisions and explosions in 1D.
- **M4** Classify collisions as elastic, inelastic, or perfectly inelastic using kinetic energy before and after.
- **M5** Explain how stopping time affects average force (airbags, crumple zones, helmets).
- **M6** Design, test, and refine a device that minimizes force in a collision (egg-drop or cart-crash design challenge).

### Unit 5: Work & Energy (HS-PS3-1, HS-PS3-2, HS-PS3-3)
- **E1** Calculate work done by a force, including when the force is at an angle (W = Fd cos θ).
- **E2** Calculate kinetic, gravitational potential, and elastic potential energy.
- **E3** Apply the work-energy theorem (net work = ΔKE).
- **E4** Apply conservation of mechanical energy to ramps, pendulums, springs, and roller coasters.
- **E5** Account for energy transformed by friction and other non-conservative forces.
- **E6** Calculate power and efficiency.
- **E7** Build and use an energy bar chart or computational model; design a device converting one form of energy to another.

## Assessment types and suggested weights

| Category | What it is | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| Formative checks | Cornell notes (checked for completeness and quality of cues), practice sets, exit tickets, quiz-app attempts | 20% |
| Labs and design challenges | Lab report using the rubric below; engineering challenge (M6, E7) | 30% |
| Unit assessments | Problems, graph analysis, and explanations tied to the objectives above | 40% |
| Semester project / performance task | Cumulative, e.g., analyze a real video of a collision or a roller coaster using all five units | 10% |

Adjust to your school's grading policy. Reassessment is allowed for any objective scored below 3 after the student completes the targeted practice listed in the unit outline.

## Quiz-app targets (formative)

| Score | Meaning | Next step |
|---|---|---|
| 90-100% | Ready for unit assessment | Try the "all topics" mix for spaced review |
| 80-89% | Nearly there | Read explanations on missed items, retake missed-only review |
| 60-79% | Gaps | Revisit Cornell notes cues for the missed objectives, redo the matching practice problems, retake |
| below 60% | Re-teach needed | Meet with instructor; use the simulation for the unit |

## Problem-solving rubric (for all calculation work, 4 points)

| Criterion | 1 pt each |
|---|---|
| Diagram / system defined | Sketch, free-body diagram, or energy/momentum bar chart with axes and sign convention |
| Given / unknown / equation | Quantities listed with units; correct equation chosen and stated |
| Algebra then numbers | Solved symbolically first, substituted with units, correct arithmetic |
| Answer check | Units, sign, significant figures, and a sense-check ("is 400 m/s reasonable for a thrown ball?") |

## Lab report rubric (4-level, per row)

| Row | 4 Extending | 3 Proficient | 2 Developing | 1 Beginning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Question and hypothesis | Testable, predicts a relationship with reasoning from physics | Testable, prediction stated | Vague or untestable | Missing |
| Procedure and variables | Controls and uncertainty sources identified and addressed | Independent, dependent, controlled variables identified | Some variables confused | Missing |
| Data and graphs | Clear table, labeled graph with best-fit line and interpreted slope | Table and labeled graph | Incomplete labels or units | Missing |
| Analysis (CER) | Claim supported by evidence and physics reasoning; quantifies % error | Claim, evidence, reasoning present | Claim without reasoning | Missing |
| Conclusion and error | Specific improvements to design | Identifies sources of error | Generic ("human error") | Missing |

## Performance-task idea: capstone

Pick a real event (a car crash test, a diver, a roller-coaster drop, a basketball shot). Using a video and free analysis software, produce: motion graphs (Unit 1), a projectile or force analysis (Units 2-3), a momentum or energy analysis (Units 4-5), and a one-paragraph claim with evidence and reasoning.
