## Key concepts

- **Momentum (p):** p = mv; a vector in kg·m/s pointing along the velocity.
- **Impulse (J):** J = FΔt = Δp; the product of average net force and time. Area under an F–t graph.
- **Conservation of momentum:** In a closed, isolated system (no net external force) total momentum before = after.
- **Elastic collision:** Both momentum and kinetic energy conserved (objects bounce, no deformation).
- **Inelastic collision:** Momentum conserved, kinetic energy NOT conserved. Perfectly inelastic: objects stick together; maximum KE loss.
- **Explosion / recoil:** System starts at rest, total momentum = 0 so pieces move in opposite directions.

## Key equations

- `p = m·v`
- `J = F·Δt = Δp = m·v_f − m·v_i`
- `m₁v₁ + m₂v₂ = m₁v₁′ + m₂v₂′`
- `Perfectly inelastic: m₁v₁ + m₂v₂ = (m₁ + m₂)·v′`
- `KE = ½·m·v²  (compare before/after)`

## Cornell notes

**Topic:** Momentum — inertia in motion  
**Name:** ____________  **Date:** ____________

| Cue / question | Notes |
|---|---|
| What is momentum? | p = mv (kg·m/s). A vector: direction of velocity. Heavier or faster → more momentum. A slow truck can match a fast bullet. |
| What is impulse? | J = FΔt = Δp. A net force acting for a time changes momentum. Units N·s = kg·m/s. Area under an F–t graph. |
| Why do airbags help? | Same Δp (stopping a person) but a longer Δt means a smaller average force: F = Δp/Δt. Crumple zones, padding, bending knees when landing, catching a ball by pulling hands back. |
| Rebound vs. stick | If an object bounces back, its Δp is larger than if it just stops (the velocity reverses sign), so the impulse is larger. 0.5 kg ball at 10 m/s bounces back at 10 m/s: Δp = 10 kg·m/s; if it merely stops, Δp = 5. |
| Conservation of momentum | Total p before = total p after when no net external force. Choose a + direction; velocities in the opposite direction are negative. |
| Types of collisions | Elastic: p and KE conserved. Inelastic: p conserved, KE lost to heat/sound/deformation. Perfectly inelastic: objects stick together. Momentum is conserved in ALL of them (isolated system). |
| Solving collision problems | 1) Sketch before/after. 2) Pick + direction; assign signs. 3) Write Σp_before = Σp_after. 4) Solve. 5) Compare KE before and after to classify. |
| Explosions & recoil | Start at rest: Σp = 0, so m₁v₁ = −m₂v₂. Smaller mass moves faster. Gun and bullet, rocket and exhaust, skaters pushing apart. |

**Summary:** Momentum (mv) measures how hard it is to stop a moving object. A net force over time (impulse) changes it. When no outside force acts on a system, total momentum stays constant even though energy may change form — that's the key to collisions, explosions and safety design: increase collision time to reduce force.

## Quiz: most-missed questions

_Interactive version: open `site/index.html`. Answers are at the end of this section._

1. A 1000 kg car moves at 20 m/s. What is its momentum?
   - A. 20,000 kg·m/s
   - B. 200,000 kg·m/s
   - C. 50 kg·m/s
   - D. 20,000 J
2. Why does an airbag reduce injury in a crash?
   - A. It reduces the change in momentum
   - B. It increases the stopping time, lowering average force
   - C. It increases the impulse
   - D. It gives the passenger more kinetic energy
3. Which quantity is conserved in ALL collisions in an isolated system?
   - A. Kinetic energy
   - B. Momentum
   - C. Velocity
   - D. Speed
4. A 2 kg cart moving at 3 m/s collides with and sticks to a 1 kg cart at rest. What is their common speed afterward?
   - A. 3 m/s
   - B. 2 m/s
   - C. 1.5 m/s
   - D. 1 m/s
5. Two 1 kg carts move toward each other, each at 4 m/s, and stick together. What happens?
   - A. They move at 4 m/s
   - B. They move at 8 m/s
   - C. They stop (v = 0)
   - D. They move at 2 m/s
6. A 0.5 kg ball hits a wall at 10 m/s and rebounds at 10 m/s. What is the magnitude of the impulse on the ball?
   - A. 0 N·s
   - B. 5 N·s
   - C. 10 N·s
   - D. 50 N·s
7. Two ice skaters (60 kg and 40 kg) at rest push off each other. The 40 kg skater glides at 3 m/s. How fast does the 60 kg skater move?
   - A. 3 m/s in the same direction
   - B. 2 m/s in the opposite direction
   - C. 4.5 m/s in the opposite direction
   - D. 0 m/s
8. A moving ball collides with a stationary ball and both move together afterward. This collision is…
   - A. elastic
   - B. perfectly inelastic
   - C. an explosion
   - D. not conserving momentum

<details><summary>Answer key and explanations</summary>

1. **A** — p = mv = 1000 × 20 = 20,000 kg·m/s. (200,000 J would be its kinetic energy; momentum is not measured in joules.) _(Misconception: Mixing up momentum (mv) and kinetic energy (½mv²).)_
2. **B** — The change in momentum is fixed by the crash. Since FΔt = Δp, a longer Δt means a smaller average force. _(Misconception: Thinking the change in momentum or the impulse is reduced.)_
3. **B** — Momentum is conserved whenever there's no net external force. Kinetic energy is conserved only in elastic collisions. _(Misconception: Assuming kinetic energy is always conserved.)_
4. **B** — 2(3) + 1(0) = (2 + 1)v → v = 6/3 = 2 m/s. _(Misconception: Forgetting to add both masses afterward.)_
5. **C** — Take right as +: (+4) + (−4) = 0 total momentum, so the combined cart has v = 0. Direction (sign) matters. _(Misconception: Ignoring the sign of velocity.)_
6. **C** — Δp = m(v_f − v_i) = 0.5[(−10) − (+10)] = −10 kg·m/s, magnitude 10 N·s. Reversing direction doubles the change compared with stopping. _(Misconception: Treating the speed as unchanged and calling Δp zero.)_
7. **B** — Total p starts at 0: 60v + 40(3) = 0 → v = −2 m/s, opposite direction and slower because of the greater mass. _(Misconception: Forgetting that recoil momentum is equal and opposite.)_
8. **B** — Objects that stick together are perfectly inelastic. Kinetic energy is lost but momentum is conserved. _(Misconception: Thinking inelastic collisions violate conservation of momentum.)_

</details>

## Practice problems

1. Find the momentum of a 0.145 kg baseball moving at 40 m/s.
2. A 0.145 kg ball arrives at 40 m/s and leaves the bat at 50 m/s the other way. Find the impulse and the average force if contact lasts 1.0 ms.
3. A 1500 kg car moving 10 m/s hits a stationary 1000 kg car and they stick. Final speed? KE lost?
4. A 3 kg cart at +2 m/s sticks to a 1 kg cart moving at −2 m/s. Final velocity?
5. A 4.0 kg rifle fires a 0.010 kg bullet at 400 m/s. Recoil speed of the rifle?

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. p = 0.145 × 40 = 5.8 kg·m/s
2. Δp = 0.145(50 − (−40)) = 13 N·s; F = 13.05/0.001 ≈ 1.3 × 10⁴ N
3. v = 15000/2500 = 6.0 m/s; KE before 75,000 J, after 45,000 J → 30,000 J lost
4. (6 − 2)/4 = +1 m/s
5. v = (0.010 × 400)/4.0 = 1.0 m/s opposite the bullet

</details>

## Lab / investigation

Dynamics-cart collisions (or Collision Lab): measure velocities before and after elastic and sticky collisions; verify that total momentum is conserved and compute kinetic energy change.

## Real-world applications

- Vehicle safety: airbags, seat belts, crumple zones and guardrails.
- Sports: follow-through in baseball and golf, boxing gloves, catching a fast ball.
- Rockets and jets: recoil and exhaust momentum.
- Accident reconstruction from momentum before and after impact.
- Billiards and Newton's cradle: nearly elastic collisions.
