## Key concepts

- **Work (W):** Energy transferred by a force acting through a displacement: W = Fd cosθ. Unit joule (J) = N·m. Only the component of force along the motion counts.
- **Kinetic energy (KE):** Energy of motion: KE = ½mv².
- **Gravitational potential energy (PE):** Stored energy due to height: PE = mgh (relative to a chosen zero level).
- **Elastic potential energy:** Energy stored in a spring: PE = ½kx².
- **Work–energy theorem:** Net work on an object equals its change in kinetic energy.
- **Conservation of energy:** Energy is never created or destroyed; with friction, mechanical energy is converted to thermal energy.
- **Power (P):** Rate of doing work/energy transfer: P = W/t. Unit watt (W).

## Key equations

- `W = F·d·cosθ`
- `KE = ½·m·v²`
- `PE_g = m·g·h`
- `PE_spring = ½·k·x²`
- `W_net = ΔKE = ½mv_f² − ½mv_i²`
- `KE₁ + PE₁ = KE₂ + PE₂ (+ thermal losses)`
- `P = W/t = F·v`

## Cornell notes

**Topic:** Work & energy — the currency of change  
**Name:** ____________  **Date:** ____________

| Cue / question | Notes |
|---|---|
| What is work? | W = F d cosθ, where θ is the angle between force and displacement. Units J = N·m. Needs force AND displacement in the direction of the force. |
| When is work zero or negative? | Zero: no displacement (pushing a wall) or force ⟂ motion (carrying a box horizontally at constant speed). Negative: force opposes motion (friction, braking). |
| Kinetic energy | KE = ½mv². Depends on v²: double the speed → 4× the KE (and 4× the stopping distance). Scalar, always ≥ 0. |
| Potential energy | Gravitational: PE = mgh (choose a zero height). Spring: ½kx². Stored in the system (field/deformation); only changes in PE matter physically. |
| Work–energy theorem | W_net = ΔKE. Positive net work speeds an object up, negative slows it. Good when forces or paths are complicated. |
| Conservation of energy | Total energy of an isolated system is constant. Without friction: KE + PE = constant (ex: roller coaster, pendulum). With friction: E_initial = E_final + thermal energy. |
| Using energy to solve | Choose start/end points; write KE + PE at each; set equal (include losses). Drop-from-rest speed: v = √(2gh), independent of mass and path (no friction). |
| Power | P = W/t = ΔE/t. 1 W = 1 J/s. Horsepower ≈ 746 W. Climbing stairs faster takes more power, not more work. |
| Energy transformation (HS-PS3) | Make energy bar charts showing where energy goes: chemical → kinetic → thermal. Efficiency = useful output / input. Energy is 'lost' only as unusable thermal energy, never destroyed. |

**Summary:** Work transfers energy; kinetic energy depends on mass and the square of speed; potential energy depends on position. The total energy of a system is conserved, so we can predict speeds and heights without knowing forces along the path. Friction does negative work and converts mechanical energy to thermal energy. Power is how fast energy is transferred.

## Quiz: most-missed questions

_Interactive version: open `site/index.html`. Answers are at the end of this section._

1. A person carries a heavy box horizontally at constant speed across a room. How much work does the person's upward lifting force do on the box?
   - A. Positive work equal to mgd
   - B. Negative work
   - C. Zero
   - D. It depends on how heavy the box is
2. A car doubles its speed. By what factor does its kinetic energy change?
   - A. ×2
   - B. ×4
   - C. ×8
   - D. ×1/2
3. A 10 N force pulls a box 5 m along the floor at 60° above the horizontal. How much work does the force do?
   - A. 50 J
   - B. 43 J
   - C. 25 J
   - D. 86 J
4. How much gravitational potential energy does a 2 kg book gain when lifted 3 m? (g = 9.8 m/s²)
   - A. 6 J
   - B. 29 J
   - C. 59 J
   - D. 196 J
5. A ball is dropped from rest from 5.0 m (no air resistance). About how fast is it going right before it hits the ground?
   - A. 5 m/s
   - B. 9.9 m/s
   - C. 49 m/s
   - D. 25 m/s
6. A machine does 600 J of work in 3.0 s. What is its power output?
   - A. 1800 W
   - B. 200 W
   - C. 600 W
   - D. 20 W
7. A car skids to a stop with the brakes locked. If its initial speed doubles, its skid distance (same road) becomes…
   - A. 2× as long
   - B. 4× as long
   - C. 8× as long
   - D. the same
8. A skateboarder rolls down a ramp and some mechanical energy is lost to friction. Where does the 'missing' energy go?
   - A. It is destroyed
   - B. It becomes thermal energy (and sound)
   - C. It becomes more kinetic energy
   - D. It becomes momentum

<details><summary>Answer key and explanations</summary>

1. **C** — The lifting force is perpendicular to the displacement (θ = 90°, cos90° = 0), so W = 0. _(Misconception: Believing that effort or holding a weight always means work is done.)_
2. **B** — KE = ½mv². Doubling v multiplies KE by 2² = 4. _(Misconception: Treating KE as proportional to v instead of v².)_
3. **C** — W = Fd cosθ = 10 × 5 × cos60° = 25 J. Only the horizontal component does work. _(Misconception: Omitting the cosθ factor (or using sin).)_
4. **C** — PE = mgh = 2 × 9.8 × 3 = 58.8 ≈ 59 J. _(Misconception: Forgetting g or using the wrong variable.)_
5. **B** — mgh = ½mv² → v = √(2gh) = √(2 × 9.8 × 5.0) ≈ 9.9 m/s. Mass cancels. _(Misconception: Forgetting the square root or that the mass cancels.)_
6. **B** — P = W/t = 600/3 = 200 W (200 J/s). _(Misconception: Multiplying instead of dividing; confusing work and power.)_
7. **B** — Friction work equals the KE: μmg·d = ½mv² → d ∝ v². Doubling speed means 4× the distance. _(Misconception: Assuming stopping distance is proportional to speed.)_
8. **B** — Total energy is conserved; friction transforms mechanical energy into thermal energy in the surfaces and air. _(Misconception: Believing energy is 'used up' or destroyed.)_

</details>

## Practice problems

1. A 50 N force pushes a crate 4.0 m in the direction of the force. How much work is done?
2. Find the kinetic energy of a 1000 kg car moving at 20 m/s. How much work is needed to stop it?
3. A 0.50 kg ball is dropped from 10 m. Find its initial PE and speed at the ground.
4. A 500 kg coaster car starts from rest at a height of 40 m. Speed at a height of 10 m (frictionless)?
5. A 60 kg student climbs 3.0 m of stairs in 4.0 s. Work done against gravity and power?

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. W = 50 × 4.0 = 200 J
2. KE = ½(1000)(400) = 200,000 J; stopping requires W = −200,000 J (200 kJ)
3. PE = 0.50(9.8)(10) = 49 J; v = √(2·9.8·10) ≈ 14 m/s
4. v = √(2g(40 − 10)) = √588 ≈ 24 m/s
5. W = 60(9.8)(3.0) ≈ 1760 J; P = 1764/4.0 ≈ 440 W

</details>

## Real-world applications

- Roller coasters, ski jumps and skateboard parks: trading PE and KE.
- Renewable energy: hydroelectric dams, wind turbines and regenerative braking in EVs.
- Efficiency labels on appliances and cars (useful output / input energy).
- Stopping distance and speed limits near schools (KE ∝ v²).
- Human power: food Calories as chemical energy and athletic power outputs.
