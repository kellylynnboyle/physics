## Key concepts

- **Scalar vs. vector:** Scalars have size only (distance, speed, time). Vectors have size and direction (displacement, velocity, acceleration).
- **Displacement (Δx):** Change in position: final minus initial. Can be zero or negative.
- **Velocity (v):** Rate of change of position: v = Δx / Δt. Slope of an x–t graph.
- **Acceleration (a):** Rate of change of velocity: a = Δv / Δt. Slope of a v–t graph. Units m/s².
- **Free fall:** Motion under gravity alone. Near Earth's surface a = 9.8 m/s² down, for rising, falling and at the top.
- **Reference frame & sign convention:** Choose +direction first and keep it for every quantity in the problem.

## Key equations

- `v = Δx / Δt`
- `a = Δv / Δt`
- `v = v₀ + a·t`
- `Δx = v₀·t + ½·a·t²`
- `v² = v₀² + 2·a·Δx`
- `Δx = ½(v₀ + v)·t`

## Cornell notes

**Topic:** Kinematics — describing motion in one dimension  
**Name:** ____________  **Date:** ____________

| Cue / question | Notes |
|---|---|
| Scalar vs. vector? | Scalar = magnitude only (distance, speed). Vector = magnitude + direction (displacement, velocity, acceleration). Use + / − signs for direction in 1-D. |
| Distance vs. displacement? | Distance = total path length (always ≥ 0). Displacement = final position − initial position (straight-line change, can be 0 or negative). One lap of a 400 m track: distance 400 m, displacement 0. |
| Speed vs. velocity? | Average speed = distance / time. Average velocity = displacement / time (v = Δx/Δt). Velocity has a sign. |
| What is acceleration? | a = Δv/Δt = (v − v₀)/t. Any change in velocity — speeding up, slowing down OR turning. Units: m/s per s = m/s². |
| Speeding up or slowing down? | Same sign for v and a → speeding up. Opposite signs → slowing down. Negative a does NOT automatically mean slowing down. |
| Graph rules | x–t graph: slope = velocity. v–t graph: slope = acceleration, area under curve = displacement. Straight horizontal line on v–t = constant velocity (a = 0). Curve on x–t = changing velocity. |
| Constant-acceleration equations | v = v₀ + at ;  Δx = v₀t + ½at² ;  v² = v₀² + 2aΔx. Pick the equation that is missing the unknown you don't need. List knowns/unknown (GUESS: Given, Unknown, Equation, Solve, check units). |
| Free fall | Only gravity acts: a = −9.8 m/s² (taking up as +) the whole trip — going up, at the top, and coming down. At the very top v = 0 but a is still 9.8 m/s². Mass doesn't change fall time without air resistance. |
| Strategy for any problem | 1) Sketch + choose a +direction. 2) List knowns with signs and units. 3) Choose equation. 4) Solve algebraically, then plug in. 5) Does the answer make sense? |

**Summary:** Kinematics describes motion without asking what causes it. Position, velocity and acceleration are linked by slopes and areas on graphs, and constant acceleration lets us predict motion with three equations. Always set a sign convention, track units, and remember that gravity gives a constant 9.8 m/s² downward during free fall.

## Quiz: most-missed questions

_Interactive version: open `site/index.html`. Answers are at the end of this section._

1. A runner completes exactly one lap of a 400 m track in 80 s. What is the runner's average velocity?
   - A. 5 m/s
   - B. 0 m/s
   - C. 400 m/s
   - D. 80 m/s
2. A ball is thrown straight up. At the very top of its path, what are its velocity and acceleration?
   - A. v = 0, a = 0
   - B. v = 0, a = 9.8 m/s² downward
   - C. v = 9.8 m/s, a = 0
   - D. v = 0, a = 9.8 m/s² upward
3. A car moves in the negative direction and its acceleration is also negative. The car is…
   - A. slowing down
   - B. speeding up
   - C. moving at constant speed
   - D. at rest
4. On a velocity–time graph, the area between the line and the time axis represents…
   - A. acceleration
   - B. displacement
   - C. speed
   - D. force
5. A car starts from rest and accelerates at 3 m/s² for 4 s. How far does it travel?
   - A. 12 m
   - B. 24 m
   - C. 48 m
   - D. 6 m
6. A bowling ball and a tennis ball are dropped from the same height in a vacuum. Which hits the ground first?
   - A. The bowling ball
   - B. The tennis ball
   - C. They hit at the same time
   - D. It depends on their volumes only
7. A ball is thrown upward at 20 m/s. About how long until it returns to the launch height? (g = 9.8 m/s²)
   - A. 2.0 s
   - B. 4.1 s
   - C. 8.2 s
   - D. 1.0 s
8. On a position–time graph, a straight line sloping upward with constant slope means…
   - A. constant acceleration
   - B. constant velocity
   - C. an object at rest
   - D. velocity increasing

<details><summary>Answer key and explanations</summary>

1. **B** — Average velocity = displacement ÷ time. After one full lap the displacement is zero, so the average velocity is 0 (the average speed is 5 m/s). _(Misconception: Confusing distance with displacement.)_
2. **B** — Gravity acts the entire flight, so a = 9.8 m/s² downward even when v is momentarily zero. _(Misconception: Thinking zero velocity means zero acceleration.)_
3. **B** — Velocity and acceleration have the same sign, so speed increases. Negative acceleration does not always mean slowing down. _(Misconception: Equating negative acceleration with slowing down.)_
4. **B** — Area under v–t = v × t = displacement. The slope of v–t is acceleration. _(Misconception: Mixing up slope and area interpretations.)_
5. **B** — Δx = v₀t + ½at² = 0 + ½(3)(4²) = 24 m. (12 m/s is the final speed, not the distance.) _(Misconception: Forgetting the ½ or confusing final speed with distance.)_
6. **C** — Free-fall acceleration is 9.8 m/s² for every object regardless of mass, so both fall together when air resistance is negligible. _(Misconception: Believing heavier objects fall faster.)_
7. **B** — Use v = v₀ + at with v = −20: −20 = 20 − 9.8t → t = 40/9.8 ≈ 4.1 s. (2.0 s is only the trip up.) _(Misconception: Stopping at the peak instead of the full round trip.)_
8. **B** — Slope of x–t is velocity. A constant slope means constant velocity (zero acceleration). _(Misconception: Reading the graph's shape as the 'path' instead of using slope.)_

</details>

## Practice problems

1. A car speeds up from 0 to 25 m/s in 8.0 s. What is its average acceleration?
2. A car traveling 20 m/s brakes with a = −5.0 m/s². How far and how long until it stops?
3. A rock is dropped from a 45 m cliff. Find the fall time and impact speed.
4. A v–t graph rises from 0 to 10 m/s in 5 s, then stays at 10 m/s for 5 s. Find the total displacement.
5. A runner covers 100 m in 12.5 s. Average speed? If she returns to the start in another 12.5 s, what is her average velocity for the round trip?

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. a = Δv/Δt = 25/8.0 ≈ 3.1 m/s²
2. t = 20/5 = 4.0 s; Δx = v²/(2|a|) = 400/10 = 40 m
3. t = √(2·45/9.8) ≈ 3.0 s; v = 9.8 × 3.03 ≈ 30 m/s
4. Triangle ½(5)(10) = 25 m + rectangle 10 × 5 = 50 m → 75 m
5. Speed = 8.0 m/s on the way out; round-trip average velocity = 0 m/s (displacement 0)

</details>

## Lab / investigation

Cart on a ramp (or Moving Man data): collect position vs. time, fit the graphs, and use slope and area to support a claim in Claim–Evidence–Reasoning format that acceleration is constant.

## Real-world applications

- Traffic safety: stopping distance grows with the square of speed (v² = 2aΔx).
- Sports analytics: sprint start acceleration, reaction time and split times.
- Aviation and rockets: takeoff run length and launch acceleration.
- Smartphone accelerometers and fitness trackers estimating steps and speed.
- Forensics: estimating a car's speed from skid-mark length.
