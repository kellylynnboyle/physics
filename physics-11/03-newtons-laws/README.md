# Topic 3: Newton's Laws of Motion

Newton's Laws explain *why* the motion described by kinematics happens — connecting force, mass, and acceleration.

## Key Concepts to Master

- **Newton's First Law (Inertia):** an object's velocity stays constant unless acted on by a net (unbalanced) force. Applies equally to objects at rest *and* objects moving at constant velocity.
- **Newton's Second Law:** $\Sigma F = ma$ — net force, not any single force, determines acceleration.
- **Newton's Third Law:** action-reaction pairs act on *different* objects, are equal in magnitude, opposite in direction, and are the *same type* of force. They never cancel each other out because they act on different bodies.
- **Free-body diagrams (FBDs):** isolating one object and drawing every force acting *on* it (not forces it exerts on other things).
- **Common forces:** weight ($F_g = mg$), normal force, applied force, tension, friction (static vs. kinetic, $f = \mu N$), air resistance.
- **Equilibrium:** net force = 0 does not mean no forces are acting — it means they balance (includes both "at rest" and "constant velocity" cases).
- **Systems of connected objects** (e.g., blocks connected by a string, Atwood machines) — same acceleration magnitude, tension found via simultaneous equations.
- **Inclined planes:** decomposing gravity into components parallel and perpendicular to the surface.

## Learning Objectives (SWBAT)

1. Draw a correct, fully-labeled free-body diagram for an object in a variety of scenarios (flat surface, incline, connected objects, elevator).
2. Apply $\Sigma F = ma$ separately along perpendicular axes to solve for unknown forces or accelerations.
3. Correctly distinguish Newton's Third Law pairs from balanced forces on the same object (a very different, unrelated concept that students conflate).
4. Solve friction problems including determining whether an object moves at all (comparing applied force to maximum static friction).
5. Solve multi-object system problems (connected blocks, pulleys) using consistent sign conventions.

## Common Pitfalls (why this topic gets missed)

- **The single biggest misconception:** thinking the normal force and gravity are a Newton's Third Law pair. They are *not* — they act on the *same* object and happen to be equal only in the simple flat-ground, no-other-vertical-force case. The real 3rd-law pair to gravity-on-object is gravity-object-pulls-Earth-up; the real pair to normal-force-on-object is object-pushes-down-on-surface.
- Believing a net force is needed to keep something moving at constant velocity (1st Law violation — this is the most persistent pre-Newtonian intuition).
- Forgetting friction acts opposite to the direction of *relative motion or attempted motion*, not always "backward."
- Using $mg$ for weight on an incline without decomposing it into components.
- For connected objects, treating the two objects as having independent (rather than equal-magnitude, linked) accelerations.

## Practice Problems

1. A 5 kg box sits on a table. Identify every force acting on it and state the Newton's Third Law reaction pair for each.
2. A 10 kg sled is pulled with 40 N at 30° above horizontal on a surface with $\mu_k = 0.2$. Find its acceleration.
3. Two blocks (3 kg and 5 kg) are connected by a string over a frictionless pulley (Atwood machine). Find the acceleration of the system and the tension in the string.
4. A 2 kg block sits on a 25° frictionless incline. Find its acceleration down the ramp.
5. A person stands on a scale in an elevator. Explain (using Newton's 2nd Law) why the scale reads more than their weight when the elevator accelerates upward, and less when it accelerates downward.

## Real-World Applications

- **Seatbelts and airbags:** Newton's First Law — why your body keeps moving forward in a sudden stop.
- **Rocket propulsion:** Third Law — exhaust gas pushed backward, rocket pushed forward.
- **Car tires and ABS brakes:** static vs. kinetic friction — why a skidding (kinetic friction) tire has less stopping force than one on the verge of skidding (static friction).
- **Cargo trucks and load securing:** why unsecured cargo continues moving during sudden braking (inertia).
- **Elevators and roller coasters:** apparent weight changes during acceleration.

Student work is assessed on:
- Completeness and correctness of free-body diagrams (all forces present, correctly labeled, correctly directed)
- Correct application of $\Sigma F = ma$ along chosen axes
- Correct identification of true Newton's Third Law pairs vs. balanced forces
- Correct friction analysis (static vs. kinetic, correct direction)
- For systems: consistent, correct sign convention linking multiple objects
