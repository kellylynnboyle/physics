# 11th Grade Physics — Semester Project Outline

A structured, self-contained unit covering five core mechanics topics. Each topic folder contains the same three files, so students and instructors can navigate consistently:

| File | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `README.md` | Key concepts, learning objectives, resources, practice problems, real-world applications, assessment criteria |
| `cornell-notes.md` | Fillable Cornell-notes template (cue column / notes / summary) pre-seeded with the topic's cue questions |
| `quiz.html` | Self-grading interactive quiz built from the questions students most often miss on this topic, with explanations |

Open any `quiz.html` file directly in a browser — no install needed.

## Unit Sequence

0. [**Distance vs. Displacement & Speed-Distance-Time**](00-speed-distance-time/cornell-notes.md) — foundational vocabulary and the speed formula, ahead of full kinematics (Apprentice/Master level notes)
1. [**Kinematics**](01-kinematics/README.md) — describing motion in 1D
2. [**Projectile Motion**](02-projectile-motion/README.md) — motion in 2D under gravity
3. [**Newton's Laws**](03-newtons-laws/README.md) — forces and why motion changes
4. [**Momentum**](04-momentum/README.md) — collisions and conservation
5. [**Work & Energy**](05-work-energy/README.md) — the energy approach to mechanics

**Test prep:** [Unit 0 Motion Test — Study Guide & Answer Key](unit-0-motion-test/study-guide-answers.md) (covers Topics 0 & 1) — transcribed from the class's answer key, plus a matching [Anki flashcard deck](unit-0-motion-test/flashcards.tsv) for the True/False conceptual questions and formulas.

This is also the intended teaching order: kinematics gives the vocabulary, projectile motion applies it in 2D, Newton's Laws explains *why* motion changes, and momentum/energy give two alternative (often faster) problem-solving lenses for the same physical situations.

## How to Use This Project

**Students:**
- Read the topic `README.md` first for the concept map and objectives.
- Fill out `cornell-notes.md` while reading/in class — don't copy definitions verbatim, paraphrase in the Notes column, then write cue questions and a summary after.
- Do the practice problems before taking the quiz.
- Take `quiz.html`; for anything missed, re-read the linked concept in the README, then retake.
- Log your quiz score and problem-set completion in the tracker below.

**Instructors:**
- Each topic README's "Assessment Criteria" section maps directly to a rubric you can use for quizzes/tests.
- The quizzes are built from the most commonly missed conceptual questions in each topic (see "Why this is tricky" notes in each quiz) — use them as a formative pre-test or exit ticket.
- Learning objectives are phrased as measurable "Students will be able to..." (SWBAT) statements suitable for a syllabus or IEP/504 documentation.

## Master Learning Objectives (Unit-Level)

By the end of this unit, students will be able to:

1. Distinguish scalar and vector quantities and apply that distinction to distance/displacement, speed/velocity.
2. Use the kinematic equations to solve for unknowns in 1D and 2D (projectile) motion, including choosing the correct equation from given/unknown variables.
3. Apply Newton's three laws to analyze forces on single objects and systems of connected objects (free-body diagrams).
4. Apply conservation of momentum to elastic and inelastic collision problems.
5. Apply the work-energy theorem and conservation of mechanical energy to solve problems that are difficult or inefficient to solve with kinematics/forces alone.
6. Select the most efficient problem-solving approach (kinematics vs. forces vs. momentum vs. energy) for a given scenario.
7. Communicate physics reasoning using correct units, significant figures, vector notation, and labeled diagrams.

## Cross-Cutting Assessment Rubric (applies to all 5 topics)

| Criterion | Weight | 4 — Exemplary | 3 — Proficient | 2 — Developing | 1 — Beginning |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Conceptual understanding | 30% | Explains *why*, not just *how*; connects to other topics | Correctly explains the concept | Partial/confused explanation | Cannot explain concept |
| Problem-solving process | 30% | Correct approach, clean work, checks reasonableness of answer | Correct approach, minor errors | Approach mostly right, several errors | Wrong approach or no clear process |
| Quantitative accuracy | 20% | Correct answer, correct units/sig figs | Correct answer, minor unit/sig-fig slip | Numeric error but sound method | Incorrect and unsound |
| Communication | 10% | Clear diagram, labeled vectors, organized work | Mostly clear | Hard to follow | Disorganized/illegible |
| Real-world connection | 10% | Insightful, accurate application | Accurate, relevant application | Superficial connection | Missing/inaccurate |

## Recommended Pacing (10th–11th grade, ~50 min/day)

| Topic | Days | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Kinematics | 4–5 | Include 1 day for graphing (position/velocity/acceleration vs. time) |
| Projectile Motion | 3–4 | Include 1 lab day (e.g., launcher or ramp-and-ball) |
| Newton's Laws | 5–6 | Free-body diagrams need heavy practice; include a friction sub-lesson |
| Momentum | 3–4 | Include 1 collision lab (carts, or video analysis) |
| Work & Energy | 4–5 | Include 1 energy-conservation lab (pendulum or roller coaster model) |
| **Total** | **~20–24 class days** | ~1 semester quarter at 5 classes/week |

## General Resources (span the whole unit)

- **Textbook-style reference:** OpenStax *Physics* (free, full-year textbook, algebra/trig-based) — https://openstax.org/details/books/physics
- **Video series:** Khan Academy Physics — https://www.khanacademy.org/science/physics ; The Organic Chemistry Tutor (YouTube) for worked problem walkthroughs
- **Simulations:** PhET Interactive Simulations (University of Colorado Boulder) — https://phet.colorado.edu/en/simulations/filter?subjects=physics — free, browser-based, no login
- **Problem sets:** The Physics Classroom — https://www.physicsclassroom.com/ (has topic-matched "Minds On Physics" and multiple-choice review sets)
- **Formula/reference sheet:** AP Physics 1 Equation Tables (College Board, public PDF) work well even for non-AP 11th grade courses as a one-page reference

## Notation & Conventions Used Throughout

- Vectors are **bold** or written with an arrow, e.g. **v**, $\vec{v}$; magnitudes are plain, e.g. $v$.
- Up/right = positive unless a problem states otherwise.
- $g = 9.8\ \text{m/s}^2$ (magnitude), directed downward.
- Significant figures follow the least-precise given value unless told otherwise.
