## Car & Ramp Lab

**Setup:** Popsicle-stick "wing" on the car, width = 1 cm. Gate A at position 20 cm, Gate B at position 60 cm.

| Measurement | Value |
|---|---|
| Time in Gate A | 0.0148 s |
| Time in Gate B | 0.0058 s |
| Time from A to B | 0.3496 s |

**Worked solutions:**

1. **Displacement A→B:** $60 - 20 = 40\ \text{cm}$
2. **Average velocity A→B:** $v_{AB} = \dfrac{d}{t} = \dfrac{40}{0.3496} \approx 114\ \text{cm/s}$
3. **Instantaneous velocity at A:** $v_A = \dfrac{1\ \text{cm}}{0.0148\ \text{s}} \approx 67.6\ \text{cm/s}$
4. **Instantaneous velocity at B:** $v_B = \dfrac{1\ \text{cm}}{0.0058\ \text{s}} \approx 172\ \text{cm/s}$
5. **Acceleration down the ramp:** $a = \dfrac{v_B - v_A}{t_{AB}} = \dfrac{172 - 67.6}{0.3496} \approx 299\ \text{cm/s}^2$

**Lab answer bank (units: cm, cm/s, cm/s²):** 40, 67.6, 114, 172, 299

## Mixed Problem Solving

*(List knowns/unknowns, show the equation, substitute, and solve.)*

**1.** A dancer moves from position 0 to position 8 m and then back to position 3 m. The entire motion takes 10 seconds.
   - a. Distance traveled: $8 + 5 = 13\ \text{m}$
   - b. Displacement: $3\ \text{m}$
   - c. Average velocity: $v = \dfrac{d}{t} = \dfrac{3}{10} = 0.3\ \text{m/s forward}$

**2.** The flight speed of a European swallow is 11 m/s. How long will it take to travel 1600 m?
   - $t = \dfrac{d}{v} = \dfrac{1600}{11} \approx 145\ \text{s}$

**3.** A car drives 500 m in 30 s, then 200 m in 60 s, then 100 m in 120 s. What is its average velocity?
   - $v = \dfrac{d_{total}}{t_{total}} = \dfrac{500+200+100}{30+60+120} = \dfrac{800}{210} \approx 3.81\ \text{m/s}$
   - *(This is the exact "don't just average the speeds" idea from the Speed-Distance-Time notes — total distance over total time.)*

**4.** A ball rolls down a hill at a rate of $2.0\ \text{m/s}^2$, starting from rest, for 4 seconds.
   - a. Distance traveled: $d = v_i t + \tfrac12 a t^2 = 0 + \tfrac12(2)(4)^2 = 16\ \text{m}$
   - b. Velocity at $t=4$ s: $v_f = v_i + at = 0 + (2)(4) = 8\ \text{m/s}$

**5.** A car initially moving at 25 m/s hits the brakes and slows at a rate of $3\ \text{m/s}^2$ (note: this is a **deceleration**, so $a = -3\ \text{m/s}^2$). If it brakes for 5 seconds, find the displacement.
   - $v_i = 25,\ a=-3,\ t=5$
   - $d = v_i t + \tfrac12 a t^2 = 25(5) + \tfrac12(-3)(5)^2 = 125 - 37.5 = 87.5\ \text{m}$

**6.** A rock climber's shoe loosens a rock; her buddy at the bottom notices the rock takes 3.2 s to fall.
   - $v_i = 0,\ a = 9.8\ \text{m/s}^2,\ t = 3.2\ \text{s}$
   - a. Height of the cliff: $d = v_i t + \tfrac12 a t^2 = 0 + \tfrac12(9.8)(3.2)^2 \approx 50.2\ \text{m}$
   - b. Speed just before impact: $v_f = v_i + at = 0 + (9.8)(3.2) \approx 31.4\ \text{m/s}$

**7.** Mike Trout hits a foul ball straight up at 30 m/s off the bat. Find the time the ball is in the air (assuming it comes back down to the same height it was hit from).
   - $v_i = 30,\ v_f = -30$ (returns at the same speed, opposite direction), $a = -9.8\ \text{m/s}^2$
   - $v_f = v_i + at \Rightarrow -30 = 30 + (-9.8)t \Rightarrow -60 = -9.8t \Rightarrow t \approx 6.1\ \text{s}$

**Mixed-problem answer bank:** 0.3, 1.2, 3, 3.81, 6.1, 8, 13, 16, 31.4, 35, 50.2, 87.5, 145
*(Note: "1.2" and "35" appear in the bank but aren't accounted for by problems 1–7 above as worked on the scan — likely distractors, or answers to sub-parts not fully captured in this transcription. Check the original scan if a homework problem needs one of these two.)*

## True/False: Distance and/or Displacement

*(⚠️ Marks for items 1 and 3 weren't fully clear on the scan — verify against the original before using as a final answer key.)*

1. **"A person makes a round-trip journey, finishing where she started. The displacement for the trip is 0 and the distance is some nonzero value."** — **True** (this is the core distance-vs-displacement definition).
2. **"The phrase '20 m, west' likely describes the displacement for a motion."** — **True** ✓ (marked on scan). It has both magnitude and direction, matching displacement (a vector).
3. **"The diagram depicts a person walking A→B→C→D. The distance for this motion is 100 yds."** — Worked calculation on the scan: $35 + 20 + 35 = 90\ \text{yds}$, not 100 → statement is **False** as stated (correct distance is 90 yds).
4. **"For the same diagram, the displacement is 50 yds."** — Calculation shown: $-50\ \text{yds}$ (with a note to include **direction**). The magnitude (50 yds) is correct, but as stated without a direction the claim is incomplete — displacement requires both magnitude *and* direction to be fully correct.

## True/False: Velocity, Speed & Acceleration

1. **"Velocity is a vector quantity and speed is a scalar quantity."** — **True**
2. **"Both speed and velocity refer to how fast an object is moving."** — **True**
3. **"Person X moves A→B in 5 s. Person Y moves the same A→B in 10 s. Person Y is moving with twice the speed of Person X."** — **False**. Person Y took *longer*, so Y is moving at **half** the speed of Person X, not twice.
4. **"The velocity of an object refers to the rate at which the object's position changes."** — **True**
5. **"The phrase '30 mi/hr, west' likely refers to a scalar quantity."** — **False**. It includes a direction, so it's a **vector** quantity (velocity), not a scalar.
6. **"An object which is slowing down has an acceleration."** — **True**. Slowing down is still a change in velocity, which is what acceleration measures.
7. **"An object which is moving at constant speed in a circle has an acceleration."** — **True**. Direction is constantly changing, so velocity is changing even though speed isn't — that's still acceleration (centripetal).
8. **"Acceleration is the rate at which the velocity changes."** — **True**
9. **"An object that is accelerating is moving fast."** — **False** (not necessarily). Acceleration only means velocity is *changing* — an object can be accelerating while moving slowly, momentarily at rest (e.g., top of a throw), or even while slowing down.
