# Topic 1: Kinematics

Kinematics is the description of motion — without asking what *causes* it. It's the vocabulary the rest of mechanics is built on.

## Key Concepts to Master

- **Scalars vs. vectors:** distance (scalar) vs. displacement (vector); speed (scalar) vs. velocity (vector)
- **Position, displacement, and the sign convention** ($\Delta x = x_f - x_i$)
- **Average vs. instantaneous velocity**
- **Acceleration** — rate of change of velocity; can be positive, negative, or zero even while speed is changing
- **Speeding up vs. slowing down:** determined by whether velocity and acceleration have the *same* sign (speeding up) or *opposite* signs (slowing down) — NOT by the sign of acceleration alone
- **The four kinematic equations (constant acceleration only):**
  1. $v_f = v_i + at$
  2. $\Delta x = v_i t + \tfrac12 a t^2$
  3. $v_f^2 = v_i^2 + 2a\Delta x$
  4. $\Delta x = \tfrac12(v_i + v_f)t$
- **Free fall** as a special case ($a = -g$, ignoring air resistance)
- **Graphical analysis:** position-time graphs (slope = velocity), velocity-time graphs (slope = acceleration, area = displacement)

## Learning Objectives (SWBAT)

1. Correctly distinguish distance/displacement and speed/velocity in a word problem.
2. Select the correct kinematic equation given three known variables and one unknown.
3. Interpret and sketch position-time and velocity-time graphs, including identifying when an object is speeding up, slowing down, at rest, or reversing direction.
4. Solve free-fall problems including "thrown upward" scenarios (object decelerates, stops, then accelerates back down).
5. Correctly apply and carry units and signs through a multi-step kinematics problem.

## Common Pitfalls (why this topic gets missed)

- Confusing negative acceleration with "slowing down" — a car speeding up in the negative direction also has negative acceleration.
- Forgetting that at the top of a vertical throw, velocity = 0 but acceleration is still $-g$ (not zero).
- Mixing up which kinematic equation to use, especially forgetting equation 3 doesn't involve time.
- Reading position-time graph slope as if it were a velocity-time graph (or vice versa).

## Practice Problems

1. A car accelerates from rest at $2.5\ \text{m/s}^2$ for 6 s. Find its final velocity and the distance traveled.
2. A ball is thrown straight up at $18\ \text{m/s}$. Find the time to reach maximum height, the maximum height, and the total time until it returns to the throwing point.
3. A train decelerates from $30\ \text{m/s}$ to rest over $500\ \text{m}$. Find its acceleration.
4. Sketch a velocity-time graph for a car that speeds up from rest to $20\ \text{m/s}$ in 4 s, cruises for 3 s, then brakes to a stop in 2 s. From the graph, find total distance traveled.
5. Two runners start 100 m apart moving toward each other, one at constant 4 m/s and one at constant 6 m/s. When and where do they meet?

## Real-World Applications

- **Reaction-time stopping distance:** why the "3-second rule" for following distance accounts for both reaction time (constant velocity) and braking (deceleration).
- **Elevator design:** acceleration limits for passenger comfort dictate elevator velocity profiles.
- **Sports:** sprinters' velocity-time graphs (acceleration phase vs. top-speed phase) — compare Usain Bolt's 100 m splits.
- **Vertical leap analysis** in basketball/volleyball using free-fall equations run in reverse from hang time.

## Assessment Criteria

Student work is assessed on:
- Correct identification of knowns/unknowns before selecting an equation
- Correct choice and algebraic use of the kinematic equation(s)
- Correct sign convention applied consistently through the problem
- Correct units and reasonable significant figures in the final answer
- For graph questions: correct reading of slope/area and correct qualitative description of motion
