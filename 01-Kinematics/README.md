# Unit 1: Kinematics

**NGSS alignment:** HS-PS2-1 (forces and motion, quantitative), MS-PS2-2 foundation, and the mathematical-modeling practices (SEP-5) embedded throughout the CA Physics & Earth Science course model.

**Suggested pacing:** 2 weeks (10 class periods), including 1 lab day and 1 review/assessment day.

## 1. Key Concepts Students Must Master

| # | Concept | Why it matters |
|---|---------|-----------------|
| 1 | Position, distance, and displacement | Foundation for all motion description; distance is scalar, displacement is a vector. |
| 2 | Speed vs. velocity | Speed is scalar (magnitude only); velocity is a vector (magnitude + direction). |
| 3 | Average vs. instantaneous velocity | Average velocity = Δx/Δt over an interval; instantaneous velocity is the slope of the tangent on a position-time graph at one instant. |
| 4 | Acceleration | Rate of change of velocity; can speed an object up, slow it down, or change its direction. |
| 5 | Sign conventions | A negative velocity/acceleration means direction, not necessarily "slowing down." |
| 6 | Reading motion graphs | Slope of x-t graph = velocity; slope of v-t graph = acceleration; area under v-t graph = displacement. |
| 7 | The four kinematic equations | Used for constant (uniform) acceleration only. |
| 8 | Free fall | Special case of constant acceleration where a = -9.8 m/s² (on Earth, ignoring air resistance). |

### The Four Kinematic Equations (constant acceleration only)

1. `v = v₀ + at`
2. `Δx = v₀t + ½at²`
3. `v² = v₀² + 2aΔx`
4. `Δx = ½(v₀ + v)t`

**Selection rule of thumb:** identify which variable is missing from the problem (of v₀, v, a, Δx, t), then pick the equation that doesn't contain it.

## 5. Practice Problems (progression: easy → hard)

1. A student walks 4 m east, then 6 m west. Find the total distance traveled and the displacement.
2. A car accelerates from rest at 3 m/s² for 5 seconds. Find its final velocity and the distance traveled.
3. A ball is dropped from a 45 m tall building. How long does it take to hit the ground, and what is its velocity on impact? (Ignore air resistance.)
4. Given a v-t graph with two line segments, calculate total displacement using the "area under the curve" method and describe the motion in words for each segment.
5. **Multi-step:** A train traveling at 20 m/s brakes uniformly and stops in 8 seconds. How far does it travel while braking? What was its acceleration?

## 6. Real-World Applications

- **Traffic safety / stopping distance:** reaction time (constant velocity phase) + braking distance (constant deceleration phase) — used in driver's ed and highway engineering.
- **Sports analytics:** sprinters' velocity-time graphs used by coaches to identify acceleration phase vs. top-speed phase.
- **Elevators & roller coasters:** engineers use kinematic equations to keep acceleration within comfortable/safe limits for riders.
- **Spacecraft landings:** mission planners use free-fall and constant-acceleration equations to time retro-burns (e.g., Mars landers).

## 7. Learning Objectives

By the end of this unit, students will be able to:

- [ ] Distinguish distance from displacement and speed from velocity in both words and calculations.
- [ ] Read and interpret position-time, velocity-time, and acceleration-time graphs, including slope and area.
- [ ] Select and apply the correct kinematic equation for a given set of known/unknown variables.
- [ ] Solve free-fall problems using g = 9.8 m/s², including correct sign conventions.
- [ ] Construct a motion graph from a written scenario and vice versa.

## 8. Assessment Criteria

| Level | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| **Exceeds (4)** | Solves multi-step kinematics problems involving graph interpretation *and* equation application; explains reasoning with correct units and sign conventions throughout. |
| **Meets (3)** | Correctly applies the appropriate kinematic equation to single-scenario problems; reads basic motion graphs accurately. |
| **Approaching (2)** | Sets up problems correctly but makes sign or unit errors; confuses distance/displacement or speed/velocity at times. |
| **Beginning (1)** | Cannot identify which variables are given/unknown; unable to select an appropriate equation without significant support. |

**Suggested evidence:** unit test (mix of graph-reading + calculation), lab report (e.g., "Buggy Lab" or Moving Man simulation activity), quiz retake option after reviewing missed-concept report from `quiz/index.html`.
