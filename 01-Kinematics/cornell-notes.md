## Topic: Describing Motion in One Dimension

| Cues / Questions | Notes |
|---|---|
| What's the difference between distance and displacement? | Distance = total path length traveled (scalar, always positive). Displacement = straight-line change in position, `Δx = x_f − x_i` (vector, can be negative). |
| What's the difference between speed and velocity? | Speed = distance/time (scalar). Velocity = displacement/time (vector — has direction). |
| How do I find average velocity? | `v_avg = Δx / Δt`. Uses total displacement over total time, NOT the average of two speeds unless time intervals are equal. |
| What is instantaneous velocity? | Velocity at one specific moment — the **slope of the tangent line** on a position-time (x-t) graph at that point. |
| What is acceleration? | Rate of change of velocity: `a = Δv / Δt`. Units: m/s². Can be positive, negative, or zero. |
| Does negative acceleration always mean slowing down? | **No.** Negative acceleration means acceleration points in the negative direction. An object speeding up in the negative direction has negative velocity AND negative acceleration. |
| How do I read a position-time graph? | Slope = velocity. Steeper slope = faster. Flat line = at rest. Curved line = accelerating. |
| How do I read a velocity-time graph? | Slope = acceleration. **Area under the curve = displacement.** |
| What are the 4 kinematic equations? | 1) `v = v₀ + at`  2) `Δx = v₀t + ½at²`  3) `v² = v₀² + 2aΔx`  4) `Δx = ½(v₀+v)t` |
| When can I use the kinematic equations? | Only when acceleration is **constant** over the interval. |
| How do I pick the right equation? | List known/unknown variables (v₀, v, a, Δx, t). Choose the equation missing the variable you don't have and don't need. |
| What is free fall? | Motion under gravity alone; `a = −9.8 m/s²` (down is negative by convention) if air resistance is ignored. |
| Common sign mistake in free fall? | Forgetting that an object thrown *upward* still has `a = −9.8 m/s²` the entire time — even while going up. |
