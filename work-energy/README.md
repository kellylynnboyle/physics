# Unit 4: Work & Energy

The study of how forces transfer and transform energy — a powerful
shortcut for solving motion problems (especially messy ones) without
tracking forces and accelerations moment by moment.

## Key concepts students need to master

- **Work** — W = Fd·cosθ, where θ is the angle between the force and the
  displacement. Only the component of force *parallel* to displacement
  does work. Work is zero if force is perpendicular to displacement (e.g.,
  the normal force on a horizontally sliding object, or the centripetal
  force in uniform circular motion — both are always ⊥ to the motion).
- **The work-energy theorem** — the *net* work done on an object equals
  its change in kinetic energy: W_net = ΔKE. This uses the total (net)
  work from all forces combined, not the work from any single force.
- **Kinetic energy** — KE = ½mv², the energy of motion. Depends on v²,
  not v — doubling speed quadruples KE.
- **Gravitational potential energy** — PE = mgh, measured relative to a
  chosen reference height (h = 0). The PE *value* depends on that choice;
  the *change* in PE does not.
- **Elastic (spring) potential energy** — PE_spring = ½kx², from Hooke's
  Law (F = −kx), where x is the displacement from the spring's natural
  length and k is the spring constant.
- **Conservation of mechanical energy** — when only conservative forces
  (gravity, springs) act, KE + PE = constant. Energy converts between
  forms but the total stays fixed.
- **Non-conservative forces** — friction and air resistance remove energy
  from the mechanical system (KE + PE), converting it to heat. Mechanical
  energy is *not* conserved when these act, but total energy (mechanical
  + heat) always is.
- **Power** — P = W/t = Fv, the *rate* of doing work or transferring
  energy. Measured in watts (W = J/s), distinct from work (J) and force
  (N).
- **Efficiency** — the ratio of useful energy output to total energy
  input, often expressed as a percentage; real machines are always less
  than 100% efficient because some energy is lost (usually to heat).

## Learning objectives

By the end of this unit, students will be able to:

1. Calculate the work done by a force, correctly accounting for the angle
   between force and displacement, including recognizing when work is
   zero.
2. Apply the work-energy theorem to relate *net* work to the change in
   kinetic energy of an object.
3. Calculate gravitational potential energy relative to a clearly stated
   reference point, and compute changes in PE independent of that choice.
4. Apply conservation of mechanical energy to solve problems (roller
   coasters, pendulums, falling or sliding objects) when friction is
   negligible.
5. Correctly account for energy removed by friction/non-conservative
   forces in energy conservation problems (KE_i + PE_i = KE_f + PE_f +
   energy lost).
6. Calculate power and relate it to work and time, or to force and
   velocity.
7. Distinguish work, energy, and power as related but distinct
   quantities with distinct units (joules vs. watts vs. newtons).

## Assessment criteria

- **Quiz target:** ≥ 80% on `quiz.html`, with correct reasoning on the
  perpendicular-force and mechanical-energy-with-friction questions
  specifically (these are the most commonly missed).
- **Problem sets:** Full given/find/equation/solve/units setup shown, not
  just a final number, including an explicit statement of the chosen
  reference height for any PE calculation.
- **Mastery check:** Given a word problem describing a system with
  friction, the student writes the correct energy-conservation equation
  *including* the loss term, without prompting, and explains in words
  where the "missing" energy went.
