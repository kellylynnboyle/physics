Waves is the mathematical and conceptual foundation for both Light (Unit
9) and Earthquakes (Unit 11) — the same `v = fλ` relationship describes
sound, seismic waves, and light, even though the physical media are
completely different.

## Key concepts to master

- **Wave properties** — amplitude, wavelength (λ), frequency (f), period
  (T = 1/f), and speed, related by `v = fλ`.
- **Transverse vs. longitudinal waves** — transverse: oscillation
  perpendicular to travel direction (light, S-waves); longitudinal:
  oscillation parallel to travel direction (sound, P-waves).
- **Mechanical vs. electromagnetic waves** — mechanical waves need a
  medium (sound, seismic, water waves); EM waves (light) do not.
- **Wave behavior** — reflection, refraction, diffraction, and
  interference (constructive/destructive), and how each depends on
  wavelength and the medium/boundary encountered.
- **Wave model vs. particle model** — evidence for each (e.g.,
  interference/diffraction supports the wave model; the photoelectric
  effect supports the particle model) and why physicists use whichever
  model fits the situation better rather than treating them as
  contradictory.

## Vocabulary checklist

amplitude · wavelength · frequency · period · transverse wave ·
longitudinal wave · mechanical wave · electromagnetic wave · reflection ·
refraction · diffraction · interference · wave-particle duality

## Learning objectives

By the end of this unit, students will be able to:

1. Calculate any one of speed, frequency, or wavelength given the other two, using `v = fλ`, for waves in different media (including seismic waves in Earth).
2. Distinguish transverse from longitudinal waves and mechanical from electromagnetic waves with correct examples of each.
3. Predict and explain wave behavior (reflection, refraction, diffraction, interference) at a boundary or obstacle.
4. Evaluate evidence for the wave model and the particle model of electromagnetic radiation, and identify situations where each model is more useful.

## NGSS alignment

**HS-PS4-1.** Use mathematical representations to support a claim
regarding relationships among the frequency, wavelength, and speed of
waves traveling in various media. *(Explicitly includes seismic waves
traveling through Earth — the direct link to Unit 11: Earthquakes.)*

**HS-PS4-3.** Evaluate the claims, evidence, and reasoning behind the idea
that electromagnetic radiation can be described either by a wave model or
a particle model, and that for some situations one model is more useful
than the other. Full text: [`../NGSS-ALIGNMENT.md`](../NGSS-ALIGNMENT.md).

**Suggested lab:** a ripple tank or slinky-wave lab measuring wavelength
and frequency to verify `v = fλ`, then comparing to published seismic
P-wave/S-wave speeds as a bridge into Unit 11.
