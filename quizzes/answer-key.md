## Kinematics

**1. A ball is thrown straight up. At the very top of its path, what is its acceleration?**

- A. Zero
- B. About 10 m/s² downward ✅
- C. About 10 m/s² upward
- D. It depends on how hard it was thrown

*Why:* Gravity keeps acting at the top. The velocity is zero for an instant, but it is still changing at about 10 m/s every second, so the acceleration is still g downward.

*Common mistake:* Thinking zero velocity means zero acceleration.

**2. A car is moving in the negative direction and its acceleration is also negative. What is the car doing?**

- A. Slowing down
- B. Speeding up ✅
- C. Moving at constant speed
- D. Turning around

*Why:* When velocity and acceleration have the same sign, they point the same way and the object speeds up. The sign of acceleration gives its direction only.

*Common mistake:* Thinking negative acceleration always means slowing down.

**3. A position-time graph is a straight line sloping steeply upward. What does it show?**

- A. The object is speeding up
- B. The object is moving at constant velocity ✅
- C. The object is going uphill
- D. The object is at rest

*Why:* The slope of a position-time graph is the velocity. A straight line has one constant slope, so the velocity is constant. Speeding up would make the line curve upward.

*Common mistake:* Reading a rising line as rising speed, or reading the graph as a picture of the path.

**4. A runner completes one full lap of a 400 m track in 80 s, finishing where she started. What is her average velocity?**

- A. 5 m/s
- B. 0 m/s ✅
- C. 10 m/s
- D. 0.2 m/s

*Why:* Average velocity is displacement ÷ time. She ends where she began, so her displacement is zero. Her average speed is 400 m ÷ 80 s = 5 m/s.

*Common mistake:* Using distance when the question asks for velocity.

**5. On a velocity-time graph, velocity rises in a straight line from 0 to 12 m/s in 4 s. How far does the object travel?**

- A. 48 m
- B. 24 m ✅
- C. 12 m
- D. 3 m

*Why:* Displacement is the area under the velocity-time graph. The area is a triangle: ½ × 4 s × 12 m/s = 24 m.

*Common mistake:* Multiplying final velocity by time (48 m), which ignores that the object was slower earlier.

**6. A bowling ball and a golf ball are dropped from the same height at the same time. Air resistance is negligible. Which lands first?**

- A. The bowling ball
- B. The golf ball
- C. They land at the same time ✅
- D. It depends on the height

*Why:* All objects in free fall have the same acceleration, g. Gravity pulls harder on the bowling ball, but it also has more mass to accelerate, and the two effects cancel exactly.

*Common mistake:* Thinking heavier objects fall faster.

**7. A car starts from rest and accelerates at 3 m/s² for 4 s. How far does it travel?**

- A. 12 m
- B. 24 m ✅
- C. 48 m
- D. 6 m

*Why:* Δx = v₀t + ½at² = 0 + ½(3)(4)² = 24 m. Check: the final speed is 12 m/s, the average speed is 6 m/s, and 6 m/s × 4 s = 24 m.

*Common mistake:* Forgetting the ½ (48 m) or forgetting to square the time (6 m), or finding the final speed (12) instead of the distance.

**8. On a position-time graph, the lines for two cars cross. What is true at that moment?**

- A. They have the same speed
- B. They are at the same position ✅
- C. They have the same acceleration
- D. They have both stopped

*Why:* The vertical axis is position, so crossing lines mean equal positions: one car is passing the other. They have the same velocity only where the slopes are equal.

*Common mistake:* Reading a position-time graph as if it were a velocity-time graph.

## Projectile Motion

**1. One ball is dropped from a table edge. At the same instant, a second ball is launched horizontally from the same height. Ignoring air resistance, which hits the floor first?**

- A. The dropped ball
- B. The launched ball
- C. They hit at the same time ✅
- D. It depends on the launch speed

*Why:* Both start with zero vertical velocity and fall the same height with the same acceleration. Horizontal motion has no effect on the time to fall.

*Common mistake:* Thinking the launched ball stays up longer because it travels farther.

**2. A soccer ball is kicked at an angle. At the highest point of its path, which statement is correct?**

- A. Its velocity and acceleration are both zero
- B. Its velocity is zero and its acceleration is g downward
- C. Its velocity is horizontal and its acceleration is g downward ✅
- D. Its velocity is horizontal and its acceleration is zero

*Why:* Only the vertical part of the velocity is zero at the top. The ball still moves horizontally, and gravity still gives it an acceleration of g downward.

*Common mistake:* Carrying over 'velocity is zero at the top' from a straight-up throw.

**3. Ignoring air resistance, what happens to the horizontal velocity of a projectile during its flight?**

- A. It decreases steadily
- B. It stays constant ✅
- C. It increases on the way down
- D. It is zero at the top

*Why:* There is no horizontal force on a projectile, so there is no horizontal acceleration. Only the vertical velocity changes.

*Common mistake:* Thinking the projectile 'runs out' of forward motion.

**4. A ball rolls off a 1.25 m high table at 4 m/s. Using g = 10 m/s², how far from the base of the table does it land?**

- A. 0.5 m
- B. 2.0 m ✅
- C. 4.0 m
- D. 5.0 m

*Why:* Vertical first: 1.25 = ½(10)t² gives t = 0.5 s. Then horizontal: Δx = 4 m/s × 0.5 s = 2.0 m.

*Common mistake:* Stopping at the time (0.5), or mixing horizontal and vertical values in one equation.

**5. A ball is launched at the same speed at different angles over level ground. Which two angles give the same range?**

- A. 30° and 45°
- B. 30° and 60° ✅
- C. 45° and 60°
- D. 45° and 90°

*Why:* Angles that add to 90° give equal ranges. The 60° launch goes higher and stays up longer but moves forward more slowly. 45° gives the maximum range.

*Common mistake:* Thinking a steeper angle always means a longer or always a shorter range.

**6. A ball is thrown at 20 m/s at 30° above the horizontal. What is the vertical component of its initial velocity? (sin 30° = 0.5, cos 30° = 0.87)**

- A. 10 m/s ✅
- B. 17 m/s
- C. 20 m/s
- D. 0 m/s

*Why:* The vertical component is v sin θ = 20 × 0.5 = 10 m/s. The horizontal component is v cos θ = 17 m/s.

*Common mistake:* Swapping sine and cosine.

**7. A plane flying level at constant velocity releases a package. Ignoring air resistance, where is the plane when the package hits the ground?**

- A. Directly above the package ✅
- B. Well ahead of the package
- C. Behind the package
- D. It depends on the mass of the package

*Why:* The package keeps the plane's horizontal velocity after release, so it stays directly under the plane while it falls.

*Common mistake:* Thinking the package drops straight down or loses its forward motion when released.

**8. A ball is launched from level ground at 15 m/s. Ignoring air resistance, how fast is it moving just before it lands?**

- A. Less than 15 m/s
- B. 15 m/s ✅
- C. More than 15 m/s
- D. 0 m/s

*Why:* The horizontal velocity never changes, and the vertical velocity returns with the same size in the opposite direction. The landing speed equals the launch speed.

*Common mistake:* Thinking the ball slows down over its flight, or speeds up because it is falling.

## Newton's Laws

**1. A large truck collides head-on with a small car. During the collision, how do the forces compare?**

- A. The truck exerts a larger force on the car
- B. The car exerts a larger force on the truck
- C. The forces are equal in size ✅
- D. Only the truck exerts a force

*Why:* Newton's third law: the two forces are always equal in size and opposite in direction. The car is damaged more because the same force gives its smaller mass a larger acceleration.

*Common mistake:* Thinking the bigger or faster object pushes harder.

**2. You push a crate across the floor at constant velocity with a horizontal force of 50 N. What is the friction force on the crate?**

- A. Less than 50 N
- B. Exactly 50 N ✅
- C. More than 50 N
- D. Zero

*Why:* Constant velocity means zero acceleration, so the net force is zero. Friction must exactly balance the 50 N push.

*Common mistake:* Thinking the push must be larger than friction for the crate to keep moving.

**3. An elevator moves upward at constant speed. How does the tension in its cable compare with its weight?**

- A. Tension is greater than the weight
- B. Tension equals the weight ✅
- C. Tension is less than the weight
- D. Tension is zero

*Why:* Constant speed in a straight line means the net force is zero, so the upward tension equals the downward weight. Tension is greater only while the elevator accelerates upward.

*Common mistake:* Thinking motion in a direction requires a net force in that direction.

**4. A book rests on a table. Earth pulls down on the book. What is the third-law partner of that force?**

- A. The table pushing up on the book
- B. The book pulling up on Earth ✅
- C. The book pushing down on the table
- D. The table pushing down on the floor

*Why:* Third-law pairs swap the two objects: Earth pulls on book, book pulls on Earth. The normal force from the table balances the weight here, but it is a different kind of force and acts on the same object.

*Common mistake:* Confusing balanced forces on one object with a third-law pair.

**5. An astronaut travels from Earth to the Moon. What happens to her mass and weight?**

- A. Both decrease
- B. Mass stays the same and weight decreases ✅
- C. Mass decreases and weight stays the same
- D. Both stay the same

*Why:* Mass is the amount of matter and does not depend on location. Weight is the force of gravity, mg, and the Moon's g is about one sixth of Earth's.

*Common mistake:* Treating mass and weight as the same thing.

**6. A 4 kg box is pushed with 20 N while friction pushes back with 8 N. What is its acceleration?**

- A. 5 m/s²
- B. 3 m/s² ✅
- C. 2 m/s²
- D. 7 m/s²

*Why:* Use the net force: 20 N − 8 N = 12 N. Then a = 12 N ÷ 4 kg = 3 m/s².

*Common mistake:* Using the applied force instead of the net force (5 m/s²).

**7. A ball is thrown straight up. After it leaves the hand and while it is still rising, which forces act on it? Ignore air resistance.**

- A. Gravity and the upward force of the throw
- B. Gravity only ✅
- C. The force of the throw only
- D. No forces until it starts to fall

*Why:* A force needs contact or a field. Once the ball leaves the hand, the hand no longer pushes on it. The ball keeps rising because of inertia while gravity slows it.

*Common mistake:* Believing a moving object carries a force in its direction of motion.

**8. A horse pulls on a cart, and the cart pulls back on the horse with an equal force. Why can the cart still accelerate?**

- A. The horse's pull is slightly larger
- B. The two forces act on different objects, so they do not cancel ✅
- C. The cart's pull happens a moment later
- D. The third law does not apply to moving objects

*Why:* Only forces on the same object can cancel. The cart accelerates when the horse's pull on the cart is greater than the friction on the cart.

*Common mistake:* Adding a third-law pair together as if both forces acted on one object.

## Momentum

**1. A rubber ball and a lump of clay with the same mass hit a wall at the same speed. The ball bounces back and the clay sticks. Which receives the larger impulse from the wall?**

- A. The clay
- B. The rubber ball ✅
- C. They receive the same impulse
- D. Neither receives an impulse

*Why:* The clay's momentum changes from mv to zero. The ball's momentum changes from mv to nearly −mv, almost twice as much, because its direction reverses.

*Common mistake:* Ignoring that momentum is a vector, so reversing direction is a large change.

**2. In a crash, how does an airbag reduce injury?**

- A. It reduces the driver's change in momentum
- B. It increases the stopping time, which reduces the force ✅
- C. It reduces the stopping time, which reduces the force
- D. It reduces the driver's mass

*Why:* The driver's change in momentum is the same either way. Since FΔt = Δp, spreading the stop over a longer time makes the force smaller.

*Common mistake:* Thinking the airbag changes how much momentum must be removed.

**3. A 2 kg cart moving at 3 m/s hits and sticks to a 1 kg cart at rest. What is their velocity afterward?**

- A. 3 m/s
- B. 2 m/s ✅
- C. 1.5 m/s
- D. 1 m/s

*Why:* Momentum before: (2)(3) + 0 = 6 kg·m/s. After: (2 + 1)v = 6, so v = 2 m/s.

*Common mistake:* Averaging the two starting velocities (1.5 m/s) instead of conserving momentum.

**4. Two cars collide and lock together. Friction with the road is negligible during the collision. What is conserved?**

- A. Both momentum and kinetic energy
- B. Momentum but not kinetic energy ✅
- C. Kinetic energy but not momentum
- D. Neither

*Why:* Momentum is conserved in every collision with no net outside force. In a sticking collision, some kinetic energy becomes thermal energy, sound, and deformation.

*Common mistake:* Assuming that if kinetic energy is not conserved, momentum is not either.

**5. An 80 kg astronaut at rest in space throws a 2 kg wrench at 8 m/s. What happens to the astronaut?**

- A. Stays at rest
- B. Moves at 0.2 m/s in the opposite direction ✅
- C. Moves at 8 m/s in the opposite direction
- D. Moves at 0.2 m/s in the same direction as the wrench

*Why:* Total momentum starts at zero and stays zero: 0 = (2)(8) + (80)v, so v = −0.2 m/s. The negative sign means opposite to the wrench.

*Common mistake:* Thinking both objects must end with the same speed.

**6. A 0.5 kg ball hits a wall at 10 m/s and bounces straight back at 10 m/s. What is the size of its change in momentum?**

- A. 0 kg·m/s
- B. 5 kg·m/s
- C. 10 kg·m/s ✅
- D. 20 kg·m/s

*Why:* Take toward the wall as positive: Δp = m(v_final − v_initial) = 0.5(−10 − 10) = −10 kg·m/s. The size is 10 kg·m/s.

*Common mistake:* Saying zero because the speed did not change.

**7. A 0.5 kg ball at rest is hit with an average force of 50 N for 0.2 s. How fast does it leave?**

- A. 5 m/s
- B. 10 m/s
- C. 20 m/s ✅
- D. 100 m/s

*Why:* Impulse = FΔt = 50 × 0.2 = 10 N·s. That equals the change in momentum, so Δv = 10 ÷ 0.5 = 20 m/s.

*Common mistake:* Stopping at the impulse (10) and calling it the speed.

**8. A large truck and a small car collide. How do the sizes of their momentum changes compare?**

- A. The truck's change is larger
- B. The car's change is larger
- C. They are equal ✅
- D. It depends on which was moving faster

*Why:* The forces on each are equal and opposite and last the same time, so the impulses are equal and opposite. The car's velocity changes more because its mass is smaller.

*Common mistake:* Confusing change in momentum with change in velocity.

## Work and Energy

**1. You carry a heavy box across a level floor at constant velocity. How much work does your upward supporting force do on the box?**

- A. A large positive amount
- B. Zero ✅
- C. A negative amount
- D. It depends on the distance walked

*Why:* Work needs a component of force along the displacement. The supporting force is vertical and the displacement is horizontal, so cos 90° = 0 and the work is zero.

*Common mistake:* Equating feeling tired with doing work in the physics sense.

**2. A car doubles its speed. What happens to its kinetic energy?**

- A. It doubles
- B. It triples
- C. It becomes four times as large ✅
- D. It stays the same

*Why:* KE = ½mv². Speed is squared, so doubling the speed multiplies the kinetic energy by 2² = 4.

*Common mistake:* Treating kinetic energy as proportional to speed.

**3. A roller coaster car starts from rest at the top of a 20 m frictionless hill. Using g = 10 m/s², what is its speed at the bottom?**

- A. 10 m/s
- B. 20 m/s ✅
- C. 200 m/s
- D. It depends on the mass of the car

*Why:* mgh = ½mv², so v = √(2gh) = √(2 × 10 × 20) = √400 = 20 m/s. The mass cancels.

*Common mistake:* Thinking a heavier car arrives faster, or forgetting the square root.

**4. Two frictionless ramps have the same height. One is steep and one is gentle. A block slides down each from rest. How do the speeds at the bottom compare?**

- A. Faster on the steep ramp
- B. Faster on the gentle ramp
- C. The same on both ✅
- D. It depends on the mass of the block

*Why:* Both blocks lose the same potential energy, mgh, so both gain the same kinetic energy. The steep ramp takes less time, but the final speed is the same.

*Common mistake:* Confusing a larger acceleration with a larger final speed.

**5. Two students with the same mass climb the same staircase. Maya takes 5 s and Jordan takes 10 s. Which statement is correct?**

- A. Maya does more work
- B. They do the same work, and Maya has twice the power ✅
- C. They do the same work and have the same power
- D. Jordan does more work because he takes longer

*Why:* Work against gravity is mgh, which is the same for both. Power is work ÷ time, so half the time means twice the power.

*Common mistake:* Mixing up work (how much energy) with power (how fast).

**6. A box slides across a rough floor and comes to rest. What happened to its kinetic energy?**

- A. It was destroyed
- B. It became thermal energy in the box and floor ✅
- C. It became gravitational potential energy
- D. It is stored in the box as kinetic energy

*Why:* Friction does negative work on the box and transforms its kinetic energy into thermal energy. Total energy is conserved.

*Common mistake:* Saying energy is used up or lost.

**7. A car's speed doubles from 30 mph to 60 mph. With the same braking force, how does its braking distance change?**

- A. It stays the same
- B. It doubles
- C. It becomes four times as long ✅
- D. It becomes eight times as long

*Why:* The brakes must remove all the kinetic energy: Fd = ½mv². Four times the kinetic energy needs four times the distance at the same force.

*Common mistake:* Assuming stopping distance is proportional to speed.

**8. Three identical balls are thrown from the top of a cliff at the same speed: one straight up, one straight down, and one horizontally. Ignoring air resistance, which hits the ground with the greatest speed?**

- A. The one thrown down
- B. The one thrown up
- C. The one thrown horizontally
- D. All three have the same speed ✅

*Why:* All three start with the same kinetic energy and lose the same potential energy, so they end with the same kinetic energy and the same speed. They land at different times and in different directions.

*Common mistake:* Reasoning about direction or time in the air instead of energy.
