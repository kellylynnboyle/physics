# Quiz answer key (instructors)

Each question targets one common misconception. Questions were written for this project from misconceptions that are well documented in physics teaching; they are not drawn from this class's own test data. Swap in questions your students actually miss as you collect results.

## Unit 1: Kinematics

| # | Question | Answer | Misconception it targets |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A ball is tossed straight up. At the very top of its path, what is its acceleration? | C. 9.8 m/s² downward | Zero velocity means zero acceleration. |
| 2 | A car goes around a curve with its speedometer steady at 20 m/s. Is the car accelerating? | B. Yes, because its direction is changing | Acceleration only means a change in speed. |
| 3 | On a position-time graph, a straight line slopes downward to the right. What is the object doing? | B. Moving at constant velocity in the negative direction | Reading a graph as a picture of the path, or reading a downward slope as slowing down. |
| 4 | An object has a negative acceleration. Which statement must be true? | C. Its velocity is becoming more negative (or less positive) | Negative acceleration always means slowing down. |
| 5 | A runner completes exactly one lap of a 400 m track in 80 s. What is the runner's average velocity? | B. 0 m/s | Average velocity is the same as average speed. |
| 6 | A bowling ball and a tennis ball are dropped from the same height at the same time. Ignoring air resistance, which lands first? | C. They land at the same time | Heavier objects fall faster. |
| 7 | A cart starts from rest with constant acceleration and travels 4 m in the first 2 s. How far has it traveled after 4 s? | C. 16 m | Distance doubles when time doubles. |
| 8 | A velocity-time graph shows a flat horizontal line at +5 m/s. What is the object doing? | B. It is moving at a constant 5 m/s | A flat line always means at rest. |

## Unit 2: Projectile Motion

| # | Question | Answer | Misconception it targets |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A ball is dropped from a table at the same instant another ball is launched horizontally from the same height. Ignoring air resistance, which hits the floor first? | C. They hit at the same time | Moving forward makes an object fall more slowly. |
| 2 | A ball is thrown at an angle. At the highest point of its path, what is its velocity? | B. Equal to its horizontal velocity, pointing horizontally | The projectile stops at the top. |
| 3 | For the same thrown ball, what is its acceleration at the highest point? | B. 9.8 m/s² downward | Acceleration is zero where vertical velocity is zero. |
| 4 | Ignoring air resistance, what happens to a projectile's horizontal velocity during its flight? | C. It stays constant | A forward force runs out, so the object slows down horizontally. |
| 5 | A ball leaves a 4.9 m high ledge moving horizontally at 3 m/s. How far from the base of the ledge does it land? | B. 3.0 m | Using the height as a horizontal distance, or mixing x and y values. |
| 6 | A ball rolls off a cliff. If it is launched twice as fast horizontally, what changes? | B. It lands twice as far away, in the same time | A faster launch keeps the object in the air longer. |
| 7 | On level ground with the same launch speed, which pair of angles gives the same range? | B. 30° and 60° | A steeper angle always sends the object farther (or always shorter). |
| 8 | A plane flying level at constant velocity drops a package. Ignoring air resistance, where is the plane when the package lands? | B. Directly above the package | A dropped object falls straight down and is left behind. |
| 9 | A ball is kicked at 20 m/s at 30° above the ground. What is its initial vertical velocity? | C. 10 m/s | Swapping sine and cosine, or using the full speed for the vertical motion. |

## Unit 3: Newton's Laws

| # | Question | Answer | Misconception it targets |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A hockey puck slides across frictionless ice at constant velocity. What is the net force on the puck? | B. Zero | Motion requires a force. |
| 2 | A heavy truck collides head-on with a small car. Compared with the force the truck exerts on the car, the force the car exerts on the truck is: | C. The same size | The bigger or faster object exerts the bigger force. |
| 3 | A book rests on a table. Earth pulls down on the book. What is the third-law reaction force? | C. The book pulling up on Earth | The normal force is the reaction to weight. |
| 4 | An elevator moves upward at constant speed. Compared with the weight of the elevator, the tension in the cable is: | B. Equal | The force in the direction of motion must be larger. |
| 5 | An astronaut travels from Earth to the Moon. What happens to the astronaut's mass and weight? | C. Mass stays the same, weight decreases | Mass and weight are the same thing. |
| 6 | A net force of 10 N gives a cart an acceleration of 5 m/s². If the cart's mass is doubled and the net force stays 10 N, what is the acceleration? | C. 2.5 m/s² | Acceleration depends only on the force. |
| 7 | A ball is thrown straight up. After it leaves the hand and while it is still rising, what forces act on it? Ignore air resistance. | C. Only gravity, downward | The force of a throw stays with the object. |
| 8 | You push horizontally on a heavy crate with 100 N and it does not move. How large is the friction force on the crate? | B. Exactly 100 N | Friction must be bigger than the push to stop motion. |
| 9 | A skydiver has reached terminal velocity. Which statement is correct? | B. Air resistance equals the skydiver's weight | Falling at speed means gravity is winning. |
| 10 | You push a crate across a floor at constant velocity using 80 N. How large is the friction force? | B. 80 N | The push must exceed friction for the object to keep moving. |

## Unit 4: Momentum

| # | Question | Answer | Misconception it targets |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | An egg is dropped from the same height onto a pillow and onto a tile floor. Why does it survive the pillow? | C. The pillow makes the stop take longer, so the force is smaller | Soft surfaces reduce the impulse or momentum change. |
| 2 | A rubber ball and a lump of clay with the same mass hit a wall at the same speed. The ball bounces back; the clay sticks. Which has the greater change in momentum? | B. The ball | Stopping completely is the biggest possible change. |
| 3 | A 2 kg cart moving at 3 m/s collides with a 1 kg cart at rest, and they stick together. What is their speed afterward? | C. 2 m/s | The speed is shared equally or stays the same. |
| 4 | A bug hits the windshield of a moving bus. Compared with the bus, the bug experiences: | B. The same size force and the same size momentum change | The smaller object feels the larger force. |
| 5 | Two carts collide and stick together. Which statement is true? | B. Momentum is conserved; kinetic energy is not | If momentum is conserved, kinetic energy must be too. |
| 6 | A 60 kg skater and a 40 kg skater stand at rest on ice and push apart. The 60 kg skater moves away at 2 m/s. How fast does the 40 kg skater move? | C. 3 m/s | Both move away at the same speed. |
| 7 | Two identical carts roll toward each other at the same speed. What is the total momentum of the two-cart system? | C. Zero | Momentum is added like a scalar. |
| 8 | A 50 N force acts for 0.2 s on a 0.5 kg ball at rest. What is the ball's speed afterward? | C. 20 m/s | Impulse equals the final speed, or the time is ignored. |
| 9 | Which has more momentum: a loaded truck parked at a light, or a skateboard rolling down the street? | B. The skateboard | Momentum is the same as mass or weight. |

## Unit 5: Work & Energy

| # | Question | Answer | Misconception it targets |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | You carry a heavy box across a level room at constant velocity. How much work does your upward lifting force do on the box? | B. Zero | Any effort or tiredness counts as work. |
| 2 | A car's speed doubles. What happens to its kinetic energy? | C. It becomes four times as large | Kinetic energy is proportional to speed. |
| 3 | You push as hard as you can on a brick wall for one minute and it does not move. How much work did you do on the wall? | A. Zero | Force alone is work. |
| 4 | Two frictionless ramps have the same height. One is steep and one is gentle. A cart is released from rest at the top of each. Compare their speeds at the bottom. | C. The speeds are the same | A steeper ramp gives a faster final speed. |
| 5 | A ball is dropped from a height of 5 m. Ignoring air resistance, about how fast is it moving just before it lands? | B. 9.9 m/s | Heavier objects arrive faster, or speed is proportional to height. |
| 6 | A pendulum swings back and forth. Where is its kinetic energy greatest? | B. At the lowest point | Energy is greatest where the object is highest. |
| 7 | A box slides across a rough floor and stops. What happened to its kinetic energy? | B. It became thermal energy in the box and floor | Energy is used up or disappears. |
| 8 | Two students with the same mass climb the same staircase. Ana takes 4 s and Ben takes 8 s. Which statement is correct? | C. They do the same work, and Ana has more power | Doing something faster means doing more work. |
| 9 | A car's speed doubles. If the braking force is the same, what happens to its stopping distance? | B. It becomes four times as long | Stopping distance is proportional to speed. |
| 10 | A 100 N force pulls a sled 10 m along level ground. The rope is angled 60° above horizontal. How much work is done? | C. 500 J | Work is always force × distance, whatever the angle. |
