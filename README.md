# Physics: Semester 1 Learning Project

A study and teaching kit for 11th grade physics covering the five first-semester topics: kinematics, projectile motion, Newton's laws, momentum, and work and energy. Each unit has Cornell notes, an interactive quiz on the most often missed questions, practice problems, resources, learning objectives, and assessment criteria.

Learning goals follow the [Next Generation Science Standards for California](https://www.cde.ca.gov/pd/ca/sc/ngssstandards.asp), high school Physics and Earth Science course model.

## How to use this project

**Students**

1. Open the unit guide and read the learning objectives.
2. Study the Cornell notes: cover the Notes column and answer each cue question aloud.
3. Work the practice problems, then check the hidden answers.
4. Take the unit quiz. Read the explanation for anything you miss, then retry the missed questions until you score 80% or higher.
5. Tick off the unit in [PROGRESS.md](PROGRESS.md).

**Instructors**

1. Adjust the suggested pacing to your calendar.
2. Use each unit's assessment criteria table as the rubric for labs, problem sets, and tests.
3. Record scores in the class tracker in [ASSESSMENT.md](ASSESSMENT.md).
4. Replace quiz questions with ones your own students miss most; edit the files in `content/` and rebuild.

## Project outline and checklist

### Unit 1: Kinematics (weeks 1–4)

*How can we describe and predict where an object will be and how fast it will be moving?*

Key concepts: position and reference point, distance vs. displacement, speed vs. velocity, acceleration, motion graphs, kinematic equations, free fall.

Learning objectives:

- [ ] I can tell distance from displacement and speed from velocity, and say which are vectors.
- [ ] I can calculate average speed, average velocity, and acceleration with correct units.
- [ ] I can read position-time and velocity-time graphs, using slope and area.
- [ ] I can choose and solve the right kinematic equation for constant acceleration.
- [ ] I can explain and calculate free-fall motion using g = 9.8 m/s² downward.

Work to complete:

- [ ] [Cornell notes](units/01-kinematics/cornell-notes.md) studied with the cover-and-recall method
- [ ] [Practice problems](units/01-kinematics/README.md#practice-problems) (6 problems, three levels)
- [ ] [Quiz](quizzes/index.html) at 80% or higher (8 questions)
- [ ] [IXL practice plan](units/01-kinematics/README.md#ixl-practice-plan) to SmartScore 80+ (17 skills)
- [ ] Lab: constant-velocity buggy lab and cart-on-a-ramp acceleration lab

Real-world connections: stopping distance and following distance; speed limits and yellow-light timing; sports timing; phone and fitness trackers.

### Unit 2: Projectile Motion (weeks 5–7)

*How can we predict where a thrown or launched object will land?*

Key concepts: vector components, projectile, independence of motion, horizontal motion, vertical motion, trajectory, range and maximum height.

Learning objectives:

- [ ] I can break a velocity vector into horizontal and vertical components.
- [ ] I can explain why the horizontal and vertical motions of a projectile are independent.
- [ ] I can solve horizontal-launch problems for time of flight and range.
- [ ] I can solve angled-launch problems for time, maximum height, and range.
- [ ] I can describe the velocity and acceleration of a projectile at any point on its path.

Work to complete:

- [ ] [Cornell notes](units/02-projectile-motion/cornell-notes.md) studied with the cover-and-recall method
- [ ] [Practice problems](units/02-projectile-motion/README.md#practice-problems) (6 problems, three levels)
- [ ] [Quiz](quizzes/index.html) at 80% or higher (9 questions)
- [ ] [IXL practice plan](units/02-projectile-motion/README.md#ixl-practice-plan) to SmartScore 80+ (12 skills)
- [ ] Lab: marble-launch prediction lab and launch-angle investigation

Real-world connections: basketball free throws and soccer free kicks; long jump and shot put; water from a fountain or hose; airdrops of supplies.

### Unit 3: Newton's Laws (weeks 8–11)

*Why do objects speed up, slow down, change direction, or keep doing what they are doing?*

Key concepts: force, net force, inertia (first law), second law, mass vs. weight, third law, free-body diagram, friction.

Learning objectives:

- [ ] I can state Newton's three laws and give an example of each.
- [ ] I can draw a free-body diagram with correctly named and sized force arrows.
- [ ] I can find the net force and use F_net = m·a to find acceleration, force, or mass.
- [ ] I can tell mass from weight and calculate weight with F_grav = m·g.
- [ ] I can identify action-reaction force pairs and explain why they do not cancel.
- [ ] I can calculate friction and explain what happens at constant velocity.

Work to complete:

- [ ] [Cornell notes](units/03-newtons-laws/cornell-notes.md) studied with the cover-and-recall method
- [ ] [Practice problems](units/03-newtons-laws/README.md#practice-problems) (6 problems, three levels)
- [ ] [Quiz](quizzes/index.html) at 80% or higher (10 questions)
- [ ] [IXL practice plan](units/03-newtons-laws/README.md#ixl-practice-plan) to SmartScore 80+ (13 skills)
- [ ] Lab: cart and hanging mass lab (acceleration vs. force and vs. mass)

Real-world connections: seat belts and headrests; rocket launches; elevators; tires and shoe soles.

### Unit 4: Momentum (weeks 12–14)

*What decides how hard it is to stop a moving object, and what happens when objects collide?*

Key concepts: momentum, impulse, impulse-momentum theorem, system, conservation of momentum, elastic collision, inelastic collision.

Learning objectives:

- [ ] I can calculate momentum and explain why it is a vector.
- [ ] I can use the impulse-momentum theorem, F·Δt = m·Δv.
- [ ] I can explain how increasing collision time reduces force.
- [ ] I can use conservation of momentum to solve collision and explosion problems.
- [ ] I can tell elastic from inelastic collisions and say what is conserved in each.

Work to complete:

- [ ] [Cornell notes](units/04-momentum/cornell-notes.md) studied with the cover-and-recall method
- [ ] [Practice problems](units/04-momentum/README.md#practice-problems) (6 problems, three levels)
- [ ] [Quiz](quizzes/index.html) at 80% or higher (9 questions)
- [ ] [IXL practice plan](units/04-momentum/README.md#ixl-practice-plan) to SmartScore 80+ (5 skills)
- [ ] Lab: cart collision lab and egg-drop design challenge

Real-world connections: airbags, seat belts, and crumple zones; helmets and padded gym floors; following through on a golf, tennis, or baseball swing; rocket and jet propulsion.

### Unit 5: Work & Energy (weeks 15–17)

*Where does energy come from, where does it go, and how can we keep track of it?*

Key concepts: work, kinetic energy, gravitational potential energy, work-energy theorem, mechanical energy, conservation of energy, power.

Learning objectives:

- [ ] I can calculate work and decide when a force does zero or negative work.
- [ ] I can calculate kinetic energy and gravitational potential energy.
- [ ] I can use the work-energy theorem to connect net work and change in kinetic energy.
- [ ] I can use conservation of energy to find speed or height, with and without friction.
- [ ] I can calculate power and tell it apart from work.

Work to complete:

- [ ] [Cornell notes](units/05-work-energy/cornell-notes.md) studied with the cover-and-recall method
- [ ] [Practice problems](units/05-work-energy/README.md#practice-problems) (6 problems, three levels)
- [ ] [Quiz](quizzes/index.html) at 80% or higher (10 questions)
- [ ] [IXL practice plan](units/05-work-energy/README.md#ixl-practice-plan) to SmartScore 80+ (10 skills)
- [ ] Lab: pendulum or ramp energy lab and staircase power lab

Real-world connections: roller coasters; hydroelectric dams; regenerative braking in electric cars; highway speed and crash severity.

## Assessment and progress tracking

Every unit is scored on a 4-point proficiency scale against three skills. A student has mastered a unit when every skill is at 3 or higher, the quiz is at 80% or higher, and the lab write-up is complete. Details, the grading evidence for each unit, and blank trackers are in [ASSESSMENT.md](ASSESSMENT.md) and [PROGRESS.md](PROGRESS.md).

## Please check before sharing

- **Learning objectives and essential questions** were written from the NGSS performance expectations, not from a class syllabus. Compare them with what the teacher has posted.
- **Pacing** is a suggestion that fits five units into about 17 weeks.
- **Quiz questions** target misconceptions that are well documented in physics teaching. They are not taken from this class's own results.
- **IXL skill codes** were copied from the live IXL Physics, Grade 8 science, and Precalculus lists on November 27, 2026. Skills marked "(New)" had no number on that date.
- **Kinematics and projectile motion** have no performance expectation of their own in NGSS. They are taught here as the foundation for HS-PS2-1.

## Rebuilding

All notes, guides, and quizzes are generated from the files in `content/`.

```bash
npm install docx            # once
python3 tools/diagrams.py   # redraw diagrams (needs matplotlib)
node tools/build.js         # rebuild guides, notes, Word files, and quizzes
```
