# Physics: Semester 1 Learning Project (Grade 11)

A shareable project outline for students and instructors covering the five first-semester mechanics units. Each unit has Cornell notes, an interactive quiz built from the questions students miss most, practice problems with answers, learning resources, real-world applications, and trackable learning objectives. Learning goals follow the California NGSS (Physics and Earth Science course model): see the [standards map](docs/standards-map.md).

## What students need to master

### Unit 1: Kinematics

*How can we describe and predict the motion of an object using words, graphs, and equations?*

- **Position and reference frame:** Where an object is, measured from a chosen origin.
- **Distance vs displacement:** Total path length (scalar) vs change in position with direction (vector).
- **Speed vs velocity:** Distance per time (scalar) vs displacement per time (vector).
- **Acceleration:** Rate of change of velocity, a = Δv/Δt, in m/s². It is a vector.
- **Motion graphs:** Slope of position-time = velocity. Slope of velocity-time = acceleration. Area under velocity-time = displacement.
- **Kinematic equations:** Four equations linking Δx, v₀, v, a, t when acceleration is constant.
- **Free fall:** Motion under gravity only. Every object accelerates at 9.8 m/s² downward, whatever its mass.

### Unit 2: Projectile Motion

*Why does a projectile follow a curved path, and how can we predict where it will land?*

- **Vector components:** Any vector can be split into a horizontal part and a vertical part: vx = v cos θ, vy = v sin θ.
- **Resultant:** The single vector that equals two or more vectors added together.
- **Projectile:** An object moving through the air with gravity as the only force on it.
- **Independence of motion:** Horizontal motion and vertical motion do not affect each other. Time is the only thing they share.
- **Horizontal motion:** Constant velocity, because no horizontal force acts (ax = 0).
- **Vertical motion:** Free fall: ay = 9.8 m/s² downward the whole time.
- **Trajectory, range, time of flight:** The parabolic path, the horizontal distance covered, and the time in the air.

### Unit 3: Newton's Laws

*How do the forces on an object determine how its motion changes?*

- **Force:** A push or pull on one object by another. A vector, measured in newtons (N).
- **Net force:** The vector sum of all forces on one object. It is what causes acceleration.
- **Free-body diagram:** A sketch of one object with an arrow for every force acting on it.
- **Inertia (first law):** Objects keep their velocity unless a net force acts. More mass means more inertia.
- **Second law:** a = F_net/m. Acceleration is proportional to net force and inversely proportional to mass.
- **Third law:** Forces come in pairs: equal in size, opposite in direction, acting on two different objects.
- **Mass vs weight:** Mass is the amount of matter (kg). Weight is the force of gravity, F_grav = mg (N).
- **Normal force and friction:** The support force perpendicular to a surface; the force along the surface that resists sliding, F_frict = μF_norm.

### Unit 4: Momentum

*Why is momentum conserved in collisions, and how can we use that to make collisions safer?*

- **Momentum:** Mass times velocity, p = mv. A vector, in kg·m/s.
- **Impulse:** Force times the time it acts, J = FΔt. Units: N·s (the same as kg·m/s).
- **Impulse-momentum theorem:** Impulse equals change in momentum: FΔt = mΔv.
- **System:** The set of objects you choose to track. It is isolated if no net external force acts on it.
- **Conservation of momentum:** In an isolated system, total momentum before equals total momentum after.
- **Elastic vs inelastic collisions:** Momentum is conserved in both. Kinetic energy is conserved only in elastic collisions.
- **Explosion / recoil:** Objects push apart from rest; their momenta are equal and opposite, adding to zero.

### Unit 5: Work & Energy

*How is energy transferred and transformed, and what does it mean to say energy is conserved?*

- **Work:** Energy transferred by a force acting through a distance: W = Fd cos θ. Units: joules (J).
- **Kinetic energy:** Energy of motion: KE = ½mv².
- **Work-energy theorem:** Net work on an object equals its change in kinetic energy: W_net = ΔKE.
- **Gravitational potential energy:** Energy of position in a gravitational field: PE = mgh, measured from a chosen zero level.
- **Elastic potential energy:** Energy stored in a stretched or compressed spring: PE = ½kx².
- **Conservation of energy:** Energy is never created or destroyed, only transferred or transformed. Without friction, KE + PE stays constant.
- **Power:** The rate of doing work: P = W/t. Units: watts (W).

## Semester checklist

### Unit 1: Kinematics (Weeks 1–4)

- [ ] Cornell notes read and self-tested
- [ ] **K1** Distinguish distance from displacement and speed from velocity, and calculate each from a description of a trip.
- [ ] **K2** Calculate acceleration and explain what its sign means for an object speeding up or slowing down.
- [ ] **K3** Interpret position-time and velocity-time graphs using slope and area.
- [ ] **K4** Select and solve the correct kinematic equation for constant acceleration.
- [ ] **K5** Solve free-fall problems and explain why all objects fall with the same acceleration.
- [ ] Practice problems complete
- [ ] Quiz 8/10 or better
- [ ] IXL Practice Plan at SmartScore 80+
- [ ] Lab or project turned in

### Unit 2: Projectile Motion (Weeks 5–7)

- [ ] Cornell notes read and self-tested
- [ ] **P1** Resolve a vector into components and combine components into a resultant with magnitude and direction.
- [ ] **P2** Explain the independence of horizontal and vertical motion and identify gravity as the only force on a projectile.
- [ ] **P3** Solve horizontally launched projectile problems for time, range, and impact speed.
- [ ] **P4** Solve angle-launched projectile problems for time of flight, maximum height, and range.
- [ ] **P5** Make a quantitative prediction of a landing point, test it, and explain the sources of error.
- [ ] Practice problems complete
- [ ] Quiz 8/10 or better
- [ ] IXL Practice Plan at SmartScore 80+
- [ ] Lab or project turned in

### Unit 3: Newton's Laws (Weeks 8–11)

- [ ] Cornell notes read and self-tested
- [ ] **N1** Identify the forces on an object and draw a correctly labeled free-body diagram.
- [ ] **N2** Use the first law to explain the motion of objects with balanced forces, including constant-velocity motion.
- [ ] **N3** Apply F_net = ma to solve for acceleration, net force, or mass in one-dimensional problems.
- [ ] **N4** Identify action-reaction pairs and explain why equal forces can give unequal accelerations.
- [ ] **N5** Calculate weight, normal force, and friction, and distinguish mass from weight.
- [ ] **N6** Analyze cart-and-hanging-mass data to support the claim that a = F_net/m (HS-PS2-1).
- [ ] Practice problems complete
- [ ] Quiz 8/10 or better
- [ ] IXL Practice Plan at SmartScore 80+
- [ ] Lab or project turned in

### Unit 4: Momentum (Weeks 12–14)

- [ ] Cornell notes read and self-tested
- [ ] **M1** Calculate momentum and compare the momenta of different objects, including direction.
- [ ] **M2** Apply the impulse-momentum theorem to find force, time, or change in velocity.
- [ ] **M3** Explain how extending collision time reduces force, with real examples.
- [ ] **M4** Use conservation of momentum to solve collision and explosion problems (HS-PS2-2).
- [ ] **M5** Classify collisions as elastic or inelastic using kinetic energy.
- [ ] **M6** Design, test, and refine a device that minimizes force in a collision (HS-PS2-3).
- [ ] Practice problems complete
- [ ] Quiz 8/10 or better
- [ ] IXL Practice Plan at SmartScore 80+
- [ ] Lab or project turned in

### Unit 5: Work & Energy (Weeks 15–18)

- [ ] Cornell notes read and self-tested
- [ ] **E1** Calculate work done by a force at any angle and identify zero and negative work.
- [ ] **E2** Calculate kinetic energy and apply the work-energy theorem.
- [ ] **E3** Calculate gravitational and elastic potential energy.
- [ ] **E4** Apply conservation of energy to solve problems with and without friction, using bar charts as models (HS-PS3-2).
- [ ] **E5** Calculate power and distinguish it from work.
- [ ] **E6** Build a spreadsheet model of energy changes (HS-PS3-1) and an energy conversion device (HS-PS3-3).
- [ ] Practice problems complete
- [ ] Quiz 8/10 or better
- [ ] IXL Practice Plan at SmartScore 80+
- [ ] Lab or project turned in

## For instructors: editing and rebuilding

All unit content lives in one file per unit under `tools/content/`. Every notes page, Word document, quiz, and checklist is generated from those files, so edit there and rebuild:

```
npm install                 # once, installs the docx library
pip install matplotlib      # once, for the diagrams
python3 tools/figures.py    # redraw diagrams
node tools/build.js         # rebuild all pages, notes, and quizzes
```
