# Physics: Semester 1 Learning Project

An 11th grade physics course outline for students and instructors, covering the five Semester 1 units: kinematics, projectile motion, Newton's laws, momentum, and work and energy. Each unit has Cornell notes, an interactive quiz built from the questions students most often miss, practice problems, labs, resources, and objectives that can be tracked.

Learning goals follow the California Next Generation Science Standards (CA NGSS), high school three-course model, *Physics in the Universe* (physics integrated with Earth and space science).

## Project checklist

### Unit 1: Kinematics

*How can we describe and predict the motion of an object using words, graphs, and equations?*

Key concepts: Scalars and vectors; Distance vs. displacement; Speed vs. velocity; Acceleration; Motion graphs; Kinematic equations; Free fall.

- [ ] **K1** Distinguish scalar from vector quantities and distance from displacement.
- [ ] **K2** Calculate average speed, average velocity, and acceleration.
- [ ] **K3** Interpret position-time and velocity-time graphs.
- [ ] **K4** Solve constant-acceleration problems with the kinematic equations.
- [ ] **K5** Analyze free fall, including the top of the path.
- [ ] **K6** Collect and graph motion data in a lab.
- [ ] Quiz 8/10 or better · IXL plan at SmartScore 80+ · lab report · unit test 70%+

### Unit 2: Projectile Motion

*Why does a projectile follow a curved path, and how can we predict where it will land?*

Key concepts: Vector components; Projectile; Independence of motion; Horizontal launch; Angled launch; Range and angle.

- [ ] **P1** Resolve a vector into components and recombine components into a resultant.
- [ ] **P2** Explain the independence of horizontal and vertical motion.
- [ ] **P3** Solve horizontally launched projectile problems.
- [ ] **P4** Solve angle-launched projectile problems.
- [ ] **P5** Predict the effect of launch angle and speed on the path.
- [ ] **P6** Predict and test a landing point in a lab.
- [ ] Quiz 8/10 or better · IXL plan at SmartScore 80+ · lab report · unit test 70%+

### Unit 3: Newton's Laws

*How do forces change the motion of an object?*

Key concepts: Force and net force; Free-body diagram; First law (inertia); Second law; Mass vs. weight; Friction and normal force; Third law.

- [ ] **N1** Identify forces and draw a free-body diagram.
- [ ] **N2** Apply Newton's first law to balanced-force situations.
- [ ] **N3** Apply Fnet = m·a quantitatively.
- [ ] **N4** Distinguish mass from weight; find normal and friction forces.
- [ ] **N5** Identify action-reaction pairs.
- [ ] **N6** Analyze lab data to support the second law (HS-PS2-1).
- [ ] Quiz 8/10 or better · IXL plan at SmartScore 80+ · lab report · unit test 70%+

### Unit 4: Momentum

*Why does making a collision last longer reduce the force, and what stays the same in every collision?*

Key concepts: Momentum; Impulse; Force-time trade-off; Conservation of momentum; Collision types.

- [ ] **M1** Calculate momentum as a vector.
- [ ] **M2** Apply the impulse-momentum theorem.
- [ ] **M3** Explain collision safety with force and time.
- [ ] **M4** Apply conservation of momentum (HS-PS2-2).
- [ ] **M5** Classify collisions as elastic or inelastic.
- [ ] **M6** Design, test, and refine a force-reducing device (HS-PS2-3).
- [ ] Quiz 8/10 or better · IXL plan at SmartScore 80+ · lab report · unit test 70%+

### Unit 5: Work & Energy

*Where does energy go when an object speeds up, slows down, rises, or falls?*

Key concepts: Work; Kinetic energy; Potential energy; Work-energy theorem; Conservation of energy; Power.

- [ ] **E1** Calculate work, including angled forces and zero-work cases.
- [ ] **E2** Calculate kinetic and gravitational potential energy.
- [ ] **E3** Apply the work-energy theorem.
- [ ] **E4** Apply conservation of mechanical energy (HS-PS3-1).
- [ ] **E5** Model energy transfers, including friction (HS-PS3-2).
- [ ] **E6** Calculate power.
- [ ] **E7** Design a device that converts energy from one form to another (HS-PS3-3).
- [ ] Quiz 8/10 or better · IXL plan at SmartScore 80+ · lab report · unit test 70%+

## How students are assessed

Every objective is scored on a 4-point scale (1 beginning, 2 developing, 3 proficient, 4 advanced). The evidence that counts as proficient is written next to each objective in the unit README. A unit is mastered when the student has:

1. Self-tested from the Cornell notes on two different days.
2. Reached SmartScore 80+ on the unit's IXL skills.
3. Scored 8/10 or better on the missed-question quiz.
4. Earned a 3 or better on the lab or design objective.
5. Scored 70% or better on the unit test.

The [progress tracker](PROGRESS-TRACKER.md) has the scale, suggested weights, and a table for every objective.

## NGSS performance expectations covered

| Code | Performance expectation | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| HS-PS2-1 | Analyze data to support the claim that Newton's second law of motion describes the mathematical relationship among the net force on a macroscopic object, its mass, and its acceleration. | 1, 2, 3 |
| HS-PS2-2 | Use mathematical representations to support the claim that the total momentum of a system of objects is conserved when there is no net force on the system. | 4 |
| HS-PS2-3 | Apply scientific and engineering ideas to design, evaluate, and refine a device that minimizes the force on a macroscopic object during a collision. | 4 |
| HS-PS2-4 | Use mathematical representations of Newton's law of gravitation and Coulomb's law to describe and predict the gravitational and electrostatic forces between objects. (Introduced here through weight; completed in Semester 2.) | 3 |
| HS-PS3-1 | Create a computational model to calculate the change in the energy of one component in a system when the change in energy of the other component(s) and energy flows in and out of the system are known. | 5 |
| HS-PS3-2 | Develop and use models to illustrate that energy at the macroscopic scale can be accounted for as a combination of energy associated with the motion of particles (objects) and energy associated with the relative position of particles (objects). | 5 |
| HS-PS3-3 | Design, build, and refine a device that works within given constraints to convert one form of energy into another form of energy. | 5 |

Standards source: [NGSS for California Public Schools, K-12](https://www.cde.ca.gov/pd/ca/sc/ngssstandards.asp) (California Department of Education), Grades 9-12 Physical Science. Kinematics and projectile motion have no performance expectation of their own; they supply the motion analysis that HS-PS2-1 depends on. The Earth and space science expectations in this course model (plate tectonics, earthquakes) belong to Semester 2.

## Items for the instructor to confirm

- **Learning targets and essential questions** were written from the NGSS and standard physics content, not from the class's own slides. Compare them with what is posted in class.
- **"Most often missed" questions** are based on well-documented student misconceptions in introductory physics (the kind probed by concept inventories), not on this class's own test data. Swap in items from your gradebook as they become available.
- **Labs** are standard suggestions (buggy lab, marble launch, cart and hanging mass, cart collisions and egg drop, stair-climb power). Replace them with the labs the class actually runs.
- **Vocabulary** follows The Physics Classroom (vi, vf, Fgrav, Fnorm, Ffrict). Change it in `content/` if the class uses different symbols.
- **g = 9.8 m/s²** is used throughout. Some classes round to 10.
- **IXL codes** were copied from the live IXL pages on 2026-11-16 and may be renumbered by IXL later.
