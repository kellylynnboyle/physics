# Physics: Semester 1 Learning Project

A shareable outline, notes, and practice set for 11th grade physics, first semester: kinematics, projectile motion, Newton's laws, momentum, and work and energy. Learning goals follow the [California NGSS](https://www.cde.ca.gov/pd/ca/sc/ngssstandards.asp) high school physical science standards (Physics and Earth Science course model).

## How to use this project

**Students:** work through one unit at a time. Each unit folder has a checklist. Read the Cornell notes, self-test with the cue column, take the most-missed quiz, then do the practice problems and IXL skills.

**Instructors:** each unit lists learning targets, NGSS connections, a lab, and assessment evidence. Track progress with the [mastery scale and tracker](docs/learning-objectives-and-assessment.md).

## Semester checklist

### Unit 1: Kinematics (Weeks 1–4)

*How can we describe and predict the motion of an object using words, graphs, and equations?*

- [ ] I can tell distance from displacement and speed from velocity, and calculate each.
- [ ] I can read position-time and velocity-time graphs using slope and area.
- [ ] I can define acceleration and decide from signs whether an object speeds up or slows down.
- [ ] I can choose and solve the right kinematic equation for constant acceleration.
- [ ] I can solve free-fall problems and explain why all objects fall with the same acceleration.
- [ ] I can collect motion data in a lab and find velocity from the slope of a graph.
- [ ] Most-missed quiz at 8/10 or better
- [ ] Lab: Constant-velocity buggy and cart on a ramp

### Unit 2: Projectile Motion (Weeks 5–7)

*Why can we predict where a launched object will land by treating its horizontal and vertical motion separately?*

- [ ] I can break a vector into horizontal and vertical components and combine components into a resultant.
- [ ] I can explain why horizontal and vertical motion are independent and what each one does during flight.
- [ ] I can solve horizontally launched projectile problems for time, range, and impact speed.
- [ ] I can solve angled-launch problems for time of flight, maximum height, and range.
- [ ] I can predict how changing launch speed, height, or angle changes the path.
- [ ] I can predict a landing spot in a lab and compare it with my measurement.
- [ ] Most-missed quiz at 8/10 or better
- [ ] Lab: Hit the target: marble off a table

### Unit 3: Newton's Laws (Weeks 8–11)

*How do the forces acting on an object determine how its motion changes?*

- [ ] I can draw a free-body diagram that shows only the forces acting on one object.
- [ ] I can use Newton's first law to explain motion when forces are balanced.
- [ ] I can use F_net = ma to find acceleration, net force, or mass.
- [ ] I can tell mass from weight and calculate weight, normal force, and friction.
- [ ] I can identify Newton's third-law force pairs and explain why they do not cancel.
- [ ] I can analyze lab data to show how acceleration depends on net force and mass.
- [ ] Most-missed quiz at 8/10 or better
- [ ] Lab: Cart and hanging mass (Newton's second law)

### Unit 4: Momentum (Weeks 12–14)

*Why is the total momentum the same before and after a collision, and how can we use that to make collisions safer?*

- [ ] I can calculate momentum and treat it as a vector with a sign.
- [ ] I can use impulse = FΔt = Δp to find force, time, or change in velocity.
- [ ] I can explain how increasing collision time reduces force.
- [ ] I can use conservation of momentum to solve collision and recoil problems.
- [ ] I can classify collisions as elastic or inelastic by checking kinetic energy.
- [ ] I can design and test a device that reduces the force in a collision.
- [ ] Most-missed quiz at 8/10 or better
- [ ] Lab: Colliding carts, plus the egg-drop design challenge

### Unit 5: Work & Energy (Weeks 15–18)

*How can tracking energy let us predict motion without knowing every force along the way?*

- [ ] I can calculate the work done by a force, including at an angle, and say when work is zero or negative.
- [ ] I can calculate kinetic energy and use the work-energy theorem.
- [ ] I can calculate gravitational and elastic potential energy.
- [ ] I can use conservation of mechanical energy to find a speed or height.
- [ ] I can account for energy transformed to thermal energy by friction.
- [ ] I can calculate power and compare how quickly work is done.
- [ ] Most-missed quiz at 8/10 or better
- [ ] Lab: Energy on a ramp (or pendulum)

pip install matplotlib
python3 tools/build.py

## Notes for the instructor

- Learning targets and essential questions were written for this project from the course description and the NGSS. Check them against what you post for your class.
- The "most-missed" quiz questions are built on well-documented physics misconceptions (the kind tested by the Force Concept Inventory), not on your own class's test data. Swap in your own missed questions in `tools/content/` when you have them.
- IXL skill codes were copied from the live IXL pages in November 2026. IXL renumbers skills from time to time.
