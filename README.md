## Course overview

This course provides lab experience, real-world connections, and a theoretical framework for many topics in physics.

- **Semester 1 (this project):** kinematics, projectile motion, Newton's laws, momentum, work and energy.
- **Semester 2 (not built yet):** electricity, magnetism, waves, light, plate tectonics, earthquakes, nuclear physics.

The learning goals are the [California NGSS standards](https://www.cde.ca.gov/pd/ca/sc/ngssstandards.asp), using the high school [physical science](https://www.cde.ca.gov/pd/ca/sc/documents/ngsshsphyicalscidci.pdf) and [engineering design](https://www.cde.ca.gov/pd/ca/sc/documents/cangsshsengdesign-dci.pdf) standards.

## Project checklist

### Unit 1: Kinematics (4 weeks)

*How can we describe and predict where an object will be and how fast it will be moving?*

**Key concepts**

- Scalars (distance, speed, time) versus vectors (displacement, velocity, acceleration)
- Average speed = distance / time; average velocity = displacement / time
- Acceleration = change in velocity / time; the sign shows direction, not "speeding up" or "slowing down"
- Position-time graphs (slope = velocity) and velocity-time graphs (slope = acceleration, area = displacement)
- The four kinematic equations for constant acceleration
- Free fall: every object accelerates at 9.8 m/s² downward when air resistance is ignored

**Learning objectives**

- [ ] **K1** Distinguish distance from displacement and speed from velocity (HS-PS2-1 (supporting))
- [ ] **K2** Calculate average speed, velocity and acceleration (HS-PS2-1 (supporting))
- [ ] **K3** Interpret position-time and velocity-time graphs (HS-PS2-1; SEP Analyzing Data)
- [ ] **K4** Solve constant-acceleration and free-fall problems (HS-PS2-1; SEP Using Mathematics)
- [ ] **K5** Collect and graph motion data in a lab (SEP Planning and Carrying Out Investigations)

**Work to complete**

- [ ] [Cornell notes](units/01-kinematics/cornell-notes.docx): 6 sections, 15 cue questions
- [ ] [Practice problems](units/01-kinematics/README.md#practice-problems) (5)
- [ ] [IXL practice plan](units/01-kinematics/README.md#ixl-practice-plan) to SmartScore 80+
- [ ] Unit quiz: 7/8 or better
- [ ] Unit lab: constant-speed buggy lab

**Real-world connections:** Stopping distance and following distance when driving; GPS and fitness trackers computing speed from position and time; Sports timing: sprint splits, pitch speed, swim pacing; Aircraft takeoff: required runway length from acceleration and liftoff speed; Free fall on the Moon versus Earth.

### Unit 2: Projectile Motion (3 weeks)

*Why does a ball thrown sideways hit the ground at the same time as a ball that is dropped?*

**Key concepts**

- Vector components: v<sub>x</sub> = v cos θ and v<sub>y</sub> = v sin θ
- A projectile is an object moving under gravity alone
- Horizontal motion: constant velocity (a<sub>x</sub> = 0)
- Vertical motion: free fall (a<sub>y</sub> = 9.8 m/s² downward)
- Time is the one quantity shared by the horizontal and vertical motion
- Maximum range at 45°; complementary angles give equal ranges (same launch speed, level ground)

**Learning objectives**

- [ ] **P1** Resolve a vector into components and recombine components (SEP Using Mathematics)
- [ ] **P2** Explain the independence of horizontal and vertical motion (HS-PS2-1 (supporting); SEP Developing Models)
- [ ] **P3** Solve horizontally launched projectile problems (SEP Using Mathematics)
- [ ] **P4** Solve angled-launch problems (SEP Using Mathematics)
- [ ] **P5** Predict and test a landing point in a lab (SEP Planning and Carrying Out Investigations)

**Work to complete**

- [ ] [Cornell notes](units/02-projectile-motion/cornell-notes.docx): 5 sections, 15 cue questions
- [ ] [Practice problems](units/02-projectile-motion/README.md#practice-problems) (5)
- [ ] [IXL practice plan](units/02-projectile-motion/README.md#ixl-practice-plan) to SmartScore 80+
- [ ] Unit quiz: 7/8 or better
- [ ] Unit lab: marble-off-the-table lab

**Real-world connections:** Sports: basketball arcs, soccer free kicks, long jump take-off angle; Water from a fountain or hose nozzle; Dropping supplies from a moving aircraft: release before reaching the target; Firefighting: aiming a water stream to reach a window; Video-game physics engines and animation.

### Unit 3: Newton's Laws (4 weeks)

*What causes the motion of an object to change?*

**Key concepts**

- Force is a push or pull, measured in newtons; net force is the vector sum of all forces on one object
- Free-body diagrams show only the forces acting ON the object
- First law: balanced forces mean constant velocity (including rest); inertia depends on mass
- Second law: a = F<sub>net</sub> ÷ m
- Third law: forces come in equal, opposite pairs acting on two different objects
- Mass (kg) versus weight (N): F<sub>grav</sub> = mg; friction F<sub>frict</sub> = μF<sub>norm</sub>

**Learning objectives**

- [ ] **N1** Draw free-body diagrams and find net force (HS-PS2-1)
- [ ] **N2** Apply the first law and inertia to explain motion (HS-PS2-1; CCC Cause and Effect)
- [ ] **N3** Solve second-law problems, including friction and weight (HS-PS2-1; SEP Using Mathematics)
- [ ] **N4** Identify action-reaction pairs (HS-PS2-1 (supporting))
- [ ] **N5** Analyze lab data to support the second law (HS-PS2-1; SEP Analyzing Data)

**Work to complete**

- [ ] [Cornell notes](units/03-newtons-laws/cornell-notes.docx): 5 sections, 16 cue questions
- [ ] [Practice problems](units/03-newtons-laws/README.md#practice-problems) (5)
- [ ] [IXL practice plan](units/03-newtons-laws/README.md#ixl-practice-plan) to SmartScore 80+
- [ ] Unit quiz: 7/8 or better
- [ ] Unit lab: cart-and-hanging-mass lab

**Real-world connections:** Seat belts and headrests; Rocket launches; Car design: engine force, mass and acceleration; Walking and swimming: pushing backward on the ground or water to move forward; Terminal velocity for skydivers and raindrops.

### Unit 4: Momentum (3 weeks)

*Why do airbags, helmets and crumple zones save lives?*

**Key concepts**

- Momentum p = mv is a vector; direction (sign) matters
- Impulse = FΔt = Δp (impulse-momentum theorem)
- Longer collision time means smaller force for the same change in momentum
- Conservation of momentum: total momentum before = total momentum after (no net external force)
- Elastic collisions conserve kinetic energy; inelastic collisions do not; momentum is conserved in both
- Engineering link: designing a device to reduce collision force

**Learning objectives**

- [ ] **M1** Calculate momentum for objects and systems (HS-PS2-2)
- [ ] **M2** Apply the impulse-momentum theorem (HS-PS2-2; HS-PS2-3)
- [ ] **M3** Explain force reduction by extending collision time (HS-PS2-3)
- [ ] **M4** Solve collision and recoil problems with conservation of momentum (HS-PS2-2; SEP Using Mathematics)
- [ ] **M5** Design, test and refine a collision-safety device (HS-PS2-3; HS-ETS1-2; HS-ETS1-3)

**Work to complete**

- [ ] [Cornell notes](units/04-momentum/cornell-notes.docx): 5 sections, 15 cue questions
- [ ] [Practice problems](units/04-momentum/README.md#practice-problems) (5)
- [ ] [IXL practice plan](units/04-momentum/README.md#ixl-practice-plan) to SmartScore 80+
- [ ] Unit quiz: 7/8 or better
- [ ] Unit lab: collision-cart lab

**Real-world connections:** Car safety: airbags, crumple zones, seat belts that stretch; Helmets and padding in football, cycling and skateboarding; Catching a ball with "soft hands"; bending the knees when landing; Rocket and jet propulsion; recoil of a fire hose; Crash reconstruction by accident investigators.

### Unit 5: Work and Energy (3 weeks)

*Where does the energy go when a roller coaster slows down?*

**Key concepts**

- Work W = Fd cos θ; only the part of the force along the motion does work
- Kinetic energy KE = ½mv²; gravitational potential energy PE = mgh; elastic potential energy = ½kx²
- Work-energy theorem: W<sub>net</sub> = ΔKE
- Conservation of mechanical energy when friction and air resistance are negligible
- With friction, mechanical energy becomes thermal energy; total energy is still conserved
- Power P = W ÷ t, measured in watts

**Learning objectives**

- [ ] **E1** Calculate work, including zero and negative work (HS-PS3-1)
- [ ] **E2** Calculate kinetic and potential energy (HS-PS3-2)
- [ ] **E3** Apply the work-energy theorem (HS-PS3-1; SEP Using Mathematics)
- [ ] **E4** Use conservation of energy with and without friction (HS-PS3-1; HS-PS3-2; CCC Energy and Matter)
- [ ] **E5** Calculate power (HS-PS3-1 (supporting))
- [ ] **E6** Design, build and refine an energy-conversion device (HS-PS3-3; HS-ETS1-2)

**Work to complete**

- [ ] [Cornell notes](units/05-work-energy/cornell-notes.docx): 5 sections, 16 cue questions
- [ ] [Practice problems](units/05-work-energy/README.md#practice-problems) (5)
- [ ] [IXL practice plan](units/05-work-energy/README.md#ixl-practice-plan) to SmartScore 80+
- [ ] Unit quiz: 7/8 or better
- [ ] Unit lab: stair-climb power lab

**Real-world connections:** Roller coasters and skate parks; Stopping distance: four times longer at double the speed; Hydroelectric dams, wind turbines and regenerative braking in electric cars; Sports: pole vault, bow and arrow, trampoline; Reading an electricity bill in kilowatt-hours.

## What to check before using this with a class

- **Learning targets and essential questions** were written from the unit content, not from a class deck. Compare them with what the teacher has posted.
- **"Most often missed" questions** are based on misconceptions that are well documented for these topics. They are not drawn from this class's own test results. Swap in questions from your own item analysis when you have it.
- **Labs** are suggested standard labs. Replace them with the labs the class actually runs.
- **IXL codes** were read from the live IXL pages on 20 November 2026. IXL renumbers skills from time to time. Skills marked (New) had no number on that date.
- **Pacing and grade weights** are suggestions.
