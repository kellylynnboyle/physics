## Practice Problems

1. A 1200 kg car accelerates from 0 to 25 m/s in 8 seconds. What net force was required?
2. A 60 kg skier is pulled up a 20° frictionless slope by a tow rope at constant velocity. What is the tension in the rope? (Hint: constant velocity → Fnet = 0)
3. Two boxes are stacked: a 3 kg box on top of a 5 kg box, both at rest on the floor. What is the normal force the floor exerts on the bottom box?
4. A 1000 kg elevator accelerates upward at 2 m/s². What is the tension in the supporting cable? (g=9.8 m/s²)
5. A 4 kg block is pulled horizontally with 20 N across a surface with μₖ=0.25. Find its acceleration.

<details><summary>Show Answers</summary>

1. a=(25-0)/8=3.125 m/s². F=ma=1200×3.125=3750 N
2. Constant velocity → Fnet=0 along the slope → T = mg sin(20°) = 60×9.8×0.342 ≈ 201 N
3. Total weight supported = (3+5)×9.8 = 78.4 N → Normal force from floor = 78.4 N
4. Fnet = ma = 1000×2 = 2000 N upward. T - mg = Fnet → T = 2000 + (1000×9.8) = 11,800 N
5. Friction: f=μₖN=0.25×(4×9.8)=9.8 N. Fnet=20-9.8=10.2 N. a=10.2/4=2.55 m/s²
</details>

## Real-World Applications

- **Seatbelts and airbags:** Newton's First Law explains why passengers keep moving forward in a sudden stop — restraints supply the net force to change the passenger's motion along with the car.
- **Rocket propulsion:** Third Law — expelling exhaust gas backward pushes the rocket forward with equal and opposite force.
- **Vehicle design/traction control:** friction coefficients (μₛ, μₖ) determine stopping distances and are engineered into tire compounds.
- **Elevators:** the "heavier/lighter" feeling during acceleration is literally the extra/reduced normal force your body exerts and receives, per Fnet=ma applied to you.

## Extension / Challenge

- Investigate why, in an elevator accelerating upward, you feel heavier — compute the *apparent weight* (normal force on your feet) versus your *actual weight* (mg) during acceleration.
