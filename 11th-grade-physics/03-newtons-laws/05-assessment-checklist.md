## Instructor Mastery Rubric

| Level | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **Emerging (1)** | Believes constant motion requires a continuous applied force; cannot draw a basic FBD; confuses mass and weight. |
| **Developing (2)** | Draws FBDs for simple horizontal cases with support; applies Fnet=ma only when a single force is given; still misidentifies Third Law pairs (commonly pairing gravity with normal force). |
| **Proficient (3)** | Independently draws FBDs including inclines and multi-force systems; solves Fnet=ma with friction and multiple forces; correctly identifies Third Law pairs by naming both objects; scores ≥80% on unit quiz. |
| **Advanced (4)** | Solves multi-object systems (e.g., connected blocks, elevator problems, apparent weight); explains *why* the misconceptions are wrong to a peer, not just the correct answer; scores ≥90% on unit quiz. |

## Gradable Evidence Checklist

- [ ] Unit quiz score recorded (target ≥80%)
- [ ] At least 3 practice problems shown with full work, including at least one free-body diagram
- [ ] Cornell notes cue-column self-quiz completed
- [ ] One real-world application explained in the student's own words
- [ ] At least one problem correctly distinguishes an action-reaction pair from an equilibrium pair (see Quiz Q6)

## Suggested Exit Ticket (3 questions, 5 minutes)

1. Draw the free-body diagram for a box being pulled to the right across a rough floor at constant velocity.
2. A 6 kg object experiences a net force of 18 N. What is its acceleration?
3. Explain why a car's tires pushing backward on the road, and the road pushing the tires forward, is a valid Third Law pair — but a car's weight and the road's normal force on the car is *not*.
