# Unit 11: Earthquakes — SAT/ACT Practice

- **SAT Math** occasionally uses earthquake magnitude as a **logarithm/exponent word problem** (since the scale is explicitly logarithmic).
- **ACT Science** frequently uses **seismograph data tables** (arrival time differences at multiple stations) — testing triangulation logic and trend-reading.

## SAT Math Style

**Q1.** If each whole-number increase in earthquake magnitude represents
approximately 32 times more energy, approximately how many times more
energy does a magnitude 9 earthquake release compared to a magnitude 6
earthquake?

<details><summary>Show Answer</summary>

**≈32,768 times (32³).**

Three whole-number steps → multiply 32 by itself three times: 32×32×32 ≈
32,768. The most-missed error: multiplying 32×3=96 instead of raising it
to the third power — the scale is logarithmic (multiplicative per step),
not additive.
</details>

## ACT Science Reasoning Style

**Q2.** Three seismograph stations record the time delay between P-wave and
S-wave arrival:

| Station | Time delay (s) | Distance to epicenter (km) |
|---|---|---|
| X | 40 | 320 |
| Y | 60 | 480 |
| Z | 80 | 640 |

Based on this data, what is the approximate relationship between P-S time
delay and distance to the epicenter?

<details><summary>Show Answer</summary>

**Directly proportional — distance (km) ≈ 8 × time delay (s)** (320/40=8;
480/60=8; 640/80=8, consistent across all three rows).

This is a "find the pattern/ratio" ACT Science question — once
established, this ratio is exactly what lets scientists convert a
seismograph's P-S delay into a distance estimate for triangulation.
</details>

---

**Q3.** Using the relationship found in Q2, a fourth station records a time
delay of 100 seconds. Estimate its distance to the epicenter.

<details><summary>Show Answer</summary>

**≈800 km** (8 × 100 = 800).

Applying the established ratio to a new data point — a common "apply the
pattern" follow-up question type on ACT Science.
</details>

---

**Q4.** Stations X, Y, and Z from Q2 each draw a circle around their
location with radius equal to their calculated distance. All three circles
are found to intersect at a single point. What does that point represent?

<details><summary>Show Answer</summary>

**The earthquake's epicenter.**

This is the triangulation method directly — three or more distance circles
narrowing down to one intersection point pinpoints the epicenter, since a
single circle only constrains distance, not direction.
</details>

---

## Answer Key Summary

1. ≈32,768×  2. Distance ≈ 8 × time delay  3. ≈800 km  4. The epicenter
