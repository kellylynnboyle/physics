## Instructor Mastery Rubric

| Level | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **Emerging (1)** | Confuses magnitude and intensity; cannot distinguish P-waves and S-waves. |
| **Developing (2)** | Distinguishes wave types and focus/epicenter with support; struggles explaining triangulation or the logarithmic scale. |
| **Proficient (3)** | Independently explains triangulation, correctly distinguishes magnitude from intensity, and identifies fault types from descriptions; scores ≥80% on unit quiz. |
| **Advanced (4)** | Explains why the magnitude scale is logarithmic with correct energy-ratio estimates; connects fault types to plate boundary mechanisms from Unit 10; scores ≥90% on unit quiz. |

## Gradable Evidence Checklist

- [ ] Unit quiz score recorded (target ≥80%)
- [ ] At least 3 practice/analysis problems shown with full reasoning
- [ ] Cornell notes cue-column self-quiz completed
- [ ] One real-world application explained in the student's own words
- [ ] A triangulation diagram (three intersecting circles) drawn and explained

## Suggested Exit Ticket (3 questions, 5 minutes)

1. Explain the difference between an earthquake's magnitude and its intensity.
2. Why do you need at least three seismograph stations to locate an epicenter?
3. A magnitude 4 earthquake and a magnitude 6 earthquake occur. Roughly how much more energy does the magnitude 6 quake release?
