## Instructor Mastery Rubric

| Level | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **Emerging (1)** | Treats horizontal and vertical motion as connected (e.g., thinks faster launch = faster fall); cannot decompose velocity into components. |
| **Developing (2)** | Can decompose velocity with a formula sheet; solves horizontal-launch problems but struggles with angled-launch (time of flight, max height, range) without heavy scaffolding. |
| **Proficient (3)** | Independently solves both horizontal-launch and angled-launch problems; correctly explains why horizontal speed doesn't affect fall time; scores ≥80% on unit quiz. |
| **Advanced (4)** | Solves projectiles that land at a different height than launch (asymmetric trajectories); explains complementary-angle range equality and where max range comes from; scores ≥90% on unit quiz. |

## Gradable Evidence Checklist

- [ ] Unit quiz score recorded (target ≥80%)
- [ ] At least 3 practice problems shown with full work
- [ ] Cornell notes cue-column self-quiz completed
- [ ] One real-world application explained in the student's own words
- [ ] PhET "Projectile Motion" sim used to test at least one claim (e.g., mass independence or 45° max range) and result recorded

## Suggested Exit Ticket (3 questions, 5 minutes)

1. A ball rolls off a table at 3 m/s and the table is 1.2 m tall. How long is it in the air, and how far from the table does it land?
2. Explain in your own words why a bullet fired horizontally and a bullet dropped at the same instant, from the same height, hit the ground simultaneously.
3. At what angle should you launch a projectile (on level ground) for maximum range, and why do 30° and 60° give the same range?
