## Practice Problems

1. A soccer ball is kicked at 18 m/s at a 40° angle. Find the time of flight, max height, and range. (sin40°≈0.64, cos40°≈0.77)
2. A stunt car drives horizontally off a 45 m cliff at 22 m/s. How far from the cliff base does it land, and what is its landing speed (magnitude)?
3. An arrow is shot at 50 m/s to achieve maximum range on level ground. What angle should the archer use, and what is the range? (g = 9.8 m/s²)
4. A ball thrown at 25 m/s lands 3 seconds later at the same height it was launched. What angle was it thrown at?
5. Compare the range of a projectile launched at 20° vs. 70° at the same speed. Explain your result using the complementary-angle idea from Q3 of the quiz.

<details><summary>Show Answers</summary>

1. Vy₀=18(0.64)=11.5 m/s, Vₓ=18(0.77)=13.9 m/s. t=(2×11.5)/9.8≈2.35 s. H=(11.5²)/(2×9.8)≈6.75 m. R=Vₓ×t≈13.9×2.35≈32.7 m
2. Fall time: t=√(2×45/9.8)≈3.03 s. Range: x=22×3.03≈66.6 m. Landing Vy=g×t≈29.7 m/s; speed=√(22²+29.7²)≈37.0 m/s
3. Max range at 45°. R=V₀²/g=50²/9.8≈255 m
4. Time up = time down = 1.5 s each. Vy₀=g×1.5=14.7 m/s. sinθ=14.7/25=0.588 → θ≈36°
5. Equal ranges (20° and 70° are complementary); 70° launch has a much higher, longer arc while 20° is flatter and faster to land.

## Real-World Applications

- **Sports:** basketball free throws, football punts, and golf drives are all optimized using projectile motion — coaches and engineers analyze launch angle vs. distance.
- **Military/ballistics history:** the mathematics of projectile motion was originally developed to predict cannonball trajectories (Galileo, 17th century).
- **Firefighting:** aiming water cannons/hoses at a target uses the same range and height equations.
- **Space debris & orbital mechanics (extension):** the same "independent horizontal/vertical motion" idea, extended to a curved Earth, is the conceptual basis for how satellites stay in orbit ("falling and missing the ground").
