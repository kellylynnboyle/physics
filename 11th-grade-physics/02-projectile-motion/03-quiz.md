# Unit 2: Projectile Motion — Interactive Quiz (Most-Missed Questions)

Take this quiz without notes. 8/10 (80%) to advance to Unit 3.

---

**Q1.** A bullet is fired perfectly horizontally from a gun at the same
instant a second bullet is simply dropped from the same height. Which hits
the ground first?

A) The fired bullet, because it's faster
B) The dropped bullet, because it goes straight down
C) They hit at the same time
D) It depends on the gun's muzzle velocity

<details><summary>Show Answer</summary>

**C — they hit at the same time.**

This is the single most-missed idea in projectile motion. Both bullets have
the same initial vertical velocity (zero) and the same vertical
acceleration (-g). Horizontal motion is completely independent and doesn't
affect how fast something falls.
</details>

---

**Q2.** A ball is launched at an angle above the horizontal. At the very top
of its trajectory, which of the following is true?

A) Both Vₓ and Vy are zero
B) Vₓ is zero, Vy is at its maximum
C) Vy is zero, Vₓ is unchanged from launch
D) Both Vₓ and Vy are at their maximum

<details><summary>Show Answer</summary>

**C.**

Only vertical velocity changes during flight (due to gravity); horizontal
velocity is constant throughout, including at the peak. Students very often
pick A, thinking "top of the arc = stopped," but the projectile is still
moving forward at full horizontal speed.
</details>

---

**Q3.** Two projectiles are launched at 30° and 60° from the horizontal, at
the same initial speed, on level ground. How do their ranges compare?

A) The 60° launch goes farther
B) The 30° launch goes farther
C) They land at the same distance
D) Not enough information

<details><summary>Show Answer</summary>

**C — they land at the same distance.**

30° and 60° are complementary angles (they add to 90°), and complementary
launch angles at the same speed always produce equal range on level ground
(from R = V₀²sin(2θ)/g — sin(60°) = sin(120°)). Maximum range specifically
happens at 45°, but 30°/60° tie each other, just with different max
heights and flight times.
</details>

---

**Q4.** A rock is thrown horizontally at 8 m/s from a bridge 20 m above a
river. How long does it take to hit the water? (g = 9.8 m/s²)

<details><summary>Show Answer</summary>

**≈2.02 s.**

Only the vertical motion determines fall time: y = ½gt² → 20 = ½(9.8)t² →
t² ≈ 4.08 → t ≈ 2.02 s. The 8 m/s horizontal speed is irrelevant to *how
long* it falls — a very common error is trying to use 8 m/s in the fall-time
calculation.
</details>

---

**Q5.** Using the answer from Q4, how far from the base of the bridge does
the rock land?

<details><summary>Show Answer</summary>

**≈16.2 m.**

x = Vₓ · t = 8 × 2.02 ≈ 16.2 m. This step uses the constant horizontal
velocity — no acceleration involved horizontally.
</details>

---

**Q6.** True or False: Air resistance is ignored in these projectile
problems, so a bowling ball and a beach ball launched identically will
follow identical trajectories.

<details><summary>Show Answer</summary>

**True, within the idealized model used in this course.**

In the idealized "no air resistance" model, trajectory depends only on
launch speed, angle, and g — not on mass or shape. In reality, air
resistance affects light, large-surface-area objects like a beach ball far
more, but that's beyond this unit's model (mentioned here so you know the
idealization, not so you assume it's always true in the real world).
</details>

---

**Q7.** A projectile is launched at angle θ with speed V₀. Which expression
correctly gives the vertical component of initial velocity?

A) V₀cos(θ)
B) V₀sin(θ)
C) V₀tan(θ)
D) V₀/sin(θ)

<details><summary>Show Answer</summary>

**B — V₀sin(θ).**

Common mix-up: students swap sine and cosine. Memory trick: at θ = 0°
(perfectly horizontal launch), there should be *no* vertical component —
sin(0°) = 0, which checks out for B (and cos(0°) = 1 correctly gives all
speed to the horizontal component in A).
</details>

---

**Q8.** For a projectile launched and landing at the same height, how does
the time to reach maximum height compare to the time to fall back down from
max height?

A) Time up is longer
B) Time down is longer
C) They're equal
D) Depends on the angle

<details><summary>Show Answer</summary>

**C — they're equal (symmetric trajectory).**

This symmetry only holds when launch and landing heights are the same. It
breaks down for "launched from a height, lands lower" problems (like Q4),
where the fall phase takes longer than the rise phase.
</details>

---

**Q9.** A student says: "Since gravity acts on the projectile, its
horizontal velocity must be decreasing throughout the flight." What's wrong
with this statement?

<details><summary>Show Answer</summary>

Gravity is a purely vertical force (near Earth's surface, no air
resistance) — it changes only Vy, never Vₓ. Horizontal velocity stays
exactly constant for the entire flight. The student is incorrectly applying
the vertical effect of gravity to the horizontal direction.
</details>

---

**Q10.** A football is kicked at 20 m/s at 37° above horizontal
(sin 37°≈0.60, cos 37°≈0.80). Find Vₓ and Vy at launch.

<details><summary>Show Answer</summary>

**Vₓ = 16 m/s, Vy = 12 m/s.**

Vₓ = 20(0.80) = 16 m/s. Vy = 20(0.60) = 12 m/s. Common error: swapping
which trig function pairs with which axis (see Q7).
</details>
