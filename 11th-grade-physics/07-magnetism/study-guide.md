# Magnetism — Quick-Reference Study Guide

## Definitions

- **Magnetic field (B)** — field around magnets and moving charges/currents, measured in Tesla (T)
- **Magnetic pole** — north/south ends of a magnet; like poles repel, opposite attract; no monopoles exist
- **Electromagnet** — a magnet created by current flowing through a coiled wire
- **Electromagnetic induction** — generating current by *changing* magnetic flux through a loop
- **Solenoid** — a coil of wire producing a strong, uniform field inside when current flows

## Formulas

- Force on a moving charge: F = qvB sin(θ)
- Force on a current-carrying wire: F = BIL sin(θ)
- Right-hand rule (charge/wire force): fingers = v, curl toward B, thumb = F
- Right-hand rule (field around a wire): thumb = current, curling fingers = B direction
- Right-hand rule (solenoid): curl fingers with current flow, thumb = north pole

## Quick-Reference Table

| Situation | Key fact |
|---|---|
| Charge moving through B field | Force is always perpendicular to velocity — changes direction, never speed |
| Charge at rest in B field | Zero force — motion is required |
| Negative charge | Force direction flips 180° compared to a positive charge in the same situation |
| Stationary magnet, stationary loop | No induced current — flux isn't changing |
| Moving magnet or changing field | Induces current — flux IS changing |
| Cutting a magnet in half | Produces two magnets, each with its own N and S — never an isolated pole |

## Practice Problems

1. An electron moves at 3×10⁶ m/s perpendicular to a 0.2 T field. Find the force magnitude. (q=1.6×10⁻¹⁹ C)
2. A wire carries 6 A, is 0.4 m long, perpendicular to a 0.3 T field. Find the force.
3. Two magnets are placed north-to-south (opposite poles facing). Attract or repel?
4. A charge moves parallel to a magnetic field (θ=0°). What is the force on it?
5. A student moves a magnet quickly through a coil, then holds it still inside the coil. When is current induced?
6. A solenoid's current flows clockwise viewed from the right end. Which end is the north pole?

<details><summary>Show Answers</summary>

1. F=qvB=(1.6×10⁻¹⁹)(3×10⁶)(0.2)=9.6×10⁻¹⁴ N
2. F=BIL=(0.3)(6)(0.4)=0.72 N
3. Attract (opposite poles)
4. Zero — sin(0°)=0, so F=qvB sin(θ)=0
5. Only while the magnet is moving through the coil; current stops once it's held still (flux is no longer changing)
6. Left end (curl right hand clockwise as viewed from the right end — thumb points left, out of the left end)
</details>
