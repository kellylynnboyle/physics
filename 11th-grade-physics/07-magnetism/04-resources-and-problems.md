## Practice Problems

1. A proton (q=1.6×10⁻¹⁹ C) moves at 2×10⁶ m/s perpendicular to a 0.5 T magnetic field. Find the force on it.
2. A wire carrying 4 A of current, 0.3 m long, sits perpendicular to a 0.2 T field. Find the force on the wire.
3. Two bar magnets are placed with south pole facing south pole. Will they attract or repel?
4. A solenoid's current direction is reversed. What happens to its north and south poles?
5. A loop of wire is held stationary next to a magnet that is also stationary. A student says no current is induced; another says current is induced because the field is strong. Who's correct, and why?
6. Explain, using the right-hand rule, how to find the direction of the magnetic field 2 cm to the right of a straight wire carrying current upward.

<details><summary>Show Answers</summary>

1. F=qvB=(1.6×10⁻¹⁹)(2×10⁶)(0.5)=1.6×10⁻¹³ N
2. F=BIL=(0.2)(4)(0.3)=0.24 N
3. Repel (like poles repel)
4. They swap — the end that was north becomes south and vice versa
5. The first student is correct — induction requires *changing* flux, and nothing is changing here even though the field is strong
6. Point thumb along the current (upward); fingers curl around the wire — at a point to the right of the wire, the field points into the page (using the right-hand rule for a wire's circular field)
</details>

## Real-World Applications

- **Electric motors:** use the force on a current-carrying wire in a magnetic field (F=BIL) to convert electrical energy into rotational motion.
- **Generators:** the reverse process — moving a wire loop through a magnetic field induces current, converting mechanical energy into electrical energy (the basis of most power plants).
- **MRI machines:** use extremely strong magnetic fields to align and detect signals from hydrogen atoms in the body.
- **Maglev trains:** use powerful electromagnets to levitate and propel trains with no wheel friction.

## Extension / Challenge

- Research how a simple DC motor uses the right-hand rule and a commutator to keep spinning in one direction, even though the force on the wire loop would otherwise reverse every half-turn.
