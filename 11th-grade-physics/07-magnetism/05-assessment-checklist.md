## Instructor Mastery Rubric

| Level | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **Emerging (1)** | Cannot apply any version of the right-hand rule; believes magnetic force can change a charge's speed. |
| **Developing (2)** | Applies F=qvB or F=BIL with support; confuses the different right-hand-rule applications (force vs. wire field vs. solenoid). |
| **Proficient (3)** | Independently calculates force on moving charges and current-carrying wires; correctly applies the right-hand rule in context; explains why magnetic force does zero work; scores ≥80% on unit quiz. |
| **Advanced (4)** | Explains electromagnetic induction's flux-change requirement with examples; connects magnetism to circuits (motors/generators); scores ≥90% on unit quiz. |

## Gradable Evidence Checklist

- [ ] Unit quiz score recorded (target ≥80%)
- [ ] At least 3 practice problems shown with full work, including at least one right-hand-rule direction problem
- [ ] Cornell notes cue-column self-quiz completed
- [ ] One real-world application explained in the student's own words
- [ ] PhET "Faraday's Electromagnetic Lab" used to confirm induction requires motion/changing flux

## Suggested Exit Ticket (3 questions, 5 minutes)

1. A charge moves through a magnetic field and curves in a circle. Explain why its speed doesn't change.
2. A wire carries 2 A, is 0.5 m long, and sits perpendicular to a 0.4 T field. Find the force.
3. Why can't you create an isolated magnetic north pole by cutting a magnet in half?
