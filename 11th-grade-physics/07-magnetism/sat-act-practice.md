# Unit 7: Magnetism — SAT/ACT Practice

- **SAT Math** rarely touches magnetism directly (it's light on vector/cross-product reasoning), but occasionally frames F=BIL or F=qvB as **direct substitution**.
- **ACT Science** favors magnetism as **experiment-comparison** questions — usually a table of field strength vs. induced current, or force vs. one varied quantity.

## SAT Math Style

**Q1.** Using F = BIL, if B=0.5 T, I=4 A, and L=2 m, what is F?

<details><summary>Show Answer</summary>

**4 N.** F=(0.5)(4)(2)=4 N. Straightforward substitution.
</details>

---

**Q2.** If F = qvB and you double both q and v while keeping B constant,
by what factor does F change?

<details><summary>Show Answer</summary>

**4×.**

F ∝ qv, so doubling both multiplies F by 2×2=4. The most-missed error:
only accounting for one of the two doubled variables (getting 2× instead
of 4×).
</details>

## ACT Science Reasoning Style

**Q3.** A student moves a magnet through a coil at different speeds and
measures the induced current:

| Magnet speed (m/s) | Induced current (A) |
|---|---|
| 0.5 | 0.2 |
| 1.0 | 0.4 |
| 1.5 | 0.6 |
| 2.0 | 0.8 |

Based on this data, how would the induced current change if the magnet
were held completely still?

<details><summary>Show Answer</summary>

**It would drop to zero.**

The trend shows current is proportional to speed (current ∝ speed here),
and extrapolating that trend to speed=0 gives 0 A — consistent with the
core concept that induction requires *changing* flux (motion). This
matches Q4/Q6 from the unit quiz.
</details>

---

**Q4.** A second student repeats the experiment with a stronger magnet.
At every speed tested, the induced current is higher than in the first
experiment. What does this suggest about the relationship between magnetic
field strength and induced current?

<details><summary>Show Answer</summary>

**Stronger field → more induced current, for a given speed** (a positive relationship between field strength and induced current, holding speed constant).

This is a "compare two experiments, one varied factor" ACT Science
question — the key move is isolating what changed between experiments (the
magnet's strength) and describing the resulting trend without introducing
unsupported claims about the exact mathematical relationship (linear vs.
otherwise), since only two magnet strengths were tested.
</details>

---

**Q5.** True or False, based on the data in Q3: doubling the magnet's speed
always exactly doubles the induced current.

<details><summary>Show Answer</summary>

**True, for this data set** — 0.5→1.0 m/s: 0.2→0.4 A (doubled); 1.0→2.0
m/s: 0.4→0.8 A (doubled). Consistent direct proportionality throughout the
given range. The trap in similar ACT questions is applying "always" to
ranges *outside* the tested data (e.g., very high speeds) where the
relationship might not hold — but strictly within this table, the pattern
holds up every time it's checked.
</details>

---

## Answer Key Summary

1. 4 N  2. 4×  3. Drops to zero  4. Stronger field → more induced current at a given speed
5. True (within the tested range)
