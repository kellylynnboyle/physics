# Unit 9: Light — SAT/ACT Practice

- **SAT Math** rarely features optics directly, but Snell's Law occasionally
  appears as a **trig-substitution** problem.
- **ACT Science** frequently features **refraction/reflection data tables**
  (angle in vs. angle out for different materials) and diagrams of light
  bending through lenses — testing data reading over formula recall.

## SAT Math Style

**Q1.** Using Snell's Law n₁sin(θ₁)=n₂sin(θ₂), if n₁=1, θ₁=30° (sin30°=0.5),
and n₂=2, find sin(θ₂).

<details><summary>Show Answer</summary>

**0.25.**

(1)(0.5) = 2·sin(θ₂) → sin(θ₂) = 0.25. Pure algebraic substitution — the
trig values are given so no calculator/table lookup is needed.
</details>

## ACT Science Reasoning Style

**Q2.** A student measures angle of refraction for light entering different
materials from air, all at the same 40° angle of incidence:

| Material | Angle of refraction |
|---|---|
| Water | 29° |
| Glass | 25° |
| Diamond | 16° |

Based on this data, which material has the highest index of refraction?

<details><summary>Show Answer</summary>

**Diamond.**

A smaller refraction angle (more bending, for the same incidence angle)
corresponds to a higher index of refraction — this can be read directly
from the trend without computing n explicitly: diamond bends light the
most, so it has the greatest optical density in this data set.
</details>

---

**Q3.** Based on the table in Q2, if a fourth material bent light to only
10° at the same 40° incidence, would you expect it to have a higher or
lower index of refraction than diamond?

<details><summary>Show Answer</summary>

**Higher.**

Following the same trend from Q2 (smaller refraction angle → higher index),
a 10° refraction angle (even smaller than diamond's 16°) means even
stronger bending, and thus an even higher index of refraction.
</details>

---

**Q4.** A diagram shows a light ray hitting a mirror and reflecting. The
angle between the incoming ray and the mirror's surface is measured as
70°. What is the angle of incidence (measured from the normal, as physics
requires)?

<details><summary>Show Answer</summary>

**20°.**

90° - 70° = 20°. This is exactly the "measure from the normal, not the
surface" trap from the unit quiz, dressed up as an ACT Science diagram
question — always convert a surface-angle measurement to a normal-angle
measurement before answering.
</details>

---

## Answer Key Summary

1. sin(θ₂)=0.25  2. Diamond  3. Higher  4. 20°
