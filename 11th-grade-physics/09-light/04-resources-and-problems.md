## Practice Problems

1. A light ray hits a mirror at an angle of incidence of 40°. What is the angle of reflection?
2. Light travels from water (n=1.33) into air (n=1.00) at an angle of incidence of 25° (sin25°≈0.42). Find the angle of refraction.
3. A red apple is viewed under white light, then under pure green light. Describe its appearance in each case.
4. A converging lens forms an image on a screen on the opposite side from the object. Is this image real or virtual?
5. Explain why the sky appears blue, using the idea that shorter wavelengths of light scatter more than longer wavelengths in the atmosphere.
6. A beam of white light passes through a prism. Which color bends the most: red or violet? (Hint: violet has a shorter wavelength and a slightly higher index of refraction in glass.)

<details><summary>Show Answers</summary>

1. 40° (angle of incidence always equals angle of reflection)
2. n₁sin(θ₁)=n₂sin(θ₂) → (1.33)(0.42)=(1.00)sin(θ₂) → sin(θ₂)≈0.559 → θ₂≈34°
3. Under white light: appears red (reflects red, absorbs other colors). Under pure green light: appears black/very dark (no red light available to reflect, and it absorbs green)
4. Real (it can be projected onto a screen)
5. Blue light has a shorter wavelength and scatters more strongly off air molecules than red/longer wavelengths, so more blue light reaches our eyes from all directions of the sky
6. Violet bends the most (shorter wavelength, higher index of refraction in glass — this is why violet is at the top or bottom edge of a rainbow depending on convention, and red bends the least)
</details>

## Real-World Applications

- **Eyeglasses and contact lenses:** converging/diverging lenses correct vision by adjusting where light focuses relative to the retina.
- **Fiber optics:** relies on total internal reflection (an extension of Snell's Law) to transmit light signals over long distances with minimal loss.
- **Rainbows:** sunlight refracting and reflecting inside water droplets separates into its component colors, the same physics as a prism.
- **Cameras:** converging lenses form real images onto a sensor or film, directly applying the real-image concept from this unit.

## Extension / Challenge

- Research total internal reflection and the "critical angle" — what happens when light tries to exit a denser medium at an angle greater than the critical angle, and why this makes fiber-optic cables work.
