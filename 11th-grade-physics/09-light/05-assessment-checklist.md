## Instructor Mastery Rubric

| Level | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **Emerging (1)** | Measures angles from the surface instead of the normal; believes light needs a medium to travel. |
| **Developing (2)** | Applies the law of reflection and Snell's Law with support; still confuses real and virtual images. |
| **Proficient (3)** | Independently solves reflection/refraction problems, correctly measuring from the normal; explains color as reflected wavelength; distinguishes real vs. virtual images; scores ≥80% on unit quiz. |
| **Advanced (4)** | Explains dispersion (why prisms separate colors) and diffraction/interference for light; connects concepts to real optical devices (cameras, fiber optics); scores ≥90% on unit quiz. |

## Gradable Evidence Checklist

- [ ] Unit quiz score recorded (target ≥80%)
- [ ] At least 3 practice problems shown with full work, including at least one Snell's Law problem
- [ ] Cornell notes cue-column self-quiz completed
- [ ] One real-world application explained in the student's own words
- [ ] PhET "Bending Light" used to verify Snell's Law at two different angles

## Suggested Exit Ticket (3 questions, 5 minutes)

1. A ray hits a mirror at 55° from the normal. What is the angle of reflection?
2. Explain why a blue object appears black under only red light.
3. Is the image in a bathroom mirror real or virtual? How do you know?
