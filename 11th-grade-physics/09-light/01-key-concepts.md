# Unit 9: Light — Key Concepts

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this unit, students will be able to:
1. Describe the electromagnetic spectrum and where visible light falls within it.
2. Apply the law of reflection and Snell's law of refraction.
3. Explain why objects appear colored (reflected vs. absorbed wavelengths).
4. Distinguish real and virtual images formed by mirrors and lenses.
5. Explain diffraction and interference of light (e.g., double-slit).
6. Explain why light doesn't need a medium to travel, unlike sound.

## Vocabulary

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Electromagnetic spectrum | The full range of EM waves, from radio to gamma rays; visible light is a small band within it |
| Law of reflection | Angle of incidence = angle of reflection, both measured from the normal (a line perpendicular to the surface) |
| Refraction | Bending of light as it passes between media of different optical density |
| Index of refraction (n) | A measure of how much a medium slows/bends light; n = c/v |
| Snell's Law | n₁sin(θ₁) = n₂sin(θ₂) |
| Converging lens | Bends parallel light rays toward a focal point (convex lens) |
| Diverging lens | Spreads parallel light rays apart (concave lens) |
| Real image | Formed by actual convergence of light rays; can be projected on a screen |
| Virtual image | Formed by the apparent (not actual) intersection of light rays; cannot be projected |
| Diffraction | Bending of light around obstacles or through narrow openings |

## Core Formulas

- Snell's Law: n₁sin(θ₁) = n₂sin(θ₂)
- Index of refraction: n = c/v (c = speed of light in vacuum ≈ 3×10⁸ m/s)
- Law of reflection: θ_incidence = θ_reflection (both measured from the normal)

## The Big Idea

**All angles in reflection and refraction are measured from the normal — a
line perpendicular to the surface — not from the surface itself.** This one
convention trips up more students than any formula in the unit. Get the
normal right, and both the law of reflection and Snell's Law become simple
plug-and-check problems.

## Common Misconceptions to Address Directly

- Measuring angles of incidence/reflection/refraction from the surface
  instead of the normal (the single most common error in this unit).
- Believing light needs a medium to travel, like sound does (it doesn't —
  light is an electromagnetic wave and travels through vacuum; this is why
  sunlight reaches Earth through the vacuum of space).
- Believing an object's color is an inherent property rather than the
  wavelength(s) of light it reflects (a "red" apple reflects red
  wavelengths and absorbs the others; under only blue light, that same
  apple would appear black, since there's no red light to reflect).
- Confusing real and virtual images — assuming any image you can "see" must
  be real, when a virtual image (like your reflection in a flat mirror) is
  just as visible but cannot be projected onto a screen.
- Believing all colors of light travel at different speeds *in vacuum*
  (they don't — all EM waves travel at c in vacuum; different speeds for
  different colors only happens inside a medium, which is what causes a
  prism to separate white light into colors).
