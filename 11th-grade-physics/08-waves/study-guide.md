# Waves — Quick-Reference Study Guide

## Definitions

- **Wavelength (λ)** — distance between two consecutive identical points on a wave
- **Frequency (f)** — number of complete waves passing a point per second (Hz)
- **Period (T)** — time for one complete cycle, T = 1/f
- **Amplitude** — maximum displacement from equilibrium; relates to energy/intensity
- **Transverse wave** — particle motion perpendicular to travel direction (e.g., light)
- **Longitudinal wave** — particle motion parallel to travel direction (e.g., sound)
- **Resonance** — large-amplitude oscillation from driving at the natural frequency

## Formulas

- v = fλ
- T = 1/f

## Quick-Reference Table

| Situation | Key fact |
|---|---|
| Wave crosses into a new medium | Frequency stays the same; speed and wavelength change |
| Frequency doubles, same medium | Wavelength halves (v stays constant) |
| Amplitude | Affects energy/intensity, NOT speed |
| Crest meets crest | Constructive interference |
| Crest meets trough | Destructive interference |
| A wave passing through a medium | Transports energy, not matter |

## Practice Problems

1. A wave has wavelength 4 m and frequency 3 Hz. Find its speed.
2. A wave travels at 15 m/s with a period of 0.5 s. Find its wavelength.
3. A sound wave's frequency triples while staying in the same medium. What happens to its wavelength?
4. Classify: a slinky pushed back and forth along its length. Transverse or longitudinal?
5. Two waves overlap and produce a point of zero displacement. What kind of interference is this?
6. A wave's period is 0.1 s. Find its frequency.

<details><summary>Show Answers</summary>

1. v=fλ=3×4=12 m/s
2. λ=vT=15×0.5=7.5 m
3. Wavelength is cut to 1/3 (v constant, f triples → λ divides by 3)
4. Longitudinal
5. Destructive interference
6. f=1/T=1/0.1=10 Hz
</details>
