# Unit 8: Waves — Key Concepts

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this unit, students will be able to:
1. Define wavelength, frequency, period, amplitude, and wave speed, and relate them with v=fλ.
2. Distinguish transverse and longitudinal waves with examples.
3. Explain that waves transport energy, not matter.
4. Describe reflection, refraction, and diffraction of waves.
5. Apply the principle of superposition to predict constructive and destructive interference.
6. Explain resonance and standing waves.

## Vocabulary

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Wavelength (λ) | Distance between two consecutive identical points on a wave (e.g., crest to crest) |
| Frequency (f) | Number of complete waves passing a point per second, measured in Hertz (Hz) |
| Period (T) | Time for one complete wave cycle, T = 1/f |
| Amplitude | Maximum displacement from equilibrium; related to a wave's energy/intensity |
| Transverse wave | Particle motion is perpendicular to the direction of wave travel (e.g., light, waves on a string) |
| Longitudinal wave | Particle motion is parallel to the direction of wave travel (e.g., sound) |
| Superposition | Overlapping waves add their displacements at each point |
| Constructive interference | Waves combine to create a larger amplitude (crest meets crest) |
| Destructive interference | Waves combine to cancel out (crest meets trough) |
| Resonance | Large-amplitude oscillation when driven at a system's natural frequency |
| Standing wave | A wave pattern that appears stationary, formed by interference of two waves traveling in opposite directions |

## Core Formulas

- Wave speed: v = fλ
- Period-frequency relationship: T = 1/f
- Wave speed is set by the medium (for a given medium, v is constant regardless of f or λ)

## The Big Idea

**A wave transports energy from one place to another without transporting
matter.** The medium's particles oscillate around a fixed position — they
don't travel along with the wave. When you doubled a wave's frequency,
its wavelength must shrink to keep v=fλ constant for the same medium — f
and λ trade off against each other, but the wave's speed (set by the
medium's properties) doesn't change just because you shook the source
faster.

## Common Misconceptions to Address Directly

- Believing a wave carries matter along with it (a floating cork bobs up
  and down as a water wave passes but doesn't travel with the wave — only
  energy propagates outward).
- Believing frequency changes when a wave crosses into a new medium (it
  doesn't — frequency is set by the *source* and stays constant; wavelength
  and speed are what change when the medium changes).
- Confusing amplitude with wavelength (amplitude = how "tall" the wave is /
  its energy; wavelength = how "long" one cycle is).
- Believing louder/brighter waves travel faster (amplitude relates to
  energy/intensity, not speed — speed depends only on the medium).
- Confusing constructive/destructive interference with "waves colliding and
  stopping" — interference is temporary and the waves pass through each
  other unchanged afterward (unlike particle collisions).
