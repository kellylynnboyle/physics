## Practice Problems

1. A wave has a wavelength of 3 m and travels at 12 m/s. Find its frequency.
2. A wave source vibrates at 8 Hz, producing waves that travel at 24 m/s in a rope. Find the wavelength.
3. If the same rope's tension is increased (making waves travel faster) while the source frequency stays the same, what happens to the wavelength?
4. A sound wave with a period of 0.002 s travels through air at 340 m/s. Find its frequency and wavelength.
5. Two identical wave sources are placed so their waves overlap. At one point, the path difference results in a crest meeting a crest. What kind of interference occurs there?
6. A playground swing has a natural frequency of 0.5 Hz. At what push rate should you push it to build the largest amplitude?

<details><summary>Show Answers</summary>

1. f=v/λ=12/3=4 Hz
2. λ=v/f=24/8=3 m
3. Wavelength increases (since v=fλ, and f is fixed by the source, faster v requires longer λ)
4. f=1/T=1/0.002=500 Hz. λ=v/f=340/500=0.68 m
5. Constructive interference
6. 0.5 Hz (push at the same rate as the natural frequency — resonance)
</details>

## Real-World Applications

- **Musical instruments:** standing waves on strings (guitar) or in air columns (flute) determine pitch — length change shifts which frequencies resonate.
- **Noise-canceling headphones:** generate a wave that's the inverse of ambient noise, using destructive interference to cancel it out.
- **Earthquake-resistant building design:** engineers avoid designing structures with a natural frequency close to typical earthquake frequencies, to prevent dangerous resonance.
- **Medical ultrasound:** uses high-frequency sound waves (longitudinal) reflecting off tissue boundaries to create images.
