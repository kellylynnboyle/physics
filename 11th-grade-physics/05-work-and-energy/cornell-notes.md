# Cornell Notes — Work & Energy

**Topic:** Work, Energy, and Power
**Date:** ______________          **Name:** ______________

| Cues / Questions | Notes |
|---|---|
| What is the formula for work, and what does θ mean? | `W = F·d·cos(θ)`, where θ is the angle BETWEEN the force direction and the displacement direction. Work is a scalar (no direction of its own), but can be positive, negative, or zero. |
| When is work equal to zero, even if a force is applied? | (1) When force is perpendicular to displacement (θ = 90°, cos = 0) — e.g., carrying something at constant height. (2) When there's no displacement at all — e.g., pushing on an immovable wall. |
| When is work negative? | When the force has a component OPPOSITE the displacement (θ between 90° and 180°) — e.g., friction acting on a sliding object, or air resistance. |
| State the work-energy theorem. | `W_net = ΔKE`. The total (net) work done on an object equals its change in kinetic energy — this connects force-based and energy-based problem solving. |
| What is kinetic energy, and why does doubling speed matter so much? | `KE = ½mv²`. Because velocity is squared, doubling speed QUADRUPLES kinetic energy (not just doubles it) — this is why higher speeds are disproportionately dangerous in collisions. |
| What are the two potential energy formulas covered here? | Gravitational: `PE_grav = mgh` (h measured from a chosen reference point). Elastic (spring): `PE_spring = ½kx²` (x = displacement from natural length). |
| State conservation of mechanical energy — and its limit. | `KE_i + PE_i = KE_f + PE_f` ONLY holds when no non-conservative force (friction, air resistance) does work. If friction is present: `KE_i + PE_i = KE_f + PE_f + Energy_lost_to_friction`. |
| How is power different from work/energy? | `P = W/t` — power is the RATE of doing work (watts = J/s). Two systems can transfer the same total energy, but the one that does it faster has more power. Power ≠ energy. |
| Common trap: does "conservation of energy" mean total mechanical energy is always the same? | No — total energy (including heat) is always conserved, but MECHANICAL energy (KE + PE) specifically is only conserved when friction/air resistance do zero work. Otherwise mechanical energy decreases as some converts to heat. |

## Summary
Work links force and displacement through the angle between them —
the most common trap is assuming any applied force automatically does
positive work, when perpendicular or zero-displacement cases give zero,
and opposing forces give negative work. The work-energy theorem
(`W_net = ΔKE`) and conservation of mechanical energy
(`KE_i + PE_i = KE_f + PE_f`, only without friction) are two versions of
the same bookkeeping idea. Power measures the *rate* of energy transfer,
not the amount — never treat higher power as automatically "more energy,"
only "faster."
