## Practice Problems

1. A 60 kg hiker climbs a hill, gaining 150 m of elevation. How much work is done against gravity?
2. A 1000 kg car slows from 30 m/s to 10 m/s. Using the work-energy theorem, find the net work done on the car (and its sign).
3. A pendulum bob (frictionless, ignore air resistance) swings from a height of 0.8 m to the bottom of its arc. Find its speed at the bottom.
4. A 1500 W motor lifts a load at constant speed for 8 seconds, doing 9000 J of work. Is the motor operating at its full rated power for that time? Explain.
5. A 3 kg box slides 4 m down a frictionless 30° incline, starting from rest. Find its speed at the bottom two ways: (a) using conservation of energy with the vertical drop, (b) using kinematics from Unit 1 with a = g sin(30°). Confirm they agree.

<details><summary>Show Answers</summary>

1. W = mgh = 60×9.8×150 = 88,200 J
2. Wnet = ΔKE = ½(1000)(10²) - ½(1000)(30²) = 50,000 - 450,000 = -400,000 J (negative, since the car slowed down — net work removed kinetic energy)
3. v=√(2gh)=√(2×9.8×0.8)=√15.68≈3.96 m/s
4. Power used = W/t = 9000/8 = 1125 W, which is less than the 1500 W rating — so no, it's not running at full rated power during that interval.
5. (a) Vertical drop h = 4sin(30°)=2 m. v=√(2×9.8×2)=√39.2≈6.26 m/s. (b) a=9.8×0.5=4.9 m/s²; v²=0+2(4.9)(4)=39.2 → v≈6.26 m/s. They match — same physics, two methods.

## Real-World Applications

- **Hydroelectric dams:** gravitational PE of stored water converts to KE, then to electrical energy — a direct, large-scale application of PE→KE→other-energy conversion.
- **Roller coasters:** entirely designed around conservation of mechanical energy; the first hill must be the tallest point because no motor adds energy after the initial lift.
- **Braking systems:** kinetic energy converts to heat via friction in traditional brakes; regenerative brakes (electric/hybrid vehicles) instead convert KE into stored electrical energy.
- **Household appliances/electricity bills:** power ratings (Watts) on appliances directly determine energy cost, since Energy = Power × time is literally how utility companies calculate a kWh bill.
