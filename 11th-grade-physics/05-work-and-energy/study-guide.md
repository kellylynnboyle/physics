# Work & Energy — Quick-Reference Study Guide

## Definitions

- **Work (W)** — W = Fd cos(θ); energy transferred by a force acting through a displacement; zero if force is perpendicular to displacement, or if there's no displacement
- **Kinetic energy (KE)** — KE = ½mv², energy of motion
- **Gravitational potential energy (PEg)** — PEg = mgh, stored energy due to height
- **Mechanical energy (ME)** — ME = KE + PE
- **Conservative force** — work done doesn't depend on path, only start/end position (e.g. gravity)
- **Non-conservative force** — removes mechanical energy from a system as heat/sound (e.g. friction)
- **Power (P)** — P = W/t = Fv, rate of doing work, measured in Watts (J/s)

## Formulas

- W = Fd cos(θ)
- KE = ½mv²
- PEg = mgh
- Work-Energy Theorem: Wnet = ΔKE = KEf - KEi
- Conservation of Mechanical Energy (no friction): KEi + PEi = KEf + PEf
- With friction: KEi + PEi = KEf + PEf + Wfriction
- Power: P = W/t = Fv

## Quick-Reference Table

| Situation | Key fact |
|---|---|
| Holding something stationary | W = 0 (no displacement), no matter how much force is applied |
| Force perpendicular to displacement | W = 0 (cos 90° = 0) |
| Frictionless system | Mechanical energy (KE+PE) is conserved |
| System with friction | Mechanical energy decreases; energy converts to heat, total energy still conserved |
| Same work, less time | Higher power (P = W/t) |
| Falling object, no air resistance | v = √(2gh), independent of mass |

## Practice Problems

1. A 50 kg hiker climbs 120 m of elevation. How much work is done against gravity?
2. A 900 kg car slows from 25 m/s to 5 m/s. Find the net work done on the car (with sign).
3. A pendulum (frictionless) swings from a height of 0.6 m to the bottom of its arc. Find its speed there.
4. A crate is pushed 8 m with 35 N applied force while 12 N of friction opposes it. Find the net work done.
5. A 1200 W motor lifts a load, doing 7200 J of work in 8 seconds. Is it running at full rated power? Explain.
6. A 4 kg block slides 5 m down a frictionless 20° incline from rest. Find its speed at the bottom.
