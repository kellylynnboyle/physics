## Instructor Mastery Rubric

| Level | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **Emerging (1)** | Equates "work" with general effort; cannot compute KE/PE; doesn't know when mechanical energy is/isn't conserved. |
| **Developing (2)** | Computes W, KE, PE with formulas given but struggles applying cos(θ) for angled forces; needs prompting to choose between work-energy theorem and conservation of energy. |
| **Proficient (3)** | Independently solves frictionless conservation-of-energy problems and general work-energy-theorem problems with friction; correctly identifies when W=0 despite force/motion; scores ≥80% on unit quiz. |
| **Advanced (4)** | Solves multi-step problems combining energy and kinematics/Newton's laws (e.g., incline problems both ways, as in practice problem 5); explains power vs. energy distinction clearly; scores ≥90% on unit quiz. |

## Gradable Evidence Checklist

- [ ] Unit quiz score recorded (target ≥80%)
- [ ] At least 3 practice problems shown with full work
- [ ] Cornell notes cue-column self-quiz completed
- [ ] One real-world application explained in the student's own words
- [ ] PhET "Energy Skate Park" used with friction on and off, and the KE/PE/thermal bar-graph behavior described in writing

## Suggested Exit Ticket (3 questions, 5 minutes)

1. A 2 kg object falls from 10 m. Find its speed just before landing (frictionless/no air resistance).
2. Explain why carrying a backpack horizontally at constant height, across a flat field, involves zero work done against gravity — even though you get tired.
3. Two workers lift identical boxes to the same height, but Worker A takes twice as long as Worker B. Compare their work done and their power output.
