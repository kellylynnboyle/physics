# Unit 5: Work & Energy — Key Concepts

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this unit, students will be able to:
1. Calculate work done by a constant force, including when force and displacement aren't parallel.
2. Calculate kinetic energy and gravitational potential energy.
3. Apply the work-energy theorem (net work = change in kinetic energy).
4. Apply conservation of mechanical energy to frictionless systems.
5. Account for energy "lost" to friction/non-conservative forces using the work-energy theorem in its general form.
6. Calculate power as the rate of energy transfer or work done.

## Vocabulary

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Work (W) | W = F·d·cos(θ) — energy transferred by a force acting through a displacement; zero if force is perpendicular to displacement, or if there's no displacement |
| Kinetic energy (KE) | KE = ½mv² — energy of motion |
| Gravitational potential energy (PE or Ug) | PEg = mgh — stored energy due to height in a gravitational field |
| Mechanical energy (ME) | ME = KE + PE — total of kinetic and potential energy in a system |
| Conservative force | A force (like gravity) where work done doesn't depend on path — only start/end position; associated with potential energy |
| Non-conservative force | A force (like friction) that removes mechanical energy from a system, converting it to heat/sound; work done DOES depend on path |
| Power (P) | P = W/t = Fv — rate at which work is done or energy is transferred, measured in Watts (J/s) |

## Core Formulas

- W = Fd cos(θ)
- KE = ½mv²
- PEg = mgh
- Work-Energy Theorem: Wnet = ΔKE = KEf - KEi
- Conservation of Mechanical Energy (no friction/air resistance): KEi + PEi = KEf + PEf
- With friction present: KEi + PEi = KEf + PEf + Wfriction (energy "lost" to friction = Wfriction, as heat)
- Power: P = W/t = Fv (for constant force in the direction of motion)

## The Big Idea

**Energy isn't created or destroyed — it changes form or transfers between
objects, and the total in a closed system (including heat/sound "lost" to
friction) is always conserved.** In a frictionless system, mechanical
energy (KE + PE) alone stays constant, which is why a roller coaster's
speed at any point can be found purely from its height, without knowing the
shape of the track.

## Common Misconceptions to Address Directly

- Believing "no motion = no work," when really it's "no *displacement in the direction of the force* = no work" — e.g., holding a heavy box stationary does zero physics-work, even though it's tiring.
- Believing energy is "lost" (destroyed) to friction — it converts to heat/sound, not disappearing; total energy of the universe/system+surroundings is still conserved.
- Forgetting the cos(θ) factor in work — assuming W=Fd always, even for forces at an angle to displacement (e.g., carrying a bag horizontally does zero work against gravity, since gravity is vertical and displacement is horizontal, θ=90°, cos(90°)=0).
- Mixing up when to use conservation of mechanical energy (frictionless only) vs. the work-energy theorem in general form (works with friction, since Wnet includes the negative work done by friction).
- Believing more powerful always means "more energy transferred," rather than "energy transferred *faster*" — two motors can do the same total work, but the more powerful one does it in less time.
