## Instructor Mastery Rubric

| Level | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **Emerging (1)** | Believes kinetic energy is always conserved in collisions; cannot set up a conservation of momentum equation; ignores direction/sign of velocity. |
| **Developing (2)** | Solves simple same-direction, perfectly inelastic collisions with support; struggles with opposite-direction (sign) problems and impulse-force-time relationships. |
| **Proficient (3)** | Independently solves 1D elastic and inelastic collisions with correct signs; applies J=FΔt=Δp correctly; explains why momentum, not KE, is always conserved; scores ≥80% on unit quiz. |
| **Advanced (4)** | Solves recoil/explosion problems (system starts at rest, separates); can verify whether a collision is elastic by comparing KE before/after; scores ≥90% on unit quiz. |

## Gradable Evidence Checklist

- [ ] Unit quiz score recorded (target ≥80%)
- [ ] At least 3 practice problems shown with full work, including at least one opposite-direction collision
- [ ] Cornell notes cue-column self-quiz completed
- [ ] One real-world application explained in the student's own words
- [ ] PhET "Collision Lab" used to verify momentum conservation in at least one elastic and one inelastic trial

## Suggested Exit Ticket (3 questions, 5 minutes)

1. A 4 kg cart at 3 m/s collides and sticks to a stationary 2 kg cart. Find the final velocity.
2. Explain why an egg dropped onto a pillow is less likely to break than one dropped onto concrete, using the impulse-momentum theorem.
3. Is momentum conserved in every collision, or only elastic ones? What about kinetic energy?
