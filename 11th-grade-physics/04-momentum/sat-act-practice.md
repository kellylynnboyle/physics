# Unit 4: Momentum — SAT/ACT Practice

- **SAT Math** turns momentum problems into **linear equations with signed
  numbers** — the algebra is easy, the direction/sign bookkeeping is where
  points are lost.
- **ACT Science** turns it into **before/after collision data tables**,
  testing whether you can verify (or apply) conservation of momentum
  directly from measured values.

## SAT Math Style

**Q1.** A 2 kg object moving at 5 m/s has momentum p = mv. What is its momentum?

<details><summary>Show Answer</summary>

**10 kg·m/s.**

Direct substitution: p = 2×5 = 10 kg·m/s. Included here as a baseline — the
"most missed" version of this question adds a direction or a second object,
as in Q2 below.
</details>

---

**Q2.** Two objects move toward each other: a 3 kg object at 4 m/s (call
this direction positive) and a 2 kg object at -6 m/s. What is their total
momentum?

<details><summary>Show Answer</summary>

**0 kg·m/s.**

p_total = 3(4) + 2(-6) = 12 - 12 = 0. The single most common error on this
exact question type: forgetting to apply the negative sign to the
second object because the problem only *describes* the direction in
words ("moving toward each other") instead of giving an explicit sign —
students add 12+12=24 instead.
</details>

---

**Q3.** A 4 kg cart moving at 6 m/s collides and sticks to a stationary
cart of unknown mass m. If their combined velocity afterward is 2 m/s,
what is m?

<details><summary>Show Answer</summary>

**8 kg.**

4(6) + m(0) = (4+m)(2) → 24 = 8 + 2m → 2m = 16 → m = 8 kg. This is a
solve-for-the-unknown-variable version of the conservation of momentum
equation — algebraically it's just isolating m, but students often
mis-distribute the (4+m)(2) term, forgetting to multiply *both* 4 and m by 2.
</details>

## ACT Science Reasoning Style

**Q4.** A student collides two carts and records momentum before and after:

| Trial | Cart A momentum before (kg·m/s) | Cart B momentum before (kg·m/s) | Cart A momentum after | Cart B momentum after |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 10 | 0 | 6 | 4 |
| 2 | 8 | -2 | 3 | 3 |
| 3 | 12 | 0 | 5 | 6 |

Which trial's data is inconsistent with conservation of momentum?

<details><summary>Show Answer</summary>

**Trial 3.**

Trial 1: before total = 10+0=10; after = 6+4=10 ✓. Trial 2: before =
8+(-2)=6; after = 3+3=6 ✓. Trial 3: before = 12+0=12; after = 5+6=11 ✗ —
doesn't match. This is the classic ACT Science "spot the data that breaks
the pattern" question — you must check *every* trial's before/after sum,
not just assume the pattern from Trials 1–2 continues, since that's exactly
the trap.
</details>

---

**Q5.** In the table above, is any of the trials' collisions elastic? (You
would need kinetic energy data, not shown here, to fully confirm — but
based on momentum data alone, what can you say?)

<details><summary>Show Answer</summary>

**Momentum data alone cannot determine whether a collision is elastic — you would need kinetic energy (mass and velocity) before and after, not just momentum values.**

This targets the same misconception as the course quiz: elastic vs.
inelastic is a *kinetic energy* question, not a momentum question — a very
common ACT Science trap is presenting only momentum data and asking a
question that requires students to correctly say "not enough information"
rather than guessing based on the momentum numbers alone.
</details>

---

**Q6.** A student claims: "Since Trial 1 and Trial 3 both start with Cart B
at rest, they should behave identically." Based on the data, is this
claim supported?

<details><summary>Show Answer</summary>

**No — Trial 3's data is internally inconsistent (doesn't conserve momentum, per Q4), so it can't be validly compared to Trial 1 at all; also, even setting that aside, the two trials have different Cart A momentum values (10 vs. 12), so there's no reason to expect identical outcomes.**

This is an ACT Science "evaluate a claim against the data" question — the
correct move is to check the data itself before evaluating the reasoning,
which is why Q4 (checking consistency) matters as a first step.
</details>

---

## Answer Key Summary

1. 10 kg·m/s  2. 0 kg·m/s  3. 8 kg  4. Trial 3
5. Not enough info (need KE data)  6. No — not supported
