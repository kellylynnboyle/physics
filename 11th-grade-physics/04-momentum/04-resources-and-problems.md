## Practice Problems

1. A 2 kg cart moving at 5 m/s collides and sticks to a stationary 3 kg cart. Find the final velocity.
2. A 60 kg astronaut at rest in space throws a 5 kg tool at 8 m/s away from the ship. What is the astronaut's recoil velocity?
3. A tennis racket exerts an average force of 400 N on a 0.06 kg ball for 0.005 s. What is the ball's change in momentum? If it started at rest, what's its speed leaving the racket?
4. A 1500 kg car traveling at 15 m/s rear-ends a stationary 1500 kg car, and they lock bumpers (stick together). Find the common velocity after impact, then compare total KE before and after to confirm it's inelastic.
5. Two skaters (50 kg and 70 kg), initially at rest, push off from each other. If the 50 kg skater moves away at 3.5 m/s, find the 70 kg skater's velocity.

<details><summary>Show Answers</summary>

1. 2(5)+3(0)=(2+3)vf → 10=5vf → vf=2 m/s
2. 60(0)+5(0)=60(v)+5(8) → 0=60v+40 → v=-0.667 m/s (recoils opposite the tool's direction)
3. Δp=FΔt=400×0.005=2 kg·m/s. v=Δp/m=2/0.06≈33.3 m/s
4. 1500(15)+1500(0)=(3000)vf → vf=7.5 m/s. KE before=½(1500)(15²)=168,750 J. KE after=½(3000)(7.5²)=84,375 J — KE dropped by half, confirming inelastic.
5. 50(0)+70(0)=50(3.5)+70(v) → 0=175+70v → v=-2.5 m/s (opposite direction to the 50 kg skater)

## Real-World Applications

- **Car safety engineering:** crumple zones extend collision time (Δt) to reduce peak force on passengers, per J=FΔt — same principle as airbags.
- **Rocket propulsion:** exhaust gas expelled backward gives the rocket forward momentum, conserving total system momentum (rocket + expelled fuel).
- **Sports:** "following through" in a golf/tennis swing or a punch extends contact time to increase impulse (and thus final velocity/force) delivered.
- **Billiards/pool:** the classic real-world lab for 1D and 2D elastic/near-elastic collisions.

## Extension / Challenge

- Research 2D (angled) collisions and how conservation of momentum applies separately to the x- and y-components — this is a common next step in AP Physics or college-level mechanics.
