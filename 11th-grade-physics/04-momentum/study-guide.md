# Momentum — Quick-Reference Study Guide

## Definitions

- **Momentum (p)** — p = mv, a vector quantity combining mass and velocity
- **Impulse (J)** — J = FΔt, the change in momentum caused by a force acting over a time interval
- **Conservation of momentum** — in a closed system with no external net force, total momentum before = total momentum after
- **Elastic collision** — kinetic energy is conserved along with momentum (objects bounce apart)
- **Inelastic collision** — momentum is conserved but kinetic energy is not
- **Perfectly inelastic collision** — objects stick together and move as one after the collision

## Formulas

- p = mv
- Impulse-momentum theorem: J = FΔt = Δp = mΔv
- Conservation of momentum (two objects): m₁v₁ᵢ + m₂v₂ᵢ = m₁v₁f + m₂v₂f
- Perfectly inelastic collision: m₁v₁ᵢ + m₂v₂ᵢ = (m₁+m₂)vf

## Quick-Reference Table

| Situation | Key fact |
|---|---|
| Any collision (elastic or inelastic) | Momentum of the system is always conserved |
| Elastic collision only | Kinetic energy is also conserved |
| Inelastic collision | KE decreases (converts to heat/sound); check by comparing KE before/after |
| Same impulse, longer time | Smaller average force (airbags, padding, follow-through) |
| Opposite-direction motion | Assign opposite signs to velocity before adding momenta |
| System starts at rest, then separates (recoil) | Total momentum stays zero — pieces move in opposite directions |

## Practice Problems

1. A 4 kg cart moving at 6 m/s collides and sticks to a stationary 2 kg cart. Find the final velocity.
2. A 70 kg astronaut at rest in space throws a 4 kg tool at 10 m/s away from the ship. Find the astronaut's recoil velocity.
3. A tennis racket exerts 350 N of average force on a 0.06 kg ball for 0.004 s. Find the ball's change in momentum and its speed leaving the racket (starting from rest).
4. A 1200 kg car at 18 m/s rear-ends a stationary 1200 kg car and they lock together. Find the common velocity, then compare KE before and after.
5. Two skaters (40 kg and 60 kg) start at rest and push off each other. If the 40 kg skater moves at 4.5 m/s, find the 60 kg skater's velocity.
6. A 0.2 kg ball moving at 5 m/s hits a wall and bounces straight back at 5 m/s. Find its change in momentum.
