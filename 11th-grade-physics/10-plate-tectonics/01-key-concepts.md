# Unit 10: Plate Tectonics — Key Concepts

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this unit, students will be able to:
1. Describe Earth's layered structure (crust, mantle, core) and the lithosphere/asthenosphere.
2. Explain the evidence for continental drift and plate tectonics (fossil match-up, seafloor spreading, magnetic striping).
3. Distinguish convergent, divergent, and transform plate boundaries and their features.
4. Explain the mechanism driving plate motion (mantle convection).
5. Connect plate boundaries to the location of earthquakes, volcanoes, and mountain ranges.

## Vocabulary

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Lithosphere | Rigid outer layer of Earth (crust + uppermost mantle), broken into tectonic plates |
| Asthenosphere | Ductile, partially molten mantle layer beneath the lithosphere that plates move over |
| Convergent boundary | Plates move toward each other (subduction zones, mountain building) |
| Divergent boundary | Plates move apart (mid-ocean ridges, seafloor spreading, rift valleys) |
| Transform boundary | Plates slide past each other horizontally (e.g., San Andreas Fault) |
| Subduction | A denser plate sinks beneath a less dense plate at a convergent boundary |
| Seafloor spreading | New oceanic crust forms at mid-ocean ridges as plates diverge |
| Mantle convection | Heat-driven circulation in the mantle, believed to drive plate motion |
| Pangaea | The supercontinent that existed ~300 million years ago before breaking apart |

## Core Ideas (no formulas — this is a conceptual/evidence-based unit)

- Earth's lithosphere is broken into large rigid plates that float and move
  atop the ductile asthenosphere.
- Plates move a few centimeters per year — roughly the speed fingernails grow.
- Evidence for plate tectonics: matching fossils and rock types across
  continents now separated by oceans, matching coastlines (like puzzle
  pieces), magnetic striping symmetric on either side of mid-ocean ridges,
  and age of oceanic crust increasing with distance from ridges.

## The Big Idea

**Continents don't "drift" through solid ocean floor — the entire plate,
continent and attached ocean floor together, moves as one rigid unit.**
Wegener's original "continental drift" idea (1912) was largely dismissed
because he couldn't explain a mechanism; plate tectonics theory (1960s)
succeeded because seafloor spreading provided that mechanism — plates carry
continents along with them, they don't plow through the seafloor
independently.

## Common Misconceptions to Address Directly

- Believing continents "drift" independently through a fixed ocean floor
  (in reality, the plate — including the ocean floor — moves as one piece).
- Confusing convergent and divergent boundaries' features (mountains and
  subduction happen at *convergent* boundaries; new crust and rift valleys
  form at *divergent* boundaries).
- Believing earthquakes and volcanoes are randomly distributed around the
  globe (they're heavily concentrated along plate boundaries, especially
  the Pacific "Ring of Fire").
- Believing Earth's plates move due to gravity alone or "just because" —
  the current best-supported mechanism is mantle convection combined with
  slab pull and ridge push forces.
- Assuming oceanic and continental crust are the same — oceanic crust is
  thinner and denser (basaltic); continental crust is thicker and less
  dense (granitic), which is why oceanic crust subducts under continental
  crust at convergent boundaries, not the reverse.
