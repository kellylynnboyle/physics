# Plate Tectonics — Quick-Reference Study Guide

## Definitions

- **Lithosphere** — rigid outer layer (crust + upper mantle), broken into plates
- **Asthenosphere** — ductile mantle layer beneath the lithosphere that plates move over
- **Convergent boundary** — plates move toward each other (subduction, mountains)
- **Divergent boundary** — plates move apart (new crust, mid-ocean ridges)
- **Transform boundary** — plates slide past each other (earthquakes, no new/destroyed crust)
- **Mantle convection** — heat-driven mantle circulation believed to drive plate motion

## Quick-Reference Table

| Boundary Type | What Happens | Example Feature |
|---|---|---|
| Convergent (oceanic-continental) | Denser oceanic plate subducts | Trench + volcanic mountain range |
| Convergent (continental-continental) | Neither subducts; crust crumples | Large mountain range (e.g., Himalayas) |
| Divergent | Plates pull apart; new crust forms | Mid-ocean ridge, rift valley |
| Transform | Plates slide past horizontally | Fault line (e.g., San Andreas) |

| Evidence | Supports |
|---|---|
| Matching fossils/rocks across oceans | Continents were once joined |
| Matching coastlines | Continents were once joined (Pangaea) |
| Symmetric magnetic striping at ridges | Seafloor spreading / new crust formation |
| Crust age increases with distance from ridge | Seafloor spreading |

## Practice Problems (conceptual)

1. Two continental plates converge. What forms?
2. A ridge shows symmetric magnetic striping. What does this indicate?
3. Which type of crust is denser: oceanic or continental?
4. What layer do plates move across?
5. Name the plate boundary type at the San Andreas Fault.
6. What is the primary force believed to drive plate motion?

4. The asthenosphere
