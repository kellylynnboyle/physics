## Practice Problems (Analysis, not calculation — this is a conceptual unit)

1. Two plates are moving toward each other, one oceanic and one continental. Predict what geologic features will form, and explain your reasoning.
2. A mid-ocean ridge is discovered with magnetic striping that is symmetric on both sides. What does this evidence support?
3. A region has frequent earthquakes but no volcanoes or major mountain building. What type of plate boundary is most likely present?
4. Explain, using plate tectonics, why South America's east coast and Africa's west coast fit together like puzzle pieces.
5. A scientist finds identical fossil species in rock layers on two continents now separated by an ocean. How does plate tectonics explain this?
6. Predict what would happen at a boundary where two continental plates (similar density) converge, rather than an oceanic-continental pair.

<details><summary>Show Answers</summary>

1. A subduction zone will form (oceanic crust sinks beneath continental crust), likely producing a deep ocean trench, volcanic mountain range, and earthquakes along the boundary
2. New oceanic crust is continuously forming at the ridge and spreading outward symmetrically — strong evidence for seafloor spreading
3. A transform boundary (like the San Andreas Fault)
4. They were once joined as part of the supercontinent Pangaea before continental drift/plate tectonics separated them
5. The continents were once connected (or much closer together) when those organisms lived, and later separated due to plate motion, carrying the fossil-bearing rock layers apart
6. Neither plate is dense enough to subduct, so the crust crumples and folds upward, forming a large mountain range (as with the Himalayas)
</details>

## Real-World Applications

- **Earthquake risk zones:** cities near plate boundaries (like San Francisco, Tokyo) build stricter seismic codes because of their proximity to active faults.
- **Volcanic hazard mapping:** the Pacific "Ring of Fire" concentrates most of Earth's active volcanoes precisely along convergent plate boundaries.
- **Mineral and geothermal resources:** many valuable ore deposits and geothermal energy sites form near plate boundaries due to volcanic and hydrothermal activity.
- **GPS-measured plate motion:** modern GPS technology can directly measure plates moving a few centimeters per year, confirming the theory in real time.

## Extension / Challenge

- Research how GPS measurements of plate motion today compare to the rates inferred from magnetic striping and seafloor age data — do they agree?
