## Instructor Mastery Rubric

| Level | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **Emerging (1)** | Believes continents drift independently through a fixed ocean floor; cannot name plate boundary types. |
| **Developing (2)** | Names the three boundary types with support; struggles connecting boundary type to resulting geologic features. |
| **Proficient (3)** | Independently identifies boundary type from described features; explains at least two pieces of evidence for plate tectonics; scores ≥80% on unit quiz. |
| **Advanced (4)** | Explains the mechanism (mantle convection, slab pull, ridge push); predicts geologic outcomes for boundary combinations not explicitly taught (e.g., continent-continent convergence); scores ≥90% on unit quiz. |

## Gradable Evidence Checklist

- [ ] Unit quiz score recorded (target ≥80%)
- [ ] At least 3 practice/analysis problems shown with full reasoning
- [ ] Cornell notes cue-column self-quiz completed
- [ ] One real-world application explained in the student's own words
- [ ] A world map or plate boundary diagram labeled with at least 3 boundary types and their associated features

## Suggested Exit Ticket (3 questions, 5 minutes)

1. Two oceanic plates converge. What geologic feature is likely to form?
2. Explain the evidence from magnetic striping that supports seafloor spreading.
3. What layer do tectonic plates move across, and what is it made of?
