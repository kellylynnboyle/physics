# Unit 1: Kinematics — Key Concepts

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this unit, students will be able to:
1. Distinguish distance/displacement and speed/velocity, including sign conventions.
2. Read and construct position-time, velocity-time, and acceleration-time graphs, and translate between them.
3. Use the area under a v-t graph to find displacement, and the slope of a v-t graph to find acceleration.
4. Apply the four kinematic equations to solve 1D constant-acceleration problems, choosing the correct equation from given/unknown variables.
5. Solve multi-stage motion problems (e.g., an object that accelerates, then moves at constant velocity, then decelerates).
6. Correctly interpret free-fall problems using g = 9.8 m/s².

## Vocabulary

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Distance | Total path length traveled (scalar, always positive) |
| Displacement (Δx) | Straight-line change in position, with direction (vector) |
| Speed | Distance / time (scalar) |
| Velocity (v) | Displacement / time, with direction (vector) |
| Acceleration (a) | Rate of change of velocity; occurs from speeding up, slowing down, **or changing direction** |
| Instantaneous velocity | Velocity at one specific moment (slope of tangent to x-t graph) |
| Average velocity | Total displacement / total time |
| Free fall | Motion under gravity alone (air resistance ignored); a = -g near Earth's surface |

## Core Formulas (1D, constant acceleration)

| # | Equation | Solve for |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | v = v₀ + at | final velocity (no displacement needed) |
| 2 | Δx = v₀t + ½at² | displacement (no final velocity needed) |
| 3 | v² = v₀² + 2aΔx | final velocity (no time needed) |
| 4 | Δx = ½(v₀ + v)t | displacement (no acceleration needed) |

**Sign convention:** Pick one direction as positive (usually "up" or "the
direction of initial motion") at the start of a problem and stay consistent.
Deceleration is *not* automatically negative acceleration — it's negative
only if velocity is positive.

## Common Misconceptions to Address Directly

- "If velocity is zero, acceleration must be zero." (False — at the top of a
  toss, v = 0 but a = -g the whole time.)
- "Negative acceleration always means slowing down." (False — it means slowing down only if velocity is positive; if velocity is negative, negative acceleration means speeding up.)
- Treating average speed and average velocity as always equal (only true for one-directional, straight-line motion).
- Reading a position-time graph's *slope* as if it were the object's speed value on a velocity-time graph, and vice-versa for area.
