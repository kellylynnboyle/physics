# Unit 1: Kinematics — Interactive Quiz (Most-Missed Questions)

Take this quiz **without your notes** after finishing the unit. Answer each
question, then click/expand the answer to check yourself. Read the
explanation even if you got it right — these are the questions students miss
most often, and the "why" matters more than the letter.

Scoring: 10 questions. 8/10 (80%) to advance to Unit 2.

**Q1.** A car travels 3 km east, then 3 km west, back to its starting point.
What is its displacement? What is its distance traveled?

<details><summary>Show Answer</summary>

**Displacement = 0 km. Distance = 6 km.**

Displacement only cares about start and end position — since the car ends
where it started, displacement is zero even though it clearly moved. This is
the #1 most-missed kinematics concept: students often answer "6 km" for
displacement because the car "did travel that far."
</details>

---

**Q2.** A ball is thrown straight up. At the very top of its path, which
statement is true?

A) Both velocity and acceleration are zero
B) Velocity is zero, acceleration is -9.8 m/s²
C) Velocity is at its maximum, acceleration is zero
D) Velocity is zero, acceleration is zero because the ball isn't moving

<details><summary>Show Answer</summary>

**B.**

Gravity never turns off — acceleration is -9.8 m/s² for the *entire* flight,
including the instant at the top. Velocity momentarily equals zero there
because the ball is changing direction, but "zero velocity" does not mean
"zero acceleration." This is the single most commonly missed idea in the
whole unit.
</details>

---

**Q3.** An object has a negative acceleration. What must be true about its motion?

A) It is definitely slowing down
B) It is definitely speeding up
C) It is slowing down only if its velocity is positive
D) It is not moving

<details><summary>Show Answer</summary>

**C.**

Sign of acceleration alone doesn't tell you speeding up vs. slowing down —
you need to compare it to the sign of velocity. Same sign (both + or both
-) → speeding up. Opposite signs → slowing down.
</details>

---

**Q4.** On a position-time graph, a straight line with a steep positive slope
represents:

A) Fast motion in the positive direction
B) Slow motion in the positive direction
C) Constant acceleration
D) The object is speeding up

<details><summary>Show Answer</summary>

**A.**

Slope of an x-t graph = velocity. A steep slope = large velocity magnitude.
A *straight* line means the slope (velocity) is constant, so there's no
acceleration — students frequently confuse "steep line" with "accelerating,"
but a straight line, however steep, is constant velocity.
</details>

---

**Q5.** On a velocity-time graph, what does the area under the curve represent?

A) Acceleration
B) Speed
C) Displacement
D) Jerk (rate of change of acceleration)

<details><summary>Show Answer</summary>

**C — displacement.**

Students often mix this up with the slope of a v-t graph, which is
acceleration. Rule of thumb: **slope of v-t = acceleration; area under v-t =
displacement.**
</details>

**Q6.** A stone is dropped from a cliff (initial velocity = 0). Using
g = 9.8 m/s², how far does it fall in 3 seconds?

A) 29.4 m
B) 44.1 m
C) 88.2 m
D) 9.8 m

<details><summary>Show Answer</summary>

**B — 44.1 m.**

Δx = v₀t + ½at² = 0(3) + ½(9.8)(3²) = ½(9.8)(9) = 44.1 m.

The most common wrong answer is A (29.4 m), from forgetting to square the
time, or forgetting the ½. Also common: forgetting that v₀ = 0 for "dropped"
(not "thrown down").
</details>

**Q7.** A car starts at rest and accelerates at 4 m/s² for 5 seconds, then
travels at constant velocity for 10 more seconds. What is the car's total
displacement?

<details><summary>Show Answer</summary>

**250 m.**

Stage 1 (accelerating): v₀=0, a=4, t=5 → Δx₁ = ½(4)(5²) = 50 m. Final
velocity after stage 1: v = 0 + 4(5) = 20 m/s.

Stage 2 (constant velocity): v=20 m/s, t=10s → Δx₂ = 20(10) = 200 m.

Total: 50 + 200 = **250 m**.

The #1 error here is applying a single kinematic equation across the whole
15-second trip instead of splitting it into stages with each stage's own
known values — the acceleration is NOT constant across the entire trip, so
you cannot use one equation for the whole interval. A very common wrong
answer is 300 m, from treating the whole 15 s as constant acceleration.
</details>

**Q8.** True or False: Average speed and average velocity are always equal in magnitude.

<details><summary>Show Answer</summary>

**False.**

They're equal only when motion is entirely in one direction with no
backtracking. If the object changes direction at any point (like Q1's
round trip), average speed (uses total distance) and average velocity (uses
displacement) will differ — and in a round trip, average velocity can even
be zero while average speed is not.
</details>

**Q9.** Which kinematic equation should you use if you know v₀, a, and Δx,
but NOT time?

A) v = v₀ + at
B) Δx = v₀t + ½at²
C) v² = v₀² + 2aΔx
D) Δx = ½(v₀ + v)t

<details><summary>Show Answer</summary>

**C.**

The skill being tested isn't memorization — it's matching the *missing*
variable to the *one equation that doesn't need it*. Equation C is the only
one of the four with no "t" in it. Make a habit of listing known/unknown
variables first, then picking the equation that's missing the unknown you
don't have.
</details>

**Q10.** A ball is thrown straight down from a height with an initial speed
of 5 m/s. Is its acceleration bigger, smaller, or the same as a ball simply
dropped (v₀ = 0) from the same height?

A) Bigger, because it starts faster
B) Smaller
C) The same — acceleration due to gravity doesn't depend on initial velocity
D) Zero, because it's thrown, not dropped

<details><summary>Show Answer</summary>

**C.**

Acceleration due to gravity (near Earth's surface, ignoring air resistance)
is the same for every object regardless of its initial velocity or mass —
g = 9.8 m/s² downward the entire time. Initial velocity changes *how fast it
hits the ground*, not the acceleration.
</details>
