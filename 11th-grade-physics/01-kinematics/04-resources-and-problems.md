## Practice Problems

1. A cyclist accelerates from rest at 2 m/s² for 6 seconds. How far does she travel, and what's her final velocity?
2. A car traveling at 25 m/s brakes at a constant -5 m/s². How long does it take to stop, and how far does it travel while stopping?
3. A rock is thrown straight up at 15 m/s from the edge of a cliff. How high above the throw point does it rise, and how long does it take to reach that height?
4. Two runners start 100 m apart, running toward each other. Runner A moves at 4 m/s, Runner B moves at 6 m/s. How long until they meet, and where?
5. A train accelerates uniformly from 10 m/s to 30 m/s over a distance of 400 m. Find its acceleration and the time it took.

<details><summary>Show Answers</summary>

1. Δx = ½(2)(6²) = 36 m; v = 0 + 2(6) = 12 m/s
2. t = (0-25)/(-5) = 5 s; Δx = v₀t + ½at² = 25(5) + ½(-5)(25) = 125 - 62.5 = 62.5 m
3. At max height v=0: 0 = 15² + 2(-9.8)Δx → Δx = 225/19.6 ≈ 11.5 m. Time: v=v₀+at → 0=15-9.8t → t ≈ 1.53 s
4. Combined closing speed = 10 m/s over 100 m → t = 10 s. Runner A covers 4(10)=40 m from her start.
5. v²=v₀²+2aΔx → 30²=10²+2a(400) → 900-100=800a → a=1 m/s². Then v=v₀+at → 30=10+1(t) → t=20 s

## Real-World Applications

- **Traffic/reaction-time safety:** stopping-distance calculations (perception + braking distance) used in driver's ed and traffic engineering directly use kinematic equations.
- **Sports analytics:** sprinters' velocity-time curves out of the blocks are analyzed with exactly these graphs to optimize acceleration phase technique.
- **Elevators and roller coasters:** engineers use kinematics to design comfortable (and safe) acceleration/deceleration profiles.
- **Space launches:** mission planners use the same four equations (scaled up) to compute burn times and staging for rockets.
