## Practice Problems

1. A radioactive sample has a half-life of 8 days and starts with 640 g. How much remains after 32 days?
2. An isotope has a half-life of 6 hours. How long until only 1/16 of the original sample remains?
3. Classify each as fission or fusion: (a) two hydrogen nuclei combining into helium, (b) a uranium nucleus splitting into barium and krypton.
4. A 2 kg mass is entirely converted to energy via E=mc². Find the energy released. (c=3×10⁸ m/s)
5. Explain why alpha particles, despite being stopped by a sheet of paper, are considered the most dangerous type of radiation if inhaled.
6. A sample of Carbon-14 (half-life ≈5,730 years) is measured to have 1/4 of its original amount remaining. Approximately how old is the sample?

<details><summary>Show Answers</summary>

1. 32 days ÷ 8 days = 4 half-lives. 640×(1/2)⁴=640/16=40 g
2. 1/16 = (1/2)⁴, so 4 half-lives × 6 hours = 24 hours
3. (a) Fusion, (b) Fission
4. E=mc²=(2)(3×10⁸)²=2×9×10¹⁶=1.8×10¹⁷ J
5. Alpha particles are large and slow, depositing energy very densely over a short distance (high ionizing power) — if inhaled, they damage lung tissue directly at close range, bypassing the skin's protection that stops them from outside the body
6. 1/4 = (1/2)², so 2 half-lives × 5,730 years ≈ 11,460 years old
</details>

## Real-World Applications

- **Nuclear power plants:** use controlled nuclear fission (typically uranium-235) to generate heat, which produces steam to drive turbines.
- **Radiocarbon dating:** uses Carbon-14's known half-life to estimate the age of organic material (fossils, artifacts).
- **Medical imaging and treatment:** radioactive tracers (like technetium-99m) for diagnostic imaging; targeted radiation therapy for cancer treatment.
- **Smoke detectors:** many contain a small amount of americium-241, which emits alpha particles to ionize air and detect smoke interference.

## Extension / Challenge

- Research nuclear fusion research (e.g., ITER, National Ignition Facility) and explain why achieving controlled, net-positive-energy fusion on Earth has been so difficult compared to fission, despite fusion being the process that powers stars.
