## Instructor Mastery Rubric

| Level | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **Emerging (1)** | Believes radiation exposure makes something radioactive; cannot apply half-life formula. |
| **Developing (2)** | Applies half-life with support for whole-number multiples; confuses fission and fusion. |
| **Proficient (3)** | Independently solves half-life problems for any elapsed time; correctly explains alpha/beta/gamma penetrating vs. ionizing tradeoff; distinguishes fission from fusion with examples; scores ≥80% on unit quiz. |
| **Advanced (4)** | Explains E=mc² and why nuclear reactions vastly outenergize chemical reactions; connects concepts to real applications (power, medicine, dating); scores ≥90% on unit quiz. |

## Gradable Evidence Checklist

- [ ] Unit quiz score recorded (target ≥80%)
- [ ] At least 3 practice problems shown with full work, including at least one half-life calculation
- [ ] Cornell notes cue-column self-quiz completed
- [ ] One real-world application explained in the student's own words
- [ ] PhET "Alpha Decay" or "Nuclear Fission" simulation used and described in writing

## Suggested Exit Ticket (3 questions, 5 minutes)

1. A sample with a 4-day half-life starts at 1,000 g. How much remains after 16 days?
2. Explain why alpha particles are both "easily blocked" and "very dangerous," without contradiction.
3. What's the difference between nuclear fission and nuclear fusion? Give a real-world example of each.
