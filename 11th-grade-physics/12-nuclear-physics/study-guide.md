# Nuclear Physics — Quick-Reference Study Guide

## Definitions

- **Isotope** — atoms of the same element with different neutron counts
- **Alpha decay** — emits a helium nucleus; least penetrating, MOST ionizing
- **Beta decay** — emits an electron; moderate penetrating and ionizing power
- **Gamma decay** — emits a high-energy photon; MOST penetrating, least ionizing
- **Half-life** — time for half a radioactive sample to decay
- **Fission** — heavy nucleus splits into smaller nuclei (current nuclear power)
- **Fusion** — light nuclei combine into a heavier nucleus (powers the Sun)

## Formulas

- E = mc²
- N = N₀(1/2)^(t/T½)

## Practice Problems

1. A sample has a half-life of 3 years and starts at 400 g. How much remains after 9 years?
2. How many half-lives does it take for a sample to drop to 1/32 of its original amount?
3. Which decay type is stopped by a sheet of paper?
4. Which decay type requires several centimeters of lead to stop?
5. Classify: two light nuclei combine into one heavier nucleus. Fission or fusion?
6. A 0.001 kg mass converts entirely to energy. Find the energy released. (c=3×10⁸ m/s)

<details><summary>Show Answers</summary>

1. 9÷3=3 half-lives. 400×(1/2)³=400/8=50 g
2. 1/32=(1/2)⁵ → 5 half-lives
3. Alpha
4. Gamma
5. Fusion
6. E=mc²=(0.001)(3×10⁸)²=0.001×9×10¹⁶=9×10¹³ J
</details>
