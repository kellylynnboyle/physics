# Unit 6: Electricity — Key Concepts

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this unit, students will be able to:
1. Explain electric charge, charging by friction/conduction/induction, and Coulomb's Law.
2. Distinguish current, voltage, and resistance, and apply Ohm's Law (V=IR).
3. Analyze series and parallel circuits — total resistance, current, and voltage at each element.
4. Calculate electrical power (P=IV) and relate it to energy cost.
5. Distinguish conductors, insulators, and semiconductors.
6. Explain what a battery/EMF source does in a circuit.

## Vocabulary

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Electric charge (q) | A fundamental property of matter; comes in + and -, measured in Coulombs (C) |
| Coulomb's Law | F = kq₁q₂/r² — force between two point charges |
| Electric current (I) | Rate of flow of charge, I = q/t, measured in Amperes (A) |
| Voltage (V) | Electric potential difference — the "push" driving current, measured in Volts |
| Resistance (R) | Opposition to current flow, measured in Ohms (Ω) |
| Ohm's Law | V = IR |
| Series circuit | One path for current; same current through every component |
| Parallel circuit | Multiple paths for current; same voltage across every branch |
| Power (electrical) | P = IV = I²R = V²/R, measured in Watts |
| Conductor | Material that allows charge to flow easily (e.g., metals) |
| Insulator | Material that resists charge flow (e.g., rubber, glass) |

## Core Formulas

- Coulomb's Law: F = kq₁q₂/r² (k ≈ 9×10⁹ N·m²/C²)
- Ohm's Law: V = IR
- Series resistance: R_total = R₁ + R₂ + R₃ + ...
- Parallel resistance: 1/R_total = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + 1/R₃ + ...
- Power: P = IV = I²R = V²/R
- Energy: E = Pt (measured in Joules, or kWh for utility billing)

## The Big Idea

**Current is the same everywhere in a series circuit; voltage is the same
across every branch of a parallel circuit.** Almost every circuit problem
in this unit reduces to correctly identifying which quantity (current or
voltage) stays constant for the configuration you're looking at, then
applying Ohm's Law component by component.

## Common Misconceptions to Address Directly

- "Current gets used up as it flows through a circuit." (False — current is
  conserved at every point in a series loop; what changes is voltage,
  which drops across each resistor as energy is delivered.)
- Confusing voltage with current — describing a battery as "current source"
  when it's more accurately a voltage (EMF) source; current depends on both
  the voltage and the resistance of the circuit it's connected to.
- Believing adding more resistors in *parallel* always increases total
  resistance (it's the opposite — parallel branches add more paths for
  current, decreasing total resistance).
- Believing adding more resistors in *series* decreases total resistance
  (it increases it — more resistors, more total opposition).
- Treating charge as something that can be created or destroyed in a
  circuit rather than a conserved quantity that flows.
