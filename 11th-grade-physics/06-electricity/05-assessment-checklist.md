## Instructor Mastery Rubric

| Level | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **Emerging (1)** | Believes current is "used up"; cannot apply Ohm's Law without heavy scaffolding; confuses series and parallel rules. |
| **Developing (2)** | Solves single-resistor Ohm's Law problems; struggles combining series and parallel resistors in one circuit. |
| **Proficient (3)** | Independently solves series, parallel, and simple combination circuits; correctly explains why current is constant in series and voltage is constant in parallel; scores ≥80% on unit quiz. |
| **Advanced (4)** | Solves multi-resistor combination circuits; connects P=IV=I²R=V²/R fluently across problem types; explains real-world wiring choices (e.g., why homes use parallel); scores ≥90% on unit quiz. |

## Gradable Evidence Checklist

- [ ] Unit quiz score recorded (target ≥80%)
- [ ] At least 3 practice problems shown with full work, including at least one combination circuit
- [ ] Cornell notes cue-column self-quiz completed
- [ ] One real-world application explained in the student's own words
- [ ] PhET "Circuit Construction Kit" used to build and verify at least one series and one parallel circuit

## Suggested Exit Ticket (3 questions, 5 minutes)

1. A 5 Ω and a 10 Ω resistor are in series with a 15 V battery. Find the current.
2. Explain why adding a resistor in parallel decreases total resistance, but adding one in series increases it.
3. A device is rated 100 W at 120 V. Find its current draw and its resistance.
