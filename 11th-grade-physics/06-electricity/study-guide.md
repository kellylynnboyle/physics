# Electricity — Quick-Reference Study Guide

## Definitions

- **Electric charge (q)** — a fundamental property of matter (+ or -), measured in Coulombs (C)
- **Current (I)** — rate of flow of charge, I = q/t, measured in Amperes (A)
- **Voltage (V)** — electric potential difference, the "push" driving current, measured in Volts
- **Resistance (R)** — opposition to current flow, measured in Ohms (Ω)
- **Series circuit** — one path; current is the same everywhere
- **Parallel circuit** — multiple paths; voltage is the same across every branch

## Formulas

- Coulomb's Law: F = kq₁q₂/r² (k ≈ 9×10⁹ N·m²/C²)
- Ohm's Law: V = IR
- Series resistance: R_total = R₁ + R₂ + ...
- Parallel resistance: 1/R_total = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + ...
- Power: P = IV = I²R = V²/R
- Energy: E = Pt

## Quick-Reference Table

| Situation | Key fact |
|---|---|
| Series circuit | Current is the same through every element; voltages add up to the total |
| Parallel circuit | Voltage is the same across every branch; currents add up to the total |
| Adding a resistor in series | Total resistance increases |
| Adding a resistor in parallel | Total resistance decreases |
| Current through a resistor | Never "used up" — conserved at every point in the loop |

## Practice Problems

1. Two charges of +6 μC and +2 μC are 0.3 m apart. Find the force between them.
2. A 9 V battery drives current through a single 3 Ω resistor. Find the current.
3. Three resistors — 2 Ω, 4 Ω, 6 Ω — are in series with a 12 V battery. Find total resistance and current.
4. Two 8 Ω resistors are in parallel. Find the total resistance.
5. A hair dryer is rated 1200 W at 120 V. Find its current draw and resistance.
6. A device uses 500 W for 4 hours. How much energy did it use, in kWh?

1. F=kq₁q₂/r²=(9×10⁹)(6×10⁻⁶)(2×10⁻⁶)/(0.3²)=(9×10⁹)(1.2×10⁻¹¹)/0.09≈1.2 N
2. I=V/R=9/3=3 A
3. R_total=2+4+6=12 Ω. I=12/12=1 A
4. 1/R=1/8+1/8=2/8 → R=4 Ω
5. I=P/V=1200/120=10 A. R=V/I=120/10=12 Ω
6. E=Pt=500×4=2000 Wh=2 kWh
