# NGSS Alignment (California, High School Three-Course Model: Physics in the Universe)

The California NGSS standards page is https://www.cde.ca.gov/pd/ca/sc/ngssstandards.asp. In the three-course model, physics and Earth and space science are taught together as **Physics in the Universe**. The performance expectations (PEs) below are the learning goals for the first semester.

## First semester performance expectations

| PE | What students do (paraphrased) | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| HS-PS2-1 | Analyze data to support the claim that Newton's second law describes the relationship among net force, mass, and acceleration | Kinematics, Projectile Motion (motion models), Newton's Laws (assessed) |
| HS-PS2-2 | Use mathematical representations to support the claim that total momentum of a system is conserved when there is no net force on the system | Momentum |
| HS-PS2-3 | Apply science and engineering ideas to design, evaluate, and refine a device that minimizes the force on an object during a collision | Momentum |
| HS-PS2-4 | Use mathematical representations of Newton's law of gravitation to describe and predict gravitational forces between objects | Newton's Laws (introduced), Projectile Motion (satellites as an extension) |
| HS-PS3-1 | Create a computational model to calculate the change in energy of one component of a system when the energy flows are known | Work and Energy |
| HS-PS3-2 | Develop and use models showing that energy at the macroscopic scale is a combination of the motions of particles and their relative positions | Work and Energy |
| HS-PS3-3 | Design, build, and refine a device that works within constraints to convert one form of energy into another | Work and Energy |
| HS-ETS1-2, HS-ETS1-3 | Break a complex problem into smaller ones and evaluate solutions against criteria and trade-offs | Momentum (egg drop), Work and Energy (design project) |

Kinematics and projectile motion have no stand-alone high school PE. They are taught because the data analysis in HS-PS2-1 and the momentum and energy PEs all depend on position, velocity, and acceleration.

## Science and engineering practices emphasized

| Practice | Where it shows up |
|---|---|
| Analyzing and interpreting data | Motion graphs, a vs. F and a vs. 1/m graphs, cart collisions |
| Using mathematics and computational thinking | Kinematic equations, momentum tables, Energy Skate Park model |
| Developing and using models | Free-body diagrams, energy bar charts, before and after diagrams |
| Planning and carrying out investigations | Buggy lab, marble launch, friction lab, stair-climb lab |
| Constructing explanations and designing solutions | Egg drop device, energy conversion device |
| Engaging in argument from evidence | Claim, evidence, reasoning in every lab report |

## Crosscutting concepts emphasized

Cause and effect (forces and motion), systems and system models (momentum and energy), energy and matter (conservation), and scale, proportion, and quantity (v² relationships).
