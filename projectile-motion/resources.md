# Projectile Motion — Practice Problems, Resources & Applications

## Practice problems

### Level 1 — Direct substitution
1. A ball is thrown horizontally at 12 m/s from a 20 m tall building. How
   long does it take to hit the ground, and how far from the base of the
   building does it land?
2. A projectile is launched at 25 m/s at an angle of 30° above the
   horizontal from level ground. Find v₀ₓ and v₀y.
3. A soccer ball is kicked with v₀y = 8 m/s straight up-component on level
   ground. Find the time it takes to reach maximum height and the maximum
   height itself.

### Level 2 — Multi-step / choose your equation
4. A projectile launched at 40 m/s at 53° above the horizontal from level
   ground. Find (a) time of flight, (b) maximum height, (c) range.
5. A stone is thrown from the top of a 30 m cliff at 15 m/s, angled 20°
   below the horizontal. Find the time to reach the ground and the
   horizontal distance traveled.
6. Two projectiles are launched from level ground at the same speed, one
   at 25° and one at 65°. Show that they have the same range, then find
   which one has the greater maximum height and by how much time it stays
   in the air longer.

### Level 3 — Graph-based
7. Sketch the trajectory (y vs. x) of a projectile launched at 45° on
   level ground next to one launched at 70° with the same initial speed.
   Label which curve is wider/flatter and which reaches greater height,
   and mark the range and peak of each.
8. Given a y-vs-t graph of a projectile's vertical motion (a downward
   parabola) and an x-vs-t graph of the same projectile's horizontal
   motion (a straight line), describe what each graph's shape tells you
   about vₓ and vy over time.

### Challenge
9. A rescue plane flying horizontally at 60 m/s at an altitude of 500 m
   needs to drop a supply package so it lands at a target on the ground.
   How long does the package fall, how far horizontally does it travel
   during the fall, and how far before the target (measured along the
   ground) should the plane release the package?

*(Answer keys are intentionally omitted here — have students show full
given/find/equation/solve/units work, set up horizontal and vertical
components separately, and check answers with an instructor or the quiz
in `quiz.html`.)*

## Real-world applications

- **Sports**: a basketball free throw's arc, a punted football's hang time
  and range, a golf ball's trajectory off the tee — coaches and broadcast
  analytics use launch angle and speed to predict distance and hang time.
- **Water fountains**: decorative fountain jets are engineered projectile
  arcs, with nozzle angle and pressure (launch speed) chosen to control
  where the water lands.
- **Artillery and ballistics**: firing solutions for cannons and mortars
  use range and time-of-flight equations (adjusted for air resistance and
  target elevation) to hit a target at a known distance.
- **Search-and-rescue payload drops**: aircraft dropping supplies must
  release the payload before the target, accounting for the horizontal
  distance it travels while falling — the same physics as the Challenge
  problem above.
- **Ski jump ramp engineering**: the ramp's launch angle and speed at
  takeoff are designed using projectile motion to maximize distance while
  keeping the landing slope safe.

## Study tips for the most commonly missed question types

- **"Constant velocity" is not "zero acceleration everywhere"** —
  horizontal velocity is constant (aₓ = 0), but vertical acceleration is
  always −g, including at the peak. Don't let "constant" on one axis bleed
  into an assumption about the other axis.
- **Decompose before you calculate** — write v₀ₓ = v₀cosθ and v₀y = v₀sinθ
  as your very first step on any angled-launch problem. Plugging the full
  speed v₀ straight into a vertical equation is the most common setup
  error.
- **Speed at the peak is NOT zero** — only vy = 0 at the top; vₓ is
  unchanged, so the object is still moving (at speed vₓ) at the highest
  point of the arc.
- **Time of flight depends only on the vertical motion** — a faster
  horizontal launch does not mean less time in the air. Compare the
  vertical component (and starting height), not the total speed.
- **Horizontal-launch (v₀y = 0) problems still need the same setup** — the
  time to fall depends only on height, not on how fast the object is
  moving horizontally off the edge.
- **Check launch height = landing height before using the shortcut
  formulas** — R = v₀²sin(2θ)/g, h_max = v₀y²/(2g), and t = 2v₀y/g all
  assume level ground. Cliff, table, and platform problems need the full
  Δy = v₀yt + ½at² setup instead.
- **Complementary angles share a range, not a height or time** — 30° and
  60° (or any θ and 90°−θ) land in the same spot on level ground, but the
  steeper angle takes longer and goes higher.
- **Watch your signs when landing is below launch** — if up is positive,
  a landing point below the launch point has negative vertical
  displacement (Δy < 0); dropping this sign is a common source of wrong
  answers on cliff problems.
- **v₀ₓ = v₀y only at exactly 45°** — this shortcut is easy to overuse;
  double-check the given angle before assuming the components are equal.
