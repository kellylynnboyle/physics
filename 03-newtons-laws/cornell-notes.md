## Notes page

| Cues | Notes |
|---|---|
| Newton's First Law | An object's velocity doesn't change unless a net external force acts on it. "At rest" and "constant velocity" are both states of equilibrium (a = 0). |
| What does "net force" mean? | The vector sum of all forces acting on an object. Multiple forces can act on something with zero net effect if they balance. |
| Newton's Second Law | F_net = ma. Always draw a free-body diagram first, then sum forces component by component: ΣF_x = ma_x, ΣF_y = ma_y. |
| Steps for an FBD | 1) Draw the object as a dot/box. 2) Draw every force acting ON it (gravity, normal, applied, tension, friction) as an arrow from that point. 3) Never include forces the object exerts on other things. |
| Newton's Third Law | For every action force, there's an equal-magnitude, opposite-direction reaction force acting on a DIFFERENT object, of the SAME type. |
| Why third-law pairs don't cancel | They act on different objects, so they don't appear together in the same object's F_net = ma equation. |
| Mass vs. weight | Mass (kg) — amount of matter, doesn't change with location. Weight (N) = mg — force of gravity on that mass, changes with local g. |
| Normal force | Perpendicular support force from a surface. Equals mg only on a flat surface with no other vertical forces — NOT a fixed formula. |
| Elevator/incline normal force | Solve ΣF_y = ma_y for N; it will differ from mg whenever there's vertical acceleration or another force with a vertical component. |
| Static vs. kinetic friction | Static: f_s ≤ μ_s N, resists the START of sliding (matches applied force up to a max). Kinetic: f_k = μ_k N, constant once sliding. μ_s > μ_k usually. |
| Equilibrium | ΣF = 0 → a = 0. This includes constant-velocity motion, not just objects at rest. |

*Reference summary: Newton's First Law says objects resist changes in
motion (inertia) unless a net force acts. The Second Law, F_net = ma,
quantifies that relationship and is applied through a free-body diagram
that accounts for every force acting on the object. The Third Law pairs
every force with an equal, opposite one acting on a different object — the
two never cancel because they act on separate bodies. Weight is the force
of gravity (mg) and is distinct from mass; normal force is whatever value
makes the vertical forces balance the vertical acceleration, so it equals
weight only in the simplest case of a flat surface with nothing else
acting vertically.*
