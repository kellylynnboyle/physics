# Cornell Notes — Newton's Laws

## Page 1: The Three Laws

| Cue | Notes |
|---|---|
| 1st Law (Inertia) | An object at rest stays at rest, an object in motion stays at constant velocity, UNLESS a net external force acts on it. "Constant velocity" needs no force to maintain — only a net force changes it. |
| 2nd Law | F_net = ma. Force and acceleration are vectors and point the same direction. Acceleration is inversely proportional to mass for a fixed force. |
| 3rd Law | For every force object A exerts on object B, B exerts an equal and opposite force on A. These act on DIFFERENT objects — they never cancel for either object individually. |
| Common trap | Don't confuse a 3rd-law pair (two objects, same force type) with two different forces acting on the SAME object (e.g., gravity and normal force on one box — NOT a 3rd law pair). |

## Page 2: Forces & Free Body Diagrams

| Cue | Notes |
|---|---|
| Weight | W = mg, always points straight down toward Earth's center. |
| Normal force | Perpendicular to the contact surface; equals weight ONLY on a flat surface with no other vertical forces/acceleration. |
| Tension | Pulls along the direction of a rope/string, away from the object. |
| Static friction | f_s ≤ μₛN — adjusts to oppose motion attempt, up to a maximum. |
| Kinetic friction | f_k = μₖN (roughly constant) once the object is sliding; usually μₖ < μₛ. |
| FBD rule of thumb | Isolate ONE object. Draw every force acting ON it (not forces it exerts on other things). |

## Page 3: Inclines & Mass vs. Weight

| Cue | Notes |
|---|---|
| Mass vs weight | Mass (kg) = amount of matter, constant everywhere. Weight (N) = mg, changes with local gravity. |
| Incline decomposition | Gravity component along the slope: mg·sin(θ) (drives motion down the incline). Perpendicular component: mg·cos(θ) (balanced by normal force). |
| Equilibrium | Net force = 0. True both at rest AND moving at constant velocity. |
