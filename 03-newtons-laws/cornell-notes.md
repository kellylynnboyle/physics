| Cues / Questions | Notes |
|---|---|
| State Newton's First Law | An object's velocity stays constant (including staying at rest) unless a net external force acts on it. Also called the Law of Inertia — objects "resist" changes to their motion. |
| State Newton's Second Law | `F_net = ma`. Net force (vector sum of all forces) equals mass times acceleration. Acceleration points the same direction as net force. |
| State Newton's Third Law | For every force object A exerts on object B, object B exerts an equal-magnitude, opposite-direction force on A. These forces act on **different objects** and never cancel each other out. |
| What is a free body diagram? | A diagram isolating ONE object, showing every external force acting **on** it as an arrow (length ∝ magnitude, direction = force direction). Forces the object exerts on other things are NOT drawn. |
| List common force types | Weight `F_g = mg` (always straight down). Normal force `N` (perpendicular to contact surface). Tension `T` (along a rope/string, pulling). Applied force `F_app`. Friction `f` (opposes relative sliding or its tendency, parallel to surface). |
| Mass vs. weight — what's the difference? | Mass (kg) = amount of matter, constant everywhere. Weight (N) = `mg`, a force that depends on gravity — your weight would be different on the Moon, but your mass would not change. |
| Static vs. kinetic friction | Static friction (`f_s ≤ μ_s N`) prevents an object from starting to slide — it adjusts to match the applied force up to a maximum. Kinetic friction (`f_k = μ_k N`) applies once the object is already sliding, and is usually smaller than max static friction. |
| What does "equilibrium" mean? | `F_net = 0`. This includes objects at rest AND objects moving at constant velocity in a straight line — both have zero acceleration. |
| Is normal force always equal to weight? | No. On a flat surface with no other vertical forces, yes. But on an incline, in an accelerating elevator, or with an applied force at an angle, normal force must be solved from the full force balance — it is NOT automatically `mg`. |
| Why don't action-reaction pairs cancel? | They act on two *different* objects, so from the perspective of either single object, there's only one of the pair acting on it. Forces only cancel when analyzing the SAME object. |

## Quick Reference

| Force | Formula | Direction |
|---|---|---|
| Weight | `F_g = mg` | Straight down |
| Normal | Solve from force balance | Perpendicular to surface |
| Static friction (max) | `f_s = μ_s N` | Opposes tendency to slide |
| Kinetic friction | `f_k = μ_k N` | Opposes actual sliding motion |
| Net force | `F_net = ma` | Same direction as acceleration |

## Common Mistakes to Flag in Your Own Work

- [ ] Did I draw a force the object exerts on something else, instead of a force acting on it?
- [ ] Did I assume normal force always equals weight, even on an incline or in an accelerating frame?
- [ ] Did I treat a Newton's-third-law pair as if the two forces act on the same object and cancel?
- [ ] Did I use `μ_s` when the object is already sliding (should be `μ_k`) or vice versa?
- [ ] Did I forget that constant velocity means `F_net = 0`, not "no forces at all"?
