## Practice Problems

1. **Net force (basic)**
   Three forces act on an object: 10 N east, 6 N west, and 4 N north. What is the net force?
   *Answer:* East-west components: 10 N − 6 N = 4 N east. North component: 4 N. Net force magnitude = √(4² + 4²) = √32 ≈ 5.7 N, directed 45° north of east.

2. **F = ma (basic)**
   A net force of 15 N acts on a 3 kg object initially at rest. What is its acceleration, and how fast is it moving after 4 seconds?
   *Answer:* a = F/m = 15 N / 3 kg = 5 m/s². v = v₀ + at = 0 + (5)(4) = 20 m/s.

3. **Finding mass from F = ma**
   A net force of 24 N produces an acceleration of 3 m/s² in an object. What is the object's mass, and what would it weigh on Earth (g = 9.8 m/s²)?
   *Answer:* m = F/a = 24 N / 3 m/s² = 8 kg. Weight = mg = 8 × 9.8 = 78.4 N.

4. **Incline problem**
   A 5 kg block sits on a frictionless incline angled at 20° above horizontal. Find (a) the normal force and (b) the block's acceleration down the incline. (g = 9.8 m/s²)
   *Answer:* (a) N = mg cos(20°) = 5 × 9.8 × 0.940 ≈ 46.0 N. (b) a = g sin(20°) = 9.8 × 0.342 ≈ 3.35 m/s² down the slope.

5. **Friction problem**
   A 12 kg box on a horizontal floor has μs = 0.5 and μk = 0.35. (a) What is the maximum static friction force? (b) If a 70 N horizontal force is applied, does the box move, and if so, what is its acceleration? (g = 9.8 m/s²)
   *Answer:* Normal force N = mg = 12 × 9.8 = 117.6 N. (a) f_s,max = μs·N = 0.5 × 117.6 = 58.8 N. (b) Since 70 N > 58.8 N, the box moves. Kinetic friction f_k = μk·N = 0.35 × 117.6 = 41.16 N. Net force = 70 − 41.16 = 28.84 N. a = F_net/m = 28.84/12 ≈ 2.40 m/s².

6. **Connected objects / tension (horizontal system)**
   Two blocks, m1 = 4 kg and m2 = 6 kg, connected by a light string, sit on a frictionless horizontal surface. A horizontal force of 30 N pulls m1 (with m2 trailing behind, connected by the string). Find (a) the acceleration of the system and (b) the tension in the string.
   *Answer:* (a) a = F/(m1+m2) = 30/10 = 3 m/s². (b) Isolate m2 (only tension acts on it horizontally): T = m2·a = 6 × 3 = 18 N.

7. **Atwood machine (connected objects, vertical)**
   Two masses, m1 = 8 kg and m2 = 5 kg, hang from a light string over a frictionless, massless pulley. Find (a) the acceleration of the system and (b) the tension in the string. (g = 9.8 m/s²)
   *Answer:* (a) a = (m1 − m2)g / (m1 + m2) = (8 − 5)(9.8) / 13 = 29.4/13 ≈ 2.26 m/s². (b) Using m2 (moving upward): T − m2g = m2a → T = m2(g + a) = 5 × (9.8 + 2.26) = 5 × 12.06 ≈ 60.3 N. (Check with m1: T = m1(g − a) = 8 × (9.8 − 2.26) = 8 × 7.54 ≈ 60.3 N ✓.)

8. **Elevator problem (challenging)**
   A 70 kg person stands on a scale in an elevator. Find the scale reading (normal force) when the elevator: (a) is at rest, (b) accelerates upward at 1.5 m/s², (c) accelerates downward at 1.5 m/s², (d) is in free fall. (g = 9.8 m/s²)
   *Answer:* (a) N = mg = 70 × 9.8 = 686 N. (b) N = m(g + a) = 70 × (9.8 + 1.5) = 70 × 11.3 = 791 N. (c) N = m(g − a) = 70 × (9.8 − 1.5) = 70 × 8.3 = 581 N. (d) In free fall, a = g downward, so N = m(g − g) = 0 N — apparent weightlessness.

## Real-World Applications

- **Seatbelts and inertia** — When a car stops suddenly in a collision, your body tends to keep moving forward at its original speed due to inertia (Newton's First Law). A seatbelt provides the external force needed to decelerate you along with the car, preventing you from continuing forward into the dashboard or windshield.

- **Rocket propulsion as action-reaction** — Rockets accelerate by expelling exhaust gas at high speed out the back (the "action" force on the gas). By Newton's Third Law, the gas exerts an equal and opposite "reaction" force that pushes the rocket forward. Because the two forces act on different objects (rocket and gas), this works even in the vacuum of space, where there is no air to push against.

- **Car tires and friction** — Static friction between tires and the road is what allows a car to accelerate, brake, and turn without skidding; as long as the tire isn't sliding relative to the road, static friction (not kinetic) is in play. If a driver brakes too hard and the tires start sliding, kinetic friction takes over — and since kinetic friction is generally less than maximum static friction, stopping distance actually increases once a skid begins, which is why anti-lock braking systems (ABS) try to prevent full skidding.

- **Tug-of-war** — Both teams pull on the rope with tension forces, and by Newton's Third Law, the rope pulls back equally on each team. The team that moves is determined not by the rope tension (which is the same throughout an ideal rope) but by which team can generate more static friction force against the ground, producing a greater net external force on their side of the system.

- **Elevators and apparent weight** — Standing on a scale in an elevator, your true weight (mg) never changes, but the scale reads the normal force, which does change: it increases when the elevator accelerates upward (N = m(g+a)) and decreases when accelerating downward (N = m(g−a)), even briefly reading zero during a free-fall-like drop. This "apparent weight" effect is the same principle astronauts experience as near-weightlessness in orbit, where they are continuously in free fall around Earth.
