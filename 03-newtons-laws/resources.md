## Practice Problems

1. A 5 kg box sits on a frictionless table. A 15 N horizontal force is applied. Find the acceleration.
2. A 10 kg object hangs from a rope. Find the tension in the rope when (a) it hangs at rest, (b) it accelerates upward at 2 m/s², (c) it accelerates downward at 2 m/s².
3. A 2 kg block sits on a 30° frictionless incline. Find the acceleration of the block down the incline.
4. Repeat problem 3, but now the incline has a coefficient of kinetic friction μₖ = 0.2. Find the new acceleration.
5. A 60 kg skater pushes off a 90 kg skater, both initially at rest. If the first skater's push accelerates them apart, explain (in terms of Newton's 3rd law) why both skaters feel the same-magnitude force, but different accelerations.
6. Two blocks (3 kg and 5 kg) are connected by a string over a frictionless pulley, with the 5 kg block hanging off a table edge and the 3 kg block on the table (frictionless). Find the acceleration of the system and the tension in the string.
7. A 1200 kg car experiences 3000 N of forward force from the engine and 800 N of friction/air resistance. Find its acceleration.
8. A book rests on a table. Identify all forces acting on the book, identify the 3rd-law reaction partner for each one, and explain why the book doesn't accelerate even though two forces act on it.

*(Encourage students to draw a labeled free-body diagram for every problem BEFORE writing any equation — this is the single highest-leverage habit for this topic.)*

## Real-World Applications

- **Car safety design:** seatbelts and airbags exist because of the 1st law — your body keeps moving forward when the car stops suddenly
- **Rocket propulsion:** rockets work purely on the 3rd law — expelling exhaust backward pushes the rocket forward, even in the vacuum of space (no "air to push against" needed)
- **Vehicle traction and ABS brakes:** engineered around the static-vs-kinetic friction distinction — ABS prevents wheels from sliding (switching to weaker kinetic friction) by keeping them at the edge of static friction
- **Elevators:** the "heavier/lighter" feeling during acceleration is literally the changing normal force (which is what a scale measures) as computed in problem 2 above
- **Sports:** sprinters push backward against the track to accelerate forward (3rd law); tackling in football involves analyzing net force and momentum transfer between players
