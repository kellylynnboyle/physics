# Topic 3: Newton's Laws — Practice, Resources & Applications

## Practice Problems

Use `g = 9.8 m/s²`. Draw a free-body diagram for every problem before writing any equations.

1. A `1200 kg` car accelerates from rest to `20 m/s` in `8 s`. What net force acted on it?
2. A `5 kg` box sits on a horizontal table. A `20 N` horizontal force is applied, and the coefficient of kinetic friction is `μ_k = 0.3`. Find the box's acceleration.
3. Two blocks, `m₁ = 4 kg` and `m₂ = 6 kg`, are connected by a string over a frictionless pulley (`m₁` on a table, `m₂` hanging off the edge). Find the acceleration of the system and the tension in the string. (Assume the table is frictionless.)
4. A `70 kg` person stands on a scale in an elevator. Find the scale reading (apparent weight) when the elevator: (a) is at rest, (b) accelerates upward at `2 m/s²`, (c) accelerates downward at `2 m/s²`, (d) is in free fall.
5. A `10 kg` sled is pulled at constant velocity across a horizontal, snowy surface by a rope angled `25°` above horizontal, with tension `40 N`. Find the coefficient of kinetic friction.
6. A `1500 kg` car goes around a flat curve without sliding. Explain, using Newton's Laws and a free-body diagram, what force provides the centripetal force keeping it on the curve.
7. **Third-law identification**: A book rests on a table. Identify the Newton's-third-law reaction force to "the table pushes up on the book," and explain why "gravity pulls the book down" is NOT that reaction force.

### Answer Key
1. `a = Δv/Δt = 2.5 m/s²`; `F_net = ma = 1200 × 2.5 = 3000 N`
2. Max static friction check aside, once moving: `f_k = μ_k mg = 0.3 × 5 × 9.8 = 14.7 N`; `F_net = 20 − 14.7 = 5.3 N`; `a = 5.3/5 = 1.06 m/s²`
3. System: `a = m₂g / (m₁+m₂) = 6(9.8)/10 = 5.88 m/s²`; Tension from `m₁`'s equation: `T = m₁a = 4 × 5.88 = 23.5 N`
4. (a) `686 N` (b) `686 + 70(2) = 826 N` (c) `686 − 70(2) = 546 N` (d) `0 N` — apparent weightlessness, since scale and person accelerate downward together at `g`
5. Vertical: `N = mg − T sin25° = 98 − 40(0.423) = 81.1 N`. Horizontal (constant velocity → equilibrium): `T cos25° = f_k → f_k = 40(0.906) = 36.3 N`. `μ_k = f_k/N = 36.3/81.1 ≈ 0.45`
6. Static friction between tires and road provides the centripetal force — it acts horizontally, toward the center of the curve, even though the car isn't sliding (static friction can act without motion occurring).
7. The reaction force is "the book pushes down on the table" (equal magnitude, opposite direction, acting on the table). "Gravity pulls the book down" is a different force entirely — it acts on the same object (the book) as the normal force, and pairs instead with "the book pulls up on the Earth" via gravity.

## Real-World Applications
- **Seatbelts and airbags**: your body's inertia (First Law) keeps it moving forward in a sudden stop unless a force (the belt) acts on it.
- **Rocket propulsion**: the rocket pushes exhaust gas backward; the gas pushes the rocket forward (Third Law) — no external medium needed, which is why rockets work in the vacuum of space.
- **Walking**: you push backward on the ground with your foot; the ground pushes you forward (Third Law) — without friction (as on ice), walking is nearly impossible.
- **Tug-of-war and vehicle traction**: understanding why the "stronger" team wins is about friction with the ground, not the tension in the rope, which is equal throughout by Newton's third law.
