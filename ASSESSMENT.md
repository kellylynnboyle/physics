# Assessment Criteria

## Mastery scale (use per learning objective)
| Level | Label | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | Extending | Solves novel multi-step problems; explains *why*; connects to another unit |
| 3 | Proficient | Correct setup, units, and answer on standard problems; ≥80% on the unit quiz |
| 2 | Developing | Correct concept but algebra, units, or sign errors; 60–79% |
| 1 | Beginning | Needs support to start problems; <60% |

## Unit grade weighting (suggested)
| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Cornell notes (completeness, cue questions, summary) | 15% |
| Interactive quiz + corrections | 15% |
| Lab / project | 25% |
| Problem sets | 15% |
| Unit test | 30% |

## Problem-solving rubric (4 pts)
- **Diagram / givens** — labeled sketch, knowns and unknown with units (1)
- **Equation chosen and justified** (1)
- **Substitution and algebra** with units carried through (1)
- **Answer** with units, sign/direction, and a reasonableness check (1)

## Cornell notes rubric (4 pts)
Cues are questions, not words (1) · Notes are in student's own words with diagrams (1) · Summary is 2–3 sentences that answer the cues (1) · Review: student covered notes and self-quizzed (1)

## Lab report rubric (CER)
Claim · Evidence (data table + graph with labeled axes/units + trendline) · Reasoning (physics law linking the two) · Error analysis · Conclusion. 4 pts each, scored with the mastery scale.

## Quiz policy
Quizzes may be retaken. Missed questions require a one-sentence correction naming the misconception. Quiz questions deliberately target the most commonly missed ideas, so a low first score is expected and useful.
