# Assessment Criteria & Progress Tracker

This document defines how mastery is measured across all five units and
provides a shared tracker for monitoring progress — either self-tracked by
a student or maintained by an instructor for a whole class.

## Assessment Philosophy

Each unit is assessed on four dimensions, mirroring how the unit materials
are structured:

1. **Conceptual understanding** — can the student explain the *why*, not just plug into a formula? (Cornell notes summary, verbal/written explanations)
2. **Procedural fluency** — can the student solve the standard practice problems correctly and show their work?
3. **Misconception check** — does the student avoid the specific errors students most often make on this topic? (interactive `quiz.html`)
4. **Application/transfer** — can the student connect the physics to a real-world example not explicitly covered in class?

## Learning Objectives by Unit (Summary)

| Unit | Core Objective |
|---|---|
| 1. Kinematics | Describe and graph 1-D motion; apply the four kinematic equations correctly |
| 2. Projectile Motion | Decompose 2-D motion into independent horizontal/vertical components |
| 3. Newton's Laws | Analyze forces with free body diagrams; apply F = ma and the third law correctly |
| 4. Momentum | Apply conservation of momentum to collisions; connect impulse to force and time |
| 5. Work & Energy | Apply the work-energy theorem and conservation of mechanical energy, with and without friction |

Full, detailed objectives for each unit are listed in that unit's own `README.md`.

## Grading Rubric (per unit)

| Criterion | Weight | 4 — Exceeds | 3 — Meets | 2 — Approaching | 1 — Beginning |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Conceptual understanding | 25% | Explains concept clearly using correct terms and a novel example | Explains concept correctly with terms from class | Explains concept with some inaccuracies | Cannot explain the core concept |
| Procedural fluency (practice problems) | 35% | Solves all problems correctly, shows clear work, checks units | Solves most problems correctly with minor errors | Solves about half correctly; frequent setup errors | Cannot set up or solve the problems |
| Misconception check (`quiz.html`) | 25% | 100% | 80–99% | 60–79% | Below 60% |
| Real-world application | 15% | Identifies and explains a novel real-world example accurately | Identifies a taught real-world example correctly | Identifies an example but explanation is weak | No application connection made |

**Suggested mastery threshold per unit:** average score of 3/4 or higher
across all criteria, AND 80%+ on `quiz.html` specifically (misconceptions
are the highest-leverage thing to catch before moving to the next unit,
since later units build on earlier ones).

## Cumulative / Capstone Check

After completing all five units, a strong cumulative check is a single
multi-concept problem that requires chaining several units together, e.g.:

> *A 0.2 kg ball is launched at 20 m/s at 35° from a 5 m high platform. Find
> its time of flight and range (Kinematics + Projectile Motion). It then
> collides perfectly inelastically with a stationary 0.3 kg ball on the
> ground (Momentum). Using energy methods, find how high the combined mass
> would rise if launched straight up at the resulting velocity, ignoring
> air resistance (Work & Energy). Identify every force acting on the
> combined mass during its rise (Newton's Laws).*

Use a problem like this — or design a similar one — as a final project or
test question to confirm students can integrate the five units rather than
solve each in isolation.
