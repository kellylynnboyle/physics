# Assessment plan

How progress is measured across the semester, for students and instructors.

## Proficiency scale

| Score | Level | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | Advanced | Applies the skill to unfamiliar or multi-step situations and explains the reasoning |
| 3 | Proficient | Meets the standard independently, with correct units and reasoning |
| 2 | Developing | Shows the idea but makes errors or needs prompting |
| 1 | Beginning | Shows a misconception or cannot start without help |

## Evidence collected in every unit

| Evidence | What it shows | Target |
|---|---|---|
| Cornell notes self-test | Recall of definitions, rules, and methods | Every cue answered aloud from memory |
| Practice problems | Problem-solving method and units | All Foundation and Proficient problems correct; one Challenge attempted |
| Misconception quiz | Conceptual understanding | 80% or higher (retakes allowed) |
| IXL practice plan | Fluency with calculations | SmartScore 80+ on each listed skill |
| Lab and write-up | Science practices: data, graphs, claims from evidence | 3 or higher on the lab rubric below |
| Unit test | Standards mastery | 3 or higher on each unit skill |

## Mastery rule

A unit is mastered when every skill in its criteria table is at 3 or higher, the quiz is at 80% or higher, and the lab write-up is complete. Students below that reteach from the missed quiz questions (each explanation names the misconception) and retake.

## Lab write-up rubric (all units)

| Part | 1 Beginning | 2 Developing | 3 Proficient | 4 Advanced |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Question and variables | Missing | Question stated; variables unclear | Testable question with independent, dependent, and controlled variables | Also justifies the design choices |
| Data and graph | Incomplete data | Data table present; graph missing labels or units | Organized table; labeled graph with units and best-fit line | Includes repeated trials and an uncertainty estimate |
| Analysis | No calculation | Calculation with errors | Correct calculation of slope, area, or the quantity asked for | Compares the result with the accepted value using percent error |
| Claim, evidence, reasoning | Claim only | Claim with some data | Claim supported by data and tied to the physics principle | Also discusses error sources and an improvement |

## Unit criteria

### Unit 1: Kinematics

Standards: HS-PS2-1; SEP 4 / SEP 5

| Skill | 1 Beginning | 2 Developing | 3 Proficient | 4 Advanced |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Describe motion with vectors** | Mixes up distance and displacement or speed and velocity | Defines each term but makes sign or direction errors | Uses distance, displacement, speed, and velocity correctly with direction and units | Applies the ideas to an unfamiliar multi-part trip and explains the reasoning |
| **Interpret motion graphs** | Reads the graph as a picture of the path | Reads values but confuses slope and height | Finds velocity, acceleration, and displacement from slope and area | Sketches one graph from another and matches graphs to a described motion |
| **Solve kinematics problems** | Cannot identify the givens | Lists givens but picks a wrong equation or drops units | Chooses the right equation and solves with units and a sense check | Solves two-stage problems and free-fall problems with both directions |

### Unit 2: Projectile Motion

Standards: HS-PS2-1; SEP 2 / SEP 5

| Skill | 1 Beginning | 2 Developing | 3 Proficient | 4 Advanced |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Resolve and add vectors** | Cannot split a vector into components | Finds components but swaps sine and cosine or drops direction | Finds components and resultants with correct trig and direction | Adds several vectors by components and explains the result |
| **Explain projectile motion** | Believes a forward force acts during flight | States that gravity acts but cannot say what is constant | Explains constant v_x, changing v_y, and constant downward acceleration | Predicts and justifies outcomes of unfamiliar scenarios (drop vs. launch, plane drop) |
| **Solve projectile problems** | Mixes horizontal and vertical quantities | Separates x and y but cannot link them by time | Solves horizontal and angled launches for time, height, and range | Solves launches that start and end at different heights |

### Unit 3: Newton's Laws

Standards: HS-PS2-1; HS-PS2-4

| Skill | 1 Beginning | 2 Developing | 3 Proficient | 4 Advanced |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Draw free-body diagrams** | Draws motion arrows or forces on the wrong object | Draws most forces but mislabels or missizes them | Draws every force on one object, labeled and sized correctly | Draws diagrams for connected objects or ramps and explains each force |
| **Apply the second law** | Uses a single force instead of the net force | Finds net force but makes unit or direction errors | Finds net force and solves F_net = m·a with units and a sense check | Combines F = ma with kinematics in a multi-step problem |
| **Use the first and third laws** | Says motion needs a force; says the bigger object pushes harder | States the laws but misapplies them to cases | Explains constant-velocity situations and identifies action-reaction pairs | Uses lab data to argue that a ∝ F_net and a ∝ 1/m (HS-PS2-1) |

### Unit 4: Momentum

Standards: HS-PS2-2; HS-PS2-3; HS-ETS1-2 / HS-ETS1-3

| Skill | 1 Beginning | 2 Developing | 3 Proficient | 4 Advanced |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Calculate momentum and impulse** | Confuses momentum with mass or force | Calculates p = mv but ignores direction | Calculates momentum, impulse, and Δp with signs and units | Uses force-time graphs and rebounds to find force or time |
| **Apply conservation of momentum** | Does not treat the two objects as one system | Sets up before = after but makes sign errors | Solves sticking, bouncing, and push-apart problems correctly | Uses lab data to support the claim that momentum is conserved (HS-PS2-2) |
| **Design to reduce collision force** | Cannot connect time and force | States that padding helps without explaining why | Explains force reduction using F·Δt = Δp | Designs, tests, and refines a device against criteria and constraints (HS-PS2-3) |

### Unit 5: Work & Energy

Standards: HS-PS3-1; HS-PS3-2; HS-PS3-3

| Skill | 1 Beginning | 2 Developing | 3 Proficient | 4 Advanced |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Calculate work and power** | Treats any effort as work | Calculates W = F·d but ignores the angle or sign | Calculates work (including zero and negative work) and power with units | Finds work from a force-displacement graph and links it to energy change |
| **Use kinetic and potential energy** | Cannot tell KE from PE | Calculates each but forgets to square the speed or choose a zero height | Calculates KE and PE and applies the work-energy theorem | Predicts how energy and stopping distance scale when speed or height changes |
| **Apply conservation of energy** | Says energy is used up | Writes energy before = energy after but omits a term | Solves for speed or height, including thermal energy from friction | Builds a bar-chart or spreadsheet model of a system (HS-PS3-1, HS-PS3-2) |

Skill key:

- **Unit 1:** (a) describe motion with vectors; (b) interpret motion graphs; (c) solve kinematics problems
- **Unit 2:** (a) resolve and add vectors; (b) explain projectile motion; (c) solve projectile problems
- **Unit 3:** (a) draw free-body diagrams; (b) apply the second law; (c) use the first and third laws
- **Unit 4:** (a) calculate momentum and impulse; (b) apply conservation of momentum; (c) design to reduce collision force
- **Unit 5:** (a) calculate work and power; (b) use kinetic and potential energy; (c) apply conservation of energy

## Semester final

A cumulative test in week 18 with questions from all five units, weighted by instructional time, plus one multi-unit problem (for example, a collision followed by a climb up a ramp, which needs momentum and energy together).
