## Notes page

| Cues | Notes |
|---|---|
| What is work? | W = F·d·cos(θ), θ = angle between force and displacement. Scalar quantity — can be positive, negative, or zero. Units: Joules (J). |
| When is work zero? | When θ = 90° (force ⊥ displacement), or when displacement = 0 (no motion), even if a force is applied (e.g., holding a heavy bag still). |
| When is work negative? | When the force has a component opposite the direction of motion (θ > 90°) — e.g., friction opposing motion, or a decelerating force. |
| Kinetic energy | KE = ½mv². Always ≥ 0. Depends on v², so doubling speed quadruples KE — a very testable relationship. |
| Work-energy theorem | W_net = ΔKE = KE_f − KE_i. Works for varying forces too, as long as W_net accounts for ALL forces' work. |
| Gravitational PE | PE_grav = mgh, relative to a chosen reference height (h = 0 is your choice). |
| Elastic (spring) PE | PE_spring = ½kx², x = displacement from the spring's equilibrium (natural) length. |
| Conservation of mechanical energy | KE_i + PE_i = KE_f + PE_f — ONLY valid when no non-conservative force (friction, applied push, air resistance) does work. |
| What if friction is present? | KE_i + PE_i = KE_f + PE_f + |W_friction| — energy "lost" to friction goes into heat, not literally destroyed (energy is still conserved overall, just not as *mechanical* energy). |
| Power | P = W/t = rate of doing work. For constant force along motion: P = Fv. Units: Watts (J/s). NOT the same as work or energy — power is about how fast energy is transferred. |

## Summary (write in your own words, 3–5 sentences)

*Reference summary: Work is the transfer of energy by a force acting
through a displacement, W = Fd cos(θ), and is zero whenever force and
displacement are perpendicular or there's no displacement at all. The
work-energy theorem connects net work directly to the change in kinetic
energy. Mechanical energy (KE + PE) is conserved only when no
non-conservative force like friction does work — otherwise that energy
"leaks" out as heat or sound and must be accounted for separately. Power
measures how quickly work is done or energy is transferred, which is a
completely different quantity from the total work or energy itself.*
