# Cornell Notes: Work and Energy

Name: ______________________  Period: ______  Date: ____________

**Learning targets:** I can calculate work, kinetic energy, potential energy, and power. I can use conservation of energy to predict speed and height.

**Essential question:** Where does the energy go when a moving object slows down or speeds up?

**How to use these notes:** cover the Notes column, answer each cue aloud, then check.

| Cues / Questions | Notes |
|---|---|
| **1. Work** | |
| What is work in physics, and what are its units? | **W = Fd cos θ**: force times displacement times the cosine of the angle between them. Unit: joule (J) = N·m. Work is a transfer of energy. |
| When is the work done by a force zero? | When there is **no displacement** (holding a heavy box still), or when the force is **perpendicular** to the displacement (carrying a box horizontally at constant velocity: the upward force does no work). |
| When is work negative? | When the force points **opposite** the displacement. Friction and braking do negative work and remove kinetic energy. |
| A sled is pulled 10 m by 100 N at 60° above horizontal. How much work is done? | Only the horizontal part of the force does work.<br>W = (100 N)(10 m)(cos 60°) = (100)(10)(0.5) = **500 J**. |
| **2. Kinetic and potential energy** | |
| What is kinetic energy? | Energy of motion: **KE = ½mv²**. Never negative. |
| What happens to kinetic energy when speed doubles? | It becomes **four times** as large, because speed is squared. Triple the speed, nine times the energy. This is why braking distance at 60 mph is four times the distance at 30 mph. |
| What is gravitational potential energy? | Energy stored by position in a gravitational field: **PE = mgh**, with h measured from a chosen reference level. |
| What does the work-energy theorem say? | **W_net = ΔKE.** The net work on an object equals its change in kinetic energy.<br>Example: a 1000 kg car at 15 m/s has KE = 112,500 J. A 7500 N braking force stops it in d = 112,500 ÷ 7500 = **15 m**. |
| **3. Conservation of energy** | |
| What does conservation of mechanical energy say, and when does it apply? | **KE + PE at one point = KE + PE at another point**, when only gravity (and springs) do work, with no friction or air resistance. |
| A coaster starts from rest 20 m up a frictionless hill. How fast is it at the bottom? (Use g = 10 m/s².) | Top: all PE = mgh. Bottom: all KE = ½mv².<br>mgh = ½mv² → v = √(2gh) = √(2 × 10 × 20) = √400 = **20 m/s**.<br>The mass cancels: a full car and an empty car reach the same speed. |
| Two frictionless ramps have the same height but different steepness. Which gives the greater speed at the bottom? | **Neither.** Speed depends only on the height dropped, v = √(2gh). The steeper ramp gets there sooner, not faster. |
| Where does the energy go when friction acts? | It becomes **thermal energy** (the surfaces warm up). Mechanical energy decreases, but **total energy is conserved**.<br>KE + PE (start) = KE + PE (end) + thermal energy. |
| Where in a pendulum's swing is kinetic energy greatest? | At the **bottom**, where height is lowest and speed is greatest. At the ends of the swing, KE = 0 and PE is greatest. |
| **4. Power** | |
| What is power, and what are its units? | The rate of doing work: **P = W ÷ t**. Unit: watt (W) = J/s. Also P = Fv. |
| Two students with the same mass climb the same stairs, one in half the time. How do their work and power compare? | **Same work** (same mgh). The faster student has **twice the power**. |
| How do you calculate efficiency? | Efficiency = useful energy out ÷ total energy in, as a percent. It is always less than 100% because some energy becomes thermal energy. |
| **5. Labs and measurement** | |
| In the stair-climb lab, what do you measure and calculate? | Measure mass (kg), vertical height of the stairs (m), and time (s). Work = mgh. Power = work ÷ time. Use the *vertical* height, not the length of the staircase. |

**Memory tricks**

- "Double the speed, four times the energy."
- "Energy is never lost, only transferred or transformed."
- Draw energy bar charts for the start and end points before writing the equation.

**Summary**

Work transfers energy when a force acts along a displacement. Kinetic energy depends on mass and the square of speed; gravitational potential energy depends on mass and height. When only gravity does work, the sum of kinetic and potential energy stays constant, which lets us find speeds from heights without knowing the path. Friction converts mechanical energy to thermal energy, but the total is always conserved. Power measures how fast energy is transferred.
