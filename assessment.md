# Assessment, Rubrics & Progress Tracking

## Mastery levels (used in every unit)
| Level | Label | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | Advanced | Applies concepts to new/multi-step situations and explains reasoning. |
| 3 | Proficient (mastery) | Meets the unit objectives with minor errors only. |
| 2 | Developing | Partial understanding; needs support with some objectives. |
| 1 | Beginning | Major gaps; reteach and reassess. |

**Unit mastery** = Level 3+ on the unit assessment **and** interactive quiz ≥ 80%. Students may retake the quiz (questions shuffle) and reassess after completing corrections.

| Category | Weight |
|---|---|

## Rubrics

### Cornell notes (4 pts)
- Cues written as questions, notes concise, in own words (1)
- Key equations with units and variable definitions (1)
- Worked example included (1)
- Summary written after the notes, 2–3 sentences, and self-quiz done by covering the notes column (1)

### Lab report – CER format (12 pts)
| Criterion | Pts |
|---|---|
| Question & hypothesis with variables identified | 2 |
| Data table + graph (labeled axes, units, line of best fit) | 3 |
| Calculations & uncertainty/error sources | 2 |
| **Claim** answers the question | 1 |
| **Evidence** cites specific data/graph values | 2 |
| **Reasoning** links evidence to the physics law | 2 |

### Engineering design challenge (HS-PS2-3, HS-PS3-3) (12 pts)
Criteria and constraints defined (2) · design sketch with physics rationale (2) · test data from ≥ 3 trials (2) · evaluation against criteria (2) · refinement based on data (2) · reflection/trade-offs (2).

### Problem-solving (per problem, 4 pts)
Diagram/FBD & knowns with signs (1) · correct law/equation chosen (1) · algebra & units (1) · answer reasonable, with a sentence conclusion (1).

## Unit assessment blueprint (45–50 min)
- 8 multiple choice (conceptual, drawn from most-missed questions)
- 2 graph interpretation items
- 3 calculation problems (one multi-step)
- 1 CER short response tied to the unit lab

## Semester capstone (choose one)
1. **Roller-coaster model** – design a track, compute v, KE/PE, and g-forces at 4 points; include friction and an energy-flow diagram.
2. **Safe-collision device** – build and test a crumple-zone/cart protector; present force–time data.
3. **Sports physics analysis** – video-analyze a projectile; apply kinematics, forces, and energy.

## Using quiz results
The quiz shows which questions were missed and lists the objective each tests (e.g., "1.3 – v–t graphs"). Students write the objectives they missed in the tracker and reteach themselves from the unit's Cornell notes before retaking.
