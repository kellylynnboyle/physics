# Unit 1: Kinematics

The study of motion — describing *how* something moves without worrying
about *why* (that's Newton's Laws, Unit 3).

## Key concepts students need to master

- **Distance vs. displacement** — distance is a scalar (total path length);
  displacement is a vector (straight-line change in position, direction
  matters).
- **Speed vs. velocity** — speed is a scalar (distance/time); velocity is a
  vector (displacement/time). Average velocity ≠ average speed when
  direction changes.
- **Acceleration** — rate of change of velocity, a vector. Includes
  speeding up, slowing down (deceleration), *and* changing direction.
- **Sign conventions** — choosing a positive direction and sticking to it;
  negative values mean "opposite direction," not "less than zero speed."
- **The kinematic equations** (constant acceleration only):
  - v = v₀ + at
  - Δx = v₀t + ½at²
  - v² = v₀² + 2aΔx
  - Δx = ½(v₀ + v)t
- **Free fall** — a special case of constant acceleration where a = −g
  (≈ 9.8 m/s² near Earth's surface), independent of mass (no air
  resistance).
- **Graphical analysis** — reading position-time graphs (slope = velocity)
  and velocity-time graphs (slope = acceleration, area under curve =
  displacement).

## Learning objectives

By the end of this unit, students will be able to:

1. Distinguish distance from displacement and speed from velocity in a
   given scenario, with correct units.
2. Choose and apply the correct kinematic equation for a constant
   acceleration problem given three known variables to solve for a fourth.
3. Solve free-fall problems (dropped, thrown up, thrown down) including
   time to reach maximum height and total time of flight.
4. Interpret and sketch position-time and velocity-time graphs, including
   identifying regions of positive/negative/zero acceleration.
5. Convert between graphical, verbal, and equation representations of the
   same motion.

## Assessment criteria

- **Quiz target:** ≥ 80% on `quiz.html`, with correct reasoning on the
  sign-convention and graph-interpretation questions specifically (these
  are the most commonly missed).
- **Problem sets:** Full given/find/equation/solve/units setup shown, not
  just a final number.
- **Mastery check:** Given a word problem with no diagram, the student
  draws their own diagram, assigns a sign convention, and picks the
  correct equation without prompting.
