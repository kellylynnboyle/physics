# Kinematics — Practice Problems, Resources & Applications

## Practice problems

### Level 1 — Direct substitution
1. A car accelerates from rest at 3.0 m/s² for 5.0 s. Find its final velocity
   and the distance traveled.
2. A ball is dropped from a 45 m tall building. How long does it take to
   hit the ground? What is its velocity on impact?
3. A cyclist traveling at 8 m/s brakes to a stop over 4.0 s. Find the
   acceleration and the stopping distance.

### Level 2 — Multi-step / choose your equation
4. A rocket sled starts at 20 m/s and accelerates at 5.0 m/s² for 300 m.
   Find its final velocity and how long that takes.
5. A ball is thrown straight up at 15 m/s from ground level. Find (a) time
   to reach max height, (b) max height, (c) total time in the air, (d)
   velocity when it returns to the ground.
6. Two cars start 100 m apart and drive toward each other, Car A at a
   constant 10 m/s and Car B at a constant 15 m/s. When and where do they
   meet?

### Level 3 — Graph-based
7. Sketch a velocity-time graph for a car that speeds up uniformly from 0
   to 20 m/s in 4 s, cruises for 6 s, then brakes uniformly to a stop in
   5 s. Use the graph to find total distance traveled.
8. Given a position-time graph with a curve that starts steep, flattens to
   momentarily horizontal, then goes negative — describe the object's
   velocity and acceleration in words at each stage.

### Challenge
9. A stone is thrown downward with initial velocity 5 m/s from a 100 m
   cliff. At the same instant, a second stone is dropped from rest at the
   same height. How much earlier does the first stone land, and by how
   much lead in velocity?

*(Answer keys are intentionally omitted here — have students show full
given/find/equation/solve/units work and check answers with an instructor
or the quiz in `quiz.html`.)*

## Real-world applications

- **Airbag/seatbelt design**: engineers use kinematic deceleration
  equations to determine how much distance/time a crumple zone needs to
  keep deceleration below injury thresholds.
- **Sports**: sprinters' reaction time + acceleration phase analysis (100 m
  dash splits); a baseball's hang time off a bat.
- **Air traffic control / drones**: computing safe stopping/landing
  distances given approach velocity and deceleration limits.
- **Elevators**: comfortable acceleration/deceleration limits are a
  kinematics design constraint (typically under ~1.5 m/s² for passenger
  comfort).

## Study tips for the most commonly missed question types

- **"Which equation do I use?"** — write down all 5 variables (v₀, v, a,
  Δx, t) and cross out the one *not* given/asked for; that identifies the
  equation.
- **Symmetric free-fall trips** (thrown up, comes back down to the same
  height) — time up equals time down, and speed up equals speed down
  (magnitude), but this symmetry breaks if it lands at a different height
  than it was thrown from.
- **Deceleration is not automatically negative acceleration** — the sign
  of acceleration depends on your chosen positive direction, not on
  whether the object is speeding up or slowing down.
