Nuclear Physics is the capstone unit, connecting the energy-conservation
ideas from Work & Energy (Unit 5) down to the atomic nucleus and back out
to the scale of stars — tying the entire year's physics and Earth science
threads together.

## Key concepts to master

- **Nuclear structure** — protons and neutrons (nucleons) in the nucleus;
  isotopes as atoms of the same element with different neutron counts.
- **Radioactive decay** — alpha decay (helium nucleus emitted), beta decay
  (electron/positron emitted, a neutron/proton converts), and gamma decay
  (high-energy photon emitted, no change in nucleons); each changes the
  nucleus differently.
- **Half-life** — the time for half of a radioactive sample to decay; used
  for radiometric dating of rocks and fossils.
- **Fission vs. fusion** — fission splits a heavy nucleus into lighter
  ones (releases energy, used in nuclear power plants); fusion combines
  light nuclei into a heavier one (releases even more energy per reaction,
  powers the sun and stars).
- **Mass-energy release (qualitative)** — nuclear reactions release far
  more energy per reaction than chemical reactions, because they convert a
  tiny amount of mass directly into energy.
- **Nuclear fusion in stars** — the sun's energy output, and the process by
  which stars fuse light elements into heavier ones over their lifetimes
  (nucleosynthesis).
- **Radiometric dating and Earth's history** — using known half-lives and
  measured isotope ratios in rocks/meteorites to determine Earth's age and
  reconstruct its early history.

## Vocabulary checklist

nucleon · isotope · alpha decay · beta decay · gamma decay · half-life ·
fission · fusion · nucleosynthesis · radiometric dating

## Learning objectives

By the end of this unit, students will be able to:

1. Model the change in nucleus composition and the relative energy released during fission, fusion, and each type of radioactive decay.
2. Distinguish alpha, beta, and gamma decay by what is emitted and how the nucleus changes.
3. Use half-life to solve basic radioactive decay and radiometric-dating problems.
4. Explain why fusion in the sun's core is the ultimate source of the energy that reaches Earth, and why that energy took millions of years to escape the sun's interior.
5. Use radiometric-dating evidence (from rocks, meteorites, or lunar samples) to support a claim about Earth's early history.

## NGSS alignment

**HS-PS1-8.** Develop models to illustrate the changes in the composition
of the nucleus of the atom and the energy released during the processes
of fission, fusion, and radioactive decay. *(Qualitative models only;
limited to alpha, beta, and gamma decay — no quantitative energy
calculations required.)*

**HS-ESS1-1.** Develop a model based on evidence to illustrate the life
span of the sun and the role of nuclear fusion in the sun's core to
release energy that eventually reaches Earth as radiation.

**HS-ESS1-6.** Apply scientific reasoning and evidence from ancient Earth
materials, meteorites, and other planetary surfaces to construct an
account of Earth's formation and early history. Full text:
[`../NGSS-ALIGNMENT.md`](../NGSS-ALIGNMENT.md). *(This unit also sets up
HS-ESS1-2 and HS-ESS1-3 — the Big Bang and stellar nucleosynthesis — as
optional extension content on the origin of the universe.)*

**Suggested lab:** a half-life simulation (dice, coins, or M&Ms as
"radioactive" atoms — each roll/flip represents one half-life) to build
the decay-curve intuition needed before tackling real radiometric-dating
problems.
