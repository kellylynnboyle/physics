## Practice Problems

1. A 10 kg box is pushed 5 m across a floor by a 20 N horizontal force. Find the work done by the applied force.
2. A 10 kg box is pulled 5 m by a rope at 30° above horizontal with 20 N of tension. Find the work done by the tension (careful with the angle).
3. A 2 kg ball is dropped from 10 m. Using conservation of energy (ignore air resistance), find its speed just before hitting the ground.
4. A roller coaster car (500 kg) starts at rest at the top of a 40 m frictionless hill. Find its speed at the bottom.
5. Repeat problem 4, but now friction does −60,000 J of work on the way down. Find the new speed at the bottom.
6. A 1500 W motor lifts a 200 kg elevator car at constant speed. Find that constant speed (hint: at constant velocity, motor power = rate of gaining PE).
7. A spring with k = 200 N/m is compressed 0.3 m and used to launch a 0.5 kg ball. Find the ball's launch speed (assume all elastic PE converts to KE).
8. A 60 kg skateboarder rides down a ramp from 3 m high and reaches the bottom at 6 m/s instead of the frictionless-predicted speed. Calculate how much energy was lost to friction, and explain where that energy went.
9. A pendulum bob swings from a 2 m height down to the bottom of its arc. Explain, using the concept of work, why tension in the string does zero work on the bob throughout the swing.

## Real-World Applications

- **Roller coaster design:** engineers use conservation of energy (with friction/air resistance factored in) to ensure a coaster has just enough energy at the top of each hill to complete the track without a motor
- **Regenerative braking (hybrid/electric cars):** converts a car's kinetic energy back into stored electrical energy during braking instead of losing it all to heat in the brake pads — a direct application of the work-energy theorem
- **Power ratings of appliances/vehicles:** a "2000 W motor" and a "500 W motor" can do the same total work (job), but the 2000 W motor does it 4x faster — this is the practical meaning of power in tool/vehicle specifications
- **Hydroelectric dams:** water's gravitational PE at height converts to KE as it falls, then to electrical energy via turbines — a direct real-world energy transformation chain
- **Archery and springs:** a drawn bow stores elastic PE, converted to the arrow's KE on release — same math as the compressed-spring problem above
