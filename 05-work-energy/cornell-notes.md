# Cornell Notes — Work & Energy

## Page 1: Work

| Cue | Notes |
|---|---|
| Definition | W = F·d·cos(θ), where θ is the angle between the force and the displacement. |
| Zero-work situations | Force perpendicular to motion (θ = 90°) does zero work — e.g., normal force on flat ground, gravity during horizontal motion, tension in circular motion. |
| Everyday-language trap | "Holding something still" does zero PHYSICS work, even though it feels effortful — no displacement means no work. |
| Units | Joules (J) = N·m. Same units as energy, since work IS a transfer of energy. |

## Page 2: Kinetic & Potential Energy

| Cue | Notes |
|---|---|
| Kinetic energy | KE = ½mv² |
| Work-energy theorem | W_net = ΔKE = KE_final − KE_initial (not total KE — the CHANGE). |
| Gravitational PE | PE = mgh. Reference height (h=0) is a free choice — only ΔPE matters physically. |
| Elastic (spring) PE | PE_spring = ½kx² — note the SQUARED relationship to compression/stretch distance x. |

## Page 3: Conservation of Energy & Power

| Cue | Notes |
|---|---|
| Mechanical energy conservation | KE + PE = constant, ONLY when no non-conservative force (friction, air resistance, applied push) does net work. |
| When friction is present | Some mechanical energy converts to heat/sound — total energy is still conserved, just not KE+PE alone. Track it as: KE₁+PE₁ = KE₂+PE₂ + |W_friction|. |
| Power | P = W/t — the RATE of energy transfer, not the amount. Same work done faster = more power, not more energy. |
| Path independence | For conservative forces (gravity, springs), the energy/speed outcome depends only on start/end states (e.g., height), not the path taken to get there. |
