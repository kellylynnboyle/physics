## 1. Work

| Cues / Questions | Notes |
|---|---|
| **What is work in physics?** | Energy transferred to or from an object by a force acting through a displacement: **W = Fd cos θ**, in joules (1 J = 1 N·m). θ is the angle between the force and the displacement. |
| **When does a force do zero work?** | (1) The object does not move (holding a box still). (2) The force is perpendicular to the motion (carrying a tray across a level room, θ = 90°). Feeling tired is not the same as doing work on the object. |
| **When is work negative?** | When the force points opposite to the motion (θ = 180°). Friction and braking do negative work: they take kinetic energy away. |
| **How much work does a 50 N pull at 60° do over 10 m?** | W = Fd cos θ = (50)(10)(cos 60°) = (50)(10)(0.50) = **250 J**. Only the 25 N part of the pull along the motion does work. |
| **How do I find work from a force-displacement graph?** | Work is the **area under the graph**. |

## 2. Kinetic and potential energy

| Cues / Questions | Notes |
|---|---|
| **What is kinetic energy?** | Energy of motion: **KE = ½mv²**. It depends on speed squared, so doubling the speed gives four times the kinetic energy. |
| **What is the kinetic energy of a 1000 kg car at 20 m/s? At 40 m/s?** | ½(1000)(20)² = **200,000 J**. At 40 m/s: ½(1000)(40)² = **800,000 J**. |
| **What is gravitational potential energy?** | Energy stored because of height: **PE = mgh**, with h measured from a zero level you choose (usually the lowest point).<br>Worked: 2.0 kg block 5.0 m up → (2.0)(9.8)(5.0) = **98 J**. |
| **What is elastic potential energy?** | Energy stored in a stretched or compressed spring: PE = ½kx², where k is the spring constant and x is the stretch.<br>Worked: k = 200 N/m, x = 0.10 m → ½(200)(0.10)² = **1.0 J**. |
| **What is mechanical energy?** | The sum of kinetic and potential energy: ME = KE + PE. |

> **IXL practice:** Physics **H.2** Conservation of mechanical energy · extension: **K.1** Hooke's law

## 3. The work-energy theorem

| Cues / Questions | Notes |
|---|---|
| **What does the work-energy theorem say?** | The net work done on an object equals its change in kinetic energy: **W_net = ΔKE = ½mv_f² − ½mv_i²**. |
| **Why does doubling a car's speed make the braking distance four times as long?** | The brakes must remove all the kinetic energy: Fd = ½mv². Twice the speed means four times the KE, so with the same braking force, **four times the distance**. |
| **How far does a 1000 kg car at 20 m/s travel while an 8000 N braking force stops it?** | KE = 200,000 J. Fd = KE → d = 200,000 ÷ 8000 = **25 m**. (At 40 m/s: 800,000 ÷ 8000 = 100 m.) |

> **IXL practice:** Physics **H.1** Calculate work

## 4. Conservation of energy

| Cues / Questions | Notes |
|---|---|
| **What does the law of conservation of energy say?** | Energy cannot be created or destroyed. It can only change form or move from one object to another, so the **total energy of a closed system stays constant**. |
| **When is mechanical energy conserved?** | When there is no friction or air resistance: KE_i + PE_i = KE_f + PE_f. |
| **How fast is a coaster moving at the bottom of a 20 m hill if it starts from rest (no friction)?** | Top: all PE = mgh. Bottom: all KE = ½mv². mgh = ½mv² → v = √(2gh) = √(2 × 9.8 × 20) = **19.8 m/s**.<br>The mass cancels, and only the height matters, not the steepness or shape of the track. |
| **What happens to the "missing" energy when there is friction?** | It is transformed into **thermal energy**; it is not destroyed.<br>Worked: 2.0 kg block starts at 5.0 m (PE = 98 J) and reaches the bottom at 8.0 m/s (KE = 64 J). Thermal energy = 98 − 64 = **34 J**. |
| **Where are KE and PE greatest for a pendulum?** | PE is greatest at the highest points (where the bob stops for an instant). KE is greatest at the lowest point (where it moves fastest). |
| **Three balls are thrown from a cliff at the same speed: up, sideways, and down. Which lands fastest?** | **All the same speed.** They start with the same KE and the same PE, so they end with the same KE. Only the landing times and directions differ. |
| **How does an energy bar chart help?** | Draw bars for KE, PE, and thermal energy at the start and at the end. The total height of the bars must match. It shows where the energy went before you write any equation. |

> **IXL practice:** Physics **H.2** Conservation of mechanical energy

## 5. Power

| Cues / Questions | Notes |
|---|---|
| **What is power?** | The rate of doing work or transferring energy: **P = W / t**, in watts (1 W = 1 J/s). |
| **How are work and power different?** | Work is how much energy is transferred. Power is how fast. Climbing the same stairs in half the time is the same work at twice the power. |
| **What is the power of a 60 kg student who climbs 3.0 m of stairs in 4.0 s?** | W = mgh = (60)(9.8)(3.0) = 1764 J. P = 1764 ÷ 4.0 = **441 W**. |

## 6. Labs and engineering

| Cues / Questions | Notes |
|---|---|
| **How can a lab test conservation of energy?** | Release a cart or pendulum from a measured height and measure its speed at the bottom. Compare mgh at the top with ½mv² at the bottom. A small shortfall is energy transformed to thermal energy by friction and air resistance. |
| **What does a computational energy model do?** | A spreadsheet row for each position calculates PE = mgh, KE = total − PE − thermal, and v = √(2KE/m). Change one input (start height, mass, friction loss) and every other value updates. |
| **How do I measure how well an energy-conversion device works?** | Efficiency = useful energy out ÷ energy in. Worked: 2.0 J of stored spring energy gives a car 1.2 J of kinetic energy → 1.2 ÷ 2.0 = **60%**; the other 0.8 J became thermal energy and sound. |

## Memory tricks

- **Work needs motion along the force:** no displacement, no work; perpendicular force, no work.
- **KE goes with v²:** twice as fast, four times the energy, four times the braking distance.
- **Energy is never used up; it changes form** (friction turns it into thermal energy).
- **Height sets speed:** v = √(2gh) when friction is ignored, whatever the path.
- **Power is work per second.**

## Summary

Work is energy transferred by a force acting through a displacement. That energy shows up as kinetic energy (motion) or potential energy (position), and the net work on an object equals its change in kinetic energy. In any process the total energy stays the same: without friction, kinetic and potential energy trade back and forth, and with friction some mechanical energy becomes thermal energy. Power measures how quickly energy is transferred.
