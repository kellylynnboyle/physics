# Topic 5: Work & Energy — Cornell Notes

## Learning Objectives
- Calculate work done by a force, including cases where force and displacement are not parallel.
- Apply the work–energy theorem to relate net work to change in kinetic energy.
- Calculate gravitational potential energy and elastic (spring) potential energy.
- Apply conservation of mechanical energy, and explain where "lost" energy goes when it isn't conserved.
- Distinguish work, energy, and power.
- Solve problems that combine energy methods with real systems.

---

| Cues / Questions | Notes |
|---|---|
| What is the definition of **work**? | `W = F·d·cosθ`, where `θ` is the angle between the force and the displacement. Work is a **scalar**, measured in Joules (`J = N·m`). |
| When is work **zero**, even though a force is being applied? | When there's no displacement (e.g., pushing on a wall that doesn't move), OR when the force is perpendicular to the displacement (`θ = 90°`, `cos90° = 0`) — e.g., carrying a bag at constant height while walking horizontally: gravity does zero work because it's perpendicular to the horizontal motion. |
| What is **kinetic energy**? | `KE = ½mv²` — the energy of motion, always positive (depends on `v²`, not `v`), scalar. |
| What is the **work–energy theorem**? | `W_net = ΔKE = KE_f − KE_i`. The NET work done on an object (by all forces combined) equals its change in kinetic energy. This works even when individual forces are complicated, as long as you use the total net work. |
| What is **gravitational potential energy**? | `PE_grav = mgh`, where `h` is height relative to a chosen reference level. Only *changes* in PE matter physically — the reference level is arbitrary and can be chosen for convenience. |
| What is **elastic (spring) potential energy**? | `PE_spring = ½kx²`, where `k` is the spring constant and `x` is the displacement from the spring's natural (unstretched/uncompressed) length. |
| What is **conservation of mechanical energy**? | `KE_i + PE_i = KE_f + PE_f`, valid when only **conservative forces** (gravity, ideal springs) do work — i.e., no friction or air resistance. Mechanical energy (`KE + PE`) stays constant. |
| What happens to energy when **friction** is present? | Mechanical energy is NOT conserved — some is converted to heat (thermal energy) and sometimes sound, via the negative work friction does. Total energy (including thermal) is still conserved — energy is never actually destroyed, just converted to a form that's no longer "useful" mechanical energy. |
| What's the difference between **work**, **energy**, and **power**? | Work/energy (Joules) measure an amount of "stuff transferred/stored." Power (Watts, `W = J/s`) measures the *rate* at which that transfer happens: `P = W/t` (or instantaneously, `P = Fv`). Two engines can do the same total work, but the more powerful one does it faster. |
| How do you handle a problem with **both energy conservation and friction**? | Use `KE_i + PE_i = KE_f + PE_f + W_friction`, where `W_friction = f·d` is the (positive) magnitude of energy lost to friction — effectively treating it as "leaking" out of the mechanical energy total. |
| When should you use **energy methods** instead of Newton's Second Law? | Energy methods are often much faster when you don't need time or when forces vary with position (like a spring) — you can skip finding acceleration entirely and jump straight from initial to final states. |

---

## Key Vocabulary
**Conservative force** (path-independent work, e.g., gravity, springs) · **Non-conservative force** (path-dependent, e.g., friction) · **Mechanical energy** (`KE + PE`) · **Spring constant `k`** (N/m, stiffness) · **Watt** (unit of power, `1 W = 1 J/s`) · **Efficiency** (useful energy output ÷ total energy input)

## Summary
Energy methods give a scalar (direction-free) alternative to force methods,
built around one central idea: net work changes kinetic energy, and total
energy is always conserved even when *mechanical* energy isn't. The most
common trap is forgetting the `cosθ` factor when force and displacement
aren't parallel, or claiming energy "disappears" instead of tracking where
non-conservative forces like friction actually send it (heat/sound).
