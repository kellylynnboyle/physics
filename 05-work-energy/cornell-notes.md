# Cornell Notes: Work & Energy

| Cues / Questions | Notes |
|---|---|
| What is the formula for work? | **W = Fd cos θ**, where F is the applied force, d is the displacement magnitude, and θ is the angle between the force and displacement vectors. Work is measured in joules (J = N·m) and is a **scalar** — it has no direction. |
| When is work zero? | Work is zero when: (1) there is **no displacement** (e.g., holding a heavy box stationary, or pushing on a wall that doesn't move), or (2) the force is **perpendicular** (θ = 90°) to the displacement, since cos 90° = 0. Examples: the normal force on someone walking on a flat floor, gravity on a satellite in circular orbit, the centripetal force in any circular motion. |
| When is work negative? | Work is negative when the force has a component **opposite** the displacement (90° < θ ≤ 180°), so cos θ < 0. Example: kinetic friction acting on a sliding block always does negative work on the block, because friction opposes the relative motion. |
| When is work positive? | Work is positive when the force has a component in the **same direction** as displacement (0° ≤ θ < 90°). Example: gravity does positive work on a falling object. |
| What is kinetic energy? | **KE = ½mv²** — the energy an object has because of its motion. Measured in joules. Because velocity is *squared*, KE grows much faster than speed: **doubling v quadruples KE** (2² = 4); tripling v gives 9× the KE. |
| What is gravitational potential energy? | **PE = mgh**, where h is height above a chosen reference level. The reference point (h = 0) is **arbitrary** — you can put it at the floor, the tabletop, or sea level. Only **changes** in PE (ΔPE = mgΔh) have physical meaning; the "absolute" PE value has no significance by itself. |
| What is elastic potential energy? | **PE = ½kx²**, where k is the spring constant (N/m, a measure of stiffness) and x is the displacement from the spring's natural, unstretched/uncompressed length. Stored in stretched or compressed springs and other elastic objects. |
| What is the work-energy theorem? | **W_net = ΔKE** — the *net* work done on an object (adding up the work from every force acting on it, positive and negative) equals the change in its kinetic energy. This holds even if individual forces do positive or negative work; only the *total* matters for changing speed. |
| When is mechanical energy conserved? | **Mechanical energy (KE + PE) is conserved only when non-conservative forces do no net work** — i.e., only conservative forces like gravity or an ideal spring act. In that case: KE_i + PE_i = KE_f + PE_f. |
| What role does friction (a non-conservative force) play? | Friction and air resistance **remove** mechanical energy from a system by converting it to **heat** (thermal energy) and sometimes sound. Mechanical energy **decreases**, but energy is never destroyed — **total** energy (mechanical + heat + other forms) is still conserved. When friction acts: KE_i + PE_i = KE_f + PE_f + E_heat(lost). |
| What is power? | **P = W/t = Fv** — the *rate* at which work is done or energy is transferred, measured in watts (W = J/s). Power is **not** the same as work or energy: doing the *same* amount of work in *less* time requires *more* power, not more work or energy. |

## Summary

Work is done on an object only when a force causes displacement, and only the component of force parallel to that displacement counts — forces perpendicular to motion, or forces acting with no displacement at all, do zero work. The work-energy theorem ties this directly to motion: the net work from all forces equals the change in an object's kinetic energy (½mv²), which grows with the square of speed. Potential energy — gravitational (mgh) or elastic (½kx²) — is stored energy tied to position or configuration, and because only differences in PE matter, the zero-height reference can be chosen anywhere for convenience. When only conservative forces act, total mechanical energy is conserved and trades freely between kinetic and potential forms; when friction or air resistance is present, mechanical energy is not conserved because some of it is converted to heat, though total energy always is. Power measures how quickly energy is transferred or work is done, separating "how much" (work/energy) from "how fast" (power).
