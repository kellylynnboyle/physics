# Cornell Notes — Work & Energy

**Topic:** Work, Kinetic/Potential Energy, and Conservation of Energy | **Date:** ___________ | **Name:** ___________

| Cues / Questions | Notes |
|---|---|
| What is the formula for work, and what does the cos θ mean? | W = Fd cos θ. Only the component of force **parallel** to the displacement does work. If force is perpendicular to motion (θ = 90°), cos θ = 0 and the force does **zero** work — no matter how big the force is. |
| Why does carrying a bag horizontally do zero work against gravity? | Gravity acts straight down; the bag's displacement is horizontal. The angle between them is 90°, so cos 90° = 0 → W_gravity = 0, even though you feel tired holding it up. |
| What is the work-energy theorem? | W_net = ΔKE. The *total* (net) work done on an object equals its change in kinetic energy — this is often a faster route to a speed than kinematics + forces separately. |
| What is kinetic energy? | KE = ½mv². Always positive (v² can't be negative), scalar, depends on speed squared — doubling speed quadruples KE. |
| What is gravitational potential energy? | PE_grav = mgh, measured relative to a chosen reference height (you pick where h = 0; only *changes* in PE matter physically). |
| What is elastic (spring) potential energy? | PE_spring = ½kx², where x is the displacement **from the spring's equilibrium (natural) length** — not the spring's total length. |
| When is mechanical energy (KE + PE) conserved? | Only when **no non-conservative forces** (friction, air resistance, applied forces) do work on the system. Gravity and springs alone: energy just converts between KE and PE, total stays constant. |
| What happens to energy when friction is present? | Mechanical energy is NOT conserved — friction removes energy from the KE+PE system, converting it into heat. Must track this as "energy lost" in the energy equation: KE₁+PE₁ = KE₂+PE₂+|W_friction|. |
| How is power different from work/energy? | Power = work/time (Watts), i.e., the *rate* of doing work. Two motors can do the same amount of work (Joules) but have very different power if one takes longer. Don't confuse W (Joules) with P (Watts). |

## Summary

Work and energy provide a second toolkit for motion problems, often
simpler than juggling forces and kinematics directly. The core rule for
work is W = Fd cos θ — a force perpendicular to displacement does zero
work, which surprises students in "carrying a bag" type scenarios. The
work-energy theorem (W_net = ΔKE) ties work directly to a change in speed.
Mechanical energy (KE + PE) is conserved only when no friction or other
non-conservative force is doing work; when friction IS present, that energy
doesn't vanish — it becomes heat, and problems must explicitly subtract it
out. Power (W/t, in Watts) is a distinct concept from energy (Joules) —
rate vs. amount.
