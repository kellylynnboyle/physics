## Topic: Defining Work

| Cues / Questions | Notes |
|---|---|
| Equation | W = Fd cos θ, where θ is the angle between the force and the displacement. |
| When is work zero? | When there's no displacement (pushing on a wall that doesn't move), OR when the force is perpendicular to the displacement (e.g., carrying a bag at constant height while walking — the support force is vertical, motion is horizontal). |
| Everyday "work" vs. physics "work" | In everyday language, effort = work. In physics, holding a heavy box still (no displacement) does **zero** work, even though it's tiring. |

## Topic: Kinetic Energy & the Work-Energy Theorem

| Cues / Questions | Notes |
|---|---|
| Kinetic energy equation | KE = ½mv² |
| Work-energy theorem | W_net = ΔKE — the net work done on an object equals its change in kinetic energy. |
| What if net work is negative? | Kinetic energy decreases — the object slows down (e.g., friction doing negative work on a sliding box). |

## Topic: Potential Energy

| Cues / Questions | Notes |
|---|---|
| Gravitational PE | PE_g = mgh — always measured relative to a chosen reference height (h = 0 is your choice, not fixed by nature). |
| Elastic PE | PE_s = ½kx² — energy stored in a stretched or compressed spring, x = displacement from natural length. |
| Does PE depend on path taken? | No — gravitational PE depends only on height, not on the path used to get there (gravity is a conservative force). |

## Topic: Conservation of Mechanical Energy

| Cues / Questions | Notes |
|---|---|
| When does it apply? | Only when no non-conservative forces (friction, air resistance, applied pushes) are doing work on the system. |
| Equation | KE_i + PE_i = KE_f + PE_f |
| What happens to energy when friction IS present? | Mechanical energy (KE + PE) is not conserved — some converts to thermal energy (heat) and sound. Total energy (including heat) is still conserved, but "mechanical" energy specifically decreases. |
| Common phrase to avoid | Avoid saying energy is "lost" — it's converted to another form, not destroyed (energy is always conserved overall). |

## Topic: Power

| Cues / Questions | Notes |
|---|---|
| Definition | P = W/t — the rate at which work is done (or energy is transferred). |
| Units | Watts (W) = Joules/second. |
| Two motors do the same amount of work, but one finishes faster. Which has more power? | The faster one — same work, less time, means higher power, even though total energy transferred is identical. |
