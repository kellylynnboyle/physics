# Cornell Notes: Work and Energy

**Topic/Date:** _____________

| Cue / Question | Notes |
|---|---|
| What is the formula for work, and what does each variable mean? | W = F d cos(θ). W = work (J); F = magnitude of the applied force (N); d = magnitude of the displacement (m); θ = angle between the force vector and the displacement vector. Only the component of force parallel to the displacement (F cos θ) actually does work. |
| Why does a perpendicular force do zero work? | When θ = 90°, cos(90°) = 0, so W = 0 regardless of how large the force is. Example: carrying a bag at constant height — gravity points straight down, the motion is horizontal, so gravity does zero work on the bag even though you feel effort holding it up. |
| What is kinetic energy, and how does it depend on speed? | KE = (1/2) m v². KE = kinetic energy (J); m = mass (kg); v = speed (m/s). Because v is squared, doubling speed quadruples KE, not doubles it. A light, fast object can have more KE than a heavy, slow one. |
| What is gravitational potential energy, and what is it measured relative to? | PE = m g h. PE = gravitational potential energy (J); m = mass (kg); g = 9.8 m/s² near Earth's surface; h = height above a chosen reference level. PE is never absolute — it depends on which height you call "zero," though changes in PE between two points are meaningful regardless of that choice. |
| What does the work-energy theorem say? | W_net = ΔKE. The net (total) work done by all forces on an object equals the change in its kinetic energy (KE_final − KE_initial). This links force-based and energy-based descriptions of motion. |
| What does it mean for a force to do negative work? | Negative work happens when the force (or a component of it) points opposite to the displacement — cos θ is negative. It removes energy from the object rather than adding it. Friction and air resistance typically do negative work on a moving object, slowing it down. |
| What is conservation of mechanical energy, and when does it apply? | KE_i + PE_i = KE_f + PE_f. This holds when there are no non-conservative forces (like friction or air resistance) doing work on the system — mechanical energy (KE + PE) stays constant, even as it converts between kinetic and potential forms. |
| Is energy still conserved when there IS friction? | Yes — total energy is always conserved. Friction converts mechanical energy into thermal energy (heat) and sometimes sound; it does not destroy energy. "Mechanical energy" only appears to decrease because the heat/sound isn't being counted as part of it. |
| What is power, and how is it calculated? | P = W / t = F v. P = power (W, watts); W = work (J); t = time (s); F = force (N); v = speed (m/s). Power measures how quickly energy is transferred or work is done — the same amount of work done faster means more power, not more work or more energy. |
| How are work, energy, and power different from one another? | Work is energy transferred by a force through a displacement. Energy is the capacity a system has to do work (stored as KE, PE, thermal, etc.). Power is the rate at which work is done or energy is transferred. They have related but distinct units: work/energy in joules (J), power in watts (W = J/s). |

## Summary

Work is the bridge between force and energy: it is done only when a force has a component along the direction of displacement, which is why perpendicular forces (like gravity on a horizontally-carried bag) or motionless efforts (like holding a weight still) do zero work. The work-energy theorem ties this directly to motion, showing that the net work on an object equals its change in kinetic energy, while gravitational potential energy stores the same kind of capacity based on height above a chosen reference level. When no friction or air resistance is present, kinetic and potential energy trade off but their sum — mechanical energy — stays constant; when friction is present, mechanical energy seems to shrink, but it is actually being converted into heat and sound, so total energy is always conserved. Finally, power separates "how much" energy is transferred from "how fast" it is transferred, which is essential for comparing engines, athletes, and machines that might do the same work in very different amounts of time.
