# Unit 5 — Work & Energy

The final mechanics unit reframes motion problems using energy instead of
forces and time directly. Energy methods are often faster than Newton's
laws for problems involving changing speeds over a distance, and this
unit ties together every previous unit into one capstone toolkit.

## Key concepts to master

- **Work** — `W = Fd cosθ`, where θ is the angle between the force and the
  displacement. Work is zero if force and displacement are perpendicular,
  or if there's no displacement at all (no matter how large the force).
- **Work-energy theorem** — the net work done on an object equals its
  change in kinetic energy: `W_net = ΔKE`.
- **Kinetic energy** — `KE = ½mv²`, energy of motion.
- **Gravitational potential energy** — `PE_grav = mgh`, energy stored due
  to height in a gravitational field (relative to a chosen reference height).
- **Elastic potential energy** (conceptual/intro) — energy stored in a
  stretched or compressed spring, `PE_spring = ½kx²`.
- **Conservation of mechanical energy** — `KE + PE = constant` **only**
  when no non-conservative forces (like friction or air resistance) do
  work on the system. When friction is present, mechanical energy
  decreases (converted to heat).
- **Power** — `P = W/t`, the rate at which work is done or energy is
  transferred; not the same thing as work or energy itself.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this unit, students will be able to:

1. Calculate work done by a force, correctly accounting for the angle between force and displacement, including cases where work is zero or negative.
2. Apply the work-energy theorem to relate net work to a change in kinetic energy.
3. Calculate gravitational potential energy relative to a stated reference point, and recognize that only changes in PE matter physically.
4. Apply conservation of mechanical energy to solve motion problems (e.g., roller coasters, pendulums, falling objects) when friction is negligible.
5. Correctly account for energy "lost" to friction as work done by a non-conservative force, and explain that total energy is still conserved (it becomes heat) even though mechanical energy decreases.
6. Distinguish power from work/energy, and calculate power for a given work and time interval.

## Assessment criteria

| Skill | Meets expectations |
|---|---|
| Work calculations | Correctly applies `W = Fd cosθ`, including recognizing zero-work scenarios (perpendicular force, no displacement) |
| Work-energy theorem | Uses `W_net = ΔKE` correctly to find final speed, force, or distance |
| Energy conservation (no friction) | Sets up `KE_i + PE_i = KE_f + PE_f` correctly across changes in height/speed |
| Energy with friction present | Correctly identifies that mechanical energy decreases and accounts for the "missing" energy as heat, without claiming energy disappears |
| Power vs. energy | Clearly distinguishes power (rate) from work/energy (total amount) in both calculations and written explanations |
