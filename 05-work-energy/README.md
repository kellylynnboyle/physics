# Unit 5: Work & Energy

**Big question:** Where does energy go when things speed up, slow down, rise, or fall?

**Suggested length:** 4 weeks · **Materials:** [Cornell notes](cornell-notes.md) · [Most-missed quiz](../quizzes/index.html) · [Objectives and rubric](../assessment/learning-objectives.md)

## Key concepts to master

- [ ] **Work:** W = Fd cos θ; only the force along the motion does work
- [ ] **Kinetic energy:** KE = ½mv²
- [ ] **Gravitational potential energy:** PE = mgh
- [ ] **Elastic potential energy:** PE = ½kx² (extension)
- [ ] **Work-energy theorem:** W_net = ΔKE
- [ ] **Conservation of energy:** energy changes form or moves between objects; the total stays constant
- [ ] **Friction and thermal energy:** "lost" mechanical energy becomes thermal energy
- [ ] **Power:** P = W / t

## Equations

| Equation | Meaning |
|----------|---------|
| W = Fd cos θ | work, in joules (1 J = 1 N·m); θ is the angle between force and displacement |
| KE = ½mv² | kinetic energy |
| PE = mgh | gravitational potential energy (h measured from a chosen zero level) |
| W_net = ΔKE | work-energy theorem |
| KE_i + PE_i = KE_f + PE_f + E_thermal | conservation of energy (E_thermal = 0 without friction) |
| P = W / t | power, in watts (1 W = 1 J/s) |

## Practice problems

Answers are at the bottom of this page. Use g = 9.8 m/s².

1. How much work is done lifting a 10 kg box straight up 2.0 m at constant speed?
2. A sled is pulled 10 m with a 50 N force directed 60° above the horizontal. How much work does the force do?
3. You carry a 20 kg box 10 m across a level room at constant velocity. How much work does your upward force do on the box?
4. Find the kinetic energy of a 1000 kg car at 20 m/s and at 40 m/s.
5. A 0.50 kg ball is dropped from 10 m. How fast is it moving just before it lands (no air resistance)?
6. A roller coaster starts from rest at the top of a 20 m hill. How fast is it moving at the bottom (no friction)?
7. A 2.0 kg block slides from rest down a 5.0 m high ramp and reaches the bottom at 8.0 m/s. How much energy was transformed to thermal energy?
8. A 60 kg student climbs 3.0 m of stairs in 4.0 s. Find the student's power.
9. A 1500 W motor lifts a 100 kg load for 10 s. How high does the load rise?
10. A car's speed doubles from 15 m/s to 30 m/s. By what factor does its braking distance change, with the same braking force?

## Real-world applications

- **Roller coasters:** the first hill sets the energy budget for the whole ride
- **Hydroelectric dams and wind turbines:** converting potential and kinetic energy to electrical energy
- **Regenerative braking:** capturing a car's kinetic energy instead of turning it into thermal energy
- **Speed limits:** braking distance grows with the square of speed
- **Food and exercise:** Calories are energy; power output in climbing or cycling
- **Earth science link:** energy stored in deformed rock is released in earthquakes; Earth's internal thermal energy drives plate motion (Semester 2)

## Answers to practice problems

1. W = mgh = (10)(9.8)(2.0) = 196 J
2. W = (50)(10) cos 60° = 250 J
3. 0 J. The force is vertical and the displacement is horizontal (cos 90° = 0).
4. ½(1000)(20)² = 200,000 J; ½(1000)(40)² = 800,000 J (four times as much)
5. v = √(2gh) = √(2 × 9.8 × 10) = 14 m/s
6. v = √(2 × 9.8 × 20) = 19.8 m/s ≈ 20 m/s
7. PE at top = (2.0)(9.8)(5.0) = 98 J; KE at bottom = ½(2.0)(8.0)² = 64 J; thermal = 98 − 64 = 34 J
8. W = (60)(9.8)(3.0) = 1764 J; P = 1764 ÷ 4.0 = 441 W
9. W = Pt = (1500)(10) = 15,000 J = mgh → h = 15,000 ÷ (100 × 9.8) = 15 m
10. 4 times as long. KE is four times as large, and Fd = ½mv².
