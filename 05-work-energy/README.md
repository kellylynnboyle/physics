# Unit 5: Work & Energy

The third major toolkit for analyzing motion — often the fastest route to an answer when you only care about speeds and heights, not the full time-history of forces.

## Key Concepts to Master

- **Work**: `W = F·d·cos(θ)` — only the force component *along* the displacement does work
- **Zero-work cases**: forces perpendicular to displacement (normal force on a flat surface, gravity during purely horizontal motion) do zero work
- **Kinetic energy**: `KE = ½mv²`
- **Work-energy theorem**: `W_net = ΔKE`
- **Gravitational potential energy**: `PE = mgh`, with an arbitrary but consistent reference height
- **Elastic potential energy**: `PE_spring = ½kx²`
- **Conservation of mechanical energy**: `KE + PE = constant`, valid only when non-conservative forces (friction, air resistance, applied force) do zero net work
- **Non-conservative forces and energy loss**: where "lost" mechanical energy actually goes (heat, sound, deformation)
- **Power**: `P = W/t` — the rate of doing work, distinct from the amount of work itself

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this unit, students should be able to:

1. Calculate work done by a force, correctly accounting for the angle between force and displacement.
2. Apply the work-energy theorem to relate net work and change in kinetic energy.
3. Apply conservation of mechanical energy to solve problems with gravitational and/or elastic potential energy.
4. Identify when non-conservative forces are present and adjust energy calculations accordingly (energy "lost" to heat).
5. Distinguish work (energy transferred) from power (rate of energy transfer) in calculations and explanations.

## Practice Problems

1. A 20 N force pulls a box 5 m across the floor at an angle of 30° above horizontal. Find the work done by the applied force.
2. A 2 kg object accelerates from 3 m/s to 7 m/s. Find the net work done on it using the work-energy theorem.
3. A 1500 kg roller coaster car starts at rest at the top of a 40 m hill (frictionless track). Find its speed at the bottom.
4. The same roller coaster car now experiences friction that does −150,000 J of work over the same descent. Find its actual speed at the bottom.
5. A spring with k = 200 N/m is compressed 0.3 m and used to launch a 0.5 kg block. Find the block's launch speed, assuming all elastic PE converts to KE.
6. A motor lifts a 50 kg crate 10 m straight up in 8 seconds. Find the power output of the motor.

## Real-World Applications

- **Regenerative braking in electric/hybrid vehicles** — converting kinetic energy back into stored (electrical) energy instead of losing it all to heat
- **Roller coaster design** — hill heights are engineered using energy conservation, accounting for friction/air resistance losses
- **Pile drivers and hydraulic presses** — converting gravitational PE or spring PE into useful work
- **Household energy bills** — power (kW) vs. energy (kWh) is exactly the work/power distinction from this unit
