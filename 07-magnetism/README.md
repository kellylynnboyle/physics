Magnetism completes the electricity-and-magnetism pair: this unit shows
that electricity and magnetism aren't separate phenomena but two faces of
the same underlying interaction — a moving charge creates a magnetic
field, and a changing magnetic field creates an electric current.

## Key concepts to master

- **Magnetic field** — a field that exerts a force on moving charges and
  other magnets; field lines run from north to south outside a magnet.
- **Electromagnetism** — an electric current produces a magnetic field
  around the current-carrying wire (right-hand rule for direction).
- **Electromagnetic induction** — a *changing* magnetic field induces an
  electric current in a nearby conductor (the basis of generators).
- **Generators and motors** — a generator converts mechanical energy to
  electrical energy via induction; a motor runs the same principle in
  reverse (electrical → mechanical), directly connecting back to the
  Work & Energy unit's energy-conversion ideas.
- **Field-based force and energy model** — describing the interaction
  between two objects through electric or magnetic fields, including how
  the objects' energy changes as they interact.

## Vocabulary checklist

magnetic field · electromagnet · induction · generator · motor ·
right-hand rule · field interaction

## Learning objectives

By the end of this unit, students will be able to:

1. Explain how an electric current produces a magnetic field, and how a changing magnetic field produces an electric current.
2. Plan and conduct an investigation demonstrating electromagnetic induction.
3. Explain how a generator converts mechanical energy into electrical energy, and connect this to conservation of energy (Unit 5).
4. Model the forces and energy changes between two objects interacting through electric or magnetic fields.
5. Identify real-world technologies (generators, MRI machines, maglev trains, induction cooktops) that rely on electromagnetic induction.

## NGSS alignment

**HS-PS2-5.** Plan and conduct an investigation to provide evidence that
an electric current can produce a magnetic field and that a changing
magnetic field can produce an electric current. *(Limited to designing and
conducting investigations with provided materials and tools.)*

**HS-PS3-5.** Develop and use a model of two objects interacting through
electric or magnetic fields to illustrate the forces between objects and
the changes in energy of the objects due to the interaction. Full text:
[`../NGSS-ALIGNMENT.md`](../NGSS-ALIGNMENT.md).

**Suggested lab:** build a simple electromagnet (wire coil + battery +
nail) and test how current and coil turns affect its strength; then
demonstrate induction by moving a magnet through a coil connected to a
galvanometer.
