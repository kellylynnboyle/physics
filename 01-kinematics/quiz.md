# Unit 1 — Kinematics — Self-Check Quiz

Built from the questions students miss most often on kinematics tests. Try each question yourself before revealing the answer — click **"Show answer"** to check.

**How to use:** Get 80%+ (8/10) before moving on. Re-read the linked note section for anything you miss.

---

**1.** A person walks 6 m east, then 6 m west. What is their total displacement? What is their total distance traveled?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Displacement = 0 m** (they ended where they started — displacement only cares about start/end position).
**Distance = 12 m** (total path length, regardless of direction).

*Most-missed reason:* students average the two legs or report 6 m for displacement instead of recognizing the return trip cancels it out.
</details>

---

**2.** A ball is thrown straight up. What is its acceleration at the exact peak of its trajectory?

- A) 0 m/s²
- B) 9.8 m/s², downward
- C) 9.8 m/s², upward
- D) depends on the throw speed

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**B) 9.8 m/s², downward.**

*Most-missed reason:* students confuse velocity = 0 at the peak with acceleration = 0. Gravity acts the entire flight — it's what causes the ball to slow, stop, and reverse.
</details>

---

**3.** On a velocity-time graph, what does the **slope** represent? What does the **area under the curve** represent?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Slope = acceleration.** **Area under the curve = displacement.**

*Most-missed reason:* students mix up slope and area between the position-time and velocity-time graphs. Memory trick: on an *x-t* graph, slope = v. On a *v-t* graph, slope = a AND area = x.
</details>

---

**4.** An object has a velocity of −4 m/s and an acceleration of −2 m/s². Is the object speeding up or slowing down?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Speeding up.** Velocity and acceleration point in the *same* direction (both negative), so the object is gaining speed in the negative direction.

*Most-missed reason:* students assume "negative acceleration" always means slowing down. Rule: same sign on v and a → speeding up; opposite signs → slowing down.
</details>

---

**5.** A car starts from rest and accelerates at 4 m/s² for 5 s. How far does it travel?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

Use x = x₀ + v₀t + ½at², with v₀ = 0:
x = ½(4)(5²) = ½(4)(25) = **50 m**

*Most-missed reason:* forgetting the ½, or plugging final velocity into the equation for v₀ by mistake.
</details>

---

**6.** True or False: An object can have zero velocity and nonzero acceleration at the same time.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**True.** Example: a ball thrown upward has v = 0 at the peak but a = −9.8 m/s² throughout. Another example: a car at the instant it switches from moving forward to reversing.

*Most-missed reason:* students think "not moving" means "no acceleration" — but acceleration describes the *change* in velocity, not velocity itself.
</details>

---

**7.** Which kinematic equation would you use if you know v₀, a, and Δx, and need to find v (but don't know t)?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**v² = v₀² + 2a(x − x₀)** — the only kinematic equation that doesn't involve time.

*Most-missed reason:* students default to v = v₀ + at even when t isn't given, and then can't solve.
</details>

---

**8.** A position-time graph shows a straight line with negative slope. What does this tell you about the object's motion?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

The object moves with **constant velocity in the negative direction** (constant speed, moving back toward/past the origin). A straight line on an x-t graph always means constant velocity — the steepness just tells you the speed, and the sign tells you the direction.

*Most-missed reason:* students think a straight-line x-t graph means "not moving" or try to read acceleration directly off it (that requires curvature, not slope).
</details>

---

**9.** Two cars start at the same position. Car A moves at a constant 20 m/s. Car B starts from rest and accelerates at 4 m/s². Set up (don't need to fully solve) the equation you'd use to find when they meet.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

Set their position equations equal: 20t = ½(4)t² → 20t = 2t².

*Most-missed reason:* forgetting Car A's position equation is just x = vt (constant velocity, no acceleration term) while Car B needs the full x = ½at² (starts from rest, so v₀ = 0 drops out the middle term).
</details>

---

**10.** A rock falls from a cliff and hits the ground after 4 s. How tall is the cliff? (use g = 9.8 m/s², ignore air resistance)

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

x = ½gt² = ½(9.8)(4²) = ½(9.8)(16) = **78.4 m**

*Most-missed reason:* using t instead of t², or using g = 9.8 without squaring time correctly (½ × 9.8 × 16, not ½ × 9.8 × 4).
</details>
