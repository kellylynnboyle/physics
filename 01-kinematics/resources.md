# Kinematics — Resources, Practice, and Applications

## Learning resources

- **OpenStax College Physics** (free textbook) — Chapter 2, "Kinematics." openstax.org/details/books/college-physics-2e
- **Khan Academy** — "One-dimensional motion" unit (videos + practice sets with instant feedback). khanacademy.org, search "1D motion"
- **PhET Interactive Simulations (University of Colorado Boulder)** — "Moving Man" simulation: drag a figure and watch live position/velocity/acceleration graphs generate. Excellent for building graph intuition. phet.colorado.edu, search "Moving Man"
- **The Physics Classroom** — "1-D Kinematics" tutorial with built-in check-your-understanding questions. physicsclassroom.com/class/1DKin
- **CrashCourse Physics (YouTube), Episodes 1–4** — fast conceptual overview of motion, velocity, and acceleration.

## Practice problems

Work these without a calculator first to build number sense, then check with one.

1. A runner covers 400 m around a track and ends up back at the starting line. Find (a) distance traveled and (b) displacement.
2. A car accelerates from rest at 3.0 m/s² for 6.0 s. Find its final velocity and the distance covered.
3. A ball is thrown straight up at 15 m/s. Find (a) the time to reach maximum height, (b) the maximum height, (c) the total time until it returns to the throwing point.
4. A cyclist traveling at 8.0 m/s brakes to a stop over 4.0 s. Find the acceleration and the stopping distance.
5. From a velocity–time graph showing a line from (0 s, 2 m/s) to (5 s, 12 m/s): find the acceleration and the displacement over those 5 seconds.
6. A stone is dropped from a 45 m cliff. Find the time to hit the ground and the impact velocity.
7. **Conceptual:** A car's speedometer reads a constant 60 km/h while going around a circular curve. Is the car accelerating? Explain using the definition of velocity as a vector.
8. **Conceptual:** Sketch a velocity–time graph for a ball thrown straight up, from the moment it leaves the hand to the moment it's caught at the same height. Label where acceleration is nonzero.

Answer keys are intentionally left for instructors to provide or for students to verify using the kinematics equations in `cornell-notes.md` — working backward from a given answer is itself good practice.

## Real-world applications

- **Airbag/crumple-zone design** — engineers use kinematics (and later, momentum) to calculate how quickly a car must decelerate to keep forces on passengers survivable.
- **Sports** — a sprinter's 100 m race is analyzed with position–time and velocity–time graphs to find reaction time, acceleration phase, and top speed.
- **Air traffic control** — controllers use velocity and displacement calculations to maintain safe separation between aircraft closing in on each other.
- **Amusement park ride design** — drop towers and roller coasters are modeled with free-fall and constant-acceleration equations to hit target speeds and g-forces.
- **Meteorology** — hail and raindrop terminal velocity calculations build on free-fall kinematics before introducing air resistance.

## Suggested student task

Pick **one** application above (or propose your own), and in 2–3 sentences
explain which kinematics quantity (position, velocity, or acceleration) is
most important to that application and why. This satisfies the "real-world
