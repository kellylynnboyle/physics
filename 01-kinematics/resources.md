## Practice Problems

1. A car accelerates from rest at 3 m/s² for 6 seconds. Find its final velocity and the distance traveled.
2. A ball is thrown straight up at 20 m/s. Find the time to reach maximum height, the maximum height, and the total time in the air.
3. A train traveling at 30 m/s brakes to a stop over 100 m. Find its acceleration (should be negative).
4. Sketch the velocity-time graph for a car that speeds up from 0 to 20 m/s in 5 s, cruises for 10 s, then brakes to a stop in 4 s. From your graph, find the total displacement.
5. Two runners start 100 m apart, moving toward each other — Runner A at 4 m/s, Runner B at 6 m/s. When and where do they meet?
6. A rock is dropped from a 45 m cliff. How long does it take to hit the ground, and what is its velocity at impact?
7. A position-time graph shows a straight line from (0 s, 2 m) to (4 s, 14 m). Find the velocity, and write the position equation x(t).
8. An object's velocity-time graph is a triangle: velocity rises linearly from 0 to 10 m/s over 5 s, then drops linearly back to 0 over the next 5 s. Find total displacement and the acceleration during each phase.

*(Answer keys are intentionally omitted here — have students show full work with units, then check answers together in class or against a solution set you provide.)*

## Real-World Applications

- **Traffic engineering:** stopping-distance calculations for speed limits and yellow-light timing use the exact same equations as problem 3 above
- **Sports analytics:** sprinters' velocity-time graphs (acceleration phase vs. top-speed phase) are used to analyze race strategy
- **Elevators and roller coasters:** ride designers use kinematic equations to keep acceleration within comfortable/safe limits
- **Astronomy:** free-fall equations (with a different g) describe motion on the Moon or Mars, letting students recompute problem 2 or 6 for other worlds
