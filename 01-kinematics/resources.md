# Kinematics Resources

## Learning Resources

- **Khan Academy — Kinematics unit** (khanacademy.org): Free video lessons and practice problem sets covering position, displacement, velocity, acceleration, and the kinematic equations, with instant feedback and progress tracking. Search "Khan Academy one-dimensional motion" within the Physics course.
- **PhET Interactive Simulations — "Moving Man"** (phet.colorado.edu): An interactive simulation from the University of Colorado Boulder where students drag a figure along a track and watch live position, velocity, and acceleration graphs update in real time — excellent for building graph-interpretation intuition.
- **The Physics Classroom — Kinematics tutorial** (physicsclassroom.com): A text-based tutorial with clear explanations, worked examples, and self-check "Check Your Understanding" questions covering 1-D kinematics, free fall, and graphing motion.
- **PhET Interactive Simulations — "Free Fall" / "Projectile Motion"** (phet.colorado.edu): Simulations that let students drop or launch objects and directly observe that acceleration due to gravity is independent of mass.
- **Textbook chapter topics to review**: Look for chapters titled "Motion in One Dimension," "Describing Motion: Kinematics in One Dimension," or "One-Dimensional Kinematics" in any standard algebra-based high school or introductory college physics textbook (e.g., OpenStax College Physics, which is freely available at openstax.org, has a full open-access chapter on this topic).
- **Graphing practice**: Any graphing calculator or spreadsheet tool (e.g., Desmos at desmos.com) can be used to plot position-time and velocity-time data and practice reading slope and area.

## Practice Problems

**1. (Distance vs. displacement — introductory)**
A student walks 6 m east, then 2 m west. Find the total distance traveled and the displacement.
Answer: Distance = 8 m. Displacement = 4 m east.

**2. (Average speed — introductory)**
A car travels 150 km in 3.0 hours. What is its average speed in km/h and in m/s?
Answer: Average speed = 150 km / 3.0 h = 50 km/h. Converting: 50 km/h × (1000 m/km) / (3600 s/h) ≈ 13.9 m/s.

**3. (Average velocity with direction reversal — intermediate)**
A cyclist rides 12 m east in 4 s, then 12 m west in 6 s, ending back at the start. Find (a) the average speed and (b) the average velocity for the entire 10 s trip.
Answer: (a) Total distance = 24 m; time = 10 s; average speed = 2.4 m/s. (b) Total displacement = 0 m (back at start); average velocity = 0 m/s.

**4. (Acceleration from velocity change — intermediate)**
A car accelerates from 10 m/s to 26 m/s in 4.0 seconds. Find its acceleration.
Answer: a = (v − v₀)/t = (26 − 10)/4.0 = 4.0 m/s².

**5. (Kinematic equation selection — intermediate)**
A skateboarder starts from rest and accelerates at 2.0 m/s² for 5.0 s. How far does she travel?
Answer: Known: v₀ = 0, a = 2.0 m/s², t = 5.0 s; unknown: Δx. Use Δx = v₀t + ½at² = 0 + ½(2.0)(5.0)² = ½(2.0)(25) = 25 m.

**6. (Free fall — intermediate/advanced)**
A ball is dropped from rest from a height of 44.1 m. Using g = 9.8 m/s² and ignoring air resistance, how long does it take to hit the ground, and what is its velocity just before impact?
Answer: Using Δx = v₀t + ½at² with v₀ = 0: 44.1 = ½(9.8)t² → t² = 9.0 → t = 3.0 s. Then v = v₀ + at = 0 + (9.8)(3.0) = 29.4 m/s.

**7. (Vertical toss, sign convention — advanced)**
A ball is thrown straight upward with an initial velocity of 19.6 m/s. Taking up as positive and g = −9.8 m/s², find (a) the time to reach maximum height and (b) the maximum height reached.
Answer: (a) At maximum height, v = 0. Using v = v₀ + at: 0 = 19.6 + (−9.8)t → t = 2.0 s. (b) Using Δx = v₀t + ½at²: Δx = (19.6)(2.0) + ½(−9.8)(2.0)² = 39.2 − 19.6 = 19.6 m.

**8. (Graph interpretation — advanced)**
A velocity-time graph shows an object's velocity increasing linearly from 0 to 20 m/s over the first 5 s (a straight sloped line), then remaining constant at 20 m/s for the next 5 s (a horizontal line). Find (a) the acceleration during the first 5 s, (b) the acceleration during the second 5 s, and (c) the total displacement over the full 10 s using the area under the graph.
Answer: (a) a = Δv/Δt = (20 − 0)/5 = 4.0 m/s². (b) a = 0 m/s² (constant velocity, flat line). (c) Area = triangle (first 5 s) + rectangle (next 5 s) = ½(5)(20) + (5)(20) = 50 + 100 = 150 m.

## Real-World Applications

- **Airbag deployment timing**: Airbag sensors must detect a sudden, large negative acceleration (rapid deceleration) during a collision and trigger deployment within milliseconds. Engineers use kinematic principles to calculate exactly how much time is available between impact and when a passenger's head would otherwise strike the steering wheel, so the airbag can inflate in time to cushion the impact.
- **Stopping distance for cars**: A driver's total stopping distance combines reaction-time distance (traveled at constant velocity before braking) and braking distance (found using kinematic equations with a negative acceleration). This is why stopping distance increases dramatically — not just linearly — with speed, since braking distance depends on velocity squared (v² = v₀² + 2aΔx).
- **Sprinter acceleration analysis**: Coaches and sports scientists analyze a sprinter's position-time and velocity-time data from race footage to find their acceleration off the blocks and top instantaneous speed, helping identify whether a runner should focus training on acceleration phase or top-speed maintenance.
- **Rocket launch phases**: Rocket engineers use kinematic equations to plan each flight phase — the accelerating powered-ascent phase, the coasting phase after engine cutoff, and the descent phase — predicting velocity and altitude at each point in time to time stage separations and orbital insertion correctly.
- **Elevator and amusement park ride design**: Engineers calculate the acceleration and velocity profiles of elevators and thrill rides (like drop towers) so that riders experience specific, safe accelerations — free fall style drops rely directly on g = 9.8 m/s² to predict speed and position throughout the ride.
