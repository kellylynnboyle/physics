## Topic: Distance, Displacement, Speed, Velocity

| Cues / Questions | Notes |
|---|---|
| What's the difference between distance and displacement? | Distance = total path length traveled (scalar, always positive). Displacement = straight-line change in position, start to end (vector, can be negative). |
| Can displacement be zero while distance is not? | Yes — e.g., walking 10 m east then 10 m west: distance = 20 m, displacement = 0 m. |
| Speed vs. velocity? | Speed = distance/time (scalar). Velocity = displacement/time (vector, has direction). |
| Average vs. instantaneous velocity? | Average = total displacement ÷ total time. Instantaneous = velocity at one specific moment (slope of position–time graph at a point). |

## Topic: Acceleration

| Cues / Questions | Notes |
|---|---|
| Definition of acceleration | a = Δv / Δt — the rate of change of velocity, not of speed. |
| Does negative acceleration always mean "slowing down"? | No. Sign of acceleration only tells direction. An object slows down when velocity and acceleration have **opposite** signs, and speeds up when they have the **same** sign — regardless of which is "positive." |
| Can an object have zero velocity and nonzero acceleration? | Yes — e.g., a ball thrown straight up has v = 0 at the top of its path, but a = −g the entire time. |

## Topic: The Kinematic Equations (constant acceleration only)

| Cues / Questions | Notes |
|---|---|
| v = v₀ + at | Solve for final velocity when displacement is unknown/not needed. |
| Δx = v₀t + ½at² | Solve for displacement when final velocity is unknown/not needed. |
| v² = v₀² + 2aΔx | Solve for velocity or displacement when time is unknown/not needed. |
| Δx = ½(v₀ + v)t | Solve for displacement (or time) when acceleration is unknown/not needed. |
| When do these equations NOT apply? | Only valid for **constant acceleration**. Not valid if acceleration changes during the interval (e.g., most real friction/air-resistance situations). |

## Topic: Motion Graphs

| Cues / Questions | Notes |
|---|---|
| Position–time graph | Slope = velocity. Curved line = changing velocity (acceleration present). Flat line = at rest. |
| Velocity–time graph | Slope = acceleration. Area between line and time-axis = displacement (area below axis counts negative). |
| Acceleration–time graph | Area under the curve = change in velocity. |
| A v–t graph crosses zero — what's happening physically? | The object is momentarily changing direction (velocity sign flips), not necessarily stopping "for good." |

## Topic: Free Fall

| Cues / Questions | Notes |
|---|---|
| What is g? | Acceleration due to gravity near Earth's surface, ≈ 9.8 m/s², directed downward, constant (ignoring air resistance). |
| Does a heavier object fall faster? | No — in the absence of air resistance, all objects fall with the same acceleration g regardless of mass. |
| An object thrown straight up — what is its acceleration at the very top? | Still −g (downward). Velocity is zero at the top, but acceleration is never zero during free fall. |
