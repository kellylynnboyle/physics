**Learning targets:** I can describe motion with words, graphs, and equations. I can solve constant-acceleration and free-fall problems.

**Essential question:** How can we predict where an object will be and how fast it will be moving?

**How to use these notes:** cover the Notes column, answer each cue aloud, then check.

| Cues / Questions | Notes |
|---|---|
| **1. Describing motion** | |
| What is the difference between distance and displacement? | **Distance** is the total path length (scalar, never negative). **Displacement** is the change in position, Δx = x_final − x_initial (vector, has direction).<br>Example: walk 5 m east then 3 m west. Distance = 8 m. Displacement = 2 m east. |
| What is the difference between speed and velocity? | **Speed** = distance ÷ time. **Velocity** = displacement ÷ time, and includes direction.<br>One full lap of a 400 m track in 80 s: average speed = 5 m/s, average velocity = 0 m/s because the displacement is zero. |
| What does acceleration measure? | **Acceleration** = change in velocity ÷ time, a = Δv ÷ Δt, in m/s².<br>It is a vector. An object accelerates if it speeds up, slows down, or changes direction. |
| When does an object slow down? | When velocity and acceleration point in **opposite** directions. When they point the **same** way, it speeds up.<br>A negative acceleration does not always mean slowing down: a car moving in the negative direction with negative acceleration is speeding up. |
| **2. Motion graphs** | |
| What does the slope of a position-time graph tell you? | The **velocity**. Straight line = constant velocity. Curved line = changing velocity (acceleration). Flat line = at rest. Steeper = faster. |
| What does the slope of a velocity-time graph tell you? | The **acceleration**. Flat line = constant velocity (zero acceleration), not "at rest" unless the line sits on v = 0. |
| What does the area under a velocity-time graph tell you? | The **displacement**.<br>Worked example: velocity rises steadily from 0 to 12 m/s in 4 s. Area of triangle = ½ × 4 s × 12 m/s = **24 m**. |
| What does it mean when two lines cross on a position-time graph? | The objects are at the **same position** at that time. They do not have the same velocity unless the slopes match. |
| **3. Constant-acceleration equations** | |
| What are the four kinematic equations? | v = v₀ + at<br>Δx = v₀t + ½at²<br>v² = v₀² + 2aΔx<br>Δx = ½(v₀ + v)t<br>They are valid only when acceleration is constant. |
| How do you choose which equation to use? | List the five variables (Δx, v₀, v, a, t). Mark the three you know and the one you want. Pick the equation that leaves out the fifth. |
| How far does a car travel if it starts from rest and accelerates at 3 m/s² for 4 s? | Knowns: v₀ = 0, a = 3 m/s², t = 4 s. Want Δx.<br>Δx = v₀t + ½at² = 0 + ½(3)(4)² = **24 m**.<br>Sense check: final speed is 12 m/s, average speed is 6 m/s, and 6 m/s × 4 s = 24 m. |
| **4. Free fall** | |
| What is the acceleration of an object in free fall? | **g = 9.8 m/s² downward**, for every object, when air resistance is negligible. Mass does not matter: a bowling ball and a marble dropped together land together. |
| What are the velocity and acceleration at the top of a straight-up throw? | Velocity = 0 for an instant. Acceleration is still **9.8 m/s² downward**. If acceleration were zero, the ball would stay at the top. |
| A ball is thrown up at 14.7 m/s. How high does it go? | Take up as positive. v₀ = +14.7 m/s, v = 0 at the top, a = −9.8 m/s².<br>v² = v₀² + 2aΔx → 0 = 14.7² + 2(−9.8)Δx → Δx = 216 ÷ 19.6 = **11 m**.<br>Time up = 14.7 ÷ 9.8 = 1.5 s, and the same 1.5 s to come down. |
| **5. Labs and measurement** | |
| In the buggy lab, which variable is independent and which is dependent? | Independent: **time** (x-axis). Dependent: **position** (y-axis). The slope of the best-fit line is the buggy's velocity in m/s. |

**Memory tricks**

- Slope of position-time is velocity. Slope of velocity-time is acceleration. Area under velocity-time is displacement.
- "Same signs speed up, opposite signs slow down" (for velocity and acceleration).
- List knowns and the unknown before picking an equation.

**Summary**

Motion is described by position, velocity, and acceleration, each measured relative to a chosen reference frame. Graphs carry the same information as equations: slopes give rates and the area under a velocity-time graph gives displacement. When acceleration is constant, four equations connect displacement, velocity, acceleration, and time. Free fall is the most common case, with a = 9.8 m/s² downward at every point of the trip.
