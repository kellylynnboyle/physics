## Cues / Questions | Notes

| Cues / Questions | Notes |
|---|---|
| What's the difference between distance and displacement? | Distance = total path length traveled (scalar, always ≥ 0). Displacement = Δx = x_final − x_initial (vector, can be + / − / 0). A round trip has distance > 0 but displacement = 0. |
| What's the difference between speed and velocity? | Speed = distance/time (scalar). Velocity = displacement/time (vector). Average speed can be greater than the magnitude of average velocity if the path isn't straight. |
| How do I read velocity off a position–time graph? | Velocity = **slope** of the position–time graph. Steeper slope = greater speed. Flat line = at rest. Negative slope = moving in the negative direction. |
| How do I read acceleration off a velocity–time graph? | Acceleration = **slope** of the velocity–time graph. |
| How do I find displacement from a velocity–time graph? | Displacement = **area** between the line and the time axis (area below the axis counts negative). |
| What does a negative acceleration actually mean? | It means acceleration points in the negative direction — nothing more. Whether the object speeds up or slows down depends on whether velocity and acceleration have the **same sign** (speeding up) or **opposite signs** (slowing down). |
| What are the four kinematics equations, and when do I use each? | See table below — pick the equation that's missing the variable you don't have and don't need. |
| How is free fall different from other 1D motion? | It's just 1D motion with a fixed acceleration, `a = −g = −9.8 m/s²` (choosing up as positive). Works identically whether the object starts moving up, down, or from rest. |
| At the top of a "thrown upward" trajectory, what is true? | Velocity = 0 for an instant. **Acceleration is still −9.8 m/s²** — it never stops being gravity's pull. This is the #1 missed idea in this unit. |

## The Four Kinematics Equations (constant acceleration only)

| Equation | Missing variable | Use when you don't have / don't need |
|---|---|---|
| `v = v₀ + at` | Δx (displacement) | You don't need displacement |
| `Δx = v₀t + ½at²` | v (final velocity) | You don't have/need final velocity |
| `v² = v₀² + 2aΔx` | t (time) | You don't have/need time |
| `Δx = ½(v₀ + v)t` | a (acceleration) | You don't have/need acceleration |

**Sign convention:** pick one direction as positive (usually "up" or "the
direction of initial motion") and stay consistent for every term in every
equation for the whole problem.

## Summary

Kinematics describes *how* something moves using position, velocity, and
acceleration — all of which (except position magnitude and speed) are
vectors whose sign encodes direction. Motion graphs and the four
constant-acceleration equations are two equivalent toolkits for the same
information: a graph's slope/area relationships mirror what the equations
calculate algebraically. The single highest-value mental model for this
unit: **velocity tells you where you're headed right now; acceleration
tells you how velocity is changing — they are independent, and their signs
can agree or disagree.**
