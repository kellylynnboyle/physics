## Notes page

| Cues | Notes |
|---|---|
| What is position? | Position is the location of an object relative to a chosen reference point (origin). Symbol: **x**. Units: meters (m). |
| Distance vs. displacement? | Distance = total path length traveled (scalar, always positive). Displacement = Δx = x_f − x_i (vector, can be negative). *Example: walk 5 m east then 3 m west → distance = 8 m, displacement = 2 m east.* |
| Speed vs. velocity? | Speed = distance / time (scalar). Velocity = displacement / time (vector). Average velocity: v̄ = Δx / Δt. |
| What is acceleration? | Rate of change of velocity: a = Δv / Δt. Units: m/s². Can be positive or negative depending on chosen positive direction — **not** inherently "speeding up" or "slowing down." |
| When is an object speeding up vs. slowing down? | Speeding up: velocity and acceleration have the **same sign**. Slowing down: velocity and acceleration have **opposite signs**. |
| The four kinematic equations (constant a only) | 1) v_f = v_i + at  2) Δx = v_i t + ½at²  3) v_f² = v_i² + 2aΔx  4) Δx = ½(v_i + v_f)t |
| How to choose which equation to use? | List your knowns and unknown. Pick the equation missing the variable you don't have/need. |
| Position–time graph: what does slope mean? | Slope of x–t graph = velocity. Steeper slope = faster. Flat = at rest. Curved = accelerating. |
| Velocity–time graph: slope and area? | Slope of v–t graph = acceleration. **Area under** the v–t curve = displacement. |
| Acceleration–time graph: area? | Area under a–t curve = change in velocity (Δv). |
| Free fall — what's constant? | Acceleration is constant at g ≈ 9.8 m/s² downward, for *any* object in free fall (ignoring air resistance), regardless of mass. |
| Free fall at the top of a throw | Velocity = 0 momentarily, but acceleration is still −g (not zero!). This is the single most commonly missed idea in the unit. |
| Sign convention | Choose "up" or "the direction of initial motion" as positive at the start of a problem, and keep every vector quantity (position, velocity, acceleration) consistent with that choice throughout. |

*Reference summary: Kinematics describes motion using position, velocity,
and acceleration without worrying about force. Displacement and velocity are
vectors (direction matters); distance and speed are scalars (magnitude
only). Under constant acceleration, the four kinematic equations connect
v_i, v_f, a, Δx, and t — pick the one missing the variable you don't have.
On motion graphs, slope tells you the next-higher derivative (position →
velocity → acceleration) and area under the curve tells you the
next-lower integral. Free fall is just kinematics with a = −g, constant for
every object regardless of mass, even at the instant velocity is zero.*
