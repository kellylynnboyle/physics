## 1. Describing motion

| Cues / Questions | Notes |
|---|---|
| **What is the difference between distance and displacement?** | **Distance** is the total path length (scalar: size only). **Displacement** is the straight-line change in position, Δx = x_final − x_initial, with a direction (vector).<br>Example: walk 30 m east then 40 m west → distance 70 m, displacement 10 m west. |
| **What is the difference between speed and velocity?** | **Speed** = distance ÷ time (scalar). **Velocity** = displacement ÷ time (vector, has direction).<br>A full lap of a track: average speed is not zero, average velocity is zero. |
| **How do I calculate average velocity?** | v = Δx / Δt, in m/s.<br>Worked: 150 m north in 30 s → v = 150 ÷ 30 = **5.0 m/s north**. |
| **What makes a quantity a vector?** | It has size **and** direction. Vectors: displacement, velocity, acceleration, force. Scalars: distance, speed, time, mass. In one dimension, direction is shown with a + or − sign. |

> **IXL practice (goal: SmartScore 80+):** Physics **C.1** Classify vectors and scalars · **D.1** Distance and displacement in one dimension · **D.2** Speed and velocity in one dimension

## 2. Acceleration

| Cues / Questions | Notes |
|---|---|
| **What is acceleration?** | The rate at which velocity changes: a = Δv / Δt = (v − v₀) / t, in m/s². "3 m/s²" means the velocity changes by 3 m/s every second. |
| **How can I tell whether an object is speeding up or slowing down?** | Compare the signs of v and a. **Same sign → speeding up. Opposite signs → slowing down.**<br>v = −8 m/s, a = −2 m/s² → speeding up (in the negative direction). Negative acceleration does not always mean slowing down. |
| **Can an object have zero velocity but non-zero acceleration?** | Yes. A ball at the top of its flight has v = 0 for an instant but a = 9.8 m/s² downward. Acceleration tells how velocity is changing, not how big it is. |
| **Can an object move fast with zero acceleration?** | Yes. A jet cruising at a steady 250 m/s in a straight line has a = 0, because its velocity is not changing. |

> **IXL practice:** Physics **D.3** Acceleration in one dimension

## 3. Motion graphs

| Cues / Questions | Notes |
|---|---|
| **What does the slope of a position-time graph tell me?** | The **velocity**. Horizontal line = at rest. Straight sloped line = constant velocity. Curve getting steeper = speeding up. |
| **What does the slope of a velocity-time graph tell me?** | The **acceleration**. Horizontal line = constant velocity (a = 0). Straight sloped line = constant acceleration. |
| **What does the area under a velocity-time graph tell me?** | The **displacement**.<br>Worked: velocity rises steadily from 0 to 12 m/s in 4 s. Area = triangle = ½ × 4 × 12 = **24 m** (not 48 m; the object was not at 12 m/s the whole time). |
| **What does it mean when two lines cross on a position-time graph?** | The two objects are at the **same position at the same time** (one passes the other). It does not mean they have the same speed; compare slopes for that. |

> **IXL practice:** no Physics-level IXL skill covers motion graphs. Use The Physics Classroom Concept Builders for position-time and velocity-time graphs and PhET *The Moving Man*.

## 4. Kinematic equations (constant acceleration)

| Cues / Questions | Notes |
|---|---|
| **What are the four kinematic equations?** | (1) v = v₀ + at<br>(2) Δx = v₀t + ½at²<br>(3) v² = v₀² + 2aΔx<br>(4) Δx = ½(v₀ + v)t<br>They work only when acceleration is constant. |
| **How do I pick the right equation?** | List the five variables (Δx, v₀, v, a, t). You know three and want one. Pick the equation that leaves out the fifth variable you neither know nor need. |
| **What steps do I follow to solve a problem?** | **GUESS:** **G**ivens (with signs and units) → **U**nknown → **E**quation → **S**ubstitute → **S**olve, then a sense check. "Starts from rest" means v₀ = 0. "Comes to a stop" means v = 0. |
| **How far does a car go if it starts from rest and accelerates at 3.0 m/s² for 6.0 s?** | G: v₀ = 0, a = 3.0 m/s², t = 6.0 s. U: Δx. E: Δx = v₀t + ½at².<br>S: Δx = 0 + ½(3.0)(6.0)² = **54 m**. Final velocity: v = 0 + (3.0)(6.0) = 18 m/s.<br>Check: average velocity 9 m/s × 6 s = 54 m ✓ |
| **How far does a car travel while braking from 25 m/s to rest at −5.0 m/s²?** | G: v₀ = 25 m/s, v = 0, a = −5.0 m/s². U: Δx. No time given → E: v² = v₀² + 2aΔx.<br>S: 0 = 25² + 2(−5.0)Δx → Δx = 625 ÷ 10 = **62.5 m**. |

> **IXL practice:** Physics **D.4** Solve problems using kinematic equations

## 5. Free fall

| Cues / Questions | Notes |
|---|---|
| **What is free fall?** | Motion with gravity as the only force (no air resistance). The acceleration is **g = 9.8 m/s² downward** for every object, whether it is moving up, moving down, or momentarily stopped at the top. |
| **Does a heavier object fall faster?** | No. Without air resistance all objects fall with the same acceleration. Gravity pulls harder on a heavier object, but the object also has more mass to accelerate. |
| **How far and how fast does an object fall in 2.0 s from rest?** | Take down as positive: v₀ = 0, a = 9.8 m/s², t = 2.0 s.<br>d = ½(9.8)(2.0)² = **19.6 m**; v = (9.8)(2.0) = **19.6 m/s** downward.<br>Distance goes with t²: twice the time gives four times the distance. |
| **How high does a ball thrown upward at 14.7 m/s go, and how long does it take to get there?** | Take up as positive: v₀ = +14.7 m/s, a = −9.8 m/s², v = 0 at the top.<br>Time: t = (0 − 14.7) ÷ (−9.8) = **1.5 s**. Height: Δx = (0 − 14.7²) ÷ (2 × −9.8) = **11 m**.<br>Coming down takes the same 1.5 s, and it returns to the launch height at 14.7 m/s downward. |

> **IXL practice:** Physics **D.4** Solve problems using kinematic equations (free-fall problems are included there)

## 6. Labs and measurement

| Cues / Questions | Notes |
|---|---|
| **What are the independent and dependent variables in a motion lab?** | The **independent variable** is the one you choose or control (usually time, on the x-axis). The **dependent variable** is the one you measure (position or velocity, on the y-axis). |
| **How do I find velocity or acceleration from lab data?** | Graph the data, draw a best-fit line, and find its slope using two points on the line that are far apart. Slope of position-time = velocity. Slope of velocity-time = acceleration. |
| **How do I find g from a drop experiment?** | From rest, h = ½gt², so g = 2h / t². Worked: h = 1.20 m, t = 0.50 s → g = 2.40 ÷ 0.25 = **9.6 m/s²** (close to 9.8; timing error explains the gap). |

> **IXL practice:** Physics **A.2** Identify independent and dependent variables · **B.1** Identify significant figures in measurements · **B.4** Calculations with significant figures

## Memory tricks

- **GUESS** for every calculation: Givens, Unknown, Equation, Substitute, Solve.
- **Graph rule:** position → (slope) → velocity → (slope) → acceleration; area under velocity-time takes you back to displacement.
- **Same signs speed up, opposite signs slow down** (signs of v and a).
- **Rest means zero:** "from rest" → v₀ = 0; "stops" → v = 0; "at the top" → v = 0 but a = 9.8 m/s² down.

## Summary

Kinematics describes motion without asking what causes it. Position, velocity, and acceleration are linked: velocity is how fast position changes, and acceleration is how fast velocity changes. Graphs show the same links through slope and area. When acceleration is constant, four equations connect displacement, initial velocity, final velocity, acceleration, and time, and free fall is the special case where the acceleration is 9.8 m/s² downward.
