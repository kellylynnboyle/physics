**Topic:** Describing motion in one dimension

## Cue Column | Notes

| Cue Column (questions/keywords) | Notes |
|---|---|
| What's the difference between **distance** and **displacement**? | **Distance** = total path length traveled (scalar, always positive). **Displacement** (Δx) = straight-line change in position, *from start to end* (vector, can be negative). A runner who does a 400 m lap has distance = 400 m but displacement = 0. |
| What's the difference between **speed** and **velocity**? | **Speed** = distance/time (scalar). **Velocity** = displacement/time (vector, signed). Average speed and average velocity magnitude are equal only if motion is one-directional. |
| What is **acceleration**? | Rate of change of velocity: a = Δv/Δt. Units: m/s². Acceleration is a vector — it can point opposite to velocity (deceleration/slowing down) or in the same direction (speeding up). |
| Does negative acceleration always mean "slowing down"? | **No.** Negative acceleration means the acceleration vector points in the negative direction. An object slows down when velocity and acceleration have *opposite* signs, and speeds up when they have the *same* sign — regardless of which is "positive." |
| What are the **"big four" kinematic equations**? (constant acceleration only) | 1) v = v₀ + at 2) Δx = v₀t + ½at² 3) v² = v₀² + 2aΔx 4) Δx = ½(v₀ + v)t — each equation is missing exactly one of the five variables (Δx, v₀, v, a, t). Pick the equation missing the variable you don't have and aren't solving for. |
| How do you read a **position-time graph**? | Slope = velocity. A straight line = constant velocity. A curve = changing velocity (acceleration). The steeper the slope, the faster the object is moving. A horizontal line = at rest. |
| How do you read a **velocity-time graph**? | Slope = acceleration. Area under the curve (between the line and the t-axis) = displacement. A line above the axis = positive velocity; below = negative velocity (moving backward). |
| What is **free fall**? | Motion under gravity alone, a = −9.8 m/s² (taking up as positive), or g = 9.8 m/s² downward. Applies the instant an object is released, including at the very top of its path when v = 0 (acceleration is NOT zero at the peak — only velocity is). |
| What sign convention should I use? | Pick **one** direction as positive *before* you start (usually "up" or "the direction of initial motion") and apply it consistently to position, velocity, and acceleration for the entire problem. |

---

## Summary

Kinematics describes *how* something moves without asking *why* (that's Newton's Laws, next topic). The core skill is keeping displacement, velocity, and acceleration as **signed vector quantities** and choosing the kinematic equation that matches the variable you're missing. Graphs and equations describe the same motion — slope and area let you move between position, velocity, and acceleration graphs without re-deriving equations. Free fall is just constant acceleration with a = g, including through the top of a toss where velocity is momentarily zero but acceleration never is.
