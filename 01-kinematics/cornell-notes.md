# Cornell Notes — Kinematics

**Topic:** Describing Motion in One Dimension | **Date:** ___________ | **Name:** ___________

| Cues / Questions | Notes |
|---|---|
| What's the difference between distance and displacement? | **Distance** = total path length traveled (scalar, always positive). **Displacement (Δx)** = straight-line change in position, with direction (vector, can be negative). A runner doing a 400 m lap has distance = 400 m but displacement = 0. |
| Speed vs. velocity? | **Speed** = distance / time (scalar). **Velocity** = displacement / time (vector). Average speed ≥ magnitude of average velocity, and they're equal only for straight-line motion in one direction. |
| What does acceleration mean if it's negative? | Acceleration is the *rate of change of velocity*, not "speeding up." Negative acceleration means velocity is decreasing (or pointing negative) — it can mean slowing down OR speeding up in the negative direction. |
| What are the 4 kinematic equations, and when do they apply? | v = v₀ + at  •  Δx = v₀t + ½at²  •  v² = v₀² + 2aΔx  •  Δx = ½(v₀+v)t. **All 4 only work when acceleration is constant.** Pick the equation that's missing the variable you don't have. |
| What is "free fall"? | Motion under gravity alone, a = −g ≈ −9.8 m/s² (choosing up as positive). Applies to anything thrown up, dropped, or in projectile flight, as long as air resistance is ignored. |
| At the top of a ball's toss, is acceleration zero? | **No.** Velocity is momentarily zero at the peak, but acceleration is still −9.8 m/s² the entire flight — gravity never turns off. |
| How do I read a position–time graph? | **Slope of x–t graph = velocity.** Steeper slope = faster. Flat = at rest. Negative slope = moving in the negative direction. Curved line = acceleration (changing velocity). |
| How do I read a velocity–time graph? | **Slope of v–t graph = acceleration. Area under a v–t graph = displacement.** A straight line = constant acceleration. A horizontal line = constant velocity (zero acceleration). |
| Common sign convention pitfall? | Pick a positive direction *before* starting the problem (usually: right/up = positive) and stay consistent for velocity, displacement, AND acceleration for the whole problem. |

## Summary

Kinematics describes motion using position, velocity, and acceleration —
without asking what caused it. The key skill is keeping vector quantities
(displacement, velocity, acceleration) distinct from their scalar cousins
(distance, speed) and tracking sign/direction consistently. The four
kinematic equations only apply under **constant acceleration**, and graphs
encode the same information as the equations: slope and area are the tools
for reading them. Free fall is just kinematics with a fixed acceleration of
g ≈ 9.8 m/s² — remember that gravity acts the whole time, even when velocity
passes through zero at the top of a toss.
