# Topic 1: Kinematics — Practice, Resources & Applications

## Practice Problems

Work each problem before checking the answer key. Show your work — the
rubric in `PROGRESS_TRACKER.md` grades reasoning, not just final numbers.

1. A cyclist accelerates from rest at `2.5 m/s²` for `6 s`. What is her final velocity and how far did she travel?
2. A car traveling at `20 m/s` brakes with a constant deceleration of `4 m/s²`. How long does it take to stop, and how far does it travel while stopping?
3. A ball is thrown straight up at `15 m/s`. How high does it go, and how long is it in the air before returning to the launch height? (Use `g = 9.8 m/s²`.)
4. A train travels at a constant `30 m/s` for `10 s`, then accelerates at `1.5 m/s²` for `8 s`. Find the total distance traveled.
5. Sketch a velocity–time graph for a car that speeds up uniformly from `0` to `20 m/s` in `5 s`, holds that speed for `10 s`, then brakes to a stop over `4 s`. From your sketch, find the total distance traveled.
6. A stone is dropped from a `45 m` tall bridge. How long does it take to hit the water, and what is its velocity on impact?
7. Two cars start `100 m` apart and drive toward each other, one at a constant `15 m/s` and the other at a constant `10 m/s`. How long until they meet, and where?

### Answer Key
1. `v = 15 m/s`; `Δx = 45 m` (using `v = v₀+at` then `Δx = v₀t + ½at²`)
2. `t = 5 s`; `Δx = 50 m` (using `v = v₀+at`, then `Δx = (v₀+v)/2 · t`)
3. Max height `≈ 11.5 m`; total time in air `≈ 3.06 s` (time up = time down by symmetry)
4. `Δx₁ = 300 m`, `Δx₂ = 30(8) + ½(1.5)(8²) = 288 m`; total `= 588 m`
5. Trapezoid shape; total distance = area = `½(5)(20) + 10(20) + ½(4)(20) = 50 + 200 + 40 = 290 m`
6. `t ≈ 3.03 s`; `v ≈ 29.7 m/s` (downward)
7. Combined closing speed `25 m/s`; `t = 4 s`; meeting point `60 m` from the `15 m/s` car's start

## Real-World Applications
- **Braking distance and reaction time** — why stopping distance scales with the *square* of speed (from `Δx = v²/2a`), which is why speed limits matter more than they'd seem linearly.
- **100 m sprinting** — analyzing a sprinter's velocity-time graph to find their acceleration phase vs. top-speed phase.
- **Elevators** — passengers feel heavier/lighter during acceleration phases, not during constant-velocity travel.
- **Airbag and crumple-zone design** — increasing the *time* over which a car decelerates in a crash (a kinematics + momentum crossover topic covered further in Topic 4).
