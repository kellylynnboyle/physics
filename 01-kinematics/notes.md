# Unit 1 — Kinematics — Cornell Notes

**Essential question:** How do we describe motion mathematically without worrying about *why* it happens?

**NGSS connection:** Foundational to HS-PS2-1 (relating force, mass, and acceleration) — you need to be fluent in describing acceleration before you can analyze its cause.

---

## Cue column | Notes

| Cue | Notes |
|---|---|
| What's the difference between **distance** and **displacement**? | Distance = total path length traveled (scalar, always positive). Displacement = straight-line change in position, from start to end (vector, has direction, can be negative/zero even if the object moved). |
| What's the difference between **speed** and **velocity**? | Speed = distance/time (scalar). Velocity = displacement/time (vector). An object can have constant speed but changing velocity (e.g., circular motion) because direction changes. |
| Average vs. instantaneous velocity? | Average velocity = Δx/Δt over an interval. Instantaneous velocity = velocity at one instant = slope of the **tangent line** on a position-time graph at that point. |
| What is **acceleration**? | Rate of change of velocity: a = Δv/Δt. Units: m/s². Acceleration can speed an object up, slow it down, or change its direction — sign matters, not just magnitude. |
| How do you read a **position-time graph**? | Slope = velocity. Steeper slope = faster. Flat line = at rest. Curved line = accelerating. Line crossing the x-axis = object passes through the origin/reference point. |
| How do you read a **velocity-time graph**? | Slope = acceleration. Area under the curve = displacement. A line above the x-axis moving toward zero means slowing down while still moving in the positive direction. |
| Why do students confuse "negative acceleration" with "slowing down"? | Negative acceleration means acceleration points in the negative direction — it only means *slowing down* if velocity is positive. If velocity is already negative, negative acceleration means **speeding up** in the negative direction. |
| What are the **kinematic equations** (constant acceleration only)? | 1) v = v₀ + at 2) x = x₀ + v₀t + ½at² 3) v² = v₀² + 2a(x − x₀) 4) x = x₀ + ½(v₀ + v)t — pick the one missing the variable you don't have. |
| What is **free fall**? | Motion under gravity alone, a = −9.8 m/s² (or 9.8 m/s² downward), ignoring air resistance. Same kinematic equations apply with a = g. |
| Common free-fall trap? | An object thrown straight up has velocity = 0 at the peak, but acceleration is **still −9.8 m/s²** the entire time (never zero) — gravity doesn't turn off at the top. |

## Key vocabulary
position (x) · displacement (Δx) · distance · speed · velocity (v) · average velocity · instantaneous velocity · acceleration (a) · scalar vs. vector · free fall · terminal velocity · reference frame · origin

## Key formulas
- Average velocity: v̄ = Δx/Δt
- Average acceleration: ā = Δv/Δt
- v = v₀ + at
- x = x₀ + v₀t + ½at²
- v² = v₀² + 2a(x − x₀)
- Free fall: a = g = 9.8 m/s² downward

---

## Summary (write in your own words after class)
Kinematics describes *how* something moves — position, velocity, and acceleration — using graphs and equations, without asking what caused the motion. Slope and area on motion graphs carry physical meaning (slope of x-t = v, slope of v-t = a, area under v-t = Δx). The four kinematic equations only work when acceleration is constant.

## Real-world applications
- Reaction time and stopping distance in driver's ed / traffic safety
- Radar gun speed detection (average vs. instantaneous velocity)
- Elevators and roller coasters (acceleration you can feel)
- Sprinting analytics in track and field (velocity-time graphs of 100m dash)
- Rocket launch ascent profiles

## Practice problems (by difficulty)
**Level 1 — plug and chug**
1. A car accelerates from 0 to 27 m/s in 6.0 s. Find its acceleration.
2. A ball is dropped from rest and falls for 3.0 s. How far does it fall?

**Level 2 — multi-step**
3. A train traveling at 20 m/s brakes at −2.5 m/s². How far does it travel before stopping?
4. A rock is thrown straight up at 15 m/s. How long until it returns to the thrower's hand? What is its velocity when caught?

**Level 3 — graph interpretation / conceptual**
5. Sketch a velocity-time graph for a car that speeds up, cruises at constant speed, then brakes to a stop. Shade the region representing total distance traveled.
6. Two runners start at the same point. Runner A has constant velocity; Runner B starts at rest and accelerates uniformly. Sketch both on the same position-time graph and mark where they meet.

## Learning objectives
- I can distinguish distance from displacement and speed from velocity.
- I can calculate average and instantaneous velocity and acceleration from data or graphs.
- I can read and sketch position-time, velocity-time, and acceleration-time graphs and connect slope/area to physical quantities.
- I can apply the kinematic equations to solve 1D constant-acceleration and free-fall problems.
